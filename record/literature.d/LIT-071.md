# LIT-071: CoCa: Contrastive Captioners are Image-Text Foundation Models

Yu et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2205.01917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01917)

## Key takeaways

- One encoder-decoder trained with **both** a contrastive and a captioning loss,
  via a single change: **the first half of the decoder layers omit
  cross-attention**, so they produce a **unimodal** text representation for the
  contrastive loss, while the cascaded remainder cross-attends to the image
  encoder for captioning
- The two objectives **share one computational graph and one forward pass**, so
  the second costs almost nothing. Contrastive wants a text embedding that has
  not seen the image and generative wants one that has — and "has not seen it
  yet" is what early decoder layers are if you withhold cross-attention
- Pretrained end-to-end from scratch on web alt-text **and** annotated images by
  **treating all labels simply as text**, so heterogeneous supervision enters one
  objective
- ImageNet **86.3% zero-shot**, **90.6%** frozen-encoder with a learned head,
  **91.0%** fine-tuned; state of the art across recognition, retrieval,
  multimodal understanding and captioning

distributions by making the timestep a per-modality *value*; CoCa unifies two
objectives by making fusion a function of *depth*. Both **replace an
architectural branch with a continuous variable**, and both get a capability or a
saving from it. Two independent instances is when a pattern is worth stating,
and the record states it nowhere.
