SmoothGrad was the strongest of the four `#290` reversals left, and the previous
two units kept promoting it: named by `LIT-725`'s Theorem 4.1 as the
kernel-smoothing meta-algorithm, by `LIT-729` as a gradient method that does not
converge, by `LIT-730` as one of two methods max-pooling does not move. Eight
held documents in total, and it is the one method in this cluster with a live
recommendation behind it — `SOTA-435` step 3.

## The finding, stated the way that survives

**The one method nothing faults is the one with the least evidence.**

| account | what it says about SmoothGrad |
| --- | --- |
| `LIT-713` randomization tests | passes both |
| `THEORY-113` input multiplier | carries none |
| `THEORY-114` rank-1 convergence | gradient methods do not converge |
| `LIT-730` image recovery | leaves the saliency family alone |

And the paper itself reports **no quantitative result at all**, by its own
statement: "since quantitative evaluation of a map remains an unsolved problem,
we again focus on qualitative evaluation." Its two assessments — visual coherence
and discriminativity — are judged by eye. Its parameters, 10–20% noise and
`n ≈ 50`, were chosen by looking at pictures.

That reads at first like a contradiction with `SOTA-430`, which exists to say
*do not validate an attribution map by how it looks*. **It is not, and the reason
is the interesting part:** `SOTA-435` recommends smoothing on `LIT-725`'s
evidence — Theorem 4.1, which is proved, plus a measured improvement in
infidelity and max-sensitivity across three datasets — not on this paper's
figures. The practice is sourced to the paper that supplied numbers two years
later rather than to the paper that supplied the method.

**That was not deliberate**, and it is the second time this week that the record
turned out to be sourced correctly by accident. A practice sourced to the
measurement rather than the invention is the arrangement `ADR-017` wants; getting
there by filing the measurement first and the invention last is luck. What v2
adds is the parameters *with* their provenance attached, so the weakest part of
the step says it is the weakest part.

## §3.1, which is a section about colour scales

The reason to file this paper turned out not to be the method. It is a
visualization-practices section that contains informal statements of two things
the record spent the last two days building accounts for.

**On signed versus absolute:** "There is considerable ambiguity in how to convert
signed values to colors." And the dataset dependence, spelled out — MNIST digits
are always white so positive gradients mean something; on ImageNet "taking the
absolute value of the gradient produced clearer pictures".

`SOTA-430` step 3 is that same ambiguity treated as a **measurement** hazard, and
it is now the best-supported thing in that practice: three groups, three model
families, opposite verdicts from signed and absolute similarity. In 2017 it was a
rendering decision.

**On the input multiplier:** multiplying a gradient map by the input "does tend to
produce visually simpler and sharper images, although it can be unclear how much
of this can be attributed to sharpness in the original image itself. For example,
a black/white edge in the input can lead to an edge-like structure on the final
visualization **even if the underlying sensitivity map has no edges**."

That is `THEORY-113`. Adebayo suspected the multiplier in 2018 and wrote that he
did not measure it; Kokhlikyan measured it in 2021. **It is stated here in 2017**,
as a caution about heatmap rendering.

**Generalising, and this is the part worth keeping.** The record's habit is to
look for a mechanism claim in the section of a paper that argues, and to read a
"lessons learned" or "visualization notes" section as craft. Both of the accounts
above were sitting in craft sections. **A mechanism stated as a rendering tip is
invisible to a search for mechanisms**, and this is now the third distinct way
this cluster hid its own content: behind a collapsed `<details>` decline, inside a
`promote_when`, and in a section about choosing colours.

`THEORY-113` v4 records the antecedent. The account does not change; who first
wrote it down does, and this record treats that as a real defect class — three
`introduced_by` defects in four days, by `#342`'s own count.

## A question and its answer, filed in reverse order

SmoothGrad's future work: "It remains an open question to understand which
properties affect the discriminativity of a given method — e.g. understanding why
Guided BackProp seems to show the weakest discriminativity."

`LIT-730`, filed in the previous unit, answers exactly that: guided backprop is
doing partial image recovery, which is class-insensitive by construction. The
record acquired the answer yesterday and the question today, which is the fourth
time this week that the order of filing has been the reverse of the order of
publication.

## What is not filed

**No practice.** The parameters go on `SOTA-435`, which already recommends the
smoothing; a second practice about one technique is the fragmentation `LIT-728`
avoided for occlusion sensitivity.

**No theory.** The paper offers no account of *why* averaging over input noise
sharpens a map beyond observing that the gradient fluctuates at small scales.

**The 99th-percentile capping is unheld and should probably stay that way for
now.** "Without this post-processing step, maps may end up almost entirely black"
is a display artefact that can be mistaken for a finding, which makes it exactly
the kind of thing `SOTA-430` would want said — but it is one sentence of craft
advice from one group, and the record has no practice about rendering a heatmap to
attach it to.

## Where this leaves the cluster

Ten papers, three accounts, two practices, and a decline class that was closed
behind two unfiled openers until three days ago.

Three `#290` reversals remain — Grad-CAM, InputXGradient, plain-gradient
saliency — and they are now plainly referent gaps rather than measurement gaps.
Every verdict about them is stated correctly from `LIT-713`, and each sits inside
an account the record holds: InputXGradient under `THEORY-113`, the plain
gradient and Grad-CAM outside all three mechanisms. Filing them would add
documents to point at.

`SOTA-430` is at v6 and its oldest outstanding defect is still its own: the
headline example leans on a figure that both Adebayo and Sixt amended, and the
fix is to state that verdict from the SSIM curves. Nothing arriving will fix it,
which is why it has survived six versions in one day.
