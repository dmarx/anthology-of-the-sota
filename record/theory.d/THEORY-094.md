# THEORY-094: Word vectors approximate PMI in low dimensions, and relations appear as clean directions despite a noisy fit, because text behaves as if generated by a slowly drifting discourse over isotropic word vectors

## Source

Arora, Li, Liang, Ma and Risteski (2016), [LIT-613](../literature.d/LIT-613.md).

## The account

**The model.** A discourse vector drifts slowly over the unit sphere. At each
step a word is emitted with probability ∝ exp(⟨c, v_w⟩). Word vectors point
in all directions about equally (isotropy), and their lengths vary, which
produces a power law of word frequencies.

**Why PMI is low-rank.** With isotropic vectors, the normalizer
Σ_w exp(⟨c, v_w⟩) is nearly the same for every discourse direction. So the
discourse can be integrated out in closed form: two words close together
share almost the same c, and averaging exp(⟨c, v_w + v_w'⟩) over the sphere
gives exp(‖v_w + v_w'‖²/2d). Taking logs, PMI(w, w') ≈ ⟨v_w, v_w'⟩/d, up to
a constant set by the window size. This holds at practical dimensions, which
[THEORY-093](THEORY-093.md)'s argument could not reach.

**Why relations are lines despite error.** Each fitted inner product is
noisy, with about 17% termwise error for PMI. That noise is larger than the
margin between the right and wrong answers to an analogy. But if a relation
shifts log co-occurrence ratios by a common factor, then v_a − v_b solves a
linear regression whose design matrix is the whole set of word vectors.
Isotropy makes that matrix well conditioned, so the regression averages the
noise down by √(d/n). The offset comes out clean even though no individual
inner product is.

## What it explains

- **Self-normalization.** Log-linear language models work when the
  partition function is ignored, because under isotropy it barely varies.
- **GloVe's bias terms**, as squared norms, and its weighted least-squares
  form, as an approximate likelihood.
- **Why low dimensions help analogies.** Isotropy needs d ≪ n. "Smaller
  models generalize better" is not the reason: the objective never
  mentions analogies.
- **Why relation offsets share one direction.** Offsets load on the first
  singular direction of their relation (mean cosine 0.51) and not the second
  (0.035).

## Where it is weak

- **Isotropy is the load-bearing assumption and is checked loosely.**
  Singular-value ratios of 1.4 and 10.1 are both called small constants.
  Later work on contextual and word embeddings reports strong anisotropy
  (a few dominant directions), and the record has not filed it.
- **The PMI-level approximation error is not small**, as the authors state.
- **The generative model is a caricature.** It has no word order and no
  syntax, and the most frequent words are down-weighted because they do not
  fit.
- **Relations must satisfy the ratio premise** of [THEORY-089](THEORY-089.md), which is itself
  assumed.

## Relation to other accounts

It `extends` [THEORY-093](THEORY-093.md): the same target (shifted PMI), derived from a
generative model instead of an optimum, and valid at low rank. It `extends`
[THEORY-089](THEORY-089.md): the same offsets-from-ratios mechanism, with the missing
denoising step. [LIT-606](../literature.d/LIT-606.md)'s account of linear representations ([THEORY-090](THEORY-090.md))
takes directions as given and asks what they mean. This account says why
they exist in word vectors at all.
