**The question came from outside and the answer was one document.** Asked
whether the record has a grokking line, I grepped and found `LIT-085`, its
reading, and a handful of passing mentions — all pointing back at the same
paper. Not a line. One paper, plus an `Active` practice staking a claim on it.

**`SOTA-200`'s history already recorded this failure once.** Its v2 note, from
the day before yesterday, says the practice "previously recommended a check
while the record held neither" the paper that made the argument nor the claim
it argues against. That was fixed for the first two checks. The third one —
grokking — was left resting on `LIT-085` alone, and `LIT-085` is itself a
paper the record once had `Rejected` and promoted specifically because this
practice needed it.

**Reading the trunk changed what the practice should say, which is the useful
outcome.** I expected `LIT-tmp4uno1` to be a formality: name the phenomenon,
cite it, move on. It is not. The data-fraction dependence is *in the founding
paper*, measured and reported as a headline — converged accuracy flat across a
range of training fractions, time-to-generalize exploding as the fraction
falls, 1% less data near 25–30% costing 40–50% more steps. So `LIT-085` did not
arrive from outside and deflate a mystery; it put a threshold on a dependence
the original authors had already measured. `SOTA-200` read the other way round.

`DP-010` in a single figure: Figure 1's left panel is the thousand-fold gap
everybody reproduced, and Figure 1's centre panel is the curve saying the gap
is a function of what you withheld.

**Then Omnigrok made the check wrong rather than incomplete.** "Data-starved
regime" names one axis. `LIT-tmpkn1i6` produces grokking on MNIST, IMDb and
QM9, and does it by shrinking the training set **and** inflating the
initialization — at standard initialization there is none on any of the three.
It also removes grokking on algorithmic data by constraining the weight norm.
`LIT-tmp069e7` adds a third axis, initial kernel–task alignment, which is the
only one of the three a practitioner could compute before training. So the
check is not "was the data starved" but **move the regime and see whether the
discontinuity moves with it** — which is a better check, and one the practice
could not have stated from the paper it had.

**The unit grew from three papers to four for a reason I did not anticipate.**
I had planned trunk + Omnigrok + one mechanistic account. Reading
`LIT-tmp069e7`'s §3 changed that: it is an explicit counterexample to
**both** weight-norm accounts — modular arithmetic, two-layer MLP, no weight
decay, and the parameter norm going *up* through the transition. Filing circuit
efficiency alone, which on evidential shape is the most impressive thing here,
would have put a mechanism in the record that a published counterexample
contradicts.

**Four theory documents, and only one `Active`.** The status vocabulary says
`Active` means "the best explanation the record holds for why this happens".
With a counterexample to two accounts and two unexplained phenomena against the
third, no mechanism earns that. What does is the regime claim: every one of
the four papers supports it, including the one arguing hardest against the
others — `LIT-tmp069e7`'s own second condition is a dataset-size window. A
claim that survives the disagreement of every account of the thing it describes
is in a different evidential position from any of them, and that is the whole
reason `THEORY-tmpdlyut` exists as a separate document.

**The `corrects` declaration took care.** `THEORY-tmplnntp` corrects both
weight-norm accounts, which is right — the paper names them and offers a
counterexample, the same test that settled the Weyl/σReparam relation three
units ago. What I had to resist was letting `corrects` imply *replaces*.
`THEORY-tmpmiiyl` predicted ungrokking and semi-grokking before anyone had
seen them, including the sub-prediction that ungrokking's endpoint is
independent of weight decay, and the lazy-to-rich account says nothing about
either. So the correction narrows its predecessor's scope and does not cover
it, and that sentence is in `promote_when` rather than buried in prose.

**One near-miss worth recording.** Writing `THEORY-tmpz9vjh` I typed an
`inactive-ok-file` directive naming `THEORY-tmp069e7` — a code that does not
exist; I had crossed a LIT code with a THEORY one. A directive naming nothing
vouches for nothing, which is the failure `ADR-039` is about, and it would have
shipped silently because an unresolvable code in a comment is not the same
finding as an unresolvable code in prose. Caught on reread, before the lint.
The real acknowledgements were written afterwards from the regenerated report.

**What the record now says it does not know.** Nobody has an account covering
both a rising-norm unregularized grokking run and a sharp ungrokking
transition. `LIT-tmp4uno1` reports binary operations that never generalize at
any data fraction up to 95% and nothing explains which ones. And every positive
result outside algorithmic data changes two knobs at once, so the axes are
established jointly rather than separately. Those are in the open-questions
sections rather than smoothed away, because the shape of this cluster is three
partial explanations, and a reader deserves to be told that rather than handed
the most impressive one.
