The owner asked how the anthology covered graph representation learning
and current graph neural networks. It held nine documents under
`graphs-and-networks`. Three papers had been read, GCN and the Ricci-flow
paper had not, and a graph-transformer practice ([SOTA-349](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-349.md)) did not carry
the topic. The owner asked for the gaps to be filled.

## The query, so a reader can see what it could not find

The candidate list was written from memory of the field, in seven
clusters: node and knowledge-graph embeddings, core message-passing
architectures and their benchmarks, expressive power past 1-WL and
positional encodings, over-smoothing and over-squashing, graph
transformers, geometric GNNs and simulators, and language models given
graphs. Forty papers, every one checked absent from the record by arXiv id
before filing. Each was read from its PDF. A NOTE says how far, 38 Read
and 6 Skimmed, including the two previously unread.

**What this query cannot have returned** ([DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4)): anything a reader of
the field would not name from memory. That means heterophily, temporal
and dynamic graphs, graph generation, recommendation, scalable training
(sampling, partitioning, Cluster-GCN), spectral filters past GCN, graph
pooling, and most work after 2024. Nothing here says those are covered.

## A validation score captioned as a test score

GIN's Table 1 has been the record's evidence for sum aggregation: 92.4
and 57.5 on featureless Reddit graphs, with mean aggregation at chance.
Four documents called those numbers test accuracy. GIN's own §6 says they
are "validation accuracies across the 10 folds", with the epoch count
chosen on the same folds. Errica et al. pointed this out in 2019 and
reran GIN with selection inside each training fold: 87.0 and 53.8. The
reader who filed Errica et al. reported it as unverified; checking took
one grep of GIN's experimental section.

This is [DP-010](../design-principles.md#dp-10) again, with a different mechanism. The hedge was not
buried in a footnote. It was in plain text in the setup paragraph, and the
paper contradicts it a page later: Table 1's caption reads "Test set
classification accuracies". The table and its caption are what travel. The practice
stands, because a mean aggregator at chance has failed to separate the
graphs at all and no protocol would lift it. [SOTA-351](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-351.md) and [THEORY-083](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-083.md) now
quote both numbers.

## Smaller things found

- [LIT-766](../../record/literature.d/LIT-766.md) said the Ricci-flow paper had no sensitivity analysis over its
  measure's parameters. Its supplement sweeps the power and the base.
- LRGB's untuned baseline rows were copied unchanged into GPS and then
  Exphormer, so the handicap [LIT-579](../../record/literature.d/LIT-579.md) found travelled two papers further
  than [SOTA-349](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-349.md) had said.
- GCN's own random-split row is lower than its fixed-split headline on
  every dataset, by 7.6 points on NELL. The paper reports both.
- GraphToken's text-prompt baselines match Talk like a Graph's PaLM 62B
  averages digit for digit, while GraphToken runs on Flan-PaLM 2 S. The
  paper does not say so.

## Left open

- NetMF `extends` Levy and Goldberg ([LIT-612](../../record/literature.d/LIT-612.md)), which is true and joins
  the graph lineage to the word-embedding one. Lint reports that the
  joined line shares no tag. Each adjacent pair shares one, and no honest
  tag binds GIN to word2vec. That is a question about what a lineage is
  for, not about either paper, and it is left for a decision.
- [SOTA-350](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-350.md) says never one fixed split, and [SOTA-450](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-450.md) says use a fixed
  split shaped like the application. Both conditions sections reconcile
  them, and the titles do not yet.
