# LIT-095: The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Deep Features as a Perceptual Metric

Zhang et al. (2018) — [ARXIV-1801.03924](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03924)

## Key takeaways

- A large human perceptual-similarity dataset is collected first, so the question "which metric agrees with people?" becomes answerable rather than argued.
- Against it, distances computed in the feature space of a pretrained network outperform every classical metric — L2, SSIM, FSIM — by a wide margin. The metric is LPIPS.
- The striking part is the insensitivity: the result holds across architectures (SqueezeNet, AlexNet, VGG) and across supervised, self-supervised and even unsupervised pretraining. Perceptual structure appears to be a general property of learned visual representations rather than a property of one training signal.
- Calibrating a small linear layer on top of the features improves agreement further, which is how LPIPS is normally used.

## Standing in the anthology

Nothing in the record cites this note, and the anthology is about training rather than evaluation, so it carries no practice. It is filed as a real paper rather than retired because the corpus's generative-vision half ([LIT-070](LIT-070.md), [LIT-073](LIT-073.md), [LIT-118](LIT-118.md)) reports FID and perceptual distances throughout, and this is the paper those numbers rest on.
