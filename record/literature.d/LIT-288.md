# LIT-288: Achieving Tighter Finite-Time Rates for Heterogeneous Federated Stochastic Approximation under Markovian Sampling

Zhu et al. (2025) — [ARXIV-2504.11645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11645)

## Key takeaways

Proposes FedHSA, the first federated stochastic approximation algorithm that
simultaneously handles agent heterogeneity (different local operator roots),
Markovian data, and sparse communication, while achieving an M-fold linear
speedup in sample complexity with no heterogeneity-induced bias term. Closes
a fundamental open problem in federated reinforcement learning theory.

- **Proposition 1 (FedAvg-style SA bias under heterogeneity).** Vanilla
  local-update federated SA with constant step size converges to a point
  that differs from the global root theta* by a non-vanishing bias
  proportional to the discrepancy between local operator roots and the
  global root.
  *Holds when:* Noiseless case (sigma=0); constant step size; heterogeneous
  agents with distinct local roots.
- **Theorem 2 (FedHSA convergence).** FedHSA converges exponentially fast to
  a ball around theta* with radius proportional to the step size; no
  heterogeneity-induced bias term appears in the bound.
  *Holds when:* Strongly monotone, Lipschitz SA operators; Markovian data; H
  local steps; two-timescale step sizes.
- **Corollary 1 (Sample complexity with decaying step size).** With linearly
  decaying global step size, FedHSA achieves sample complexity
  O-tilde(1/(MHT)) to reach epsilon-accuracy, giving M-fold linear speedup
  over single-agent SA.
  *Holds when:* Linearly decaying alpha_g; same operator and sampling
  assumptions as Theorem 2; complexity hides log factors and mixing time
  tau_mix.

## What the evidence does not cover

- Requires agents to transmit their local operator evaluated at the new
  global parameter each round, adding one extra communication per round
  compared to vanilla FedAvg.
- Convergence guarantees require Lipschitz and strong-monotonicity
  assumptions that may not hold for general nonlinear RL operators.
- Agents' Markov chains must share a common finite state space, limiting
  applicability to some continuous-state RL settings.
- Analysis assumes independent data across agents; correlated inter-agent
  data is not handled.
