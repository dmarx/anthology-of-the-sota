**Auditing the curation log for practices skipped the way batch A's were
found three, and then found two more by the opposite failure.** The first
three are the pattern already named: a recommendation refused because nobody
at frontier scale has adopted it, which is the `Active` bar applied to the
question of whether the document should exist. The other two had no story at
all, and that is the more interesting half.

## The three refused for adoption

- **The hyper-connections trunk.** [ADR-015](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-015.md) says outright: "The trunk
  practice that paragraph describes is deliberately not filed here… converting
  a hedge into an assertion is an editorial act." But `Proposed` *is* the
  hedge, so filing it converts nothing. This one is the worst shape of the
  three, because the record had already filed both **branches** — the
  doubly-stochastic constraint and the free mixing it replaced — and not the
  trunk they fork from. The disputed part had a node and the agreed part did
  not, which is how the record ended up recommending a layer ratio defined in
  terms of a structure it had never stated.
- **Data mixing laws** ([LIT-201](../../record/literature.d/LIT-201.md)): refused because "none of the frontier
  reports in this record says it used a mixing law". The note itself draws
  the parallel to [SOTA-142](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-142.md), which sets the peak learning rate by a fitted
  power law and *is* filed. Same move, one filed and one not.
- **Mamba-3** ([LIT-165](../../record/literature.d/LIT-165.md)): "Nothing in the record ships Mamba-3, so it stays
  a note."

## The two nobody refused, because nobody noticed

**Deduplication** and **multi-token prediction** were not skipped by an
argument. No note says "not filed as a practice" about either. They simply
never came up, because *a step everyone agrees on generates no debate to
notice*.

[LIT-202](../../record/literature.d/LIT-202.md)'s own standing section had said it plainly for weeks — "the
foundation under a step that appears in every pipeline this record describes
and had no source" — and the sentence reads as a compliment to the paper
rather than as a hole in the registry. [LIT-163](../../record/literature.d/LIT-163.md) is the same: five model
reports ship an MTP head and each mentions it as settled, so there was
nothing to argue about and therefore nothing to write.

## The shape, which is the useful part

The two failures are mirror images and they have opposite tells:

|  | refused | unnoticed |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | a paper too new to endorse | a technique too settled to discuss |
| **Tell** | a sentence saying "not filed as a practice" | silence |
| **How found** | grep the record for the refusal | ask which notes no practice sources |

Only the first leaves evidence. Grepping for refusals is a complete method
for one half of the problem and finds nothing at all of the other — which is
the same lesson the earlier entry drew about passes finding the defect their
query is shaped like, arrived at again from a different direction. The
inverse query here is *which notes does no practice source?*, and it returns
115 of 218.

Most of those 115 are correct: the pre-migration vision and generative
corpus, which the practice scheme cannot express because `tags.yaml` has no
vision category, and the model reports, which are adoption rather than
evidence ([ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md)). Narrowing to post-migration notes carrying a
`SOTA`-eligible primary topic leaves 38, of which these five are the ones the
curation log pointed at. The other 33 are a sweep, filed as an issue rather
than done here, because each needs the same judgement — is there an
instruction in this paper — and thirty-three of those in one sitting is how a
taxonomy gets baked in badly.

## One thing the lint got right

`SOTA-132` was cited with an `inactive-ok-block:` written from memory of when
it was `Proposed`. It is `Active` now, and the lint said so: "annotation no
longer applies". That is the acknowledgement mechanism working in the
direction nobody designs for — catching a vouch that has outlived its reason,
rather than a citation that lacks one.
