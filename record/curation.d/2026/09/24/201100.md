`#331`'s last follow-up asked for a practice on training at native size
rather than fixed crops. It was to start from NaViT, the paper Sora cites
for the idea. Reading NaViT narrowed the practice.

## What the paper measures, and what it is remembered for

NaViT is remembered as "native resolution beats square crops". What it
measures is a package: aspect ratio, sampled resolution, token dropping and
sequence packing, against a square 224 ViT at matched compute. The package
wins across 12 budgets. The paper then says why: "the chief contributor
here is the significant increase in the number of training examples
processed". That is about 5x more images, because small, packed images are
cheap.

Pulling the parts apart:

- **Variable resolution** is cleanly isolated. Fig. 5 has native aspect in
  both arms at equal FLOPs.
- **Aspect ratio** is isolated once, in a linear probe on fairness
  attributes over frozen features (p = 0.02). That is a statement about
  probe inputs, not pretraining.
- **Its pretraining does not use native resolution.** It samples
  r ~ U(64, 256) and resizes to that area (App. A.1). The authors chose
  sampling for control over throughput.

So the practice is filed at the width the evidence supports. The title
still names aspect ratio, because that is what the package lets you keep.
The table in the body says which part rests on what. This is [DP-010](../design-principles.md#dp-10) again:
"native resolution" is the citable part, and it is the part with the least
support.

## Found along the way

- **The out-of-distribution gains depend on how the square baseline is
  evaluated.** With ViT's aspect-preserving centre crop, ObjectNet is
  about tied (57.7 against 57.9). With a plain square resize, the gap is 9
  points (39.8 against 48.8). Part of NaViT's robustness result is the
  square baseline's resize distorting extreme-aspect images.
- **[SOTA-251](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-251.md) is the rival and nobody has compared them.** [SOTA-251](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-251.md) holds
  resolution low and raises it in the decay phase. NaViT mixes resolutions
  throughout and objects that staged schedules cannot be reversed. Both
  are `Proposed`, and each is one lab's recipe. Recorded as a relation in
  prose, not as `contested_by`: neither paper tests the other's schedule.
- **No generative evidence exists yet.** Sora and SDXL's crop conditioning
  are qualitative. The generative half of `#331`'s follow-up stays open,
  and `promote_when` says what would close it.
