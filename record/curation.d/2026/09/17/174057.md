The report was that the anthology had no dropout. It was correct and it was
worse than it sounded: no practice, no explanation, and **not one of the 327
notes was about the paper**. The word appears in 22 documents. Ten of them are
the federated and decentralized literature, where "dropout" means a node
failing — a different word that happens to be spelled the same. The other
twelve mention the regularizer, every one of them in passing: an operation a
fused kernel has to cover (`SOTA-088`, `SOTA-114`, `SOTA-081`), one sequence
parallelism shards (`SOTA-058`), a rate somebody set (`LIT-119`), a training
choice that turned out to correlate with modularity (`LIT-031`), a source of
noise that complicates an objective (`LIT-241`). Twelve mentions, zero
citations, because until now there was nothing to cite.

## The explanation offered was wrong, and the true one is worse

The report came with a hypothesis: that this should have been caught by the
import from `dmarx/anthology-of-modern-ml`, which does hold dropout — and
holds Baldi and Sadowski, Gal and Ghahramani, and the batch-norm/dropout
variance-shift paper besides, under the headings `dropout`, `dropout as
ensemblification`, and `batch-norm and dropout in tug-of-war`.

**There was no such import.** The history has exactly one bulk import, the
pre-migration YAML in `data/` — 119 papers, 2014 through 2023, `ADR-008`. Every
paper since arrived one reading at a time. The connection to
`anthology-of-modern-ml` is a *link in the README*, added by [#98](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/98) in the same
change that read the vision and generative cluster, and nothing was ever taken
across it. The two repositories have been adjacent and disconnected for the
whole life of this one.

Worth being exact about why the confusion is reasonable: [#98](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/98) was a pass over a
large inherited cluster, it was recent, and it touched the README. It looked
like an import from the outside, and the commit it produced is the only place
the other anthology is named. But the corpus it read was the one already here.

So the gap was never a dropped item from a queue. Nothing had ever put dropout
*in* a queue. And that is `DP-007` exactly: the filing habit runs on arrivals,
agreement has no arrival, and **the twelve passing mentions are what the
absence looks like from the inside** — twelve authors each assuming the reader
knows, which is precisely the signal the principle says you cannot find by
reading what is here.

The `LIT-119` mention is the sharpest version. A 2026 model report reaches for
dropout 0.1 under heavy repetition of a small corpus, the record filed that
note, and the record still had no dropout. The refinement filed before the
thing it refines, in the principle's own words.

## What the reading changed

Two things I expected to confirm and did not.

**The practice is conditional, and the 2014 paper is where the condition is
measured.** I expected §7.4 to support "dropout helps small-data regimes". It
supports something stricter and more useful, because it reports *both* edges:
on MNIST at 100 and 500 examples dropout gives **no improvement at all** — the
model overfits through the noise — and the gain then rises with data size "up
to a point and then declines". A practice derived from the left edge alone
would say *more data, more dropout*; from the right edge alone, the opposite.
The curve supports a ratio claim, which is why `SOTA-238` is stated as one.

**The two explanations are one paper's, and they are not independent
evidence.** The natural filing is "ensemble averaging" and "adaptive
regularization" as two accounts converging on dropout from different
directions. Baldi and Sadowski derive both, and the regularization result for
a sigmoidal unit *uses* the NWGM averaging result via a Taylor expansion.
`THEORY-015` and `THEORY-016` each say so in their own bodies, because
citing them as agreement would be counting one derivation twice.

The averaging result also turns out to be sharper than its reputation. What
one forward pass with scaled weights computes is the **normalized weighted
geometric mean**, exactly, and the only functions with that property are the
constants and the logistic functions. The test-time trick is a fact about
sigmoids. The familiar gloss — "averaging exponentially many networks" — elides
both the word *geometric* and the reason the rule works at all, and every
result in this line is for sigmoidal units, not the ReLU networks dropout is
mostly used in now.

## What was left undone

Three papers from the same cluster are filed nowhere and each is a real
document: Gal and Ghahramani on dropout as approximate Bayesian inference,
Li et al. on the variance shift between dropout and batch normalization, and
Hara et al. on dropout as ensemble learning. The second is the one with a
practice in it — an interaction, which is the shape `SOTA` can express — and
it is the one to file next.

And the sentence `SOTA-238` most wants and cannot have: that large-scale
pretraining sets dropout to zero *because* of the right edge of §7.4. The
practice says explicitly that this is the anthology reading a 2014 curve
forward and not a claim anyone has checked at scale. Confirming it means
reading pretraining recipes for what they set and why, which is a different
pass and a query nobody has run.
