# LIT-067: Progressive Distillation for Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models

Salimans et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2202.00512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512)

## Key takeaways

- **Progressive distillation**: distil an `N`-step DDIM sampler into a student
  taking `N/2` steps, whose target is two teacher steps; promote the student and
  repeat. **8192 → 4 steps, FID 3.0 on CIFAR-10**, for no more total compute
  than training the original model
- **This is where v-prediction comes from**, and the reason is diagnosed: as
  signal-to-noise → 0, "the effect of small changes in the neural network
  output on the implied prediction in x-space is increasingly amplified", and
  distillation drives evaluation toward exactly that end. The stated criterion
  is that the implied `x̂` must **stay stable as `λ_t = log(α²/σ²)` varies**
- Three parameterisations satisfy it — predict `x`; predict `x` and `ε` on
  separate channels and merge; or predict **`v ≡ α_t·ε − σ_t·x`**. All three
  also work for training an *original* diffusion model, which is how
  v-prediction escaped into general use
- The loss weighting must change too: DDPM's implicit `exp(λ_t)` weighting
  **gives weight zero to zero-SNR data**. Replacements are `max(α²/σ², 1)`
  ("truncated SNR") and `(1 + α²/σ²)`, which is exactly `‖v − v̂‖²`
- **Halving beats quartering** at fixed budget — better to take fewer parameter
  updates per halving than to skip distillation iterations
