The reading-time triage of this morning (`2026/09/25/160228`) declined 34
works on scope and asked, in writing, whether the anthology wants them. The
owner's answer: the out-of-scope reading gets its own catchall record,
*nucleation*, and **when in doubt, it goes in the anthology**. Nothing is
filed in both. 29 went there; these five came here.

All five are `Deferred`, seeded from the abstract and a skim and not read
in full, each with a `Skimmed` NOTE naming the sections the skim did and did
not cover. Their LITs say why each is on the boundary; what follows is what a
deeper reading has to settle, highest priority first.

- **Posani et al., *Rarely categorical, highly separable representations
  along the cortical hierarchy*** (`concept-geometry`, *Nature* 2026). High.
  It measures in cortex what the topic asks of networks — categorical clusters
  versus separable mixed-selective codes — and its caveat about what
  separability does and does not show bears on every linear-probe claim the
  topic holds. The skim did not cover the figures or the method that
  separates the task conditions, which is where the claim has to be checked.
  `published:` is the bioRxiv preprint's month (2024-11); that preprint, under
  another title, was not read.
- **Gallego & Orús, *Language Design as Information Renormalization***
  (`signal-structure`, 2017). Medium. It argues from how correlations in
  language decay with distance that deep convolutional networks fit language
  and RNNs and HMMs do not. It predates the transformer, and the claim about
  RNNs has to be read against Lin & Tegmark's finding that LSTMs do capture
  power-law correlations.
- **Belfiore & Bennequin, *Topos and Stacks of Deep Neural Networks***
  (`model-architecture`, `concept-geometry`). Medium. 152 pages, largely
  conjectural. Read chapter 1 and the definitions of semantic information in
  §3.4, and go further only if those yield a claim that can be checked. Its
  mathematics is nucleation's Abramsky & Brandenburger, which is that
  record's first-priority reading; the two should be read in that order.
- **Piao et al., *AgentSociety*** (`deployment-and-society`). Medium. A
  systems paper: more than ten thousand LLM agents as a stand-in for a
  society. Its claims to reproduce real findings rest on qualitative
  agreement, which is what a deeper reading should test.
- ***Rigor with Machine Learning from Field Theory to the Poincaré
  Conjecture*** (`analysis-and-evaluation`). Medium. The seed filed it under
  "other sciences"; it came here because its transferable claim is a method
  — let a learned model search and an exact procedure verify, as the RL
  ribbon search did in ruling out more than 800 candidate counterexamples.
  The primary papers it maps matter more than a second pass over the map.

No practice or theory is drawn from any of them. A `Skimmed` note is not
enough to source one, and that is the point of `Skimmed`.
