# LIT-130: Olmo 3

Olmo Team, Ai2 (2025) — [ARXIV-2512.13961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961)

## Key takeaways

- A family of fully open models at 7B and 32B, targeting long-context
  reasoning, function calling, coding, instruction following, chat and
  knowledge recall. The release is the *model flow*: every training stage,
  intermediate checkpoint, dataset and dependency, not only the final
  weights.
- Pretraining on Dolma 3; post-training on the Dolci datasets. The Think
  variants are built by the standard reasoning curriculum — a base model,
  then supervised fine-tuning on reasoning traces, then preference tuning,
  then reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards — and Olmo 3 Think 32B
  is described as the strongest fully open thinking model at release.
- The RL-Zero track runs RLVR directly on the base model, skipping the SFT
  stage, with four domain-focused series (math against a rule-based
  verifier with reference answers, code against test cases, instruction
  following, and general chat against an LLM judge). The authors frame it as
  an experimental pathway and an open benchmark for RL algorithms and for
  the effect of pretraining data on RL, not as the production recipe.
- The RL-Zero data is aggressively decontaminated, verified with negative
  controls trained against random rewards.

## Standing in the anthology

Here as the clearest open statement of the recipe [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) departs from. It
is the source for both the three-stage curriculum ([SOTA-129](../practices.d/SOTA-129.md)) and the
<!-- inactive-ok: SOTA-130 — the Proposed variation this paper is the source of -->
Zero-RL variation that drops the middle stage ([SOTA-130](../practices.d/SOTA-130.md)); the tiny-scale
anti-curriculum ([SOTA-123](../practices.d/SOTA-123.md)) collapses the first two stages instead. Keeping
all three lets a reader follow the sequence rather than only its current
end.

**Its architecture has since been tested twice by its own laboratory.** Olmo
Hybrid ([LIT-799](LIT-799.md)) keeps this model's 7B architecture, recipe and
evaluation and replaces the three-in-four sliding-window layers with Gated
DeltaNet layers. Against this model it reaches the same MMLU in 49% fewer
tokens and leads in every domain after mid-training. RULER at 64K is 76.9
against 70.9 with YaRN for both. Data mix, schedule and mid-training batch
also changed, so the comparison is close but not exact. The long-context
ablations ([LIT-801](LIT-801.md)) pretrain this architecture for 140B tokens beside
25 variants and extend them identically. With layerwise QK norm, post-norm
and sliding windows it scores 47.5 on HELMET at 32K, against 52.4 for the
Llama 3 architecture on the same data, and dropping QK norm and post-norm
recovers about 6 points. The authors offer this as why Olmo 3 Base is harder
to extend than Llama 3 Base.
