Issue [#139](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/139) lists four unreconciled contradictions and calls them the valuable
part of the backlog. The first is LAMB against Shallue: *"Whichever is right,
they cannot both be applied."* I took that one first.

It dissolved on contact with the papers, and the way it dissolved is the
finding.

**LAMB cites Shallue. Approvingly. As motivation.** Its related-work section
summarises `LIT-058` as showing that "learning rate scaling heuristics with the
batch size do not hold across all problems or across all batch sizes", and then
proposes a different optimizer rather than a better heuristic. The two papers
have never disagreed about anything.

**LAMB labels its own headline tables *untuned*.** Tables 4 and 5 say so in the
title. The body says three separate times that manual tuning does better — at a
batch of 16K, changing one learning rate moves SQuAD F1 from the reported 91.345
to 91.688. The claim was never that the square-root rule is *correct*. It is
that the rule is *sufficient to reach a fixed target without a sweep* under an
optimizer that normalizes per layer and per coordinate.

**And Shallue never swept an optimizer of that kind.** Three optimizers, all
SGD-family, all with one global step size. LAMB is outside its sample, not a
counterexample to it.

So the record had constructed a conflict out of two compatible claims by
collapsing *sufficient for one target* into *the heuristic is right*, and by
reading a result about three optimizers as a result about heuristics in general.
`SOTA-218` closed with a paragraph announcing the standoff; `NOTE-132` refused a
practice on its strength. Both are now the other thing.

The reconciliation needed a third document to hold it, and `THEORY-tmpih985` is
it: a scaling rule is a guess about how the usable step grows with the batch,
that step is bounded by the worst-conditioned direction the optimizer does not
normalize away, and an optimizer that normalizes more directions carries the
same rule further. Shallue's own discussion says this — momentum extends perfect
scaling past plain SGD, and "optimizers that estimate local curvature
information might be able to benefit more from large batches". That sentence is
in the paper most often cited against the position.

## The part that should worry someone

I went to the papers to settle an argument and came back with four wrong
numbers, none of which had anything to do with the argument.

`SOTA-218` was filed yesterday, well written, and said the study ran **71
million training runs across seven workloads and four optimizers**. The paper
ran **168,160 models across 35 workloads with three optimizers**; 71,638,836 is
its count of *loss measurements*, the published database of training curves.
The run count was off by a factor of 426. The practice also merged the two
ranges the paper reports separately — where perfect scaling ends (2^4 to 2^13)
and where the plateau begins (2^9 to 2^16) — into a single span running from the
bottom of the first to the top of the second, which is not a quantity the paper
names. `LIT-058` had the model-family and data-set counts transposed.

Every one of those figures is in the abstract or the conclusion. Nothing in the
record could have caught them: the lint checks that citations resolve and that
cited documents are live, and all of these citations resolved to a live document
that simply says something else.

This is exactly the loose end [#139](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/139) names — *"No reading in the port was
re-verified against its paper. The check that ran was on identifiers... nothing
says the error rate is lower in the fields nothing can check."* It is now one
observed instance rather than a worry, and it was found the only way it could
be: by needing a number badly enough to go and look.

`DP-004` is the principle, arriving from a direction it did not list. The
identifier check was a pass whose query was *does this code resolve*, and a
clean run of it is evidence about codes. The complement — *does this sentence
describe the paper* — has no mechanical query at all, which makes it the shape
of defect this record is least equipped to find and most likely to accumulate.

## What I did not do

Three contradictions remain on [#139](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/139) (DiLoCo's `H=500` against post-local SGD,
the duplicated noise-scale statistic, SlowMo against `SOTA-155`), five practice
candidates, and two class-closing ADRs. I took one, and took it all the way
down, on the theory that a contradiction half-reconciled is worse than one left
open — the second reader cannot tell which half was checked.
