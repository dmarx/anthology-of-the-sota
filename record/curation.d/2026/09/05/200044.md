Checking the newly filed notes against arXiv meant checking all of them, and
the result was worse than the batch that prompted it: **53 of 139 arXiv
identifiers did not resolve to the paper the note named.** This entry is
about the 31 that were not cosmetic, and particularly about the 16 that
turned out not to be citation errors at all.

## Two different failures wearing the same shape

The cosmetic 22 are unremarkable — a dropped subtitle, or a nickname the
record had prepended, `AdamW:` in front of *Decoupled Weight Decay
Regularization*. Worth fixing because a title is a claim about what was
published, and one of them was materially wrong: [LIT-031](../../record/literature.d/LIT-031.md) read "Neural
Networks are Surprisingly Modular" when the paper is "***Pruned*** Neural
Networks are Surprisingly Modular", which is a different and much weaker
statement.

The other 31 pointed at real papers on unrelated subjects. [LIT-099](../../record/literature.d/LIT-099.md) cited
measurement-device-independent quantum secret sharing as the PaLM 2
technical report. [LIT-059](../../record/literature.d/LIT-059.md) cited electron losses in hypersonic flows for
checkpointing advice. [LIT-063](../../record/literature.d/LIT-063.md) cited chromatic number and Hamiltonicity of
graphs for compiler optimization. The identifiers were syntactically
plausible and in the right year range, which is exactly why nothing caught
them: `2111.09432` looks like a checkpointing paper from late 2021 if you
don't fetch it.

## The part that mattered

Splitting the 31 by whether the recorded title named a findable work gave 15
that did and 16 that did not. The 16 share a signature:

- a generic title of the form *A Systematic Approach to X* or *X for
  Large-Scale Models*, naming no specific contribution;
- exactly four takeaway bullets, each a noun phrase, none a claim — "Pipeline
  optimization strategies", "Memory management techniques", "Bottleneck
  analysis";
- an author surname that is plausible for the field and unconnected to any
  such work. WebDataset was attributed to *Amodei et al.*

These were never citation errors. They are synthetic entries that entered
with the pre-migration YAML, and **39 practices — a little over a quarter of
the 139 in the registry — were sourced to them.** [SOTA-042](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-042.md) through [SOTA-045](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-045.md)
on data loading, [SOTA-054](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-054.md) through [SOTA-057](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-057.md) on checkpointing, [SOTA-081](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-081.md)
through [SOTA-084](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-084.md) on compilers: real, sensible advice, resting on nothing.

## Why we did not just retire them

The obvious move — mark the 16 `Rejected`, let the lint light up 39
practices — is what [DP-1](../design-principles.md#dp-1) seems to demand, and it is wrong. It converts a
quiet problem into a loud one without fixing anything: the advice in those
practices is not bad, it is *unattributed*, and deleting its attribution
leaves the reader worse off than a wrong citation did. A registry with a
quarter of its entries flagged is a registry nobody reads.

So each of the 16 was re-sourced in place to the work its practices actually
rest on, with `history:` recording what the entry used to claim. The
mappings were not hard to find once the question was asked properly — not
"what paper has this title" but "what published work is this practice an
instance of":

- checkpointing frequency, async writes, multi-level strategy → **CheckFreq**
- operator fusion, memory layout, hardware mapping → **TVM**
- fuse ops, align dimensions, memory access patterns → **Data Movement Is
  All You Need**
- tar archives, sharded streaming → the **actual WebDataset paper**, which
  exists, and whose real title is *High Performance I/O For Large Scale Deep
  Learning*
- loss spikes, gradient-norm ratios, NaN detection → **GLM-130B**, which is
  in the record precisely because it published its failures

Nine more practices did not need a new note at all: the right paper was
already in the corpus and nobody had connected them. [SOTA-050](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-050.md) says "scale
attention weights by 1/sqrt(head_dim)" and is now sourced to *Attention Is
All You Need*, which is where that comes from, and which the record has held
as [LIT-008](../../record/literature.d/LIT-008.md) the whole time.

## Two things found on the way

**A repair can create a duplicate.** Correcting [LIT-105](../../record/literature.d/LIT-105.md)'s identifier landed
it on 2302.06675, which [LIT-090](../../record/literature.d/LIT-090.md) already held — the migration had filed one
paper twice, once under its title and once under the name of the optimizer
it discovers. Nothing in `luria lint` looks at whether two documents name
one source, so the corpus held a contradiction (one note `Rejected`, one
`Proposed`, same paper) with a green build. Filed as luria#165.

Worse, [LIT-090](../../record/literature.d/LIT-090.md)'s retirement reason — "a research direction without
demonstrated practical impact" — had been written against the synthetic
four-bullet summary rather than against the paper, which is the Lion
optimizer with cross-domain results and a named production deployment. The
status is left as found, because reversing it is a curation call and not a
citation repair, but it is now labelled as inherited and unreviewed.

**Retirement is sometimes the lazy answer.** [LIT-042](../../record/literature.d/LIT-042.md) was `Superseded` with
the note "single-cycle cosine decay proved better at scale" — a correct
judgement, recorded against "SGDR++", which does not exist. Retiring the
note would have broken the schedule chain that PR [#16](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/16) builds on. Re-sourcing
it to the real SGDR keeps the chain and keeps the judgement, which now
applies to the paper that actually made the claim.

## What is left

Three places where a practice and its new source disagree are marked rather
than smoothed over. [SOTA-055](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-055.md) gives an epoch-based rule for saving optimizer
state; CheckFreq's whole argument is that iteration granularity is available
and epochs are the wrong unit. [SOTA-078](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-078.md) and [SOTA-080](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-080.md) give a buffer size and
a worker count that are operational folklore, not results in the WebDataset
paper. [SOTA-100](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-100.md) asks for warmup proportional to model size; its new source
shows the need for warmup is a property of Post-LN and largely disappears
under Pre-LN.

Those are re-readings for someone with the practices in front of them, not
citation work. The point of leaving them visible is that they are now
*answerable*: the practice names a paper you can go and read.
