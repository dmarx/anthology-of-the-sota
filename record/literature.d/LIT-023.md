# LIT-023: Root Mean Square Layer Normalization

Zhang et al. (2019) — [ARXIV-1910.07467](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07467)

## Key takeaways

- LayerNorm is credited with handling **re-centering and re-scaling** of both
  inputs and the weight matrix, and its computational overhead "significantly
  slows the underlying network, e.g. RNN in particular"
- **The central claim is a hypothesis**, in the paper's own word: "we
  hypothesize that re-centering invariance in LayerNorm is **dispensable**"
- **RMSNorm** normalizes by root mean square alone, keeping **re-scaling
  invariance** and an **implicit learning-rate adaptation** — the effective
  step on a weight vector scales inversely with its norm
- **7–64% less time per step**, the range depending on how much of the step
  the normalization was, at comparable quality
- **`pRMSNorm`** estimates the RMS from `p%` of the summed inputs, cheaper
  again and with its own uncharacterised error

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-182](../practices.d/SOTA-182.md).

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-022](../notes.d/NOTE-022.md). The practice is
**confirmed**.

One thing the reading adds: the paper frames its claim as a **hypothesis** it
tests, not a result it proves. What re-centering *does* is never measured
directly. `SOTA-182` is right that a centered LayerNorm is now the choice
needing justification — but that is the field's verdict, arrived at after this
paper proposed the question and was not contradicted.

Its near-twin is [LIT-025](LIT-025.md), same year, which also concludes part of LayerNorm
is unnecessary and picks **the other part**.
