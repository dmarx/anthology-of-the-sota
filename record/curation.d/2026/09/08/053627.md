title: 'Widening the scope, and the axis I built and took back out'
created: '2026-09-08T05:36:27'
tags: [ontology]
---

The scope was drawn around language-model training, and that turned out to be
wrong in both directions at once.

**Too narrow, already.** [SOTA-113](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-113.md) and [SOTA-115](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-115.md) are `Active` recommendations
about serving, filed under `systems-optimization`, and they had been sitting
outside the written scope for a year with nobody noticing. So had the
quantization practices. [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) asserted "scoped to language-model training by
construction" as a fact about the record; it was a fact about the sentence.

**Too broad, in the sense that mattered.** A scope drawn by domain declines
work whose transferable content is exactly the kind of claim the seven topics
collect. EDM contributes a preconditioning scheme, a noise-level sampling
distribution and a loss weighting. Segment Anything contributes a
model-in-the-loop annotation bootstrap. Those are a `training-optimization`
claim and a `data-pipeline` claim that happen to have been found in image
models.

So: scope by the kind of claim. That is the whole decision, and it needed no
new machinery — which is not where this started.

**The part worth writing down is the axis I built and then removed.** The
first draft added a `domain:` field to the practice scheme: a many-valued
vocabulary saying what a practice applies to, with `domain-general` marking
what transfers. It survived a design review with the user, got sharpened
(many-valued rather than scalar, because where a practice holds is a set that
accumulates), was implemented, backfilled, linted and pushed.

Then the objection that should have come first: **it lets a practice assert
where it applies while naming no evidence for the assertion.** In a record
whose first principle is [DP-001](../design-principles.md#dp-1), and which had just spent a contribution
establishing that `source:` holds the work that produced evidence about the
claim. The draft demonstrated its own failure in the same diff — three of the
eight backfills claimed `domain-general`, meaning "transfers across every
modality", each on the strength of one language-model paper.

Three more, any one sufficient:

- **It would be ~99% default.** One note in 218 is a cross-domain transfer
  result. A field that is overwhelmingly default is not neutral: absence gets
  read as a value. `consensus:` gets away with it because you assess consensus
  while touching the practice; you cannot assess domain without reading papers
  in another field.
- **It aims at the weakest link.** Blindness has a pipeline — never
  encountering a paper, refusing it on scope, filing a note and drawing no
  practice, filing a practice nobody finds. The scope widening fixes the
  second. [#85](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/85) says the third is the binding constraint: 53 bare notes. The
  field addresses only the fourth.
- **[DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8)'s own corollary** — a category added to admit a single document is
  how a scope stops being one — applied to the decision that amends [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8).

**What I got wrong about the alternatives, too.** I pitched "derive the
practice's domain from its sources" as the evidence-respecting option. It is
not available: only two of the twelve `LIT` topics carry domain information at
all, the other ten being kind-of-claim words. Deriving would mean first adding
a domain axis to the notes — so that option is not cheaper than the one it was
meant to replace, it is the same thing plus a second step.

The shape that *would* work is [ADR-016](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-016.md)'s: a value asserting that specific
other work exists must name it. `shown-to-transfer` requiring a
`transfers_shown_by:` reference needs no new machinery. Recorded in the ADR
along with the three-row decomposition, so the next person inherits the
reasoning rather than the field.

**The convergence that settled it.** The one cross-domain transfer result the
corpus already holds is [LIT-090](../../record/literature.d/LIT-090.md) — Lion, reported across vision,
vision-language, diffusion and language modelling. It sits `Rejected`, on an
inherited retirement its own note flags as unreviewed. It is at once the
evidence a transfer axis would need and a question the widened scope makes
live. Read that first; if a second one turns up, build the axis.

**Cost of the detour:** one design, one implementation, one backfill, all
reverted. Cheap in absolute terms and worth naming anyway, because the
objection that killed it — *what evidence backs this field?* — is the
project's own first principle, and I applied it to every other field in the
record before I applied it to the one I was adding.
