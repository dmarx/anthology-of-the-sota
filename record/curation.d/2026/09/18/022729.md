Finished [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137)'s benchmark tier. The interesting part was the check I had
promised to run rather than the filing.

## The split, and it is not a split

Yesterday I left Stream of Search / Countdown as the largest remaining
candidate at 22 files, with a caveat: *the Countdown task and the
Stream-of-Search method are cited for different things and I have not checked
which the 22 are.* Checked:

- **Countdown**: 24 files.
- **Stream of Search**, or `SoS`: **one** file — the curation entry in which I
  wrote that caveat.

So there is no split. The record cites the task and has never once cited the
method. That is a cleaner answer than the ARC case, and it points the note in
a different direction: not "which paper is this", but "this paper is here for
the half of it nobody reads".

Worth noticing that my own writing was the single false positive. A count run
over a corpus that includes the note you just wrote about the count is a
corpus that has been contaminated by asking. Small here — one file, obvious on
inspection — and the same mechanism would be invisible at scale.

## What the Countdown note is actually for

Countdown is the arithmetic puzzle the evolution-strategies cluster is
measured on throughout: `SOTA-154` averages across Qwen2.5 and LLaMA3 on it,
`SOTA-212` runs OLMo3-7B on it across 200 GH200s, `SOTA-213` reads a
500-iteration run on it. Four practices, one task, and until now nowhere
saying what the task is.

What it buys is mechanical and is why this literature borrowed it: **a
solution is verifiable by evaluating it**, so a reward exists without a judge
or a reference answer. What it costs is the same fact read the other way: the
space is small and arithmetic, so a gain on Countdown is a gain at bounded
symbolic search.

And the record already knows it is a strange member of its suite, in a place
nobody would look. `NOTE-082` records that ES reaches peak accuracy quickly on
every task **except** Countdown, and that HellaSwag declines steadily as
Countdown training continues past the point Countdown itself converged. Four
practices report on it as one task among several; the note says it is not.

## Three ways a number can mislead

With this batch the benchmark notes have now recorded three distinct failure
modes, and they are genuinely different:

- **Saturation** — MMLU, where the frontier has compressed into the top of
  the range and differences stop being differences.
- **Difficulty defined against a model generation** — HellaSwag (`LIT-404`),
  whose distractors were adversarially filtered against 2019's systems, so it
  ages by obsolescence rather than by ceiling.
- **A coarse ruler with a low ceiling** — GPQA, where experts reach 65% and
  448 items make one question worth 0.22 points, so a one-point gap is below
  the instrument's granularity.

[#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137) asks whether saturated benchmarks get retired and nothing in the record
has needed the answer. Three named failure modes is the beginning of one, and
it suggests the answer is not a status: `Rejected` would say "do not use
this", where what these notes actually say is "read a number on this against
*that*". That is a caveat, not a retirement, and caveats live in the note.

## MATH, and a line it straddles

Every MATH problem ships a full step-by-step solution, and the paper ships an
auxiliary pretraining corpus built from that. So the dataset is a training
signal as much as a benchmark, and "trained on MATH" and "evaluated on MATH"
are both ordinary sentences.

That matters here specifically: the record holds `SOTA-127`, which says to
filter chain-of-thought traces out of the training data of tiny specialised
models. A dataset whose problems arrive with worked derivations is exactly the
thing that lands on both sides of that practice, and nothing in the record
said so.

## A gap in the worklist, not in the record

**OlympiadBench appears in five documents here and is not on [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137)'s list at
all.** It sits beside GSM8K, MATH and Countdown in the ES evaluations —
`NOTE-082`'s accuracy comparison runs across all four — so it is a peer of
three benchmarks the list does name.

The list came from sweeping twelve papers' bibliographies for arXiv
identifiers, so a benchmark reaches it only if those particular twelve cited
it that way. This is the second time the worklist's shape has turned out to be
the query's shape rather than the record's, after yesterday's re-rank. The
first correction was to the order; this one is to the membership.
