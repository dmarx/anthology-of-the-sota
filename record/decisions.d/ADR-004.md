# ADR-004: Retire work by changing its status, never by deleting it

<!-- inactive-ok-file: LIT-031, LIT-041 — this decision is about the attic;
     naming its entries is the whole point -->

## Context

The project's README promised "an attic in which to deprecate former best
practices that have been supplanted", and the attic existed: nine papers
carried an `attic:` mapping with a `reason`, two of them naming a
`superseded_by` and one a `see_also`.

Everything about it was unreachable. The reasons were good — "requires
careful per-case tuning and hasn't shown consistent benefits across domains",
"too domain-specific for general ML training recommendations" — and appeared
in no generated output. The cross-references were bare arXiv ids that nothing
resolved; three of them named papers not in the corpus, which had been true
for an unknown period because nothing could have reported it. And because the
attic lived on the paper rather than on the practice, atticking a paper and
keeping a recommendation from it were mutually exclusive ([ADR-002](ADR-002.md)).

## Decision

The attic is a status.

`Rejected` means retired with no successor: the entry stops being believed
and the reason says why. `Superseded` means later work covers it better, and
the status note names the successor as a code — `LIT-041`, not an arXiv id
in a nested field — so the fixer links it and the lint checks it resolves.

The file stays. Its body stays. A retired entry keeps its takeaways and gains
a "Standing in the anthology" section explaining the retirement.

Nothing is deleted from the record. When a citation of a retired document is
correct — pointing at history, or at a rejection worth pointing at — the
`inactive-ok:` comment at the citing site vouches for it; the rest are listed
in the reference status report.

## Alternatives considered

- **Delete retired entries.** The default reading of "living document", and
  the reason such documents cannot be argued with: the list always looks
  right because the record of it being wrong is gone. The whole value of an
  anthology of current practice is the contrast with what it replaced.
- **Move them to an `attic/` directory.** Preserves the text and breaks every
  citation, then needs a redirect scheme to unbreak them. A status is the
  same information without moving the file.
- **Keep the nested `attic:` mapping and validate it.** Possible, and it
  leaves the reasons unrendered and the retirement invisible to anyone
  browsing. The point is not that the data exists; it is that a reader
  meeting a practice sees that it is retired.

## Consequences

The corpus arrived with a case this immediately surfaced: `LIT-031` is
`Superseded` by `LIT-041`, and `LIT-041` is itself `Rejected` — the
successor named in the attic was later judged uninteresting. That chain was
present in the YAML and unreadable from it. It is now visible in the index
and reported by the lint, and somebody has to decide what `LIT-031` should
actually point at.

The three attic cross-references that name papers outside the corpus are
written as `ARXIV-` codes and stated as such in the note bodies, rather than
being silently dropped or silently kept.
