title: 'The tag is not the scope boundary — and I applied that error twice in one hour'
created: '2026-09-08T04:59:40'
tags: [curation]
---

Second pass at [#85](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/85). The issue proposed that the inherited notes tagged
`generative-modeling`, `vision-and-graphics`, `adaptation-and-tuning`,
`inference-optimization` and `analysis-and-evaluation` are unfiled because
`practices.d/tags.yaml` declines those subjects — [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8), a stated scope
decision rather than a backlog. About 36 notes, expected yield zero, one
paragraph of confirmation.

That premise is wrong, and I got it wrong twice on the way to saying so.

**The first error was the issue's.** The record already holds twelve
post-training and quantization practices — [SOTA-126](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-126.md) on DPO epochs, [SOTA-129](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-129.md)
on the pretrain/SFT/RLVR staging, [SOTA-154](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-154.md) on evolution strategies, [SOTA-160](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-160.md)
and [SOTA-163](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-163.md) on quantization — filed under `training-optimization` and
`systems-optimization`. Every one of them sources a *post-migration* note. So
the anthology has not declined to hold fine-tuning, preference training or
quantization. It holds them, under the seven topics.

The `LIT` scheme has twelve topics and the `SOTA` scheme has seven, and the
extra five are not a scope boundary. **The two vocabularies overlap; they do
not nest.** What happened is that post-migration papers on these subjects were
read and filed, and pre-migration ones got a tag outside the practice seven
and were never looked at again. The tag concealed them rather than excluding
them. LoRA — one of the most widely adopted training practices in the corpus
— sat unfiled for a year behind `adaptation-and-tuning`.

**The second error was mine, and it is the same error.** Having written that
paragraph, I turned around and asserted that the 24 notes tagged
`generative-modeling` and `vision-and-graphics` were "genuinely out of scope
by construction" — on the strength of their tags, without opening one of
them. That claim went to the user and into a comment on [#85](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/85) before it was
challenged.

It does not survive contact with the notes. The seven topics range over *how
you train*, not over what you are training, so the domain a paper comes from
decides nothing:

- [LIT-075](../../record/literature.d/LIT-075.md), the EDM paper, is substantially a training-recipe paper —
  preconditioning of network inputs and outputs, the noise-level sampling
  distribution used during training, loss weighting.
- [LIT-096](../../record/literature.d/LIT-096.md), Segment Anything, contributes a three-stage model-in-the-loop
  annotation bootstrap. That is a `data-pipeline` practice and it has nothing
  to do with segmentation.
- [LIT-062](../../record/literature.d/LIT-062.md) trains in a compressed latent rather than at full resolution, which
  is a claim about where to spend compute.
- [LIT-067](../../record/literature.d/LIT-067.md) and [LIT-093](../../record/literature.d/LIT-093.md) are distillation schedules and training objectives.

Some of the 24 probably *are* clear — [LIT-107](../../record/literature.d/LIT-107.md), [LIT-109](../../record/literature.d/LIT-109.md), [LIT-111](../../record/literature.d/LIT-111.md) and [LIT-113](../../record/literature.d/LIT-113.md)
read as task-specific method papers — but "probably" is exactly the word this
audit exists to eliminate, and it has to be established one note at a time.

**The corrected surface.** Of 71 inherited notes that source no practice: 18
carry a written reason, 13 are retired, and **53 are bare** — no practice, no
reason, no retirement. Not 37. The largest bare cluster is the one I had
written off.

**Why the same mistake twice in an hour.** Both times the move was to let a
cheap categorical signal stand in for reading. The issue used the LIT tag; I
used the domain. Both feel like scope reasoning and neither is: a scope
decision is a statement about what kind of *claim* the record will hold, and
both substitutes are statements about what a paper is *about*. [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) is
sound and says a subject with no category is one you declined to hold — the
failure is in deciding what the subject is by reading a label instead of the
work.

Filed two practices as the demonstration that the concealment is real rather
than theoretical, and left the rest for a pass that reads them.
