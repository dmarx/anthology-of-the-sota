# LIT-838: A Fair Comparison of Graph Neural Networks for Graph Classification

Errica, Podda, Bacciu, Micheli, University of Pisa (ICLR 2020) —
[ARXIV-1912.09893](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09893)

## Key takeaways

- **The audit** (Table 1): of DGCNN, DiffPool, ECC and GIN, none released
  model-selection code. DiffPool and ECC report no standard deviations,
  DiffPool's splits are unstratified and unseeded, and GIN reports the
  validation accuracy of its 10-fold CV, which the GIN paper states
- **The protocol** (§5, Table 2): an outer stratified 10-fold CV for
  assessment. Inside each training fold, a 90/10 holdout selects
  hyperparameters from a 32–72 point grid. The selected configuration is
  retrained three times and the test fold scored as their mean. Every model
  gets the same node features
- **Chemical results** (Table 3, mean ± sd over 10 folds): the
  structure-agnostic baseline, a sum over atom types then an MLP, wins D&D
  (78.4 ± 4.5), PROTEINS (75.8 ± 3.7) and ENZYMES (65.2 ± 6.4). On NCI1 every
  GNN beats it, GIN most (80.0 ± 1.4 against 69.8 ± 2.2)
- **Social results** (Table 4, with degree features): the baseline is best
  on IMDB-MULTI (49.1 ± 3.5) and 0.4 behind GIN on IMDB-BINARY (70.8 against
  71.2), which the text calls a baseline win. GIN is best on REDDIT-BINARY
  (89.9 ± 1.9), REDDIT-5K (56.1 ± 1.7) and COLLAB (75.6 ± 2.3)
- **Node degree as a feature** (§6.2) lifts the baseline by about 15 points
  and changes the ranking of the GNNs. Most models then select about one
  fewer layer
- **Fitting** (§6): on NCI1 a 10,000-unit baseline with no regularization
  reaches about 67% training accuracy, while GIN fits the training set
  almost perfectly

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from the graph-coverage pass** (gap list from the record's own
review, 2026-10-22). It is the second source of [SOTA-350](../practices.d/SOTA-350.md), for graph
classification, where [LIT-581](LIT-581.md) is the first for node classification. The
paper cites [LIT-581](LIT-581.md) as its model and follows its advice on multiple
splits.

It `corrects` how GIN ([LIT-580](LIT-580.md)) has been read: GIN's §6 says its Table 1
reports validation accuracy across the 10 folds, with the epoch chosen on
them, and this paper is where that was pointed out and rerun. It is also
`compared_against` GIN. Under the shared protocol GIN's
test accuracy on featureless REDDIT-BINARY is 87.0 ± 4.4 and on REDDIT-5K
53.8 ± 5.9, against the 92.4 and 57.5 GIN's paper reported as validation
accuracy. GIN still beats the structure-agnostic baseline on both, which
reaches 72.1 and 35.1 with only a constant node feature.

It is also `compared_against` GraphSAGE ([LIT-814](LIT-814.md)), run on whole
neighborhoods with max-pooling readout. Featureless, GraphSAGE scores 86.1
± 2.0 on REDDIT-BINARY, 49.9 ± 1.7 on REDDIT-5K and 71.6 ± 1.5 on COLLAB,
behind GIN on all three, and ahead of it on both IMDB sets.

**What this does and does not say about [SOTA-351](../practices.d/SOTA-351.md).** GraphSAGE's
aggregator was itself a hyperparameter here, chosen from mean, max and sum
in each fold, so its near-tie with GIN on REDDIT-BINARY is not a test of
mean against sum. The chance-level collapse of mean aggregation in GIN's
paper is not re-run. What this paper adds is that GIN's margins shrink
under test-fold reporting, and that on three of four chemical datasets
neither aggregator beats ignoring the graph.
