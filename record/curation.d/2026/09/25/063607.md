Tier B unit 3 of `#342`. Yesterday's unit filed an account whose best evidence
is an architectural intervention. Today's discovered that the record held
nothing at all on the intervention, and that when somebody did measure it at
scale the sign came out the other way.

## What the previous unit left behind

`THEORY-103` says two-hop composition fails out of distribution because the
generalising circuit keeps a second copy of the atomic facts in the upper layers
and only stores what training queried from there. Its strongest evidence is that
the derived fix works: tie the two halves of the stack together, and
out-of-distribution composition goes from flat zero at two million optimization
steps to generalizing.

The fix is Universal Transformer's parameter-sharing scheme. After that unit
merged, this record contained **one** mention of Universal Transformer — in the
changelog fragment the unit itself wrote — and **seventeen** mentions of ALBERT,
which is the paper that took cross-layer sharing to production scale, across
**zero** practices. Every one of the seventeen either measures *on* ALBERT
(`THEORY-065`, `THEORY-066`, `LIT-528`, `LIT-530`) or names "ALBERT-style layer
sharing" as an instrument (`LIT-303`, `LIT-316`).

So the shape is the DINO one from yesterday, and worth naming again because it
has now happened twice in two days: **a queue cannot see the dependencies its own
work creates.** No count taken before this morning would have ranked ALBERT above
EDM2 (41 mentions) or the CLIP cluster (199). What moved it was a document filed
hours earlier.

The difference from the DINO case is the direction. There, filing the registers
paper made DINO load-bearing and DINO *supported* what was said about it. Here,
filing the composition account made sharing load-bearing and the paper that
measured sharing **disagrees** — which is the more useful of the two outcomes and
the one a ranking by mention count will never find.

## The asymmetry

What ALBERT supplies is not a verdict on sharing. It is a split nobody in this
record had:

| sharing strategy | `E = 128` | `E = 768` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| attention parameters only | **+0.1** | **−0.7** |
| feed-forward parameters only | −1.4 | −2.8 |
| everything | −1.5 | −2.5 |

A transformer block has two halves and they are not equally compressible across
depth. Sharing every attention block in a 12-layer stack is free and removes a
quarter of the parameters. Sharing the feed-forward blocks is where the cost is,
and all-sharing is barely worse than FFN-sharing alone — the FFN *is* the cost.

Filed as `SOTA-403`, a practice, not an explanation, because there is a
number and no mechanism. The document says so, and says what the obvious
candidate mechanism would be, and declines to claim it.

## Why the pricing matters to yesterday's theory

The two results are about the same knob and they do not merely coexist.

`THEORY-103` is an argument about **where facts are stored**: the upper
layers hold a second copy of the atomic facts, and only the ones training
queried. If that is right, the per-layer store the account is about is
feed-forward weights — which is exactly the half ALBERT measured as expensive to
share. The fix the account endorses therefore cannot be had cheaply, **and the
reason it cannot is the account's own premise.** A theory that explains why its
own remedy is costly is in better shape than one that does not, and the
document now carries it as a section rather than a footnote.

It also yields an implication neither paper tested, filed as an implication: if
sharing the FFN costs because it removes per-layer storage, the cost should
concentrate wherever the upper layers had something of their own to store, and a
model whose upper layers were storing little should share them cheaply.

## Two measurements, opposite signs, and a paper naming it

Worth recording as a piece of scholarly hygiene rather than a finding about
transformers. ALBERT's related work:

> Different from our observations, Dehghani et al. 2018 show that networks with
> cross-layer parameter sharing (Universal Transformer, UT) get better
> performance on language modeling and subject-verb agreement than the standard
> transformer.

Same intervention, opposite sign, stated plainly instead of glossed. That is what
makes the disagreement usable — and what makes it safe for this record to hold
both without pretending to resolve them. Universal Transformer is now the
clearest single gap the sharing line has, and it is an unranked candidate rather
than work, because the two papers this record *does* hold on the subject already
disagree and a third measurement will not adjudicate a two-way split by arriving.

## A number that keeps coming back

ALBERT-xxlarge has fewer parameters than BERT-large and **3.17× lower
throughput**, which the discussion concedes in its first sentence. That is unit
4's flash-attention finding wearing different clothes: a resource saving in one
currency advertised as a saving in another. The abstract says "increase the
training speed of BERT"; the configuration that produces the headline results is
three times slower per step.

What rescues it is that Lan et al. ran the comparison that settles it —
equal wall-clock, BERT-large at 400k steps in 34h against ALBERT-xxlarge at 125k
in 32h — and won by **+1.5 average and +5.2 on RACE**. The parameter count was
never the argument. The record now holds the equal-time table beside the
parameter table, which is the whole point of keeping both.

## Nothing was wrong, for once

Four consecutive units each found a defect: three `introduced_by` misattributions,
one `source:` crediting the paper that used a result. This one found none, and
the reason is structural rather than lucky. The gap here was an **absence** —
nothing in the record said anything about which half of a block you may share, so
there was nothing to be wrong. An absence produces no defect report and no
warning; it is only visible when something else in the record starts leaning on
it.

Which is the argument for filing the dependency in the same week rather than
adding it to a queue. `SOTA-240` is the same story from the other end: its v2
said in plain words that its own right edge was "an inference, not a reported
result", and that admission sat there for four days until a paper that reported
it turned up for an unrelated reason. **A gap the record has written down is
still a gap** — the third time that sentence has been the right one this week.

## Measured, not closed

Neither `1810.04805` nor `1907.11692` is in this record. BERT and RoBERTa, in
any scheme.

The count, taken rather than guessed: **89 files in this record say "BERT"**,
51 of them reading notes, practices or explanations. `THEORY-066` and `LIT-530`
measure rank collapse on it. `LIT-303` and `LIT-316` use ALBERT-style sharing as
an instrument, which is a variant of it. This unit's own subject is a *lite* one
and every ablation in it is against a BERT configuration. The record has been
reasoning about a model it does not hold, at a scale that makes the
position-encoding gap unit 1 found look small, and the reason is presumably that
nobody has needed to look it up — which is exactly the condition under which a
wrong claim about it would go unchallenged.

Filed as a finding rather than worked, because it is larger than a unit: BERT,
RoBERTa and probably the NSP/SOP line together, and the honest scoping of that is
its own reading session rather than a paragraph at the end of this one.
