# LIT-145: Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training Beyond Fixed Training Durations

Hägele et al., EPFL and Hugging Face (2024) — [ARXIV-2405.18392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18392)

## Key takeaways

- Cosine schedules tie the result to a training length chosen in advance,
  so every point on a scaling law is its own run. A constant learning rate
  followed by a cooldown scales as reliably and predictably as cosine and
  reaches the same or better loss.
- Because the cooldown can be launched from any point, all intermediate
  checkpoints of one long run become valid data points, which cuts the
  compute of a scaling-law study by a large factor.
- The cooldown's length and shape matter; a cooldown of a modest fraction
  of the run, decaying to zero with a 1-sqrt shape, is what the paper
  recommends.
- Stochastic weight averaging along the trajectory yields improved
  performance at every scale tested without extra training compute.

## Standing in the anthology

The controlled evidence behind [SOTA-140](../practices.d/SOTA-140.md): [LIT-144](LIT-144.md) shipped the schedule,
this paper measured it against cosine across scales and durations. Also the
record's first note on weight averaging as a training-time tool.

ScheduleFree+ ([LIT-682](LIT-682.md)) ran the warmup-stable-decay schedule this paper
validated as a baseline, from 120M to 2B parameters at 20 and 100 tokens per
parameter, and WSD lost to it at every size. The same paper found WSD
"consistently outperformed by the linear decay schedule" when both are tuned
to the horizon. Two caveats attach. That paper attributes WSD to Zhai et al.
and Hu et al. and describes a linear anneal over about 10% of the run, not
this paper's 1-sqrt cooldown. It cites this paper only for checkpoint
averaging and for the Schedule-Free limitations its modifications address.
