# LIT-394: Improving neural networks by preventing co-adaptation of feature detectors

Hinton et al. (2012) — [ARXIV-1207.0580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0580)

## Key takeaways

- **Randomly omitting half the feature detectors on each training case
  greatly reduces overfitting** when a large feedforward network is trained
  on a small training set. That is the whole procedure, and it is stated
  here first.

- **The named mechanism is co-adaptation, not ensembling.** The claim is that
  overfitting takes the specific form of "complex co-adaptations in which a
  feature detector is only helpful in the context of several other specific
  feature detectors", and dropout works by destroying the context those
  co-adaptations depend on. Each unit is then pushed to detect something
  "generally helpful ... given the combinatorially large variety of internal
  contexts in which it must operate."

- **The regime is stated in the first sentence and is a condition, not an
  aside.** *A large feedforward neural network trained on a small training
  set.* The paper does not claim dropout helps a network that is not
  overfitting, and the size relationship between model and data is part of
  the claim from the very first statement of it.

- Results were reported as records on speech and object recognition
  benchmarks of the day.

## Standing in the anthology

This is the origin document, and it is filed as one. It is not where the
evidence for the practice lives — that is [LIT-391](LIT-391.md),
which has the dataset-size sweep, the hyperparameter guide and the averaging
experiments. The practice cites the two in the shape the scheme asks for —
`introduced_by:` here, `source:` there — which is the distinction
[ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) draws between the work that produced the
evidence and the work that first stated the recommendation.

The record went a year without it, which is [DP-007](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-7) in its plainest
form. Dropout is assumed by name in five practices and two notes already here
— [SOTA-088](../practices.d/SOTA-088.md), [SOTA-114](../practices.d/SOTA-114.md) and [SOTA-081](../practices.d/SOTA-081.md) list it among the operations a fused kernel
has to cover, [SOTA-058](../practices.d/SOTA-058.md) among the ones sequence parallelism shards, and
[LIT-119](LIT-119.md) reaches for it by rate — and none of them could cite it, because
nobody had ever needed to argue for it.
