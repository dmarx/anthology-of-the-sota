Group 2 of [#17](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/17) was catalogued as "extends the Muon chain". It does, and it
also does something the catalogue only hinted at: it takes a number out of a
live practice.

## The 2× was not wrong, it was unaccompanied

[SOTA-121](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-121.md) recommends Muon, sourced to the Falcon-H1-Tiny blogpost and
resting on [LIT-122](../../record/literature.d/LIT-122.md)'s scaling-law claim of "roughly 2× the computational
efficiency of AdamW". Wen et al. tune ten optimizers *separately* — the
baseline included — across 0.1B–1.2B and 1–8× Chinchilla, and measure at the
end of training. They get **1.4× at 0.1B, falling to 1.1× at 1.2B**.

Two of their methodological findings explain how a larger figure is reached
honestly:

1. **Optimal hyperparameters do not transfer between optimizers.** Handing
   the challenger the baseline's schedule, or vice versa, is the ordinary way
   a comparison tilts, and it tilts toward whichever one the schedule was
   tuned for.
2. **Rankings flip mid-run.** Two optimizers compared before the target
   budget can swap places by the end, because learning-rate decay has not
   finished. Any number read off an intermediate checkpoint is provisional.

Neither is an accusation against [LIT-122](../../record/literature.d/LIT-122.md) — different scales, budgets and
baselines, and Moonshot's runs are at a scale Wen et al. never reach. But the
record was quoting one number as though it were the answer, and it is the
high end of a range whose low end is 1.1×.

**The practice did not move; the number did.** 1.1× at 1.2B is still free
once the recipe is in place; the optimizer-state saving and the
hyperparameter transferability are not what that study measures; and every
frontier adopter the record holds — Kimi K2, K3, DeepSeek-V4 — trains orders
of magnitude above the largest model tested. What changed is that [SOTA-121](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-121.md)
now carries a "How much it buys" section with the range in it, and says
plainly which figure to stop repeating.

This is the first time a backlog item has revised a practice rather than
adding to one, and it is an argument for working the backlog rather than
only extending the frontier: the papers that check earlier claims are less
exciting to file and are where the corrections live.

## A convergence worth the filing

The Hyperball follow-up and Learnable Multipliers ([LIT-121](../../record/literature.d/LIT-121.md)) turn out to be
about the *same phenomenon*, from groups citing different literatures.

Both start from the observation that under weight decay a matrix settles at
an equilibrium norm determined by the hyperparameters rather than by the
data. LRM concludes the scale should be **learned** and gives it its own
parameter. Hyperball concludes the scale should be **removed** as a degree of
freedom and pins the Frobenius norms to constants. Both report gains over the
same baseline; Hyperball adds the claim that this equilibrium is *why*
matrix optimizers' advantage shrinks with scale, which — if it holds — makes
the shrinkage an artefact of a tuning convention rather than a property of
the optimizer.

Nothing here promotes [SOTA-122](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-122.md). Its stated condition is a result from outside
the authors' group, and this is the same group with a different remedy. But
the two notes now sit next to each other, which is the point of filing the
predecessors.

## Filing notes

Seven notes, all written from the papers. All six with arXiv identifiers pass
`source-mismatch` against what the API serves, which is the first time that
check has run on notes as they were written rather than on a corpus being
repaired.

The Muon blog post is the third `url:`-only source after Falcon-H1-Tiny and
BytePS, and the clearest case for [ADR-009](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-009.md) yet: three laboratories pretrain
frontier models with this optimizer and there is no paper to cite. [LIT-122](../../record/literature.d/LIT-122.md),
[SOTA-121](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-121.md) and [SOTA-131](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-131.md) each described it as "not in the corpus"; three
sentences deleted.

[SOTA-121](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-121.md) is also the first practice to use [ADR-010](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-010.md)'s plural `source:` for what
it was designed for — an entry whose body already leaned on four notes while
the field named one.

## What is left

Groups 3, 4 and 5 of [#17](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/17): architecture (MoE, MLA, Mamba, MTP — about fifteen
papers), data (five), post-training (seven). All 33 identifiers in the issue
were verified against the API before any of this was written, and all 33
resolve. Two of the issue's own titles were slightly off — 2501.00656 is
titled "2 OLMo 2 Furious", and the "seen by title only" guess for 2606.16899
was exactly right.
