# LIT-tmp5vqlh: VSA: Faster Video Diffusion with Trainable Sparse Attention

Zhang, Chen, Huang, Lin, Liu, Stoica, Xing and Zhang, UC San Diego, MBZUAI
and UC Berkeley (2025), NeurIPS 2025 — [ARXIV-2505.13389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13389). Read at v5 (28 Oct
2025), main text and Appendices A–F; v1 is 19 May 2025. The arXiv listing
carries the "VSA:" prefix and the v5 PDF title does not.

## Key takeaways

- **The mechanism** (§2.2, App. B). The (T, H, W) latent is cut into cubes of
  (C_t, C_h, C_w) = (4, 4, 4). Tokens are re-indexed so that each cube is 64
  consecutive positions, and one cube is one GPU tile (B = 64). The coarse
  stage mean-pools Q, K and V per cube and runs dense cube-to-cube attention.
  It produces an output O_c and a score matrix whose top-K entries per row
  pick the key cubes for each query cube. The fine stage runs token attention
  over those cubes only. The output is O = O_c ⊙ G_c + O_f ⊙ G_f, with both
  gates linear projections of the hidden state. Selection is top-K only
  (default K = 32). There is no threshold or cumulative-mass rule.
- **Ablations at 120M** (§3.1, Table 1; 16×32×32 latents, 4.5×10²⁰ FLOPs, one
  run each). With extended training (4×10²¹ FLOPs) VSA has loss 0.12687
  against 0.12703 for full attention and 0.12716 for a strided-window pattern
  (Table 1a). At the compute-optimal budget every sparse variant beats full
  attention (VSA 0.13162, full 0.13877), which the authors attribute to the
  short budget. Data-dependent selection beats a fixed (3,3,3) local window
  with and without the coarse output (0.13296 against 0.13330, 0.13162
  against 0.13220, Table 1b). Adding a local stage, excluding local cubes or
  forcing them in changes loss by under 0.0004.
- **Cube size trades loss for speed** (Table 1c–d). Loss falls as the tile
  shrinks: 0.13375 at B = 256 (4,8,8), 0.13244 at 128 (4,8,4), 0.13162 at 64
  (4,4,4) and 0.13155 at 16 (2,4,2). Kernel throughput falls from 478 to 408
  to 181 TFLOPS, and the 16-token tile runs 2.26× slower than the 64-token one.
  A coarse stage at 64 with a fine stage at 256 (Exp. 16) scores 0.13328, near
  the 256/256 arm. Both granularities matter. Mean pooling beats max
  (0.13929). A strided 3D convolution destabilizes training (0.27787,
  Table 1e).
- **Scaling** (§3.2, Fig. 2). Models from 60M to 1.4B, trained up to
  4×10²¹ FLOPs on 128 H200s at 16,384 tokens, give a VSA frontier parallel to
  full attention's. That is the 2.53× training-FLOP reduction, with attention
  FLOPs down 8×. At a 410M scale the two loss curves nearly coincide. The
  best K depends on length and budget. K = 32 is best at 8K–25K tokens, while
  K = 16 wins at 61,440 tokens and 4.5×10²⁰ FLOPs and loses again by 10²¹.
  The total is about 90k H200 hours.
- **Retrofit** (§2.3, §3.3, App. C.5). Dropping VSA into a dense checkpoint
  directly is unstable. The recipe sets G_c = 0, removes G_f, starts at
  K = L/B (dense), trains 50 steps dense, then lowers K by 4 every 50 steps.
  Wan2.1-1.3B is fine-tuned for 4,000 steps on 80,000 clips generated by
  Wan-14B at 448×832×61 frames, at K = 32 (91.2% sparsity). VBench total is
  82.77, against 82.56 for the original model and 83.63 for the same model
  fine-tuned densely on the same clips (Table 3a). Wan2.1-14B at 720p and 90%
  sparsity is reported as preferred about equally to the official model by
  human raters on 200 MovieGen prompts (Fig. 3b, figure only).
- **Speed** (§3.4, Fig. 4, App. D). The fine-stage kernel (ThunderKittens)
  reaches about 85% of FA3's MFU and nearly 7× FA3 at long sequences and
  87.5% sparsity, and over 6× with the coarse stage counted. FlexAttention with
  the same 64×64 block mask gets 2×. The coarse stage is under 0.2% of
  attention FLOPs but 14% of runtime, dominated by top-K. End to end,
  Wan2.1-1.3B goes from 31 s (dense, compiled) to 18 s, and 14B from 1,274 s
  to 576 s (abstract only).
- **What the coarse stage selects** (§3.5, Fig. 5). On the fine-tuned 1.3B
  model, the top-32 cubes hold at least 60% of the attention mass in most
  layers and timesteps, up to 90%, against 8% for a random 32. Accuracy rises
  with timestep and zig-zags across layers. Heads show local, same-frame,
  same-plane and global patterns.
- **Sparse distillation** (§3.3, App. C.6). A DMD2 student with VSA at 80%
  sparsity, against a dense teacher and with all DMD2 hyperparameters
  unchanged, gives a 3-step generator with 50.9× lower denoising time. Quality
  is shown only as a human-preference bar against a dense DMD2 student.

## Where the hedges are

Per [DP-010](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-10):

- **"Better scaling than full attention" rests on very small loss gaps.** In
  the extended-training row VSA leads full attention by 0.00016 in loss. In
  Table 1b the three locality variants sit within 0.0004 of the default, and
  one of them (C&F&L&E, 0.13124) is lower than the default VSA chose
  (0.13162). The paper calls them "similar". No row reports a seed
  or an interval, and the paper does not say how far loss moves between
  reruns. The 2.53× figure is read off fitted frontiers in Fig. 2b, whose
  fitted values are not tabulated.
- **The ablation baseline was tuned and the variants were not.** The grid
  search over batch size and learning rate was run for the full-attention
  baseline, and its best setting was reused for every variant (App. C.2).
  This is favourable to the baseline, so it does not inflate VSA's lead.
- **"Even slightly outperform the original model" is a data effect.** VSA's
  82.77 beats the original Wan2.1-1.3B (82.56) because both fine-tuned arms
  trained on Wan-14B outputs. Against the same-data dense fine-tune VSA is
  0.86 points lower (83.63), and the semantic score is 2.38 points lower. The
  authors call this "all models perform closely".
- **The comparison with training-free sparsity is confounded.** VSA is
  preferred to Sparse VideoGen (SVG, 82.5% sparsity) in a human study, but
  VSA was also fine-tuned on Wan-14B outputs, and SVG ran on the original
  weights (Fig. 3b top). The study shows the trained-and-distilled model is
  preferred. It does not isolate training as the cause. Its win rates are in
  the figure only.
- **A count does not add up.** §3.5 says a (16, 28, 52) latent has 386 cubes.
  At (4, 4, 4) that is 4 × 7 × 13 = 364 cubes. K = 32 of 364 is the 91.2%
  sparsity reported in §3.3, so 364 is the right count and the 8% random
  baseline should be about 8.8%.
- **No 1D baseline for the cube layout.** Every block in Table 1c–d is a 3D
  cube. The paper never trains a block-sparse arm over raster-order blocks of
  64 consecutive tokens, so it does not measure what the cube layout buys
  over a 1D layout at the same block size.
- **Sparse-Distill is labelled preliminary**, and its quality evidence is one
  human-preference bar.

## Which comparisons are like for like

- **Table 1a–e** hold model (120M), data, latent shape, FLOP budget and
  hyperparameters fixed, and vary only the attention. This is the controlled
  core of the paper, with the single-run caveat above.
- **Fig. 2b** compares VSA and full attention at matched model sizes and
  matched FLOP budgets on the same data.
- **Table 3a** has one like-for-like pair: VSA fine-tune against dense
  fine-tune, same 80,000 synthetic clips and same step count. The "Ori-Wan"
  row differs in data.
- **Fig. 4b** compares kernels at fixed 87.5% sparsity and head dimension 64.
  The FlexAttention arm uses the identical block mask, so the 6× against 2×
  gap is implementation, not pattern.

## Standing in the anthology

It is the record's clearest from-scratch evidence that trainable
block-sparse attention can match dense attention in a video DiT under a
controlled compute budget. The from-scratch analogue in language models is
Native Sparse Attention ([LIT-143](LIT-143.md)), and the paper names it as an inspiration
along with MoBA (App. E). VSA keeps NSA's compress-and-select pair, drops
NSA's sliding-window branch because Table 1b found local modules add nothing,
and pools queries as well as keys because video attention is bidirectional.
It bears on [SOTA-138](../practices.d/SOTA-138.md), which recommends training sparse attention natively
and warming it up under dense attention. VSA's retrofit recipe is that
warm-up in another form: it starts at K = L/B, which is dense attention, and
anneals K down, because a direct swap was unstable. Unlike [SOTA-138](../practices.d/SOTA-138.md)'s source,
it selects with the mean-pooled scores of a coarse attention stage and has
no separate indexer.

VSA extends Sliding Tile Attention ([LIT-tmp1yfvi](LIT-tmp1yfvi.md)), from the same group.
STA's contribution was the tile layout: tokens re-indexed so that a 3D cube
is one FlashAttention block, which makes every block either dense or skipped.
VSA keeps that layout and replaces STA's fixed, profile-chosen local window
with a learned top-K choice of cubes anywhere in the volume. Its §3.5
visualizations show some heads that look like STA's local window and others
that STA's window could not express.

Against Wan ([LIT-619](LIT-619.md)), the retrofit of Wan2.1-1.3B scores 82.77 on VBench
against 82.56 for the released model. That edge comes from fine-tuning on
Wan-14B outputs, since a dense fine-tune on the same clips scores 83.63.
Against DMD2 ([LIT-646](LIT-646.md)), the sparse-distillation run uses DMD2's recipe and
hyperparameters unchanged and swaps only the student's attention to VSA. The
paper reports human preference against a dense DMD2 student without a
quality loss, in a figure only.

Later sparse-attention papers in this batch use VSA as a trained baseline.
SpargeAttention2 ([LIT-tmpbgw07](LIT-tmpbgw07.md)) retrains it on Wan2.1 at 90% sparsity, where
it loses heavily on text alignment at 1.3B (VQA-a 33.35). Prism
([LIT-tmpe78xc](LIT-tmpe78xc.md)) retrains it at 2K at 90% sparsity (aesthetic 0.39, MotionQ
0.46). Prism's fixed isotropic 4×4×4 ablation uses VSA's own cube, so the gap
between that ablation (0.55, 0.77) and the VSA row comes from things other
than block shape: top-K against Prism's top-k ∪ top-p union, VSA's gated
coarse output, and sparsity level. Prism's related work describes VSA as
"coarse-to-fine attention without adapting to local structure" and groups it
with methods that "target inference acceleration at 480P/720P". The second
description does not fit. VSA's headline result is a cut in pretraining
FLOPs.
