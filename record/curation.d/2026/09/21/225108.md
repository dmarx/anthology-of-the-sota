**Last of the five-revisit tier on [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180).** Block coordinate descent for LLM
training: freeze all but one block of layers, train it to local convergence,
rotate. Filed as `LIT-tmpxiqj4`, `NOTE-tmpyl1mo` and a new practice,
`SOTA-tmpz9mlh`.

**The grep decided the shape again, and for the fifth time in five units.**
Searching the practices for block coordinate descent, layer freezing and
memory-efficient full-parameter training came back empty, and the two nearest
practices are near-misses of a specific kind: `SOTA-184` trains a low-rank
update and says outright that this is not full-parameter training;
`SOTA-155` distributes across poorly connected workers, which presumes you
have workers. The question "every parameter, one small GPU" had no answer in
the record. A genuinely new practice, then, rather than a version bump.

**What the paper actually contributes is a partition rule, and it is nearly a
priori.** BCD itself is old and its convergence on deep networks was studied
by others, which the paper cites rather than reproving. The adaptation is the
cut: most operators live entirely inside a layer, so freezing whole layers
keeps the optimized kernels, while a finer partition keeps the same parameter
count and loses them. That argument does not need an experiment to be
believed, which is a reason to file the practice and not a reason to promote
it.

**The abstract prices a graphics card.** 2.6% of the cost, on RTX 4090s. The
body says the 4090's hourly cost is about a quarter of an A100's, so that
figure is the method multiplied by the hardware substitution. The same-device
number is 33%, on A100/A800 at 7B, and even that is largely "one GPU instead
of two". Both numbers are in the paper; only one of them is about the method.

**Three times the iterations is in Table 2 and not in the abstract.** Nothing
is concealed — the iteration column is right there — but a reader taking the
headline gets an efficiency claim and what the paper supports is an
affordability claim. So the practice states the trade in its own first
section: this is for the memory-bound, and if a deadline binds it is the
wrong answer.

**Counted, not generalized.** Several units this session found a reported
number sensitive to a choice the report never stated. This is not one of
them. It is the shape the Gemini unit found instead — full disclosure in the
body, a flattering number in front — and the right response is to record it
in the practice's conditions rather than to declare a second pattern.
`DP-009`, `DP-010`.

**The gap and the promotion condition line up exactly.** Quality is compared
at ResNet, GPT-2 0.15B and 2B; 7B appears only in the cost experiments, and
1.6B, 5.4B and 10B are estimated from single-round speeds rather than trained.
The recommendation is "train a big model on a small machine" and the big model
is the part not checked. One run each way at 7B to a fixed token budget, final
loss and one downstream metric, would close it.

**One loose end worth someone's curiosity.** The LLaMA 2B alpaca row costs
1.08× the iterations where every GPT-2 row costs about 3×. The paper does not
remark on it. If that difference is architectural it is the most interesting
result in the paper, and it is sitting unexplained in a table.
