# LIT-045: RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding

Su et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2104.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864)

## Key takeaways

- Rotation-based positional encoding: queries and keys are rotated by an
  angle proportional to their absolute position, so the dot product depends
  only on the relative distance between them.
- Improved relative position modeling, without an additive bias term or a
  learned embedding table.
- A theoretical justification, which is the part later work disputes — see
  below.

## Two of this note's original claims did not survive the record catching up

This note was migrated as four bullets, and two of them are now contradicted
by papers the record holds. Both are left corrected rather than deleted,
because the claims are still what most readers believe about RoPE.

**"Better extrapolation capability" is not one of RoPE's properties.** The
record's own [SOTA-151](../practices.d/SOTA-151.md) exists because RoPE does not extrapolate: [LIT-192](LIT-192.md)
reports fine-tuning at the longer length moving LLaMA's effective window from
2048 to 2560 in more than 10,000 batches, and every note in this line —
[LIT-192](LIT-192.md), [LIT-193](LIT-193.md), [LIT-207](LIT-207.md), [LIT-208](LIT-208.md) — starts from that failure. What RoPE
improved on was the alternatives available in 2021, at the lengths tested in
2021.

**The theoretical justification was the long-range decay of attention with
distance, and [LIT-210](LIT-210.md) argues it is not why RoPE works.** The decay holds
under the paper's assumption of aligned or constant query and key vectors and
fails for Gaussian ones; there is always a query making RoPE's contribution
maximal at any relative distance you choose. That does not make the encoding
worse — the same work finds trained models using its high frequencies to build
robust positional attention heads, which is a better reason than the one
given here.
