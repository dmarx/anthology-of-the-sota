Tier B unit 2 of `#342`. `THEORY-071` asked to be promoted on a measurement,
and the paper that made the measurement possible showed the measured quantity
does not exist.

## What the condition said

`THEORY-071` is the circuit-efficiency account of grokking: a memorising and a
generalising circuit both fit the data, the generalising one is cheaper per unit
logit, weight decay moves norm from one to the other, and they cross at a
critical dataset size `D_crit`. `Proposed`, on scope: all the evidence is
modular addition, where the generalising circuit is a known trigonometric
construction. Its condition, written 2026-09-22:

> The efficiency crossover is demonstrated outside algorithmic data — a measured
> `D_crit`, or an ungrokking transition, on a task where the generalising
> circuit is not a known trigonometric construction.

That is a well-formed condition. It names a kind of result, it cannot be
satisfied by a paper that mentions grokking in passing, and it says what would
close the one gap the document admits.

## What the paper did

Wang et al. observe grokking on knowledge-based reasoning — a domain nobody had
reported it in — trace the generalising circuit by causal tracing rather than
constructing it, and run the circuit-efficiency argument on it. Every clause of
"outside algorithmic data, on a task where the generalising circuit is not a
known trigonometric construction" is satisfied.

Then they vary the training-set size and **nothing happens.** With the
inferred/atomic ratio held fixed, scaling the data changes neither the gap
between the train and test curves nor the level of generalization reached. There
is no `D_crit` in this setting to measure, and the paper says so by name,
citing `LIT-539` among the four papers whose *critical data size* hypothesis it
proposes to replace with **critical data distribution**.

So the condition was satisfied in every part except the one that carried the
measurement, and it failed there not because nobody measured but because the
quantity is not there.

## The call

Not a promotion. Not a demotion either. The claim is amended and the condition
is rewritten.

The amendment is available because the efficiency argument *transports* even
though `D_crit` does not. `C_mem` has to store the inferred facts as well as the
atomic ones; `C_gen` stores the atomic facts twice at most. So `N_mem` grows with
the ratio and `N_gen` is bounded, and what the regularizer compares is the
ratio. Scaling the data at fixed ratio scales both. **Dataset size was a proxy**
— an exact one in modular addition, where every example is an inferred fact and
there is nothing else to trade against, and a useless one as soon as the data
has two kinds of thing in it.

The title now says what the crossover is indexed on — whatever makes memorising
dearer while leaving generalising alone — which covers both settings and commits
to neither as the mechanism's native variable.

The new condition asks for the efficiency ordering to be **measured**: parameter
norm per unit logit, on the mixed networks that actually grok rather than on the
`C_mem`-only and `C_gen`-only networks Varma et al. built to measure it on. That
gap was always there and the previous condition looked past it, because it was
busy asking for a second domain. Wang et al. argues the ordering by **counting
facts** over a traced circuit, which is a better thing to have than a counting
argument over a hypothesised circuit, and is still not a measurement of norm.

## Why this is not the same as `#346`

`THEORY-032`'s condition was sharpened yesterday, and the two look alike from a
distance. They are different failures and both are worth naming.

There, the ablation was **the right shape and the wrong measurement** — somebody
ran the experiment the condition described and reported a quantity that did not
settle it. The condition was under-specified.

Here the condition was *fully* specified and named **a quantity that does not
exist in the setting it demanded results from**. It could only have been
satisfied by a paper that found grokking outside algorithmic data *and* found
that dataset size was what moved it — and the first half of that is what makes
the second half unlikely, because a domain with more than one kind of example is
a domain where size and composition come apart.

The generalisable form: **a `promote_when` that names a quantity rather than a
relationship inherits the assumptions of the setting it was written in.**
`D_crit` is not a quantity every task has. "Whatever makes memorising more
expensive" is. Conditions written the second way survive contact with a new
domain; conditions written the first way get refuted by the result that was
supposed to confirm them.

## The measurement-design finding underneath

Worth separating out, because it is the thing a reader can take away without
caring about grokking.

Four papers agree that dataset size controls the transition. Every one of them
establishes it by varying the training *fraction* of a fixed universe of
examples. That is one knob moving two things — how much data there is, and what
proportion of the possible examples it covers — and no amount of agreement among
experiments with that design says which of the two was doing the work.

Wang et al. has two knobs and turns them one at a time. That is the whole
methodological contribution, and it overturns a four-paper consensus in the
setting where the separation is possible.

The reverse inference is not available and the record now says so twice:
modular addition has no atomic/inferred distinction, so there is no ratio there
to be the real variable. **Those experiments could not have told the
difference** is the finding. *They were wrong* is not.

This also cost `THEORY-069` — `Active`, and the document that holds the regime
claim above the three mechanisms that contradict each other — its unanimity
argument. Its text read *"a dataset-size condition appears in all four papers,
including in `LIT-537`'s own list of three conditions, which is the paper
arguing hardest against the others."* Agreement among four papers that share a
confound is worth less than the sentence implies, and the sentence was the
reason given for the status. The status stands, on the regime claim rather than
on the knob.

## What the unit added besides corrections

**A mechanism for a failure the field has only described.** The
"compositionality gap" is a well-known observation: models that know both hops
fail to compose them. `THEORY-tmp6pl9b` says why, in a form that is testable and
was tested — composition's circuit is sequential and therefore keeps a second
copy of the atomic facts in the upper layers, containing only the facts that
appeared as a second hop during training; comparison's is parallel and keeps one
copy. The out-of-distribution failure is then **one absent copy of one fact**,
not an inability to compose, and the paper confirms it the hard way: the bridge
entity and the second relation are still correctly encoded at layer 5 on
out-of-distribution inputs. The first hop works on facts the model never
composed.

**Filed `Proposed`, against two `Active` theories on single sources in the last
two days.** The standard this session has been applying is *a successful
intervention derived from the hypothesis, not a correlation consistent with
it* — and the intervention here succeeded: sharing parameters between the two
halves of the stack takes out-of-distribution composition from flat zero at two
million steps to generalizing. What holds it at `Proposed` is that the result is
an appendix figure with no number in the text, and that the entire setting is
synthetic. The standard is about the *kind* of evidence; it does not excuse a
missing quantity.

**A pair the record can now hold.** `THEORY-038` says a decoder cannot compose
over a long *context* in few layers, because each position forgets what it
forwarded — an expressivity bound. `THEORY-tmp6pl9b` says parametric composition
works fine and generalizes only to what training queried — an incentive
argument. Two different reasons transformers struggle to compose, and neither
subsumes the other. That pairing is only visible because the record files by
kind of claim (`ADR-026`): one is about what can be expressed, the other about
what gets stored.

## A number that needs its frame stated

The source's headline table is a grokked transformer at **99.3%** where
GPT-4-Turbo and Gemini-1.5-Pro sit at or below chance (33.3%). That is a real
result about parametric against non-parametric memory, and the paper frames it
that way.

It is not a capability comparison, and read as one it is badly misleading: the
grokked transformer was trained to convergence on the exact fact set the
frontier models were handed once, in context. The `LIT` note says this
explicitly, because the number is the kind that travels without its conditions.

The CoT column travels better and is filed on the practice: eliciting
verbalization *lowered* Gemini's accuracy from 37.3% to 12.0%, with **70.7% of
the verbalized responses concluding the answer cannot be decided**. `SOTA-279`'s
consensus note has been saying since it was filed that its evidence was one 2022
paper plus adoption. This is the first measured counter-case the record has been
able to attach to it — and the note's caution, written before anyone went
looking, is what made the slot for it.
