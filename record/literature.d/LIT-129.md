# LIT-129: Can Tiny Language Models Reason?

Jakimovski (2025) —
[tiny-reasoning-language-model](https://shekswess.github.io/tiny-reasoning-language-model.html),
with code at github.com/Shekswess/tiny-reasoning-language-model. Not a
paper; the second source in this record filed with a `url:` alone.

## Key takeaways

- Base: SmolLM2-135M-Instruct. Three stages of full fine-tuning on one AMD
  MI300X: SFT on 58k dialogues without chain of thought (3 epochs, peak LR
  3e-4, cosine); SFT on 78k reasoning traces wrapped in `<think>` tags added
  as special tokens (1 epoch, LR 3e-4); DPO on 50k chosen/rejected pairs
  (1 epoch, LR 1e-5, β 0.1).
- The DPO stage converges at this scale and improves reasoning style and
  preference adherence — the fact [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) cites when asking whether DPO
  would work at 90M ([SOTA-126](../practices.d/SOTA-126.md)).
- Against the base model: ARC-Challenge 40.6 vs 37.3, BBH 36.8 vs 28.2,
  MMLU 35.0 vs 29.3, IFEval 35.5 vs 29.9, GSM8K 2.6 vs 1.4.
- The author's own caveat: a research prototype, not for use.

## Standing in the anthology

One run, no ablations, no baseline other than the untouched base model;
here because [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) leans on it as the prior for DPO at 100M, and because
it is the second non-paper source the record carries ([ADR-009](../decisions.d/ADR-009.md)). Its
recipe is a curriculum — pretrain, SFT, then DPO — which is the approach
[LIT-119](LIT-119.md)'s anti-curriculum result argues against for the pretraining half.
