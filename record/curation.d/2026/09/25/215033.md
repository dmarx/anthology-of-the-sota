`SOTA-425` was filed a unit ago. It recommends reporting precision and recall
beside FID, and it said — in its Conditions, as the reason `SOTA-337` applies to
it — that the two metrics are "computed with the same ImageNet network FID uses".

They are not. The metric uses **VGG-16 activations after the second fully
connected layer**. FID uses Inception-v3. The same sentence, in slightly
different words, had also gone into `SOTA-337` v2 an hour before that.

## Why this is the interesting kind of wrong

The sentence was not a guess. It was an inference from something true — both
networks are ImageNet classifiers, so the hazard `SOTA-337` describes does apply —
compressed into a claim about identity that nothing in the record could have
checked, because the record did not hold the paper that says which network it is.

And the inference's *conclusion* survives intact. It arguably gets stronger:
`LIT-tmptzz06`'s own Figure 3c reports that Inception-v3 features give
"substantially similar" results to VGG-16 for this metric. So precision and
recall are a **second, different** ImageNet classifier that agrees with the first
one — which is a cleaner statement of the problem than "the same network",
because two independent-looking instruments converging is exactly the situation
`SOTA-337` exists to make a reader distrust.

**The generalisable shape:** a correct conclusion resting on a fabricated
mechanism reads exactly like a correct conclusion. Nothing in the lint can see
it, no reviewer of the practice would flag it, and the only thing that catches it
is holding the paper. Two of the last three units have now been "file the
instrument a practice already depends on", and both times the practice turned out
to be wrong about the instrument in a detail it had asserted confidently.

## What else the paper had that the practice was missing

**A much better demonstration.** `SOTA-425` argued from ADM's table — ADM-G
beating BigGAN-deep on FID while losing precision, which requires the reader to
accept a cross-architecture comparison. Figure 4 here is four setups of *one*
generator on one dataset:

| setup | FID | what it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 91.7 | heavily truncated; high quality, all alike |
| B | **16.9** | good quality, lower variation |
| C | 4.5 | the FID-optimised config, **faces starting to distort** |
| D | **16.7** | "nearly all of the generated images have low quality" |

B and D are **0.2 FID apart and perceptually opposite**, and the paper says the
obvious thing out loud: "it is unclear which application might favor setup D
where practically all images are broken over setup B". Meanwhile C, the
configuration FID selects, has visible artifacts.

**Two conditions the practice did not have.** `k = 3` is a default, not a
constant, and both precision and recall rise with `k` until they saturate — so
precision at one `k` against precision at another is not a comparison, the same
failure the guidance-weight practices are about. And two metrics make model
selection **multi-objective**: with FID you take the best snapshot, with two
columns the snapshots of one run span a range of tradeoffs, and the paper's answer
is a Pareto frontier rather than a choice about which axis matters. That last one
is a real cost the practice was quietly not charging.

## A theory that two papers support and neither one claims

Everyone knows FID conflates fidelity and coverage; `SOTA-425` says so. What this
paper measures is that **the conflation has a direction.** Over six StyleGAN
configurations, "FID favors configurations with high recall over the ones with
high precision", and the best-recall configuration set a new state-of-the-art FID
for the dataset. The mechanism is given, not inferred: FID is a Wasserstein-2
distance in feature space, so "low intrinsic variation implies low FID even when
much of that variation is missed".

Then `LIT-699`, filed two units ago from a different group, a different
architecture family and a different dataset, reports ADM-G beating BigGAN-deep on
FID at 128, 256 and 512 while **losing precision at all three** and winning
recall. Same direction. Neither paper cites the other for this, and neither states
the general claim.

That is `THEORY-tmpv11wm`, `Proposed`. If it holds, a field that selects on FID
has been selecting for coverage without saying so, which would explain the habit
the first paper points at in its introduction: methods that "claim to optimize
FID" and then produce their uncurated figures from a truncated model that
"explicitly sacrifices variation, and often FID". The `promote_when` asks for the
magnitude, because two measurements of a direction with no measurement of a size
is consistent with the effect being real and negligible.

## The variance number, and what it prices

`SOTA-307` calibrates a 1–2% FID floor for SiT on ImageNet and says plainly to
port the protocol and measure your own. This paper is the one case in the record
of somebody having a number from elsewhere: FID varying "by up to **±14%**
between consecutive training iterations" of StyleGAN on FFHQ — mentioned in
passing, as the reason they amortise over snapshots.

It is not a correction and I have been careful not to file it as one. A ±range
over consecutive snapshots of one run is not a coefficient of variation over
independent seeds, and a GAN on faces is not a flow model on ImageNet. What it
does is put a price on the warning: the two numbers are an order of magnitude
apart, so a reader who carries the 2% into a GAN paper is making the mistake
`SOTA-307`'s Conditions section already predicts. **Neither cell of the obvious
2×2 has been measured** — nobody has a GAN's seed-to-seed floor or a diffusion
model's snapshot-to-snapshot one.

## Deliberately deferred

Sajjadi et al. (2018), `1806.00035`, is the formulation this paper replaces, and
this paper's account of it is damning: it calls three of the four StyleGAN setups
"essentially perfect", gives the heavily truncated one the *lowest* precision, and
under a truncation sweep reports both precision and recall rising as truncation
is removed — backwards. Filing the two together would let that account stand as
the record's knowledge of it, which is the position `THEORY-109` is in with
respect to classifier guidance and the reason that theory is still `Proposed`.
Named in the note's Standing section so the next reader inherits the reason rather
than the gap.
