`#163`'s Gemma 3n item points at a documentation page and gives its reason:
Qwen3.8-Flash-Next cites it as the source for its n-gram embedding. The first
thing to find was the paper that cites it. The GitHub link in the issue is a
PDF, and the PDF is *On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture*. That is
[LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md), filed on 2026-09-05. The record already held both the citing paper
and every measurement in it.

**The citation says less than the issue says.** §2.3 cites Gemma 3n twice.
Both times it is in a list, and both times it is for *unigram* lookup memory
or for keeping tables in host memory. The n-gram generalization is credited
to a different list: N-Grammer, the Over-Tokenized Transformer, and Cheng et
al.'s *Conditional memory via scalable lookup*. PLE is also a different
shape: a small token-keyed table read at every layer, where Qwen reads one
large n-gram table at one layer. "PLE is the source of the n-gram embedding"
is the kind of compression [DP-010](../design-principles.md#dp-10) is about, one step removed. It is a true
sentence about influence, carried as a claim about origin.

**Gemma 3n has no paper and no measurement.** The overview, the developer
guide and the Gemma 4 report together say what PLE is for and how big it is.
None of them says what it is worth. The mechanism is fully specified only in
the Hugging Face implementation, which is what the reading note is written
from. So it is filed as a `url:` note with its standing stated as adoption
([DP-005](../design-principles.md#dp-5)).

**The practice came from [LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md), not from the item.** [LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md)'s v1
summarized §2.3 in one clause, and that clause was the least useful thing in
the section. It said loss falls while downstream saturates, and left out the
three instructions the ablations actually support. Those are now
[SOTA-326](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-326.md), filed `Proposed` because they are one team's single runs and
placement differences are under a point. [DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7) in its usual form: the paper
that ablated the thing was filed for a different finding, and the finding
nobody argued about went unrecorded.

**Queued, not filed.** Cheng et al. (conditional memory / n-gram lookup) is
the real primary source for the n-gram form and the offloading argument, and
it is the obvious next paper for this practice. It is not on `#163`. It
should be its own item rather than slipped in here.
