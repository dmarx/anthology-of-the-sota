**[#243](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/243) was opened two hours ago because GaussianToken landed on an empty
trunk. This fills it: VQ-VAE, VQGAN, MaskGIT and LlamaGen, with readings for
the first two, and the two practices the issue predicted.**

I want to record that the prediction was not obviously going to hold. The
issue said "likely practices that would then have a home", which is the kind
of thing that is easy to write and often turns out to be nothing. Both
arrived, and both are better evidenced than I expected.

**The codebook one has a mechanism.** LlamaGen's Table 2(a): drop the code
vector dimension from 256 to 8 and codebook utilization goes from **0.29% to
97%**, rFID from 9.21 to 2.19. Three codes in a thousand were being used. The
reason is the obvious one once stated — in 256 dimensions nearly every encoder
output has the same nearest neighbour, so `K = 16384` describes a vocabulary
of about fifty entries.

What makes it a practice rather than a fact is that **GaussianToken found the
same shape independently**, on a different architecture, a different dataset
and a different metric, and attributed it to the same mechanism. Neither
paper cites the other. That is the corroboration the record looks for, and it
was completely invisible while the trunk was missing: two hours ago
GaussianToken's Table 2 read as a one-off quirk of a Gaussian quantizer.

**And both sources bound their captions to the monotone range.** LlamaGen:
"Lower vector dimension (from 256 to 8) improves both reconstruction quality
and codebook usage significantly." The next row is dimension 4, at **9.88
rFID** — worse than 256, while still using 82% of the codebook. The
codebook-size caption is bounded identically: "from 4096 to 16384", where
32768 is worse. Nothing is false; the ranges are named. A reader who takes
captions rather than tables gets "smaller is better" and will pick dimension
4. GaussianToken, to its credit, says its own curve rises then falls.

So the practice says shrink and sweep, and refuses to name a number — the two
optima are at 8 and 3 on two models, which is not a constant anybody can
carry.

**The second practice is sharper and comes from one table.** LlamaGen Table 3,
same tokenizer, nothing retrained:

| tokens | rFID |
|---|---|
| 256 (16×16) | 2.19 |
| 576 (24×24) | **0.94** |
| 1024 (32×32) | **0.70** |

All scored on reconstructions resized to `256×256`. Three-fold, from a knob
that is not the tokenizer.

And the abstract says: "An image tokenizer with downsample ratio of 16,
reconstruction quality of 0.94 rFID". Every clause true. A reader maps
"downsample 16" onto a `256×256` image, gets 16×16 = 256 tokens, and the
number there is 2.19. The 0.94 is the 384-input row. §4's prose then gives the
same pair as 2.43 and 0.99 — three versions of one comparison in one paper.

This is `DP-010` in an unusually clean form, because the table that
disambiguates it is published two pages away and is *the paper's own
contribution*. Nothing is hidden. The compression is just not selective for
the thing that determines the answer.

**A small vindication for GaussianToken.** It quotes LlamaGen at **2.19** —
the correct matched row, not the flattering one. I was hard on that paper this
afternoon and the criticism stands, but the rate mismatch I found there runs
through its own five continuous parameters per token, not through
misquotation. Having both papers in the record is what lets that be said
precisely instead of insinuated.

**Neither VQ-VAE nor VQGAN yields a practice, and I think that is right.**
Straight-through plus a codebook loss; `β = 0.25`; perceptual-plus-adversarial
reconstruction; the adaptive `λ = ∇[L_rec]/(∇[L_GAN]+δ)`. Every one of these
is universally copied and none is measured against its alternative anywhere in
this record. A recommendation nobody can decline is not a recommendation — it
is the definition of the object — and `DP-005` is exactly about not promoting
universal adoption into evidence.

Two candidates went into the readings instead, both cheap and both apparently
unpublished:

- **Does `β`'s insensitivity survive?** The 0.1–2.0 range was reported at
  `K = 512` in 2017 and has been inherited unexamined to `K = 16384`, where
  utilization dynamics are a different problem entirely.
- **Does the adaptive `λ` beat a tuned constant?** Presented with a formula
  and, as far as this reading found, never ablated. Everybody uses it.

**What I did not do is fill the next gap.** LlamaGen credits the whole
codebook design — `ℓ₂` normalization, low dimension, large size — to
ViT-VQGAN (Yu et al., 2021), which the record still does not hold. So the two
practices filed here are two independent *measurements* of somebody else's
recommendation rather than two independent discoveries, and the consensus
notes say so rather than letting `emerging` imply more than it should. Named,
not filled; the trunk was the unit of work, and stopping at its edge is how
the unit stays a unit.
