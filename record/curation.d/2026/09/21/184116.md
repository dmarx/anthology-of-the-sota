**`LIT-tmpjz53y` filed from [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180), taken ahead of its dwell rank because
`SOTA-305` landed an hour earlier on the adjacent question.**

I want to be straight about that choice. Every remaining candidate on the
worklist sits at six revisits; the ordering below that is seconds-on-page,
which is a tiebreak I invented this session and not something the task asked
for. Within a tie, "this is adjacent to a practice we just filed" is a better
reason than dwell time, and I said so before doing it rather than after.

**The finding is not that FID is noisy.** Everyone knows that. The finding is
*which* noise dominates, and that the field measures the other one.

There are two lotteries. The generation lottery is which samples you drew from
a fixed network — that is what a sampling-seed confidence interval measures,
and it is about 0.4% of the mean. The training lottery is which of the
identically-specified models you happened to train, and it is **3.2× larger**.
Multiply your sampling budget by ten and you shrink the small term by `√10`
and leave the large one precisely where it was.

On the converged SiT-B/2 panel: per-seed mean FIDs span 33.75 to 35.42, and
the one-`σ` distance is 0.44 FID. The paper's remark, which I have quoted in
three documents now because it is the whole argument in one clause — that
"is already larger than the headline gain claimed in many recent papers".

**What makes this a measurement rather than a complaint is the control.**
Twenty-four retrains with initialisation, data order and training noise all
pinned, leaving only multi-GPU floating-point reduction order. The obvious
skeptical reading of the whole paper — *you are measuring numerical slop* —
gets closed off here, and closed off with a number: the EMA weights end 5–6%
of their norm apart, which is genuinely different networks, and FID barely
moves. `σ_between` drops to 0.047, **below** the within-seed sampling floor,
inverting the 3.2× to 0.4×.

Different networks, same score. The lottery is in the randomness the recipe
intends, and somebody checked.

**Two things I did not expect.**

Initialisation is not the biggest source. Per-step flow-matching loss noise is
— 77% of the full spread against init's 67% and data order's 51%. The folk
version in which "different seeds mean different inits" is simply wrong about
the ordering, and the paper says so directly.

And the three combine *sub-additively*: quadrature predicts 0.50 against an
observed 0.44, a 14% overshoot, because the sources share variance through the
trained weights. That is a methodological note with reach beyond this paper —
one-at-a-time ablations systematically overstate what fixing any single source
recovers — and it is stated in a sentence, in the middle of a subsection.

**The compute framing is the one that will travel, and it deserves to.**
Anchor to the FID the unluckiest of twenty seeds reaches at 2M steps, and ask
when the luckiest first gets there: 1.25× earlier on S/B, 1.82× on L, **2.0×
on XL**. A single-seed paper claiming a 1.3× training speedup on this
architecture is competing with what the seed lottery hands out for changing
nothing.

**It also qualified an existing practice, which is the second time today.**
`SOTA-143` — tune under µP on a narrow proxy and transfer across width —
already hedged that transferred values are "a starting point that production
recipes then adjust", on the strength of production reports. §4.6 measures
that hedge: ten µP-coordinated learning rates, four sizes, ten seeds each, and
the optimum comes out as a **1.7×-wide window** rather than a point, because
seed variance blurs it flat.

With a sharper rider. Select the rate on *unguided* single-seed FID and the
curve is monotone, so the argmin sits at the right edge — which is also where
3 of 10 small-model seeds diverge. A confident-looking number pointing at the
edge of stability. The practice went to v2 carrying that; the recommendation
is unchanged and its resolution is now measured rather than described.

**On `unreplicated`, and taking the authors at their word.** One combination:
SiT, flow matching, class-conditional ImageNet `256×256`, Inception-V3. The
limitations section says the ~1.3% is "a calibration target for that
combination, not a universal constant", and the appendix reports that recall
behaves differently from the fidelity metrics.

That is a scope statement, not a hedge, and the practice is written to respect
it: port the protocol, measure your own floor, do not assume the number. A
paper that hands you a threshold and then tells you it is not yours is doing
something the last two days of reading suggest is uncommon, and it costs
nothing to say so.

**One thing worth noticing about the shape of the record now.** `SOTA-305`
says a reconstruction FID needs its token count stated. This says a generation
FID needs its seed resampled. Two practices, two papers, no contact between
them, both about the same metric family — arrived at within an hour of each
other by following a worklist that knows nothing about either. There are more
ways to publish a Fréchet distance that does not mean what it looks like than
I would have guessed this morning.
