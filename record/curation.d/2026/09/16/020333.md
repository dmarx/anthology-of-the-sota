The retagging pass in [#140](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/140) moved fifteen documents. Review:

> you've replaced a bunch of tags where you just needed to add an additional
> tag. the old tags still seem applicable in the cases I reviewed.

Re-reading them, that is right about eleven of the fifteen. `LIT-192`
(Positional Interpolation) and `LIT-193` (YaRN) went from
`attention-techniques` to `representation-and-encoding` — and
`attention-techniques` names *context length* in its own blurb, which is
exactly what a context-extension paper is about. `LIT-208` proposes a hybrid
**attention** strategy; the title says so. In each case the new topic is
better and the old one was not wrong.

## The part I did not notice I was choosing

`fields.tags.groups.primary_topic` required `exactly-one` of the thirteen, so
a second topic was a hard lint violation. **There was no additive option.**

That is the uncomfortable part. I did not weigh "replace" against "add" and
pick replace — the schema offered one move and I took it without registering
that it was a constraint rather than a decision. A constraint you do not
notice reads exactly like a judgement you made, and the artefact it leaves
behind is indistinguishable: fifteen documents that assert their old topic was
wrong, when eleven of them assert no such thing.

`luria.yaml` already records this corner from the other side, at [#101](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/101):
*"the only move available under `exactly-one` — it satisfied the check rather
than answering it."* There the constraint forced an incidental secondary tag
*in*. Here it forced a true topic *out*. Same constraint, opposite damage, and
the note about it was three screens above the rule while I was editing the
file.

## What changed

`require: any`, on all four schemes. `primary_topic` still derives
`{tags[0]}`, so the primary is still one value and the chain invariants still
compare one against one — verified, because it was the thing most likely to
break: `docs/reports/unbound-lineage.md` is byte-identical at 24 relations and
10 lines with the group relaxed and a second topic added.

The four that stay replacements are the ones where the old tag named **where
the claim was found** rather than what it is about — `SOTA-060` filed
`distributed-optimization` because its source is a Megatron paper,
`SOTA-092`/`093`/`094` filed `model-architecture` because theirs is a model
report. Restoring those would preserve a mistake rather than information.

## What this costs, and what it does not

**Tag order is now load-bearing.** Reordering `tags:` silently retags a
document, and nothing checks it. Measured before changing anything: all 596
documents currently list a topic first, so nothing is broken today — but the
convention was already unguarded and the ways to trip it just got more
plausible. `LU-#229` is the upstream ask.

**The invariant is deliberately untouched.** The obvious follow-on — let a
shared *secondary* bind a relation — is the failure `luria.yaml` rejects by
name: a shared incidental label reads as binding when nothing about the
subject matter is. So the flash-attention pair stays unbound, and giving both
ends an `attention-techniques` secondary would now be possible and still
wrong. Widening what a document may **say** is not widening what a relation
may **assert**.

## The pattern, updated

This is the fifth correction in three days, and the first that is not about
asserting something unverified. The previous four were claims about the
tooling made from memory. This one is the opposite: I read the tooling
correctly, did what it allowed, and never asked whether what it allowed was
what I wanted. The check that would have caught it is not "run the tool" but
"when the tool permits exactly one move, say out loud that it is the tool
choosing" — because that sentence, written down, is obviously a question for
the person who owns the schema.
