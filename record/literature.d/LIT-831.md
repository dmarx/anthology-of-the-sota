# LIT-831: LINE: Large-scale Information Network Embedding

Tang, Qu, Wang, Zhang, Yan, Mei, Microsoft Research Asia, Peking University
and University of Michigan (WWW 2015) — [ARXIV-1503.03578](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03578)

## Key takeaways

- **Two proximities, trained separately** (§4.1): LINE(1st) fits a sigmoid
  of u_i·u_j to observed edges, and only works on undirected graphs.
  LINE(2nd) gives each node a vertex vector and a context vector and fits
  p(v_j | v_i) to the normalized out-weights, with negative sampling at noise
  ∝ degree^(3/4). LINE(1st+2nd) concatenates the two, and is used only where
  a supervised classifier can reweight the halves
- **Edge sampling** (§4.2): an edge's weight multiplies its gradient, and
  word co-occurrence weights range from one to tens of thousands, so no
  single learning rate works. Sampling edges with probability ∝ weight
  through an alias table, and treating them as binary, keeps the objective
  and removes the weight from the gradient. Cost is O(dK|E|)
- **Word analogy on a 2M-node, 1B-edge Wikipedia network** (Table 2):
  LINE(2nd) 66.10% overall, SkipGram on the raw text 63.02%, graph
  factorization 51.93%, DeepWalk 43.65%. The same objectives trained by
  plain SGD reach 8.50% (1st) and 14.49% (2nd). Times are 2.44–2.55 hours
  for LINE and 16.64 for DeepWalk on a 40-core machine
- **Flickr, five largest communities** (Table 5, Micro-F1 at 10% labeled):
  LINE(1st+2nd) 63.20, LINE(1st) 63.27, LINE(2nd) 62.83, DeepWalk 60.38
- **YouTube, average degree about 5** (Table 6, Micro-F1 at 1% labeled):
  DeepWalk 39.68, LINE(1st+2nd) 39.01, LINE(1st) 35.43, LINE(2nd) 32.98.
  At 10%, LINE(1st+2nd) leads 46.08 to 45.23. Adding neighbors of neighbors
  to low-degree nodes (up to 1,000) lifts LINE(2nd) to 36.78 at 1%
- **Citation networks** (Tables 7–8): DeepWalk beats LINE(2nd) on the sparse
  author network until the same densification is applied, and LINE(2nd)
  wins clearly on the directed paper network, where a random walk can only
  follow citations backwards in time (Micro-F1 58.42 against 52.83 at 10%)

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from the graph-coverage pass** (gap list from the record's own
review, 2026-10-22).

**It is `compared_against` DeepWalk ([LIT-837](LIT-837.md))**, which it re-ran on
every network with γ = 40, t = 40, w = 10. LINE wins on the weighted
language network, where DeepWalk has to drop the weights, and on directed
citations, where DeepWalk's walks are one-way. On the sparsest graphs
DeepWalk's longer walks win unless LINE's input graph is densified first,
and the paper's fix for that is a preprocessing step, not part of the
objective.

**NetMF later puts both in one family.** Qiu et al. ([LIT-824](LIT-824.md)) derive
that LINE(2nd) with negative sampling factorizes
log(vol(G)·D⁻¹AD⁻¹) − log b, which is DeepWalk's matrix with a window of
one. [THEORY-130](../theory.d/THEORY-130.md) states the account. node2vec ([LIT-809](LIT-809.md)) re-ran LINE
at an equal sample budget and got much worse numbers than this paper's,
which it attributes to LINE not reusing samples.

**What the headline does not say.** The concatenated model is the one that
wins, and it is only defined for supervised use. The significance stars in
Table 6 are against DeepWalk, yet DeepWalk's own number is higher at 1%.
The Flickr task here is the five largest communities, not the 195-label task
DeepWalk used, so the two papers' Flickr numbers do not compare.
