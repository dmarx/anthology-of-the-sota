# NOTE-tmpszelm: LTX-Video

Read in full from arXiv v1 (30 December 2024): §§1–7, the sample figures
(Figs. 18–19) and the references. There is no appendix. The text is
defective in one place: §4.1 breaks off mid-sentence ("…ADAM-W
optimizerAfter pre-training…"), so the training schedule, steps, batch size
and data size are absent from the paper. That was not a failure to read.

## Contribution

A latent video diffusion model designed for speed. The VAE absorbs the
patchifier: 32×32×8 downsampling into 128 channels, total compression 1:192,
with a 1:8192 pixels-to-tokens ratio at the transformer input (Table 1). The
VAE decoder is trained as a conditional denoiser, so the last diffusion step
happens during decoding, in pixel space. The paper calls this a "holistic
approach to latent diffusion". It also contributes several VAE losses, a
positional-encoding scheme, and per-token timesteps for image conditioning.

## Key insight

**If the latent is too compressed to hold high-frequency detail, generate
the detail at decode time rather than trying to reconstruct it.** The
decoder D(z_t, t) is trained on noisy latents at t ∈ [0, 0.2] with
pixel-space losses. It maps between spaces of different dimensionality, so
it cannot be iterated, but it can do the final step "in a manner
inaccessible to the base model" (§2.1.1). Movie Gen and Sora pay for this
with a separate upsampler. LTX-Video folds it into the decoder.

## Assumptions

- **Artifacts from residual noise are the dominant cost of high
  compression.** "Our experiments show" the effect worsens with compression
  (§2.1.1). The experiments are not shown.
- **Tokens dominate cost.** The speed argument is attention's quadratic cost
  in tokens (§2.1). VAE decode cost, now larger because the decoder
  denoises, is not measured.
- **Survey preference at 768×512, 40 steps, measures quality for all
  models.** Every model was run there "with their default configurations"
  (§4.2).

## Key results

- **Compression (Table 1).** 32×32×8 × 128 channels gives 1:192 total and
  1:8192 tokens to pixels. CogVideoX and HunyuanVideo are 1:48 and 1:1024.
  Movie Gen and PyramidFlow are 1:96 and 1:2048. That makes LTX-Video 8×
  fewer tokens than the first pair and 4× fewer than the second. The paper
  says "four times the typical ratio".
- **Model (Table 1).** 1.9B parameters, 28 blocks, width 2048, self plus
  cross attention, T5-XXL, PixArt-α lineage, RMSNorm, QK-norm and RoPE.
- **Speed (§1, abstract).** 121 frames at 768×512 in about 2 s on one H100
  at 20 steps. No timing table, no baseline timings, and no breakdown of VAE
  and text-encoder cost.
- **Survey (Table 2, Fig. 15).** 1,000 T2V prompts and 1,000 I2V pairs (the
  first frames generated by FLUX.1), 5 s at 768×512, 40 steps, 20
  participants, all model pairs. Win rate is wins/(wins + losses), with
  skips allowed.
  - Overall: LTX-Video 85% T2V and 91% I2V. CogVideoX-2B 38%/47%,
    PyramidFlow 51%/35%, Open-Sora Plan 20%/20%.
  - Pairwise T2V: 0.96 against Open-Sora Plan, 0.85 against CogVideoX-2B,
    0.72 against PyramidFlow.
  - Two groups of 10 raters differed by less than 2%.
- **RoPE frequency spacing (Fig. 17).** Same architecture, hyperparameters
  and data. Exponential spacing keeps a lower training loss than
  inverse-exponential over about 140k steps.
- **Latent redundancy (Fig. 3).** PCA over 128 videos shows latent-channel
  correlations high at 4% of training and near zero at the end.
- **Timestep density (§2.5.2, Fig. 10).** SD3's distribution, which the
  paper calls "log-normal", shifted towards high noise by token count (μ = 1
  at ~256 tokens, μ = 2 at ~4096) and clamped at the 0.5 and 99.9
  percentiles.

## Method

Encode with a causal 3D-conv encoder, the first frame as its own latent
frame (Fig. 4a). Train the VAE on MSE, a 3D-DWT L1 loss (8 transforms), LPIPS
and a reconstruction GAN whose discriminator sees original and
reconstruction side by side (Fig. 5). Multi-layer noise injection with a
learned per-channel level. One shared log-variance.

The decoder is conditioned on t via adaptive normalization and trained at
t ∈ [0, 0.2]. The transformer runs rectified flow with velocity prediction.
Training covers mixed resolutions and durations at roughly constant token
count, dropping 0–20% of tokens (§2.5.3), with images as one more bucket
(§2.5.4). For I2V, the first-frame tokens occasionally get a small random
timestep during training. At inference they get t_c and the rest t = 1
(§2.4, Fig. 9).

## Concepts

- **Pixels-to-tokens ratio** — total pixels per transformer token after both
  the VAE and the patchifier. The paper's unit for comparing latent designs.
- **Denoising decoder** — a VAE decoder that is also the last diffusion step
  and outputs pixels.
- **Reconstruction GAN** — a relative discriminator that is given the pair
  and must say which is the original.
- **Per-token timestep** — noise level set per token, which turns
  conditioning into "this token is nearly clean".

## Connections

It builds on PixArt-α (DiT with text cross-attention) and DC-AE (high
spatial compression with many channels), and cites SimpleDiffusion and SD3
([LIT-449](../literature.d/LIT-449.md)) for the resolution shift. Per-token timesteps come from Open-Sora
1.x. Step-Video ([LIT-624](../literature.d/LIT-624.md)) cites it as a route to combining autoregression
and diffusion. Open-Sora 2.0 ([LIT-634](../literature.d/LIT-634.md)) later uses the same 32× spatial
compression, in a 128-channel DC-AE. It reports that the high-channel latent
slows diffusion convergence and that its adapted 11B model stays below the
original (§4.3). Neither paper compares against the other.

## Recommendations

- **R1** — When compressing aggressively, report reconstruction at the same
  token rate as the baselines, and report where the detail comes from.
  *Topic:* representation-and-encoding. *Status:* standard.
  *Strength:* moderate, by absence. This paper's VAE cannot be compared with
  any other. *Conditions:* applies to any latent that is claimed lossless.
- **R2** — Consider making the VAE decoder denoise the final step when the
  latent is too small to carry high frequencies. *Topic:* generative-modeling.
  *Status:* experimental. *Strength:* weak. *Conditions:* no numbers exist.
  Decoder cost rises.
- **R3** — Prefer exponentially spaced RoPE frequencies for video
  transformers. *Topic:* model-architecture. *Status:* experimental.
  *Strength:* weak. *Conditions:* one loss curve, at one scale.

## Limitations

- **The central idea is unquantified** (§4.4), and there is no ablation of
  denoising-decoder cost.
- **No VAE metrics**: no PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS or rFVD.
- **The training recipe is missing** (§4.1 truncated).
- **Speed and quality are measured at different step counts** (20 and 40).
  No model's timing is tabulated.
- **Baselines are run at 768×512 "with their default configurations"**, which
  is contradictory for models whose default is another resolution. The
  survey compares only 2024 open models of about 2B.

## Open questions

- What does the denoising decoder cost at decode time, and what is it worth?
- How does the VAE reconstruct against 1:48 and 1:96 VAEs at equal pixels?
- Does the exponential RoPE spacing result hold beyond training loss?

## Corrections to the LIT note

- **Imprecise:** "The transformer therefore sees 8× fewer tokens." **Fix:**
  8× relative to CogVideoX and HunyuanVideo (1:1024), and 4× relative to
  Movie Gen and PyramidFlow (1:2048). The paper says "four times the typical
  ratio" (§2.1, Table 1).
- **Overstated:** "It is trained on t ∈ [0, 0.2] and run at t = 0.05."
  **Fix:** t = 0.05 is the condition used in the §4.4 user study. The paper
  gives no default inference t.
- **Imported:** "CogVideoX-2B's native resolution is 720×480." That is not
  in this paper. The paper says every model ran at 768×512 "with their
  default configurations". The tension is the paper's own, and the 720×480
  should be cited to [LIT-622](../literature.d/LIT-622.md).
- **Missing:** the LIT note does not say that the VAE has **no
  reconstruction metric**. That is the reason "quality is maintained"
  cannot be checked. It belongs among the hedges.
