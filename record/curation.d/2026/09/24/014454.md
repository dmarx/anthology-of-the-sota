`#290` triaged 128 catalogue identifiers and declined 81. Its largest decline
group, `D-variant` at 27, used the rule *"catalogue variant; nothing held
depends on it and the incumbent is filed"*. The metric sweep found a false
negative in it, and re-reading the class under a corrected question reversed
**6 of the 27** — filed as `#326`.

This entry is about the rule, not the papers.

## The two questions, and why only one of them can find substrate

The `D-variant` rule asks **"is this paper's contribution already covered?"**
That is a question about the paper. It is cheap, it is answerable from a
title, and it is why the pass could run over 128 identifiers in one sitting.

The question that finds substrate defects is **"does this paper measure
something the record relies on?"** That is a question about the *record*. It
needs the abstract at minimum and the paper at best.

The first question cannot return the second's answers, and no amount of
running it harder helps — which is `DP-004` exactly, arriving in a new place.
A filter has a complement and the complement needs a different filter.

## The measurement that showed it

`1801.01401` was declined on **four** record mentions of KID. The four
mentions are of KID; what the paper carries is a proof that FID's plug-in
estimator is biased, that **no unbiased estimator exists**, and a
construction where 50,000 samples return the wrong ordering in all 100 trials
with a standard deviation small enough to hide it. The record held three
documents about FID's failure modes and not one of them knew this.

**A candidate ranked by the name in its contribution gets ranked by the wrong
thing, and a mention count inherits the error.** For a paper whose
contribution is a *correction*, "what the paper made" and "what the record
depends on" come apart completely — and a triage that counts occurrences of
the former is measuring the wrong quantity with no indication that it is.

## The part worth keeping: the correction was written down and not applied

`#290` found that 23 identifiers had no bibtex entry and had been labelled by
the nearest README heading, that 4 of those labels were materially wrong, and
it stated the rule: *a heading names the feature a citation supports, not the
paper.* It promoted three of the four.

**The fourth it declined, with the leaked heading still in its row** — in the
same document that had just explained why that heading was untrustworthy. Four
more leaks sit in the same decline list, among them Gopher recorded as "Root
Mean Square Layer Normalization" and AlphaCode recorded under the title of a
different paper entirely.

This is the second instance this week of one shape: **identifying a defect and
fixing its instances are separate acts, and finishing the first feels like
finishing the second.** The other was `#324`, where a uniqueness violation was
cleared correctly and the duplication it was a symptom of stayed live. Both
times the cheap half is the one a check can point at, and the expensive half
is the one that needs reading.

So a pass that discovers a systematic error mid-flight owes itself a second
sweep for that error specifically, over everything it already decided.
Recording the rule in the write-up is not that sweep.

## What this does not license

**Re-reading every decline is not the lesson.** `D-biblio` (27) is mostly
2025–2026 preprints with no feature section, and the corrected question
probably returns little there; the honest expectation is near zero. `D-roster`
(7) is checkpoint rows and dataset cards and the question does not apply.

Where it does apply is `D-cluster` (18), and for a different reason: those were
declined *behind openers*, and at least one opener (`2103.14030`, Swin) was
promoted by `#290` and **never filed**. A decline whose condition was "promote
this with its opener" goes stale the moment the opener is promoted and
dropped, and nothing reports that.

## The generalisable form

A decline needs to record which question it was answering, because the
question is what goes out of date. `#290` recorded its triggers — *"a held
document starts depending on the mechanism"* — and those are still right.
What it did not record is that the *disposition* was reached by asking about
the paper, which is the thing a later reader needs in order to know the
decline is worth revisiting at all.
