This unit started from a wrong guess that was pointing at the right place. The
question was whether the record held VQGAN-CLIP, on the reasoning that it is "a
good citation for early classifier guidance work, which CFG builds on." It is
not: `2204.08583` is April 2022, eleven months *after* ADM, and its mechanism is
optimising a latent against a CLIP score, not adding a classifier gradient to a
denoiser's score estimate. But the thing being reached for exists, one paper
over, and the record did not hold it either.

**Classifier guidance is `2011.13456` §5, Eq. 14, November 2020** — six months
before ADM:

    dx = {f(x,t) − g(t)²[∇ₓ log pₜ(x) + ∇ₓ log pₜ(y|x)]}dt + g(t)dw̄

together with the recipe for the noise-conditioned classifier that supplies the
second term. ADM says as much in its own words, which this record has been
quoting for weeks without holding the referent: a "score-based conditioning
trick adapted from Song et al."

## Three citing sites, and the same shape a fourth time

| site | what it leaned on this paper for |
| --- | --- |
| `NOTE-358` | the DDIM-variant guidance substitution, "adapted from Song et al." |
| `NOTE-347` | that reconstruction guidance *corrects* this paper's imputation approach |
| `NOTE-067` | that DDIM is **not** the continuous-time/SDE paper — a distinction drawn against it |

Four consecutive units have now found the same defect: the record cites a paper
as an instrument or an authority, repeatedly, and holds nothing. `NOTE-067` is
the variant worth naming. It is not a gap *sentence* — nobody wrote "the record
does not hold X." It is a note whose entire corrective purpose is to separate
paper A from paper B, filed while B was unheld. **A distinction is only as
checkable as its least-held term**, and this one had been uncheckable since
September 10th, in a note whose visible job was to fix a confusion.

Nothing in the toolchain sees any of these. `luria lint` checks relations
between documents; a surname in prose is not a relation.

## A rejection that did not name what it rejects

`SOTA-397` tells you to condition an unconditionally trained diffusion model
with reconstruction guidance and not with **replacement**, on VDM's FVD 136
against 451. v1 argued against replacement as though it were folklore — the
obvious wrong thing people do.

It is not folklore. It is this paper's §I.2, and the paper writes down the exact
step where it breaks. It wants `pₜ(z(t) | Ω(x(0)) = y)`, calls it "in general
intractable", and approximates

    pₜ(z(t)|A) = 𝔼[pₜ(z(t)|Ω(x(t)), A)] ≈ 𝔼[pₜ(z(t)|Ω(x(t)))]

dropping the conditioning on the *exact* known values and keeping only the
*noised* known dimensions. The discarded term is precisely the requirement that
new content agree with the real frames rather than with a noisy copy of them.
Quote that and a 136-against-451 gap stops being a surprising fact about
splicing and becomes the size of a named approximation error.

**Generalising:** a practice with no `source:` fails the lint. A practice that
rejects a named alternative with no source *for the alternative* fails nothing,
and reads as more confident than it is, because "everyone knows X is worse" and
"X's own authors published the approximation that makes it worse" are the same
sentence on the page and very different claims. v2 changes no recommendation, no
status and no consensus — only which of its assertions a reader can follow to a
document.

## Verified against the paper, not against the note

`NOTE-347`'s summary said reconstruction guidance corrects Song et al.'s
imputation approach. That is what sent this unit at §I.2 — and it would have
been enough to write the `SOTA-397` insert from, without opening the appendix.
Two of the last three units filed a correct conclusion resting on a mechanism
detail I had asserted rather than read (the precision/recall feature space,
twice in one afternoon). So §I.2 was read in the paper before anything was
written about it. The summary was right. That is the outcome to expect from the
check, not a reason to skip it.

## No practice, no theory, deliberately

The conditional-score decomposition is a derivation, not a contested
explanation; a `THEORY` for it would be filing on novelty rather than on a
defect — the call already made about EDM2's magnitude-preserving layers.
Predictor-corrector sampling *is* a recommendation, but the record's sampler
practices (`SOTA-203`, `SOTA-410`, `SOTA-416`) live on the deterministic branch
and this paper runs no controlled comparison against them, so filing it would
mean filing a preference with nothing behind it. Filing the substrate three
documents were standing on is the whole contribution.

## Deliberately not in this unit

VQGAN-CLIP goes to the reading list, not to the record: CLIP-scored latent
optimisation is a lineage covered nowhere here, which makes it a genuine gap and
a lower-priority one than a paper three notes already cite.

And the standing finding from the guidance cluster is now two units old and
unaddressed: `LIT-693` and `LIT-tmpbpv9d` both ask what guidance costs in
coverage, and neither measures it on guided samples. That is a better argument
for `SOTA-425` than anything currently in `SOTA-425`.
