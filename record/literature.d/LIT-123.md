# LIT-123: Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?

Pipis et al. (2025) — [ARXIV-2512.12895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12895)

## Key takeaways

- Open reasoning models frequently loop under greedy or low-temperature
  decoding, repeating the same span indefinitely. Temperature matters;
  larger models loop less; and distilled students loop significantly even
  when the teacher they were distilled from rarely does.
- The pattern points at the mismatch between the training distribution and
  what the model actually learned — errors in learning — rather than at
  decoding alone.
- A synthetic graph-reasoning task isolates two mechanisms. The first is
  risk aversion from hardness of learning: when the correct progress-making
  step is hard to learn and an easy cyclic step is available, the model
  puts relatively more mass on the cycle and gets stuck. That is the one
  [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) invokes.

## Standing in the anthology

The explanation [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) gives for [SOTA-127](../practices.d/SOTA-127.md): a 90M model shown
chain-of-thought traces beyond its capacity learned the cheaper pattern,
repetition, and stopped producing function calls. Filed for the mechanism;
its own remedies are not yet drawn into a practice.
