Fourth unit down the reading feed. This one went back to something I filed
two days ago and both corroborated and bounded it, which is the first time
that has happened in the same week.

## What I was looking for

[THEORY-025](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-025.md) — a transformer holds about 3.6 bits per parameter — shipped
with `explains:` empty, and the curation entry at the time said why: the
experiment connecting capacity to multi-epoch overfitting had not been run by
anyone. I went looking down the feed for anything that would give it a
neighbour.

Three papers turned up and they do different things to it.

## Corroboration, in different units

[LIT-tmpvt6e3](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmpvt6e3.md) measures how many Wikidata triples a model can recall and
finds capacity **linear in model size**. [THEORY-025](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-025.md) measures storage in
*bits*, on uniform random bitstrings where generalisation is impossible by
construction. This measures storage in *facts*, on a real knowledge base,
where generalisation is possible and is separately quantified.

Two instruments, two units, one functional form. That is better evidence
than either alone, and I wrote it into both documents.

**What it is not is agreement on a magnitude.** Nobody has converted facts to
bits, so the two rhyme rather than check each other. The open question is now
written down in both places rather than left as an impression.

## The bound, which is the more interesting half

[LIT-tmphrxdn](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmphrxdn.md) mixes a knowledge-dense dataset into web text at realistic
shares and the linear law disappears. Not degrades — disappears. Below a
critical model size, or below a critical mixing ratio, the model memorises
almost nothing, **and training longer does not help**. Past the threshold it
jumps to over 60%.

The account: a model with bounded capacity training on a mixture is solving
an allocation problem, and an allocation problem has a discrete answer, and a
discrete answer moves in jumps when its inputs move smoothly.

**So the clean laws everyone has been fitting were all measured on one
dataset at a time** — which is the case where the budget has exactly one
claimant and fills smoothly. That reframes [THEORY-025](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-025.md) without
contradicting it: the plateau it measures is the shape competition does not
produce.

## Two practices this contests, and how carefully

[SOTA-166](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-166.md) fits a mixing law on small runs and extrapolates. [SOTA-238](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-238.md) sets
domain weights with a small proxy under group DRO. Both assume the fitted
quantity is continuous in scale. If a domain's threshold falls between the
proxy scale and the target scale, the small run is measuring a different
regime's answer rather than a noisy version of the right one.

I moved both to `contested` and wrote the same caution into both: **this is
not a refutation.** Both methods were measured, both work where they were
measured, and a mixture whose domains all sit on the same side of their
transitions at both scales transfers fine — plausibly the common case for the
large web domains those methods were validated on. The exposure is to
precisely the case they are most wanted for: small, high-value,
knowledge-dense domains at low share.

The failure mode is also silent, which is what makes it worth writing down. A
proxy below threshold reports a confident weight, not an error.

[SOTA-103](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-103.md) I deliberately did *not* contest. It adjusts proportions online
from per-domain loss during the run being trained, so it never extrapolates
across scale. Whether an online rule can cross a discontinuity it cannot see
the far side of is a real question, and it is a different one — noted as
open rather than filed as a contest.

## A plateau that turned out to be a thing being built

[LIT-tmpbtdlg](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmpbtdlg.md) is the best-instrumented paper in the batch. Factual recall
trains through a long plateau, and the plateau is the attention extraction
circuit forming: until it exists, the error at the attribute token does not
reach the name tokens, so the key-value store gets no usable signal.

The evidence is an **intervention**, which is why [THEORY-tmp8a4gm](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-tmp8a4gm.md) is `Active`
and [THEORY-tmpijbl9](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-tmpijbl9.md) is `Proposed` on the same day. Patch a reference
model's attention patterns into a training model and the plateau disappears;
patterns from later in the plateau work better; attention to name tokens
rises through it. Three signatures and one of them is causal.

The knapsack account, by contrast, fits its data well and has not been pried
apart from an optimisation story that would fit it too. Same batch, same
week, different standards — and the difference is whether anyone intervened.

## The absence nobody was complaining about

[LIT-060](../../../../literature.d/LIT-060.md) is RETRO. It has been in this record since early on. The only
practice drawn from it is [SOTA-197](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-197.md), about contamination in evaluation.

**The record held the paper that established retrieval-augmented pretraining
works and had no practice saying to use it.** That is the second time this
week a gap has surfaced not from a complaint but from an arriving paper
pointing at it — last time it was an `implementations:` entry naming a system
whose paper was absent. Both are structured-field shapes rather than prose
complaints, and [#86](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/86)'s trunk detector reads prose.

## One over-read I caught

I nearly titled the retrieval practice as a claim about factual competence.
It is not. The same paper shows models **generalise** to unseen facts at
roughly ordinary pretraining scaling, with generalisability varying sharply
by fact type — and the same underlying quantity, input-output correlation
strength, appears to drive both how easily a fact type is memorised and how
well it generalises.

So the advice is about an *enumerable knowledge base* — the thing you could
have put in a database — and a reader who takes it as "models can't do facts"
would conclude, wrongly, that factual capability has to be bolted on rather
than trained. That distinction is now a section in the practice rather than a
sentence I hoped someone would notice.
