# LIT-090: Symbolic Discovery of Optimization Algorithms

Chen et al., Google (2023) — [ARXIV-2302.06675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06675)

## Key takeaways

- Optimizer design posed as program search, with the selection and
  simplification strategies that stop a proxy-task winner from being an
  artefact of the proxy.
- What it found is **Lion** (Evolved Sign Momentum): keep only the momentum
  — half of Adam's optimizer state — and take the *sign* of an interpolation
  between momentum and gradient, so every parameter's update has the same
  magnitude. It is a rule simple enough to read, which is the point of
  searching program space rather than tuning a parametrized family.
- Results across domains: ViT up to +2% on ImageNet and up to 5× less JFT
  pretraining compute; 88.3% zero-shot ImageNet on vision-language
  contrastive learning; a better FID with up to 2.3× less training compute
  on diffusion models; on autoregressive and masked language modelling,
  similar to or better than Adam.
- Two consequences of the sign update that are recipe, not trivia: the gain
  grows with batch size, and the learning rate must be smaller than Adam's
  because the sign function makes the update norm larger.
- The paper states its own limits — scenarios where the improvement is small
  or not statistically significant — and reports production deployment on
  Google's search-ads CTR model.

## Standing in the anthology

**What the retirement was.** The migration filed this paper twice — once
under its real title and once as

*Lion: Learning with Implicit Optimization* ([LIT-105](LIT-105.md), retired as the
duplicate) — and put this copy in the attic as "a research direction without
demonstrated practical impact". That sentence was written against a
four-bullet description of the paper as automated discovery and theory. It is
not what the paper is.

**Why it is reversed rather than left standing.** Re-examined against the
work: NeurIPS 2023, 705 citations and 116 influential citations on Semantic
Scholar, a concrete optimizer, results reported in four domains, and a named
production deployment on Google's search-ads CTR model. "Not interesting
enough to carry" is not a defensible reading of that, and `Rejected` is a
claim about standing in this anthology rather than about the work being
wrong — so leaving it there was asserting something the record does not
believe.

**And it was partly a scope artefact.** Three quarters of this paper's
evidence is vision, vision-language and diffusion. Under the scope
[ADR-tmpqq6xc](../decisions.d/ADR-tmpqq6xc.md) replaces, that material could not have supported a
recommendation here at all, so a reader following the old rule would have
found little in the paper that counted. Retiring it was over-determined:
a wrong description, and a scope that discounted most of its results.

<!-- inactive-ok-block: ADR-tmpqq6xc — Proposed, named as the decision that considers and declines the axis this note would seed -->
**What it is evidence of, beyond its own claim.** This is the corpus's only
cross-domain optimizer result — one method, one group, four modalities. That
makes it the natural first entry for a `transfers:` axis if the record ever
builds one, which [ADR-tmpqq6xc](../decisions.d/ADR-tmpqq6xc.md) sets out and declines to build on the strength
of a single document. One is not enough; it is also not nothing.

Read alongside the record's live optimizer line, which runs the other way:
[SOTA-165](../practices.d/SOTA-165.md) preconditions the gradient as a matrix and [SOTA-121](../practices.d/SOTA-121.md)'s Muon
orthogonalises the momentum, where Lion is entrywise and discards magnitude
altogether. The record holds no comparison between the two branches.
