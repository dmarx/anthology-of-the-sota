Unit A of `#304`, which audited 29 papers in the self-supervised
representation-learning lineage and found the record holding **one**.

## The gap was a vocabulary gap, not only a coverage gap

Before this, `InfoNCE`, `negative sampling`, `siamese` and `triplet loss`
returned **zero matches anywhere in the record**. That is a different kind of
absence from "we have not filed SimCLR". The record held `SOTA-359`, whose
source note `LIT-588` explicitly credits Oord et al. for the loss it uses,
and `SOTA-250`, which recommends predicting representations rather than
pixels — two practices resting on an objective the record could not name.

Worth generalising: **a coverage audit that counts papers will miss the case
where the missing document is a word.** The `#290` substrate test (how many
held documents lean on it) found ViT and CLIP because the *names* were in the
prose. It cannot find a paper whose absence means the prose never got
written.

## The arithmetic the field dropped

CPC derives `I(x;c) ≥ log(N) − L_N` and remarks that the bound "becomes
tighter as N becomes larger". What it does not say, and what follows in one
line, is that `L_N` is a cross-entropy over `N` classes and therefore
non-negative — so the bound **can never certify more than `log N`**. At
CLIP's batch of 32,768 that is about 10.4 nats, whatever the image and its
caption actually share.

Two sentences in the paper get merged downstream and they are not the same:

- **the estimator's optimum is independent of `N`** — the paper proves it;
- **the MI certificate is bounded by `N`** — the paper states it, with the
  ceiling left implicit.

The folklore that came out is "more negatives is better", which is a claim
about the second, saturates, and gets applied as though it were about the
first. This is `DP-010` in its exact shape: the citable sentence is the one
whose qualification lives in the algebra rather than the prose. It is filed
as `THEORY-tmpptbj9`, separately from the practice, because `ADR-031`'s split
is precisely for this — the practice can be right while the reason given for
it is the wrong reason, and a live literature argues that the MI framing *is*
the wrong reason.

## A relation that needed a tag, handled the way [ADR-049](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-049.md) asks

`SOTA-359` (CLIP's caption practice) plainly `extends` the new general one —
`LIT-588` cites Oord for the loss, and CLIP's recipe is the image-text case.
But the two shared no tag, so the edge would have come back unbound.

The resolution was not to invent a label. `training-optimization` is *true*
of `SOTA-359` on its own terms: that practice's entire justification is a
training-efficiency measurement, 3× and 4× in rate of transfer per unit
compute, and someone browsing that topic would be right to expect it. The
history note says the relation is what prompted the re-reading, which is the
honest version — `ADR-049` wants a true topic added, and wants it visible
that the relation is why somebody looked.

The near-miss worth recording: the tempting move was to put
`multimodal-learning` on the CPC practice, since CPC runs on speech, images,
text and RL. That would have been the "nearest wrong tag" failure. CPC runs
one mechanism on four modalities *separately*; `multimodal-learning` is about
what changes when one model takes in more than one signal at once. Four
unimodal experiments are not a multimodal claim.

## Filing note

The practice takes `representation-and-encoding` as primary, following
`SOTA-250`, which is the record's existing precedent for a pretraining-
objective practice. No new topic was needed. That is worth saying because the
first instinct was that the vocabulary had no word for "training objective" —
it does not, exactly, but the existing reading of `representation-and-encoding`
as *how the representation is made* already covers it, and one precedent is
better than a twenty-second topic.
