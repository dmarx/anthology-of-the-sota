**The owner supplied fourteen papers against one open question**: does
`THEORY-059`'s spectral claim — weight spectra are steep, quantization-error
spectra are flat — hold outside diffusion transformers? Five were filed. The
question is answered in one direction and untouched in the other, and the
half that got answered did not come back the shape the account assumed.

**The triage was the highest-value ten minutes.** Before reading anything in
depth, grep every paper for `quantiz`: zero hits for a quantization-residual
spectrum in all fourteen. That settled immediately that the flat half of the
account could not be met by this set, whatever else came out — so the reading
could be organized around the steep half and around what else the papers
happened to say, rather than around hoping.

**The steep half is met decisively and the account still moves.** Four papers
put `W`'s spectrum outside diffusion: BERT, Pythia-410M, Llama-3.1-8B,
LLaMA-2 7B and 13B, Mistral-7B, and eleven GPT-2-style checkpoints varying
size, language, tokenizer and corpus. Steepness is real. It is also **strongly
non-uniform** — Query, Key and MLP Gate converge to low rank, MLP Up, Down and
Value do not, middle blocks resist while the ends give way — and
`THEORY-059` said "a weight matrix is the steep case" as though it were a
property of weight matrices. It is a property of some of them, and the spread
is large enough to dominate a compression decision.

**Random matrix theory gave the account something it did not have: a name for
its own flat case.** `THEORY-059` argues `W - Q(W)` is flat because
rounding is near-independent across entries. Near-independent entries with
finite variance is exactly the Marchenko-Pastur hypothesis. So the two cases
are "MP plus outliers" and "MP" — which is a sharper statement than the
account makes, and is still not a measurement. The `promote_when` is now
narrowed to that one plot, and `ADR-014` is satisfied in a way it was not
before: the old condition could be half-met by papers that leave the
load-bearing premise untouched, which is what just happened.

**The finding that was not on the worklist is the one worth keeping.**
Eckart-Young says the truncated SVD is the optimal low-rank approximation in
Frobenius norm, and the whole account runs on Frobenius norm — legitimately,
because its propositions bound output error by magnitudes. What nobody states,
and every reader supplies, is that the directions holding least magnitude are
the ones the model needs least. In non-square transformer matrices that is
false: zeroing the smallest decile of Llama-3 8B's Down-Projection costs 41
points of GSM8K, second only to zeroing the largest. The square
Attention-Output matrix, same operation, costs three points. The distinction
is geometric — a rectangular matrix's MP support has a lower edge above zero,
so singular values can fall *below* the bulk, and square matrices cannot do
that.

**Two practices, and the grep said both were absent.** The record holds LoRA,
SVDQuant, error compensation, block scaling, quantization-aware training and a
spectral-optimizer measurement instruction, and none of them says the rank is
a per-matrix question or that magnitude order is not importance order.
`SOTA-274` is the nearest thing and it is the same shape one floor over —
measure a block's stable rank before adopting a spectral optimizer. Counted as
a second instance in the note, promoted to nothing. `DP-009`.

**Three instruments, one agreed conclusion, two disputed cells.** Hessian gaps
and heavy tails; activation-covariance overlap and perplexity ablation;
effective-rank entropy across eleven checkpoints. All three find
non-uniformity. They place Value and Gate differently. That disagreement is
not a defect in the filing — it is the reason the practice says *measure
yours* instead of shipping a table of which matrices compress.

**A reconciliation that cost nothing and settles a real dispute.** Two
published results contradicted each other on whether small singular values
matter. The resolution is procedural: one fine-tunes before pruning, the other
does not, and varying only that order reproduces both. Fine-tuning writes into
the small directions. The consequence is unpleasant and belongs in the record:
removing them can improve a reasoning benchmark and delete alignment in the
same operation, and the benchmark will not show you the second thing.

**What the answer cost the account it was meant to support.** `THEORY-059`
was leaning on an assumption it never stated — that the residual left after
peeling the dominant directions is undifferentiated. The bottom outliers live
in that residual. Flagged in `THEORY-059` and `SOTA-314` and claimed in
neither, because zeroing is not quantizing and nobody has drawn the damage
curve against bit-width. That curve is now the cheapest open question in the
cluster.

**Nine papers not filed, listed rather than forgotten.** Spectral norm,
attention entropy collapse, token clustering dynamics and low-sensitivity
functions are about other objects; the CLIP paper uses SVD as a fine-tuning
method rather than measuring a spectrum. Saying so here costs a paragraph and
saves the next pass from re-triaging fourteen PDFs. `DP-004`: the query was
"which paper plots a singular value distribution of a weight matrix", and it
is the query that did the work.
