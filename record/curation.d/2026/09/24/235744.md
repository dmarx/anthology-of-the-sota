Tier B unit 1 of `#342`. A new defect shape, and a cheap one to check for
once named.

## The shape

`SOTA-153` recommends dropping positional encoding from the global-attention
layers of a hybrid. Its `source:` list carries a careful frontmatter comment:

> `LIT-207` is not a hybrid paper — it is the result the other three rest on —
> but it is the source of the claim that the global layers lose nothing by
> dropping the encoding.

That comment is doing exactly the right work. It distinguishes the papers that
*ship the layout* from the paper that *establishes the result the layout needs*,
which is a distinction most source lists collapse. And the paper it names is the
wrong one.

`LIT-207` is Kazemnejad et al., **2023**. Its own summary in this record reads
*"A decoder-only transformer without positional encoding is shown to represent
both absolute and relative position anyway."* The absolute half of that is
Haviv et al., **2022** — a year earlier, with the probing that shows where the
position appears and the mechanism that explains why.

## Why this is not the `introduced_by` defect

Three units of `#342` turned on `introduced_by` pointing at an adopter. This is
adjacent and distinct, and the difference is worth keeping.

`introduced_by` asks *who first made this recommendation.* The failures there
were about **practices** crediting the wrong paper for advice.

Here the field is `source:` and the object is a **result**. `LIT-207` is a
legitimate source for `SOTA-153` — it ran the comparison the practice's
extrapolation argument rests on. What went wrong is that a *second* result,
which `LIT-207` reports because it inherited it, got attributed to `LIT-207` as
well. The paper says the true thing; the record read it as the origin of
everything it says.

**A paper that restates a prior result in its own abstract will be credited
with it by anyone reading only that abstract.** This is the same failure mode as
`#343`'s — an abstract states a contribution in the strongest defensible form —
one level over: not about priority of a *recommendation* but priority of a
*finding*, and detectable by the same move, which is to read the related-work
section.

The cheap check, now that it is named: for a `source:` entry that supplies a
result rather than a recommendation, **ask whether that paper's related work
credits someone else for it.**

## What the correction bought

Not just a citation. Three things the cluster did not have.

**A mechanism.** 23 documents here say "NoPE" and none said why it works. It is
the causal mask: a token that can count how many predecessors it may attend to
has its absolute index. Filed as `THEORY-102`.

**A boundary.** The mechanism is causal, so it is a decoder-only fact. The
paper's control is a bidirectional MLM, where NoPos perplexity is **147.18
against 4.00** — a failure to train, not a degradation. That is both the
strongest evidence for the account and the clearest statement of where it
stops.

**A depth profile.** Position is absent at layer 1, recovered within four
layers, as good as learned embeddings by mid-network. Which matters because
`SOTA-153` is a *layout* decision: a hybrid choosing which layers may go
without an encoding is choosing among layers that differ in how much implicit
position has accumulated. **No document in this record says what that does**,
including the two filed here, and it is the obvious question the layout raises.

## A qualification the practice was overstating

"Lose nothing by dropping the encoding" is stronger than the evidence. Haviv et
al.'s own limitations section:

> although the margins are very small, **NoPos is always slightly worse**,
> suggesting that the inductive bias of positional encoding is indeed
> important.

And their scale sweep shows smaller models benefiting measurably from fixed
non-parametric encodings, with the gap closing as scale grows — so the parity is
a large-model phenomenon.

What NoPE actually *wins* is extrapolation past the training length, which is
`LIT-207`'s result. So the practice's grounds are sound and were misdescribed:
it stands on the extrapolation, not on parity. Untangling the two papers is
what made that visible — **the wrong attribution was also hiding which argument
the practice was making.**

## `Active` on one source, for the second time in two days

`THEORY-102` is `Active` on a single paper, and the justification is the
one `THEORY-101` used: the account made a prediction with a sharp consequence,
and the consequence was tested. Here the prediction is that removing the causal
mask removes the mechanism, and the MLM table is the test.

Worth restating the standard, because this session has now filed four theories
on single sources and split them two-two: **a successful intervention derived
from the hypothesis, not a correlation consistent with it.** `THEORY-099` (Muon) and
`THEORY-100` (quantization outliers) are `Proposed` because their mechanisms are
measured alongside their effects; these two are `Active` because somebody acted on the account and
got what it required.
