# LIT-119: Falcon-H1-Tiny: A series of extremely small, yet powerful language models redefining capabilities at small scale

Falcon-LLM Team, Technology Innovation Institute (2026) —
[tiny-h1-blogpost](https://huggingface.co/spaces/tiiuae/tiny-h1-blogpost).
Not an arXiv paper; the release's own citation is
`@misc{falcon_h1_tiny, author={Falcon-LLM Team}, year={2026}}`.

## Key takeaways

- Fixing the model at 90M parameters and varying everything else shows that
  data strategy, architectural hyperparameters and the optimizer are three
  independent levers, each of which moves the result at this scale.
- Muon, with weight decay applied and the update RMS scaled to match AdamW
  as in [ARXIV-2502.16982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16982), trains stably at nearly the same optimal learning
  rate as AdamW and evaluates better; every released model uses it.
- Learnable multipliers on each row and column of every weight matrix
  ([ARXIV-2601.04890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04890)) let the layer's norm be learned instead of being fixed
  by the LR/WD equilibrium. Two otherwise identical 200 GT runs — Muon alone
  against Muon plus LRM, same data mixture, WSD with a 50 GT decay — give
  what the authors call a considerable and consistent boost across every
  benchmark tested; the blogpost reports it as a plot rather than a table, so
  the quantified version is the preprint's ([LIT-121](LIT-121.md)), not this one's.
- A model forgets training tokens seen long enough ago. The authors call the
  delay after which the loss gap closes the *memorization window* — roughly
  100 GT, or 500 GT conservatively, for FalconMamba-7B — and argue that a
  high-quality source whose epoch size exceeds the window can be repeated
  without overfitting. SFT sources like Tulu3 were repeated 100 or more
  times across the 800 GT of SFT-pretraining with no visible artefacts, which
  the authors offer as implicit confirmation rather than a measurement. They
  call this understanding early and leave the systematic study to future
  work.
- *Anti-curriculum*: because a 90M model's window scales down to about 5 GT,
  SFT data can make up the whole mix. Pretraining from scratch on 25% SFT
  data beat pretrain-then-SFT on IFEval (50.1 vs 40.8 before DPO, 66.1 vs
  53.5 after), and one checkpoint serves as both base and instruct model; a
  further SFT stage on top added nothing. Pretraining on reasoning traces
  beat a reasoning-SFT stage the same way (AIME24 pass@16 6/30 vs 3/30,
  MATH500 0.4 vs 0.2). It did *not* help for multilingual or tool-calling
  data, where the two strategies tied.
- Architecture at a fixed 90M budget: a 24–27 layer, 512 hidden
  configuration beat shallower and wider ones; a large SSM state dimension
  beat a large MLP expansion at every width tried; doubling depth to 50
  layers helped MMLU but halved throughput. Vocabulary 32k, tied embeddings.
- DPO works at 90M: one epoch at LR 1e-6 to 3e-6 takes IFEval from about 50
  to over 65 while other benchmarks hold. A second epoch degrades the model
  even as the DPO reward keeps rising.
- Chain-of-thought traces in the tool-calling mix sent the 90M model into
  repetition loops; filtering them out fixed it. Structured capabilities
  like tool calling looked capacity-bound rather than exposure-bound.
- Fill-in-the-middle: train the loss on every token, not just the middle
  span; PSM format; include samples where the model must produce the
  indentation itself. Dropout 0.1 after the linear projections recovered
  HumanEval-FIM under the heavy repetition of a small FIM corpus.
- A 0.6B model pretrained on reasoning traces alone, plus 60 GRPO steps at
  LR 3e-6, reaches AIME24 pass@1 75.0 and MATH500 94.0; GRPO is sensitive to
  the learning rate above all else.
- Common recipe: LR 256e-5, WD 0.1, batch 4 MT, WSD over 800 GT ([SOTA-140](../practices.d/SOTA-140.md))
  with a 100 GT ×64 exponential decay and a power-law decay of the rate from
  100 GT ([SOTA-142](../practices.d/SOTA-142.md)), µP multipliers ([SOTA-143](../practices.d/SOTA-143.md)) carried over from Falcon-H1
  with the transfer done through forward multipliers.

## Standing in the anthology

The first source in this record that is not a paper. It is here because it
is the only published ablation of these choices at this scale, and because
several of its findings are stated as recipes the reader can follow — the
test a practice has to pass. Its evidence is single-run ablations at one
size on benchmarks the authors themselves call noisy at 90M (BBH, GPQA,
MuSR); every practice drawn from it is scoped to tiny models for that
reason. The work it leans on for those decisions is filed alongside it: the
Falcon-H1 report ([LIT-120](LIT-120.md)), the learnable-multiplier preprint ([LIT-121](LIT-121.md)),
the Muon scaling report ([LIT-122](LIT-122.md)), the looping analysis behind its
chain-of-thought filtering ([LIT-123](LIT-123.md)), the three FIM papers ([LIT-124](LIT-124.md),
[LIT-125](LIT-125.md), [LIT-126](LIT-126.md)), GRPO ([LIT-127](LIT-127.md)), Falcon-H1R ([LIT-128](LIT-128.md)) and the tiny-DPO
write-up ([LIT-129](LIT-129.md)), and Olmo 3 ([LIT-130](LIT-130.md)) for the three-stage reasoning recipe
it departs from ([SOTA-129](../practices.d/SOTA-129.md)).
