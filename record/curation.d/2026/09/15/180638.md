**The first pass filed the evidence and then argued it away.** `SOTA-154` had
a promotion condition: an independent group running evolution strategies
against a policy-gradient baseline that got the same tuning budget, on a task
outside Countdown and the conciseness objective. `LIT-tmphm6g2` did exactly
that — six tasks, three model families, matched training FLOPs, and the RL arm
grid-searched over learning rate and batch or group size while ES ran one
fixed configuration. The curation entry filed an hour earlier looked straight
at it and wrote that "a baseline result is run by people who want it to lose…
it is *not* a second group choosing to test the claim".

That reasoning is backwards and it is worth keeping the wrong version
written down, because the error has a shape that will recur. **A baseline is
a replication run by people with an incentive to make it fail.** The
condition's own "what would not move this" clause names the failure mode it
feared — "a comparison in which the RL arm was left at defaults" — and this
comparison is the opposite of that: the RL arm was tuned *harder* than the ES
arm. Everything the condition asked for was present, and the pass invented a
category ("baseline, not replication") that the condition never contained, to
avoid acting on it.

The general form: **a promotion condition is a promise, and re-reading it
strictly enough to avoid keeping it is how a provisional status becomes
permanent.** [DP-006](../design-principles.md#dp-6) says a provisional status with no condition attached is a
refusal wearing a document's clothes; this is the next failure along, where
the condition exists and the bar quietly rises to stay above the evidence.

**Then the opposite problem arrived in the same session.** Following the line
forward turned up Hyper-ES (`LIT-tmp9pcfv`), which says in its own second
sentence that "directly applying ES to billion-parameter LLMs is highly
ineffective". Having just promoted the practice, the tempting move was to
find that dissent unpersuasive. Two facts are worth separating, and the
practice now separates them:

- **Hyper-ES never runs the method it says does not work.** Its ES baseline is
  CMA-ES over LoRA. The case against direct ES is two lemmas about
  near-orthogonal perturbations in high dimensions, plus a figure, plus a
  citation. That is a real argument and it is not a failed reproduction.
- **Every model it tests is at 1.5B or below**, while its abstract says
  "billion-parameter LLMs".

The second is what makes this interesting rather than merely awkward.
`THEORY-tmp38myz`, filed the same day from a different paper, holds that the
density of task-improving perturbations rises with model scale, with gains
appearing sharply from about 1.5B and absent beneath it. **Hyper-ES's negative
result sits exactly where that account predicts a negative result**, and its
lemma is the needle-in-a-haystack regime under another name. The two papers
were posted five months apart and neither cites the other.

That reconciliation is labelled, in both documents, as *this record's reading
and not either paper's*. It is the kind of claim a record like this should be
able to make and the kind it can most easily make badly: it resolves a
tension, it is unfalsified, and nobody involved would have to agree with it.
What would settle it is the experiment neither ran — full-parameter ES
against a well-tuned GRPO at 7B and above — and that is what the practice now
says.

**On `introduced_by:` and where a recommendation comes from.** `SOTA-tmpm80i3`
was filed with `introduced_by:` pointing at its own source, which was
tidy and wrong. The source paper says in plain words that it is not making
the recommendation. Qiu et al. are; RandOpt is their instruction with the
iteration count set to one and an ensemble on the end. This is the case
[ADR-030](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-030.md) exists for, arriving about four hours after the field became
required, and the first pass still filled it in by reflex — which is a small
piece of evidence that a required field with a sensible default answer gets
the default answer.

**What this pass did not cover, stated so nobody has to guess.** The search
for the ES line returned five papers and this pass filed two, the two named
in the request. The three left out, in case they are wanted:

- `ARXIV-2609.10980` — *EGGROLL, Unrolled*, which introduces LOO-ROLL, a
  leave-one-out estimator replacing EGGROLL's two antithetic evaluations per
  direction with one.
- `ARXIV-2608.27351` — *Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning*,
  which reports ES ahead on pass@k and GRPO ahead on pass@1 and proposes
  running them in sequence. This one bears directly on `SOTA-154`'s
  `contested` reading and would likely sharpen it.
- `ARXIV-2608.12679` — *Beyond the Best Guess*, on ES and solution coverage.

And the reading depth, since the record's convention is that a `LIT` with no
`NOTE` is an unread paper: EGGROLL and Hyper-ES were read through their
abstracts, introductions, related work, results and hyperparameter
appendices, and **not** through their theory sections — EGGROLL's convergence
analysis and Hyper-ES's two lemmas are taken at their stated results. Neither
carries a reading note, and neither is in any practice's `source:`, which is
the constraint that makes that acceptable.
