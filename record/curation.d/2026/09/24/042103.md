Unit 2 of `#326`. The finding generalises past the paper, so it gets its own
entry.

## The shape

`SOTA-358` says to drop a domain inductive bias once pre-training data is
large enough and keep it when it is not, and it is careful — `Active`,
`converged`, with a consensus note explaining that the field adopted the
conclusion and dropped the condition. Its first measurement is that ViT-Large
is worse than ViT-Base on ImageNet-1k *"despite tuned regularisation"*.

Beyer et al. measured what that ImageNet-1k configuration was worth:
**66.8% top-1, against 80.0% from five changes, none of them a regulariser**.
And the original recipe goes 66.8 → 67.2 → 67.1 across 90, 150 and 300 epochs
— it had stopped learning before any of those comparisons were drawn.

So one side of the crossover was measured with a configuration that was 13
points from reachable, and the phrase carrying the argument named the wrong
lever.

## The general form

**A crossover measured with an under-tuned configuration on one side is
evidence about the tuning as much as about the crossover, and which one it is
cannot be told from the crossover alone.**

That is worth stating because a crossover *looks* like strong evidence. Two
curves meeting is a qualitative fact, robust to small errors, and reads as
more trustworthy than either curve's absolute level. The trap is that the
crossing point is exactly where a constant offset on one curve matters most:
shifting one side by 13 points moves the intersection anywhere, and nothing in
the shape of the plot indicates that it would.

## What was NOT concluded, and why that is the discipline

The temptation here was to mark `SOTA-358` `contested`. It would have been
wrong.

Beyer et al. **ran ViT-S/16 only.** They did not retest ViT-Large against
ViT-Base under their recipe, so whether the ordering flips is unknown. The
honest operation is not to weaken the practice's status but to move its weight
onto the measurement that can hold it — `SOTA-358`'s *second* bullet, the
random JFT subsets at 9M / 30M / 90M / 300M with hyperparameters and
regularisation held fixed. That comparison never depended on anyone tuning
ImageNet-1k well, because it is ViT against ResNet inside one protocol across
data scales.

**A practice can lose a measurement without losing its conclusion**, and the
record should be able to record that. Doing it by editing the evidence rather
than the status is what keeps `consensus` meaning something: nothing about the
field's agreement changed here, only which of the source's own numbers is
load-bearing.

## Why the gap existed for two years

ViT says so itself: *"The addition of results when pre-training only on
ImageNet-1k was an afterthought, mostly to ablate the effect of data scale."*

An afterthought experiment, reported honestly, becomes a citable number the
moment it is in a table — and then it is quoted by people who need an
ImageNet-1k ViT baseline rather than by people ablating data scale. **The
authors' own framing of an experiment does not travel with the number.**

This is the reading that made the paper worth promoting out of `#290`'s
`D-variant` pile. Filed as "SimpleViT", a variant, it looked like one more
architecture tweak. It changes no architecture. What it does is re-measure the
incumbent's least-defended setting, which is the least glamorous kind of
contribution and the kind a record of practice most needs.
