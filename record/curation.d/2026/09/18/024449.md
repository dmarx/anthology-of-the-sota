Three more of `#137` filed: BBH (`ARXIV-2210.09261`), OlympiadBench
(`ARXIV-2402.14008`) and HybridFlow/verl (`ARXIV-2409.19256`). Two findings
worth keeping, both about how the worklist was built rather than about the
papers.

## The issue argues from a benchmark its tables do not list

`#137`'s "What a note looks like for these" section makes its case with
this sentence: *"`SOTA-210` (report pass@k as well as pass@1) rests on results
measured on GSM8K, MATH-500 and OlympiadBench, and there is currently nowhere
to write down what that bounds."* GSM8K and MATH are in its tables.
OlympiadBench is not — not in the three-or-more tier, not in the twice tier,
not in the once list. The same is true of BBH, which seven documents here
report numbers on and which carries the load in `SOTA-235`'s central
argument.

This is the third time the same shape has turned up on this issue, and
`DP-004` is the name for it: the worklist is a sweep of **twelve
evolution-strategies papers' bibliographies**, so it sees what those twelve
cite and nothing else. A benchmark the *record's own prose* reports numbers
on, in documents that came from elsewhere, is invisible to that query no
matter how load-bearing it is. Grepping the record for benchmark names finds
a different population than grepping twelve bibliographies, and the issue's
tables are the second population presented as if it were the first.

The remainder of that population, counted over record documents and
excluding the changelog and this journal: LAMBADA 6, MMLU-Pro 5, WinoGrande 5,
Minerva 4, PIQA 4, SciQ 2, DROP 2. Only ROCStories, at 2, is on `#137`'s list
at all.

## A mention count nearly struck the right paper

HybridFlow was the one entry on the list I was ready to propose striking:
grepping the record for `HybridFlow|\bverl\b` returns two files, and one of
them is `ADR-032` — the decision *discussing the worklist*, not a document
arguing on verl. One genuine citation, in a parenthetical in `LIT-168`.

That test was measuring the wrong thing. `ADR-032` puts
`ARXIV-2409.19256` in its own consequences as one of the four references at
the top of the queue, cited by three of the twelve swept papers. Mention
count in this record's prose and citation count in the swept bibliographies
are two different populations again — the same `DP-004` shape, one level
down, and this time it would have removed something rather than merely
missed it. The standing directive to carry when in doubt is what stopped it,
and the filing criterion `#137` states (*something in the record has to cite
it*) is satisfied by `LIT-168` alone.

## Two of the issue's three open questions now have a worked answer

`#137` asks whether a benchmark note gets a `Standing in the anthology`
section or whether "cited by X" is the whole standing. All three notes here
take the first option, and what fills the section is neither influence nor a
count: it is **the bound the citing document assumes and does not state**.
BBH's is that a BBH number is prompt-conditioned, so only matched-protocol
comparisons mean anything — which is why `SOTA-235`'s argument survives, both
its conditions being 10-shot. OlympiadBench's is the floor effect: the best
model at publication scored 17.97%, and every one of the record's six uses is
at 32B or below.

It also asks how benchmark notes interact with the contamination practice.
OlympiadBench answers that one by example — olympiad problems and their worked
solutions long predate every model evaluated on them, so the note says
contamination is live here in `SOTA-197`'s sense and that no document in the
record reports a probe. That is the natural home for it, and it costs one
paragraph.

## The trap in the BBH note

BBH is not BIG-bench. `LIT-077` is the 204-task suite and the source for
`SOTA-200` and `SOTA-197`; BBH is the 23-task subset selected by where the
best model in that paper *failed*. A grep for `BBH\|BIG-Bench` returns both
and they are not interchangeable — exactly the ARC-AGI versus AI2 ARC
collision `LIT-405` and `LIT-407` were filed to settle a day earlier, and the
second instance of it in a week. A benchmark whose name is a substring of
another benchmark's name is worth checking twice.
