Unit C of `#304`: BYOL, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, VICReg. The unit the audit
ranked third and the one that turned out to be the most `THEORY`-shaped,
which is why it produced a `Proposed` document rather than another set of
confident practices.

## The contradiction, stated plainly

A joint-embedding loss with no negatives has an exact trivial minimum. Four
methods avoid it and reach **74.3 / 67.7 / 73.2 / 73.2** on ImageNet linear
evaluation — within a couple of points of each other — and each explains its
success differently and incompatibly:

- **BYOL**: there is no loss jointly minimised over both parameter sets, so
  there is no reason to reach the trivial minimum. "Similar to GANs."
  Essential: predictor **and** EMA target.
- **SimSiam**: an EM-like alternating optimisation, "analogous to k-means".
  Essential: the stop-gradient. It argues the momentum encoder's apparent
  importance was a confound, because a momentum encoder "is always
  accompanied with stop-gradient".
- **Barlow Twins**: the off-diagonal cross-correlation penalty makes constant
  outputs unavailable. Essential: neither asymmetry nor negatives.
- **VICReg**: a hinge on per-dimension standard deviation forbids collapse
  arithmetically. Essential: none of the above, and it lists six mechanisms
  it does without.

And one disagreement is not interpretive at all. **BYOL reports 0.3%
accuracy when its momentum encoder is removed. SimSiam is that removal and
reports 67.7%.** Two careful groups, nominally the same ablation, three
orders of magnitude apart, unresolved in either paper.

## Why the record should not pick a side, and what it filed instead

The tempting move was to read the four and declare a winner — SimSiam's
account is the most parsimonious, Barlow Twins and VICReg look like the
decisive counterexamples to asymmetry being fundamental. That would have
been a judgement dressed as a finding.

**The reason to refuse is structural, and it is the generalisable part.**
Every piece of evidence here is a paper removing components of *its own*
method under *its own* recipe — its own learning rate, weight decay,
projector width, augmentation set. BYOL's own aside, that removing weight
decay makes both BYOL and SimCLR diverge, is the tell: a great deal of these
recipes is load-bearing for reasons unrelated to collapse. A
0.3%-versus-67.7% gap on the same nominal ablation is exactly what a
recipe-level confound looks like, and no amount of reading the papers against
each other dissolves it.

So `THEORY-tmpf89jm` is `Proposed`, and its `promote_when` names the
experiment none of the four ran: **one codebase, one recipe, the candidate
mechanisms varied independently.** It also says what would *not* settle it —
a fifth account that fits its own method — because that is the kind of paper
this literature reliably produces.

The practice it explains, `SOTA-tmp1kmsu`, is `Active`. A converged recipe
with a contested explanation is the pairing `ADR-031` exists to allow, and
this is the cleanest instance the record has.

## The metric endorsed the failure, again

Third time in three units. `SOTA-tmpw2bcy` (unit B): the batch-norm leak
makes training loss fall *faster*. `SOTA-tmp6nbsn` (unit B): the model
"almost perfectly identifies the positive pairs" while the representation is
poor. Now `SOTA-tmp3wrpo` (unit C): removing the stop-gradient sends the loss
to **the minimum value the objective can take**, immediately, and that is
collapse.

Stated as a rule: **in self-supervised pretraining, the objective is a proxy,
and a proxy hitting its floor is the failure mode rather than the success
condition.** Every one of these three was found by measuring something the
objective does not optimise — a kNN probe, a transformation-recovery probe,
an embedding standard deviation.

`SOTA-tmp3wrpo` is the cheapest of the three and the one worth internalising:
the per-channel standard deviation of the ℓ2-normalised embedding is **0**
when collapsed and **1/√d** when scattered on the sphere, and `1/√d` follows
from the embedding width alone — so it is one of the rare diagnostics with
an absolute scale, needing no baseline run to interpret.

## A consensus column left empty on purpose

`SOTA-tmp3wrpo` is filed `unassessed`. The derivation is arithmetic, the
check is three lines, and it is plainly good advice — but this reading gives
no grounds about whether practitioners actually run it, and `luria.yaml`'s
own warning is that "a value invented to fill the column is worse than an
admission". Recorded here because the temptation to write `emerging` on the
strength of the advice being *good* rather than *adopted* was real, and that
is precisely the confusion the column is designed to prevent.
