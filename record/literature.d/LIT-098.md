# LIT-098: PipeMare: Asynchronous Pipeline Parallel DNN Training

Yang et al. (2019) — [ARXIV-1910.05124](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05124)

## Key takeaways

- Runs pipeline stages **asynchronously on stale weights** rather than paying
  GPipe's bubble or PipeDream's per-microbatch weight stash — staleness is
  corrected statistically instead of eliminated
- Two techniques: **T1, learning-rate rescheduling** (scale the step by the
  stage's known delay) and **T2, discrepancy correction** (approximate the
  weights the backward pass should have seen). The delay is *deterministic* —
  a function of stage index — which is what makes a correction definable
- **4.6× the pipeline utilization of GPipe** and **up to 2× less weight and
  optimizer memory than PipeDream**, at a quality PipeDream does not reach;
  largest gap to synchronous training is **0.1% top-1 on ImageNet**
- **T1 alone gives 7.6× utilization over GPipe** at 95.0% accuracy / 34.1
  BLEU (synchronous: 95.0% / 34.5). **T2 looks optional until the pipeline is
  deep** — on a 150-stage ResNet-152 it is required to prevent divergence
- Activation memory scales **quadratically** in stage count while throughput
  scales linearly, so recompute is done across a *segment* of stages rather
  than within one

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmpl8316](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl8316.md).

**Nothing is sourced to this paper and the reading files no practice** — the
record's pipeline practices are GPipe-shaped and synchronous, which is what
frontier training runs.

What the reading changes is how `SOTA-017` should be read. The record presents
the bubble as a cost to be *minimised*, with `M ≥ 4K` as the answer. This paper
is the standing demonstration that it can instead be **declined**, and that the
price of declining it is two corrections — one of which only starts to matter
once the pipeline is deep, which is the ablation's most instructive cell. No
practice changes; the neighbourhood gains a second option.
