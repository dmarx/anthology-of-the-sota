# LIT-109: NeuS2: Fast Learning of Neural Implicit Surfaces for Multi-view Reconstruction

Wang et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2212.05231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05231)

## Key takeaways

- Cuts NeuS surface reconstruction from **about 8 hours** per static object to
  **minutes**, and to **as little as 20 seconds per frame** for a moving-object
  sequence, at comparable quality
- **The speedup is an enabling condition, not an optimisation**, and the paper
  says so: 8 hours per object "makes it almost impossible to apply them to
  dynamic scenes with thousands of frames". A thousand frames at 8 hours each is
  not slow, it is impossible; at 20 seconds it is an afternoon
- Per-frame reconstruction of a sequence warm-starts from the previous frame,
  which is where the 20-seconds figure comes from and what it assumes

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-046](../notes.d/NOTE-046.md).

A source of [SOTA-205](../practices.d/SOTA-205.md) — the fourth paper in the convergence, and the
one that carries it out of radiance fields into implicit surfaces. It inherits
the hash-encoding line [LIT-064](LIT-064.md) established, which is what makes it
corroboration rather than a fifth independent idea.

Worth recording because the record's own efficiency practices — `SOTA-086`,
`SOTA-019`, and the systems work in `LIT-102` — are all stated as **ratios**, and
a ratio does not say whether a result crossed a threshold that changes what can
be attempted. A 2× speedup and a 1000× speedup are qualitatively different
claims, and the record has no way of saying so.
