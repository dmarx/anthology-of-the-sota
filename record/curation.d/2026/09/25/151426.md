The previous unit amended `SOTA-373` to say, in as many words, that its text
anchor was "an unexamined default rather than a rival datum", and named the paper
that would settle it. This unit took that paper. The answer changed the
explanation and left the recommendation alone, which is what the practice/theory
split was built for and the first time this record has watched it work.

## What was open, and what closed it

`SOTA-373`: set the masking ratio by the signal's redundancy, not by the ratio
that worked on text. `THEORY-088` explains it: the optimum tracks the signal's
information density. The contrast carrying both was MAE's 75% of image patches
against BERT's 15% of tokens.

Wettig et al. swept the text side. Within English text, one architecture family,
one protocol:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 354M parameters | **40%** optimal |
| 124M | 20% |
| 51M | 15% |

A factor of nearly three, with the signal identical. And the strategy matters
too: uniform masking is an easier task than span or PMI masking at a given rate,
so uniform admits a higher optimum. Neither capacity nor masking strategy is a
property of the text.

Then the result that does the real damage. At an **80%** masking rate, text
validation perplexity is above **1,000** — reconstruction is hopeless — and the
model preserves **95% of fine-tuning performance** and **90% of BLiMP probing
accuracy** against the 15% baseline. Redundancy is exactly what governs whether
a hidden region can be recovered. Recovery turns out not to govern representation
quality. The mechanism and the thing it was invoked to explain have come apart.

## The split, tested

`THEORY-088`'s own Standing section, written 2026-09-23, two days before any of
this:

> a wrong theory here would leave the recommendation untouched, since "measure
> whether an interpolator can solve your masked task" is an empirical procedure
> that needs no account of density at all.

That was a prediction about the record's own structure, and it held. `SOTA-373`
is on its third version in two days and its recommendation has not moved once:
v2 removed a false claim about the record's contents and demoted the 15% from
result to convention; v3 supplied the measured text optimum. Meanwhile
`THEORY-088` lost a term and had its promotion condition rewritten, because a
cross-modality correlation drawn from models of different sizes measures capacity
as much as redundancy.

This is `ADR-031` earning its keep. Under the old single-scheme schema these
would have been one document, and the only available move would have been to
weaken the advice — when the advice is *more* justified now than it was, because
the number it says not to inherit has turned out to be wrong in its own modality
as well.

**The generalisable version:** an explanation and a recommendation fail
independently, and a record that files them together will propagate the wrong
one. Which is the argument for `ADR-031` stated as a consequence rather than as a
principle, and this is the first case in the journal where the two verdicts
actually diverged.

## Three reversals, and the third is the useful one

`SOTA-404` — filed this morning on two sources, now on three — says that
ablating a component without ablating its scaffolding measures the pair. The two
original cases were defects: BERT removed the NSP loss and kept its input format;
four grokking papers varied a training fraction that moved size and composition
together.

Wettig et al. is different in kind. They do not trip over the confound; they name
it and build the decomposition. A masking rate is a **corruption rate** and a
**prediction rate**, tied by convention, and untying them shows each direction
separately.

> when we tune the masking rate, we are tuning the corruption rate and the
> prediction rate together, which have antagonistic effects.

**Antagonistic** is the word to keep, and it extends the practice rather than
merely confirming it. In the first two cases the wired-together quantities pushed
the same way, so the confound hid an *attribution* — the effect was real and
credited to the wrong component. Here they push opposite ways, so the confound
can hide an *effect*: a rate sweep that finds no shift may be watching two real
effects cancel. A null result under a fused knob is not evidence of no effect.

The status stays `Proposed`. Its `promote_when` asks for the case where an
original conclusion **survives** separation, and three reversals is three
reversals.

## 80-10-10, and what it cost to leave unexamined

BERT replaced 10% of selected positions with the original token and 10% with a
random one, arguing a pretrain/fine-tune mismatch: `[MASK]` never appears
downstream. RoBERTa copied it. So did SpanBERT and DeBERTa. Wettig et al.
measured it against plain `[MASK]` at a matched rate and found it **worse on
everything but SST-2**, with the motivation not materialising — "the model can
adapt to full, uncorrupted sentences, regardless of the use of alternative
corruption strategies in pre-training."

The part worth recording is that the rule's own evidence never supported it.
BERT's Appendix C.2 puts 80/10/10 at **84.2** on MNLI and 100/0/0 at **84.3** —
the condition being dropped was already, very slightly, ahead. The rule spread on
an argument, and the one table that bore on it pointed the other way by an amount
too small to matter in either direction.

Filed as `SOTA-tmpz04ck`, and its `promote_when` explicitly refuses to count
another model shipping the rule or dropping it, because shipping without a
comparison is how it spread.

## A live field and a dated one

`LIT-668`, filed this morning, contained: *"Neither `1810.04805` nor
`1907.11692` — BERT and RoBERTa — is in this record, in any scheme."* Both were
filed hours later.

The paragraph is updated, and the changelog fragment and curation entry from the
same unit that say the same thing are **not**. That distinction is worth stating
because it will recur: a reading note's *Standing in the anthology* is a live
claim about the record as it is now, and a changelog fragment or a journal entry
is a dated claim about a moment. Rewriting the second kind would destroy the
thing that makes the journal worth keeping — that it says what was believed when.
Leaving the first kind stale means a reader is told something false about the
record they are holding.

The practical rule: **when a unit closes a gap a previous unit measured, fix the
standing sections and leave the history.**
