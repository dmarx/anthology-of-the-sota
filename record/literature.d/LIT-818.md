# LIT-818: Sign and Basis Invariant Networks for Spectral Graph Representation Learning

Lim, Robinson, Zhao, Smidt, Sra, Maron, Jegelka, MIT, CMU and NVIDIA
(ICLR 2023) — [ARXIV-2202.13013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13013)

## Key takeaways

- **The symmetries:** for k eigenvectors there are 2^k sign choices, and an
  eigenspace of dimension d admits any rotation in O(d). 64.1% of ZINC graphs
  have an eigenspace of dimension above 1, as do 99.9% of IMDB-M graphs
  (Table 5)
- **SignNet:** `ρ([φ(v_i) + φ(−v_i)]_i)`, with φ a GIN applied to each
  eigenvector and ρ an MLP, or a sum then an MLP for a variable count.
  **BasisNet:** `ρ([IGN(V_i V_iᵀ)]_i)` per eigenspace. The unconstrained
  versions are universal for the invariant functions. The
  permutation-equivariant SignNet and BasisNet used in practice are not
  (Table 4)
- **What they can express** (Theorems 2–3, Proposition 4): SignNet
  approximates every spectral graph convolution, and both approximate the
  heat-kernel and random-walk encodings, RWPE included. BasisNet computes
  graph angles, which determine 3-, 4- and 5-cycle counts. The angles are
  still no stronger than 3-WL
- **ZINC** (Table 1, 500k budget, 4 seeds): GatedGCN no PE 0.252, LapPE
  with sign flip 0.198, SignNet without the sign-invariant sum (`φ(v)`
  only) 0.148, SignNet 0.121 (8 eigenvectors each), SignNet on all
  eigenvectors 0.100. For GINE and PNA, LapPE with flips does no better than
  no PE (0.178 vs 0.170, 0.132 vs 0.133) and SignNet does (0.147, 0.105).
  Best: PNA with SignNet on all eigenvectors, 0.084. Canonicalizing the sign
  scores 0.298, worse than no PE
- **Without edge features** (Table 7): GIN 0.348 no PE, 0.341 LapPE, 0.238
  SignNet. GAT 0.464, 0.462, 0.243
- **Elsewhere:** SignNet beats LapPE with flips on substructure counting
  and graph-property regression with a GIN (Figure 3), and improves the
  intrinsic neural field's texture reconstruction LPIPS from 0.189 to 0.147
  on the cat mesh (Table 3)

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from the graph-coverage pass** (gap list from the record's own
review, 2026-10-22). It sources [THEORY-133](../theory.d/THEORY-133.md), through the Table 1
ablation that separates the sign symmetry from the rest of the encoder, and
is the second source of [SOTA-452](../practices.d/SOTA-452.md). It is `compared_against` GIN
([LIT-580](LIT-580.md)), whose edge-feature variant GINE is one of the four base models it
reruns with and without encodings. It is also `compared_against` LSPE
([LIT-833](LIT-833.md)), whose best ZINC result (0.090) it quotes against its own
0.084. That comparison uses the published number, not a rerun.

**The title promises two networks and the graph experiments test one.**
BasisNet appears only in Appendix J.3, fitting synthetic spectral filters
on a 32×32 grid, where training error is the metric and there is no test
set. Basis invariance is argued to matter (64% of ZINC graphs need it) and
is never measured on a real graph task. Every ZINC number is sign-invariant
only.

**The "All eigenvectors" rows change two things at once.** They use a
different ρ (sum over eigenvectors) and more eigenvectors than the LapPE
baseline, which stays at 8 or 16. The like-for-like comparison is at fixed
k: 0.198 vs 0.121 for GatedGCN.

**A parameter count to check.** Appendix K.2 gives the input eigenvector
model 907,371 parameters, against the 500k budget Table 1 states, and does
not say which configuration it describes.
