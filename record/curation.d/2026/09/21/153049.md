`ADR-052` added a topic that named nothing, and wrote down what would show it
had been a mistake:

> If the first filing into `deployment-and-society` produces a literature note
> and nothing actionable — no practice, no theory — then the topic is a shelf
> rather than a category.

This is that filing. It produced both, and the practice is the more useful of
the two.

## Why an audit of Twitter/X belongs in a record about ML practice

Not for the finding. That is one platform, six weeks, one election, and it
describes a ranking system its owner was actively changing during the
measurement window.

For the methods section, which is a sequence of stated trades about measuring
a system you cannot inspect, whose operator will not help you, and whose
research API may be withdrawn while you work. That is not a niche problem and
it is getting less niche.

The decisions generalise cleanly:

- **Do not let the puppets interact.** Against the practice in radicalization
  studies, and argued rather than asserted: feedback loops make the system's
  prior inseparable from what your own clicks taught it; different arms would
  engage differently, so comparison stops being clean; and the question is
  what the system does before a user teaches it anything.
- **Curate the follow sets to the question, not to realism.** Also against
  prior practice, and for the obvious reason once stated — real users follow
  diverse things unrelated to what you are measuring, and each one is a
  confound.
- **Weight by rank.** A raw count of appearances is not exposure because
  nobody reads to the bottom.
- **Baseline against an arm of your own experiment**, so "amplified" means
  relative to a timeline the same algorithm produced.

Each of those is a decision a practitioner would face again, each is reasoned
in the source, and each has a cost the source names.

## The control arm carried the result

Four arms: following nobody, left, right, balanced. The no-follow arm exists
to show what the algorithm does with no signal — a control, in the sense of a
condition you report to show the others are doing something.

It came out **most diverse of the four.** Right-leaning most concentrated,
then balanced, then left, then the arm with no personalization at all, and
every pairwise difference significant at p < 0.001.

So the thing that was supposed to anchor the comparison is the comparison.
Personalization, on this measurement, narrows. The baseline it narrows from is
the untargeted feed, and the intuition that a recommender broadens what you
would otherwise have found runs the wrong way.

And the dose is tiny: ten media follows, seven of them moderate, four
political accounts, no clicks, no history — enough for aligned voices to run
more than 50% above baseline.

That is why the practice says to include a no-follow arm and says why. It is
not the null condition; it is the measurement.

## What I kept out of the account, and why it took as long as what went in

Three things the theory document explicitly declines:

**Causation.** The arms differ in *what they follow*, which changes both the
personalization signal and the candidate pool. Separating those needs an arm
that follows many accounts chosen to be uninformative, and that arm was not
run. The ordering is real; "personalization causes it" is one reading of it.

**Harm.** A concentrated feed of things a reader wants is a working
recommender by most operators' definition. The record files direction and
magnitude and stops.

**The partisan asymmetries.** That default timelines skew right — 30.16%
against 12.92% of exposure in the top 20 for the no-follow arm — is the
finding the paper's title is about and the one most likely to be quoted. It
rests on political leanings assigned by hand from a media bias chart and
profile text, which the authors say may be inaccurate or stale. It is a
separate claim with a separate evidential basis, and the account does not
depend on it.

Keeping those three out is most of what makes the account filable at all. A
version that asserted all three would be a stronger-sounding document resting
on a hand-labelling step the authors themselves flagged.

## One number that should not travel unqualified

The exposure metric decays attention exponentially with rank, calibrated so
the top 20% of a timeline carries about 70% of it. That figure comes from
**TikTok and YouTube** studies, transplanted to Twitter/X.

Every exposure number in the paper inherits it, and there is no sensitivity
sweep over the decay parameters. That is `DP-010`'s shape once more — not an
unsupported claim, but a load-bearing assumption sitting quietly one level
below the numbers that get quoted. The practice tells anyone reusing the
design to run the sweep the source did not.
