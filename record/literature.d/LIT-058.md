# LIT-058: Measuring the Effects of Data Parallelism on Neural Network Training

Shallue et al. (2018) — [ARXIV-1811.03600](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03600)

## Key takeaways

- **168,160 trained models**, yielding 71,638,836 loss measurements, over **454 (workload, batch size) pairs**, all aimed at one relationship: how the number of steps to a target accuracy varies with batch size. The 71 million figure is measurements, not runs — it is the published database of training curves, and it is the number most often misquoted as the run count.
- A **workload** here is a (model, data set, training algorithm) triple, and there are **35** of them: six model families (fully connected, a simple CNN, ResNet, VGG, Transformer, LSTM), seven data sets (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, Open Images, LM1B, Common Crawl) and three optimizers (SGD, momentum, Nesterov momentum). All three optimizers carry a single global step size — no layerwise or curvature-normalized method is in the sweep, which is what [SOTA-221](../practices.d/SOTA-221.md) turns on.
- The shape is consistent even when its position is not. There is an initial regime of near-perfect scaling — double the batch, halve the steps — then a region of diminishing returns, then a plateau past which more parallelism buys nothing. Perfect scaling ends anywhere from a batch of 2^4 to 2^13; the plateau begins between roughly 2^9 and 2^16. Two ranges, two transitions, routinely merged into one.
- Where the transitions fall depends on the model, dataset and optimizer, and the paper finds no simple rule that predicts it. That negative result is the practical content: the maximum useful batch size has to be measured for a workload rather than inherited.
- Careful metaparameter tuning at every batch size is what makes the comparison mean anything; several apparent batch-size effects in prior work are artefacts of tuning the schedule for one batch size and reusing it.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-218](../practices.d/SOTA-218.md) — retune at every batch size you compare — and a
corroborating source for [SOTA-221](../practices.d/SOTA-221.md), which is the other half of the same
subject. [THEORY-012](../theory.d/THEORY-012.md) reads this paper together with [LIT-265](LIT-265.md) and argues that
its negative result about heuristics is really a result about optimizers, on the
strength of the one comparative finding in it: momentum extends perfect scaling
past plain SGD.

The paper also asks, in its future work, for a prospective predictor of the
scaling behaviour that would spare the sweep. [SOTA-198](../practices.d/SOTA-198.md) is that, from a paper
published two months later that does not cite this one.

The practice a future contributor is most likely to write here — "pick the
largest batch that still scales" — is not filed, and this is the evidence it
would need.
