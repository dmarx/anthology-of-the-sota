The request was to add the phrase-as-lemma paper. Goldberg and Shirtz (2025),
*Language* 101(2) — English syntax, four preregistered surveys, 755
participants, a billion-word corpus, and **not one sentence about a machine**.
Filing it was five minutes. Working out whether it belonged took the rest, and
the useful finding is that the question had no answer here.

## What I expected to find, and did not

I expected a scope rule to consult, and specifically I expected the record to
be a CS-literature collection with a stated edge. It is not either of those.

**Twenty-seven notes carry no arXiv identifier**, and the venues include
*Physical Review Letters*, *Reviews of Modern Physics*, four volumes of *IEEE
Transactions on Automatic Control*, *SIAM*, and a 1963 *Proceedings of the
AMS*. "Outside computer science" was never the boundary; the record crossed it
repeatedly and nobody argued, because each of those is machinery that ML work
runs on.

So the question is not the field. It is what those papers have that this one
does not, and [ADR-032](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-032.md) already named it: **something in the record cites
them.** Nothing here cites Goldberg and Shirtz. On the letter of the only
decision that speaks to admission, this paper is out.

## Why declining it would have been the wrong kind of easy

[DP-009](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-9) is about exactly this asymmetry and it is worth quoting, because
the principle predicted the shape of the temptation before I met it:

> The first is the cheaper reading, it requires no work, and it is
> *self-confirming*: declining the document leaves the vocabulary looking
> complete, because the evidence that would have shown otherwise has just been
> turned away.

That is precisely what declining would have bought. The record would have gone
on looking like it had a scope, when what it has is a list of things nobody
has yet had a reason to file.

And the principle demands the right kind of argument for declining: "an
argument about the *kind of claim* — not a shrug at the absence of a slot." A
real one is available — the anthology advises on machines, and human
interpretation is not a machine — and it is narrower than it first looks,
which is the whole substance of the pass.

## The thing that made it close

`representation-and-encoding`'s blurb is "how the signal is encoded before the
expensive network sees it — tokenizers and learned latents". **Every practice
under that topic presupposes there is a defensible answer to where a unit of
meaning ends.** None of them measures it; the question is upstream of all of
them and simply assumed.

This paper measures a case where English's answer is "a whole sentence, used
as one word", and where speakers demonstrably act on the word-slot — 77%,
82%, 85% against normed near-paraphrases, on three independent judgements.

The result that makes it more than an anecdote is **Study 4**. The obvious
deflation is that PALs are memorized strings, so the authors re-ran the whole
thing on high-frequency phrases only — `anything goes`, `do it yourself`,
`wait and see` — and every effect reproduced at roughly the same size, with no
frequency dependence anywhere in the main analyses either. **The effect is
carried by the construction, not by the list.** That is what makes it a claim
about structure, and it is the reason the paper is evidence about tokenization
even though it never mentions it.

## The mistake, which is the useful part of this entry

I filed it first as a note that sources nothing, admitted on a special rule
about measuring what a topic's practices presuppose. That framing lasted until
somebody said *I'd argue this is a theory paper*, and it was right.

The record's own test is one sentence in `CLAUDE.md`: **if it tells the reader
what to do it is a practice, and if it says what is true it is a theory.** It
is a test about the kind of claim and it mentions no apparatus. What made the
paper look hard was that the measurement happened on people — and that is a
fact about the instrument, not about the claim. The claim is that a lexical
unit can be an arbitrary-length phrase, delimited by construction rather than
by frequency. **That is a fact about language, and language is the signal.**

[ADR-026](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-026.md) had already drawn this distinction on the neighbouring axis
and I had read it that morning: a preconditioning scheme discovered in
diffusion takes its *kind*, not its domain. Domain of discovery does not
determine filing there. Apparatus of measurement does not determine it here
either, and the two are the same argument.

The tell I missed is that the exception was load-bearing. I was building a
narrow special rule to admit a well-evidenced finding about the record's own
subject matter — and **a scheme that needs an exception for that has
mis-stated its subject matter**, not found an edge case. The rewritten
`ADR-045` is the general rule instead, and it keeps the exception
framing in its alternatives because it looked right for about an hour.

## What I did, and what I declined to do

`ADR-045` proposes two conditions, and the second is the one doing the
work: the document must **state what it does not license** — and for work like
this, that has to include *this was not measured on a model*. The
failure mode is not the filing. It is a later pass finding an unexplained note
in the reading list and reading a recommendation out of it — which is
`LIT-052` and `LIT-025` again, the two notes whose plausible-looking takeaways
turned out not to be about their papers and which eight practices were built
on. The `note-statuses` vocabulary exists because of those two.

**I did not source a practice from it**, and the temptation was real, because
a practice would make the filing self-justifying. Any practice here would have
been this record's inference wearing Goldberg's citation. [ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md) is the
rule against exactly that, and the honest form is a note that says the
recommendation does not exist yet.

**I did not add a topic for it.** [DP-008](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-8)'s corollary is explicit that a
category added to admit a single document is how a scope stops being one. So
the paper sits under a tag that is the closest available and is not a good
fit, and the ADR says so in its consequences rather than resolving it. One
document is not enough to move a vocabulary.

**The decision is `Proposed` and names what would settle it**: a second
finding about the signal, measured outside the ML literature and filed under
these conditions — or a case where the first condition does real work by
excluding something. If neither arrives, this is a single exception and should
be relabelled as one rather than left standing as a rule.

**And what the theory document declines is the part I expect to be revisited.**
`explains:` is empty. The obvious next claim is that a tokenizer splitting a
construction forces the network to reassemble it, and that this is early-layer
work — the detokenization and inner-lexicon line, `ARXIV-2410.05864` and
`ARXIV-2406.20086`, which reports models fusing multi-token words into single
representations in exactly that position. **The record holds none of it.**
Until it does, the bridge from this finding to anything a model does is a
conjecture, and the theory says so in its own body rather than in a comment
somebody has to find.

## The part that generalizes

Two passes in two days, and both found the same thing from opposite
directions. Dropout was missing because nothing had ever prompted anyone to
file what everybody agreed on. This paper was nearly missed because nothing
had ever prompted anyone to say what the collection is *about*. In both cases
the record's silence read as a decision and was not one — and in both cases
the evidence was sitting in the record already, as twelve passing mentions
there and twenty-seven non-arXiv notes here.

The query that finds the second kind is cheap and nobody had run it: **list
the venues.** It takes one `grep` and it says more about scope than any
statement of scope in this repository.

## The bridge, filed the same day, and still not a bridge

The theory above says the unit is construction-delimited in the *data* and
stops there, because the record held nothing about what a network does with a
unit its tokenizer split. It does now: `LIT-417` and its two theories,
added in this same change on the observation that "early-layer behavior" is
where this would show up.

It is a good deal better than a coincidence. Lad et al. delete and swap layers
to find out which ones matter, find the first layer is the fragile one, and
name the stage immediately after it **detokenization** — local context
integrated to turn raw token representations into coherent units. Two
literatures that share no methods, no authors and no venue, arriving at the
same seam from opposite sides: one measuring where the unit boundary is in the
signal, the other measuring where the network puts it back.

**And the record says explicitly that this is not yet one account.** Nothing
measured either against the other. The reassembly half of Lad et al.'s stage 1
is imported from the inner-lexicon line (`ARXIV-2410.05864`,
`ARXIV-2406.20086`) rather than established there — and **that line is still
unfiled**, which is now the most valuable gap this pass has produced. Reading
the pair as "the network repairs what the tokenizer broke" is the
satisfying synthesis, and it is the one nobody has run the experiment for.
Both documents say so in their own bodies rather than in a comment somebody
has to find.

The pattern is worth naming because it is the third instance in two days. A
gap gets filled, the fill reveals the *next* gap, and the next gap is sharper
than the one that was filled. Dropout's filing produced three named papers and
one answerable question; this one produced a two-paper line whose absence is
now the thing standing between the record and a claim it would like to make.

## The gap closed, and what it did to the claim

The entry above named the inner-lexicon line as "the most valuable gap this
pass has produced". It is filed now, and filing it changed the shape of the
thing it was supposed to support.

**The evidence is better than the gap-naming implied.** Kaplan et al. ran the
control that matters: if reassembly were recognition of frequent token
sequences, a probe on the *penultimate* token of a long word — which
co-occurs with the same prefixes just as often — would do as well as one on
the last token. It gets 61% against 89%. And the representations survive
arbitrary splits, typos and words the tokenizer never had, which
memorization would not. `THEORY-021` is `Active` on three groups using
three methods, which is a stronger footing than anything else filed in this
pass.

**And the overlap with the signal side got narrower and more interesting.**
I expected the connection to stay at the level of "both concern
tokenization". It does not: Feucht et al. define a lexical item as something
that functions as a single unit of meaning, note that such items are
*non-compositional*, and give `break a leg` as the example. That is
`THEORY-022`'s subject, arrived at from inside a model, by authors who
have plainly never read Goldberg.

**Which is exactly when to be careful.** A convergence this tidy is the point
at which a record starts asserting the synthesis nobody measured. The
experiment that would connect them is specific and has not been run: are the
units in a model's implicit vocabulary the ones a language delimits by
construction, and what happens to the ones that are not? Both documents say
so in their own bodies. The satisfying sentence — *the network repairs what
the tokenizer broke* — is still not the record's to write.

**What the filing did license was a practice**, which I did not expect.
Kaplan et al. do not stop at the finding: they put the detokenized
representation back into the embedding matrix and the frozen model uses it,
keeping its accuracy on existing tokens where the obvious baseline degrades.
`SOTA-247` is `Proposed` on one group and one model. Its body says the
thing the paper's own framing understates — "finetuning-free" means the core
parameters are frozen while two refinement matrices train on 20M tokens —
because that distinction is the whole cost of the method.

## [LIT-191](../../record/literature.d/LIT-191.md), held for ten days with its explanation missing

The attention-sink paper has been in the record since 2026-09-07, sourcing
`SOTA-134`, and the record had no document for **why** sinks exist. The note's
own standing section had already worked it out in prose: the gate and the
streaming fix are answers to the same pressure from opposite ends, which is
why the gate removes sinks rather than relocating them.

`THEORY-019` states it and `explains: SOTA-134`. Two things that are
worth having written down rather than re-derived:

**The mechanism is an accounting constraint, not a preference.** Softmax
normalizes to one, so a head with nothing to attend to still has mass to
shed, and the only positions every query can see under causal masking are the
first. That is why the sink is positional — replacing the first four tokens
with linebreaks costs 0.2 perplexity — and why evicting it costs 5158.

**Sinks are therefore not a defect**, and the document says so explicitly,
because the record was already recommending their removal. In an ungated
model the sink is load-bearing. It is eliminable only by changing the
architecture so the pressure that creates it is gone, which is what
`SOTA-134` does.

This is `DP-007` once more and by now it should be predictable: the practice
registry recommended removing a phenomenon for ten days without holding the
explanation of what it was for. Nothing prompted the filing, because everyone
involved already knew.

## Following the sink one paper further, and finding a wrong attribution

The entry above closed by noting that `LIT-191` gestures at SoftMax-off-by-One
and the record held no result on it. Chasing that gesture turned up two
documents and a correction to a practice that has been `Active` since
2026-09-05.

**The popular version is not the evidenced version.** Miller's post is the
one everybody has read — it is where the phrase comes from — and it reports
**no experiments at all**. The post says so: *"I'm 99.44% sure that it will
resolve the outlier feedback loop"* is the entirety of the case. It is filed,
because reach and provenance are real things a reading list should record, and
its standing section says plainly that the record is not endorsing softmax1.

**And it is downstream.** Miller (July 2023) cites the Qualcomm work on
outliers at whitespace and punctuation positions. That work is Bondarenko et
al. (June 2023), which had already diagnosed the no-op mechanism and tested
**two** remedies. The chronology runs
diagnosis-and-evidence → popularization → large-scale confirmation, and the
record had only the last of the three.

## The attribution

`SOTA-134` recommends gating each attention head's output with a sigmoid after
the scaled dot-product, and was filed with `introduced_by: LIT-138`, Qiu et
al. (2025). Bondarenko et al. equation 5 is that gate:

    Gated_attention(x) := sigmoid(G(x)) ⊙ softmax(QKᵀ/√d_head) V(x)

with `G` **per head** — "gating modules are shared between different token
positions but not shared across attention heads" — a single linear layer,
under 0.009% of BERT-base. Two years earlier.

I did not have to infer this. Qiu et al. write it down: *"The work most
closely related to ours is Quantizable Transformers (Bondarenko et al.,
2023) ... Building on these insights, we scale up gated attention models."*
The record's note on Qiu et al. did not carry the sentence, so the record
attributed the recommendation to the paper that scaled it.

**This is what `introduced_by:` was separated from `source:` for**, and it is
the first time the distinction has caught something rather than merely been
stated. `ADR-017`'s argument was that with one field every practice reads as
though it began with the paper it cites — and that is exactly what happened
here, to a practice filed after the decision was `Active`.

**The correction relocates the claim, which is the part worth keeping.** The
gate was not invented to improve quality. It was invented to let a head do
nothing so it would stop producing the activation outliers that break INT8
quantization, and the quality gain Qiu et al. measured is a later discovery
about a mechanism introduced for quantizability. That is a more interesting
history than "the Qwen team found a good gate", and the record was telling the
less interesting one.

## The general shape, three passes in

Dropout was missing because agreement has no arrival. The attention sink had
no explanation because everyone involved already knew it. This attribution was
wrong because the paper that scaled an idea is more visible than the paper
that had it. All three are the publication record's shape showing through into
a collection assembled from it, and none was a judgement anyone made.

The cheap query for this one, by analogy with **list the venues**: for every
practice whose `source[0]` is markedly newer than the technique feels, read
the related-work section of that source. It is where a paper says what it did
not invent, and it is the one section nobody reads.
