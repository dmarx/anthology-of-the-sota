[LIT-170](../../record/literature.d/LIT-170.md) left this open with an
argument, not a shrug: whether the anthology should carry MoE routing as
practice "is not obvious… since the choice is upstream of almost everything it
does recommend."

That argument is worth answering rather than dismissing, because it is half
right. Being upstream is exactly why the record should say it — once, plainly
— rather than leave it as the unexamined premise of everything downstream.
[SOTA-146](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-146.md) is what made the cost visible:
it carries a caveat that the RL objective is unstable on mixture-of-experts
specifically, qualifying an architecture the registry had never recommended.
A caveat with nothing behind it.

## What the corpus actually supports

The claim is filed `Active`, `converged`, on four labs and every frontier model
the record holds from 2024 on — DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, NVIDIA. The one
dense frontier recipe in the corpus is Llama 3, from 2024.

The evidence is ratios rather than scores, which is how an efficiency claim
should be read: DeepSeekMoE 16B matching LLaMA2 7B at ~40% of the compute,
145B approaching the same team's dense 67B at 28.5%.

The design half is separate and weaker — `emerging`, not `converged`. Fine
segmentation plus a shared expert is DeepSeek's shape, taken further by Kimi
K3's 16-of-896, and the record does not know whether Qwen or NVIDIA segment
this finely. You can accept sparsity and reject fine segmentation, so the two
are filed apart.

## The part that was wrong, and then was not

[LIT-170](../../record/literature.d/LIT-170.md) did not introduce
mixture-of-experts. It diagnoses "conventional top-K-of-N routing, as in
GShard" and improves on it — and the corpus held no GShard, no Switch
Transformer, no Shazeer 2017. The trunk practice was drafted sourced against
[#17](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/17)'s own rule
that a practice names the paper that introduced the change, naming the paper
that measured it instead.

That gap is closed in the same contribution rather than deferred. All three
are filed, the practice is sourced to Shazeer et al. (2017), and the record
holds the line end to end:

    the sparsely-gated layer      2017   capacity without compute, and the
                                         auxiliary balancing loss
      GShard                      2020   into the transformer, sharded; top-2
        Switch Transformers       2021   top-1, float32 router, a trillion
        DeepSeekMoE               2024   many small plus one shared

Reading it as a line is what makes it worth holding. GShard says route to two
experts, Switch says one, DeepSeekMoE says many small ones and take more of
them — three answers to how finely the router should choose, and the record
recommends the third.

The same span covers load balancing, which is the nicer find. Shazeer's paper
introduces an auxiliary loss for balance because the gate self-reinforces;
[SOTA-148](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-148.md) recommends taking that loss
back out seven years later. The record now holds both ends of one argument
that ran for the whole history of the technique.

## Three documents, one family

[SOTA-148](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-148.md) gains an `extends:` so the
chain page shows what [LIT-171](../../record/literature.d/LIT-171.md)
predicted — "three practices' worth of material with nowhere to sit" — now
sitting somewhere:

    make the layer sparse            (Active, converged)
      keep the experts balanced      (Proposed, unreplicated)
      cut the experts up finely      (Active, emerging)

Three claims a reader can accept or reject independently, which is why they
are three documents.
