Unit E of `#304`. A different shape of substrate defect from the previous
ones, and worth naming as its own kind.

## A fourth way to be missing

The audit's test was "does a held document lean on it". Units A–D turned up
three variants: a paper used as background (ViT, CLIP), a paper used as a
measurement instrument (SwAV, DINOv2), and a *word* the record could not say
(InfoNCE). This one is the fourth:

**a paper the record holds a position against.**

`SOTA-250` says to predict representations of masked regions rather than
their pixels, and names MAE as the competitor it beats on compute —
"more efficient than a ViT-H/14 pretrained with MAE". `LIT-216` is the paper
that ran that comparison. So the record held **the winner of a named
comparison and not the loser**, which is a worse state than holding neither:
a reader could see the verdict and not the defendant.

`compared_against` now runs from `LIT-216`, which is where `ADR-011` puts it
— the paper that ran the comparison carries the edge.

## The move that made the unit work

The tempting framing was MAE-versus-I-JEPA, and it would have been wrong.
`SOTA-250`'s position is supported by the evidence the record holds, and this
unit does not disturb it.

What is worth filing is the part of MAE that is **orthogonal to the thing
being argued about.** The argument is over the *target* — pixels or
representations. Two of MAE's contributions say nothing about the target:

- the encoder runs on visible tokens only, mask tokens go to a disposable
  decoder;
- the masking ratio is derived from the signal's redundancy.

And the check that this is right: **`SOTA-250`'s own architecture has the
same asymmetry** — a context encoder that never sees the masked regions and a
separate predictor. Two objectives that disagree about the target, one
structure. That is the signature of a design that belongs to the computation
rather than to the claim, and it is the test worth reusing: *when two papers
argue, look for what both of them do anyway.*

## A theory that is easy to overrate

`THEORY-tmpgwgsb` files MAE's information-density account — language is dense
so 15% is enough, images are redundant so you need 75%. It is a satisfying
explanation and it is filed `Proposed`, because the evidence is **two points
from two literatures**, chosen by different groups with different
architectures, protocols and compute budgets, with "information density"
invoked qualitatively and never measured on either signal.

The `promote_when` is written to exclude the paper this literature would
otherwise produce: a fourth modality where a high ratio works would not
settle it, because the claim is that the optimum *tracks* redundancy, which
needs a correlation and not another point.

Worth separating clearly: `SOTA-tmpzm1n3` is `Active` while this is
`Proposed`, and the split is unusually clean. A wrong account of density
would leave the recommendation untouched, because "check whether an
interpolator can solve your masked task" is an empirical procedure that needs
no theory of density at all. When a practice can be stated as a measurement
rather than as a consequence, it survives its own explanation.

## The protocol disagreement, a third time

MAE's ablations show **linear probing and fine-tuning disagreeing about two
different hyperparameters**: the masking ratio (linear probe rises to a sweet
spot, fine-tuning flatter) and the decoder depth (matters for linear probing;
a single block already gives 84.8% fine-tuned).

That is `SOTA-196` arriving from a third independent direction — after
OWL-ViT's learning-rate sweep and CLIP's supervised-adaptation result. Three
papers, three unrelated hyperparameters, the same finding: **the protocol you
evaluate with selects the optimum you find.** `SOTA-196` currently carries
two sources and could carry a third; not done here, because MAE's is an
ablation table rather than the paper's claim, and the record should not
promote a passing observation to a source without saying why. Noted so the
option is on the record rather than lost.
