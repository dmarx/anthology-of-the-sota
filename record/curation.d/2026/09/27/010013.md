`SOTA-430`'s verdict table has four rows. Two of them name methods that, until
today, the record had no document for: GradCAM, which the practice reports as
*passing* the parameter-randomization test, and Guided GradCAM, which it reports
as failing. Eight live documents carried verdicts about one or the other.

They are the same paper. And the second method is **defined as the first
multiplied by the third**.

## Two rows, one construction

Grad-CAM §3.2 exists because Grad-CAM's map is 14×14 at VGG-16's last
convolutional layer and "lacks the ability to highlight fine-grained details
like pixel-space gradient visualization methods". The fix is to upsample it
bilinearly and multiply it element-wise by a guided backpropagation map.
Replacing guided backprop with the deconvnet "gives similar results".

So the record had been reporting, as two findings from one sweep of curves, a
method that passes and the same method with a factor bolted on that fails. Every
pixel of fine structure in a Guided GradCAM image — the part a human reads — is
contributed by the factor that fails.

`LIT-713` states the construction. In a subordinate clause, in its method
preamble: "For pixel level granularity GradCAM, can be combined with Guided
Backpropagation through an element-wise product." It draws nothing from it, and
neither does Grad-CAM, where the same fact is a rendering improvement.

**No theory is filed.** A `THEORY` needs a `source:`, and the source would have
to be a paper that makes the argument. Neither does. This is the same call as
`LIT-722`'s conditional-score decomposition two weeks of units ago: a derivation
from a definition is not a contested explanation, and filing one on novelty is
filing the record's own reading under somebody's name. It is stated in
`SOTA-430`'s prose, where it is short and where it is clearly the record
talking.

What is worth naming is how the record came to hold a composite's verdict without
its components'. The three failure mechanisms this cluster now holds
(`THEORY-113`, `THEORY-114`, `THEORY-115`) each partition *methods*. Nothing in
the scheme asks whether a method in one partition is built out of a method in
another. **A verdict table organized by method treats "Guided GradCAM" and
"Guided Backprop" as two entries; the construction makes them one entry and a
multiplier.** That is not a defect in the table — the empirical verdicts are
independent measurements and worth having as such — but it is a shape the table
cannot show.

## A number that waited three years for its sentence

`SOTA-430`'s negative half says: do not accept a method because its maps look
like the object. It has stood on an untrained edge detector producing
"strikingly similar" maps, and since yesterday on `THEORY-115` for why.

Grad-CAM §5.1 measured it, differently, in 2016. Four visualizations of the same
image, 90 PASCAL VOC 2007 pairs with exactly two annotated categories, 9
Mechanical Turk ratings each, one question — which of these two classes is this
a picture of?

| visualization | humans got the class right |
| --- | --- |
| Guided Backpropagation | 44.44% |
| Deconvolution | 53.33% |
| Deconvolution Grad-CAM | 60.37% |
| Guided Grad-CAM | 61.23% |

**Guided backprop is last.** The method whose maps the field found most
convincing — the one `LIT-713` chose as its headline failure because its maps
"look as convincing as ever" — is the one humans read a class off least often.

Then the version history. Those four numbers are in arXiv **v1, 7 October
2016**. The sentence that says what they mean —

> *Interestingly, our results indicate that Deconvolution is more
> class-discriminative than Guided Backpropagation (53.33% vs. 44.44%),
> although Guided Backpropagation is more aesthetically pleasing. To the best of
> our knowledge, our evaluations are the first to quantify this subtle
> difference.*

— is in **v4, 3 December 2019**. Not in v1, not in v2 (30 December 2016), not in
v3 (21 March 2017); each PDF was pulled and checked rather than inferred from
the dates. Adebayo et al. is October 2018.

So: the measurement is three years and two months older than its own gloss, and
the gloss — which claims priority for quantifying the effect — arrives fourteen
months after the paper that made the point famous made it by a different route
entirely.

**Generalising.** Yesterday's unit found SmoothGrad stating `THEORY-113`'s
mechanism in 2017 as advice about colour scales, and called the finding *a
mechanism stated in the wrong register is invisible to a search for mechanisms*.
This is the sharper version, because it is not a register mismatch and not two
groups talking past each other. It is **one table, one group, and a three-year
gap between taking a number and writing down what it showed**. The numbers were
in the abstract's supporting evidence the whole time; what was missing was the
sentence that makes them a finding rather than a ranking.

A record that files claims can hold the sentence. A record that files papers
would have held the table. This one now holds both, which is the argument for
`LIT` notes being readings rather than summaries: the four numbers have been
publicly available for a decade and are only evidence for `SOTA-430` once
somebody writes down that the last row is the prettiest one.

## The citation that lands on the wrong paper

`LIT-713`'s bibliography cites GradCAM as Selvaraju et al., *"Grad-cam: Why did
you say that?"*, `1611.07450`. That is a different arXiv entry: the November 2016
workshop note, about 3,000 words, with a different author order. Same method,
same group.

It matters slightly and it matters exactly: the workshop note has Guided Grad-CAM
at 61.23% and Guided Backprop at 44.44%, but **not Deconvolution's 53.33%** — so
not the row that turns the table into a finding — and none of the ablations. A
reader following `LIT-713`'s citation to check the verdict lands on the thinner
document. The note is filed for `1610.02391` and says so.

This is the third citation-resolution defect this cluster has produced, after
`LIT-725`'s "for different reasons (Sixt et al. 2020)" and the record's own
`NOTE-067` distinction drawn against an unheld paper. **The pattern is that a
citation is a claim about which document supports something, and nothing checks
it** — `luria lint` verifies that a *code* resolves, not that a surname and year
in prose resolve to the code a reader would need.

## What was checked rather than assumed

Two habits from the last four units earned their keep:

- **Four PDFs, not one HTML render.** ar5iv serves the latest version regardless
  of the `v1` suffix in the URL — verified by finding 2018 references in what was
  requested as a 2016 document. Had that gone unnoticed, this entry would have
  dated the "aesthetically pleasing" sentence to October 2016 and claimed a
  two-year priority over `LIT-713` that does not exist. **A version-pinned URL
  that silently serves something else is worse than no version pinning**, because
  it looks like diligence.
- **Every number in the new note is marked with the version it comes from where
  v1 and v4 differ.** Table 2's four accuracies and all of Table 3 are in v1; the
  localization table, the segmentation IoUs, the pointing game, the doctor/nurse
  section and the five-way occlusion comparison are not. That is not padding: the
  whole finding above is a claim about which version said what.

## What is left in this cluster

`SOTA-430` is at v8, eleven papers, three accounts, two practices. **One
referent remains**: the plain gradient, Simonyan, Vedaldi and Zisserman
`1312.6034`, which is `SOTA-430`'s other passing method and the antecedent of
everything in the table. It is a referent gap of exactly the kind this unit
closed, and it is the last one.

Also still unfiled, and recorded rather than filed twice now: SmoothGrad's
99th-percentile capping advice, which no second group has measured.
