# ADR-NNN: What the field thinks is a second axis, and this record's endorsement is not it

## Context

`statuses.yaml` opens with the reason this record has a status vocabulary at
all: *"A status every record shares is indistinguishable from no status at
all."* 133 of 144 practices are `Active`. The vocabulary is being exercised,
barely, and the reason is not laziness — it is that the one axis is being
asked to carry facts that are not the same fact.

Take mHC. Today it is simultaneously:

| | |
|---|---|
| **adoption** | shipped at 1.6T parameters in DeepSeek-V4 — one lab, production |
| **consensus** | two independent groups attacked its central constraint within a month, by different arguments, with opposite remedies |
| **our position** | `Proposed` |

Those are three orthogonal readings and `status:` holds one. "Standard at one
lab, contentious everywhere else" is a real and common state of a practice,
and this record could not say it.

The deeper point, and the one that prompted this: **advancement in a field is
largely a social process.** A paradigm shifts when the community behaves as
though it has, not when a decisive experiment lands. A record whose only axis
is its own endorsement is therefore structurally unable to distinguish *we
are not convinced* from *the field is not convinced* — and those call for
opposite actions. The first is a reason to go read something. The second is a
fact to write down.

[ADR-014](ADR-014.md) is where this bites hardest. It requires a provisional practice to
say what would settle it, and the rule for phrasing one — *name a kind of
result, not a count* — reads as though only evidential kinds qualify. Two of
the eight conditions written under it are already social: [SOTA-120](../practices.d/SOTA-120.md) waits on a
report that *states which choice it makes*, and [SOTA-141](../practices.d/SOTA-141.md)'s first clause waits
on a frontier recipe adopting the schedule and saying so. The mechanism was
broader than its own rule.

## Decision

**A second axis, `consensus:`, backed by a scheme-local vocabulary
([LU-ADR-076](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-076.md)) and defaulted rather than required:**

    [luria.schemes.SOTA.fields.consensus]
    vocabulary = "consensus"
    default    = "unassessed"

Six values, in `consensus.yaml`: `unassessed`, `unreplicated`, `contested`,
`emerging`, `converged`, `universal`.

**`unreplicated` and `contested` are the distinction that motivated this.**
One group with one result that nobody has examined is not the same as a claim
credible people are publicly arguing about, and `Proposed` covers both.
[SOTA-122](../practices.d/SOTA-122.md) is the first; [SOTA-136](../practices.d/SOTA-136.md) is the second; they read identically today.

**The axes are orthogonal, and the corners are the useful part.**

- `Deferred` + `converged` — [SOTA-120](../practices.d/SOTA-120.md). AdamW is the default optimizer, so
  decoupled weight decay is what almost everyone runs; what is unmeasured is
  whether anyone *chose* it. High consensus, low editorial confidence, and
  the two-axis version says exactly that.
- `Active` + `contested` — this record being ahead of the field and saying
  so. Worth stating as legitimate, because the alternative reading of the
  Kuhnian point is that an anthology should wait for consensus, which would
  make it a lagging indicator by construction. Being early is part of the
  value; being early *without flagging it* is the failure.

**`unassessed` is the default and most of the corpus carries it.** A
consensus reading is a judgement about a community and needs grounds. Ten
practices are assessed here, each with a `consensus_note:` citing what the
record actually holds; the other 134 say `unassessed`, which is true and
countable. A value invented to fill a column would be worse than the
admission.

## Branched conflict

The second half of the same question: the record could not distinguish an
agreed trunk from the forks contending off it.

The relations [ADR-011](ADR-011.md) added to the literature are now declared on practices
too — `extends:` and `compared_against:` — and walked into a second chain.
The two layers answer different questions and can disagree, which is why they
are separate chains rather than one: papers fork where the **designs** differ,
practices fork where the **recommendations** do.

With the consensus axis rendered beside the status
(LU-[#173](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/173)), the residual line
now reads:

    [SOTA-137](../practices.d/SOTA-137.md) — freely learned mixing            (Superseded, unassessed)
      [SOTA-136](../practices.d/SOTA-136.md) — doubly-stochastic mixing       (Proposed, contested)
    alongside: [SOTA-133](../practices.d/SOTA-133.md) — attention residuals   (Active, emerging)

That is the shape the record had already worked out in prose and could not
state. [LIT-181](../literature.d/LIT-181.md)'s standing section says the
constraint is "the least well-defended part of the design, disputed by two
groups within a month, while the thing all four papers agree on — widen the
residual stream, constrain the mixing somehow — is untouched."

**The trunk practice that paragraph describes is deliberately not filed
here.** [LIT-181](../literature.d/LIT-181.md) says the record should *probably* carry it *eventually*, and
converting a hedge into an assertion is an editorial act, not a mechanical
one. The structure now exists to hold it the day someone decides.

## Alternatives considered

**More statuses.** `Contested`, `Emerging` alongside `Active` and `Proposed`.
Rejected on two grounds. The status vocabulary is closed at five upstream
([ADR-003](ADR-003.md)), and more importantly it would re-commit the original error: mHC
would still need one word for three facts, and the word would now be even
harder to choose.

**A numeric score** — 0–5, or a fraction of adopting labs. Rejected as false
precision. Nobody can defend 3 over 4, and a number invites arithmetic on a
judgement.

**Derive it from the graph** — count production adoptions, count citing
groups. Rejected, and it is the tempting one, for the reason
[ADR-014](ADR-014.md) already gives: a derived value is necessarily a
**count**, and counting is the error that produced that decision. Two
critiques and two endorsements is not a wash; it depends entirely on what
they say.

**Put it on `LIT` instead.** A paper is not accepted or disputed — a *claim*
is. Two papers can agree on a design and disagree on its constraint, which is
precisely the residual case. The axis belongs where the claim lives.

## The cost, stated plainly

**A consensus value ages with nothing to trigger a re-read.** Unlike a
`promote_when:` condition, which names the evidence that would reopen the
question, a consensus reading just quietly rots as the field moves. This is
the real risk and there is no mechanism here that addresses it. Two things
make it survivable for now: the default means only assessed entries can rot,
and each assessment carries a `consensus_note:` saying what it rested on, so
a reader can tell whether the grounds still hold. If the count of assessed
practices grows past what one person can re-read, this needs a staleness
report of its own.

## Consequence for [ADR-014](ADR-014.md)

Amended in place (version 2): its phrasing rule now says explicitly that a
threshold of *adoption* is a kind of result. The requirement is unchanged —
before it existed, four of 144 practices stated a condition at all, and the
failure was silence, not over-precision.
