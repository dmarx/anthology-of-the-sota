`#137` is done. One paper filed, nine struck, and the ratio is the finding.

## The criterion did the work the ranking could not

The issue ranked its worklist by how many of twelve swept bibliographies
cited each entry, and was explicit that this was "a proxy for *the record's
own practices are argued on this*, not a quality ranking". The proxy held at
the top — GSM8K, Qwen2.5, MBPP, verl all turned out to be genuinely
load-bearing — and fell apart completely at the bottom.

Measured against the record rather than against the bibliographies:

| entry | documents citing it here |
|---|---|
| SmolLM2 | 1 |
| PRM800K, MATH-Beyond, DeepSeek LLM, Meyerson, MiniPile | 0 |
| Brax, Craftax, NAVIX, JaxMARL | 0 |

Nine of ten fail the issue's own stated criterion — *something in the record
has to cite it* — and filing them would be the "category added to admit a
single document" that the issue itself says `DP-008`'s corollary forbids.
The issue predicted this for the four RL environments, calling them
"plausibly out of scope even under the decision". It turns out to be true of
five more that it had not flagged.

So the tail was never a backlog. It was the part of a ranked list that the
ranking had no information about, presented in the same table as the part it
did.

## Two traps in the counting, both caught

**DeepSeek is not DeepSeek LLM.** A grep for `DeepSeek` returns 53 documents
and would have made `ARXIV-2401.02954` look strongly cited. Every one of them
is a later model — V3, V3.2, V4, R1, DeepSeekMath — and the record holds
those papers already. The 2024 scaling paper is a different document with
zero mentions. A family name that outlives the paper that started it is the
same shape as ImageNet and BBH, one level up: not two instruments under one
name, but a *lineage* under one name.

**Brax's only mention is `ADR-032` itself.** The decision that opened the
worklist enumerates the categories, and Brax appears in that enumeration.
Grepping the record finds it and counts one. That is the identical false
positive that nearly got HybridFlow struck in the opposite direction a few
hours ago — there the decision's own text made a real entry look thin, here
it makes an empty entry look populated. **A worklist stored inside the corpus
it describes will always be a source of self-citations**, and any count over
that corpus needs `ADR-032` excluded before it means anything.

## Why SmolLM2 survived on one citation

The same reason The Pile did on five: the citation is load-bearing rather
than incidental. `LIT-129` runs its entire three-stage fine-tuning experiment
on SmolLM2-135M-Instruct, and its finding that DPO converges at 135M is what
`LIT-119` cites when asking whether DPO would work at 90M — which is what
`SOTA-126` turns on. A chain that ends in a live practice rested on a base
model the record could not name.

And the note has something to say that the citing documents do not: SmolLM2
is overtrained to an extraordinary degree. "DPO converges at 135M" is
therefore a claim about *this* 135M model, whose data budget per parameter is
nothing like a compute-optimal one. Nothing here establishes the result
transfers to a 135M model trained the ordinary way.
