# LIT-503: First Contact: Unsupervised Human-Machine Co-Adaptation via Mutual Information Maximization

Reddy, Levine and Dragan (2022) —
[ARXIV-2205.12381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12381), read as [NOTE-250](../notes.d/NOTE-250.md).

## Key takeaways

- **The idea is one sentence and it is a good one.** Whatever the user is
  trying to do, a more intuitive interface produces less noisy commands — so
  the mutual information `I(x_t, (s_t, s_{t+Δ}))` between the command and the
  induced state change scores the interface without knowing the task.
- **It works in four domains out of five**, across 540K examples of keyboard
  and eye-gaze interfaces for typing, simulated robot control and games:
  average Spearman **ρ = 0.43** against ground-truth task completion. The
  abstract says "a variety of domains"; the body says 4 of 5.
- **The fifth failed by inverting**, and the reason is the most interesting
  thing in the paper. In the shared-autonomy Lunar Lander data the assistant
  helps by *overriding* the user to prevent crashes, which mechanically lowers
  one-step mutual information — while raising the user's influence over later
  states, because they have not crashed. One-step MI gave a **strong negative
  correlation** with reward.
- **The fix introduces the dependency the method was built to avoid.**
  Generalizing to `I(x_t, (s_t, s_{t+Δ}))` and setting `Δ` to the episode
  length restores a positive correlation — and "choosing the value of `Δ`
  requires prior knowledge of the timescale of the user's desired influence
  over the system", which the paper names as "the primary limitation on the
  generality of our method".
- **Online, it is much stronger than offline.** In the 12-participant cursor
  study the correlation between the MI reward and the true task reward is
  **ρ = 0.87**, against 0.43 across domains offline, and the learned interface
  approaches an oracle that always moves straight to the target.
- **What it converges to is worth noticing.** Over 12 users the learned
  perturbation angle `θ` piles up at **two** values: no perturbation
  (`θ ≈ 0`) and exact inversion (`θ ≈ π`). Both are *consistent* mappings.
  The objective rewards a channel the user can use reliably, which is not
  quite the "intuitive" the motivating sentence promises.
- **Scale is small and the paper says so:** the largest interface has **8
  parameters**, limited by user-study duration and the data efficiency of the
  Bayesian-optimization RL.

## Standing in the anthology

Filed for [SOTA-309](../practices.d/SOTA-309.md), and specifically for the horizon
result rather than the headline. An objective that *inverts* when the
assistant is doing its job is a measurement trap of the kind this record has
been accumulating all day — beside [SOTA-305](../practices.d/SOTA-305.md) on reconstruction FID,
[SOTA-307](../practices.d/SOTA-307.md) on seed variance, and [SOTA-308](../practices.d/SOTA-308.md) on recall bought
with relevance. This one is the sharpest of the four, because the sign of the
correlation flips rather than its size.

The domain is assistive interfaces, and under [ADR-026](../decisions.d/ADR-026.md) the test is
the kind of claim rather than where it was found. The claim — score a
human-in-the-loop channel by how much the operator's input explains the
outcome, and pick the horizon — is one the eighteen topics express, and it
reaches any setting where a person steers a system and nobody can label what
they wanted.
