**The record held `SOTA-121` through `SOTA-128` — eight `tiny-models`
practices — and did not hold TinyStories ([#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180)).** That is the shape of gap a
worklist built from reading history produces, and it is the third time this
journal has recorded the pattern: the incoming papers are the unsettled ones,
because those are the ones someone goes back to. Settled work is what a
practice should be sourced to, and nobody revisits it. `DP-004` says a pass
finds only defects its query is shaped like, and a revisit ranking is shaped
like doubt.

Worth noticing that the gap was invisible to every instrument the record
has. TinyStories is cited by nothing, so no unresolved code pointed at it; no
relation was unbound, because nothing declared a relation to a document that
was not there. An absent trunk produces no findings at all. It showed up only
because a descendant came up the worklist.

**A reading trap, and it would have produced a confident wrong note.**
SimpleStories' ar5iv HTML is **v1**; the arXiv API's abstract is **v3**. The
v1 body says "We seek to train a suite of small language models ourselves" in
the future tense and contains no model suite, no tokenizer ablation, no
probing and no interpretability section. The v3 abstract announces "ablations
on a newly trained model suite show improved sample efficiency". Build the
takeaways from the rendered full text and the claims from the abstract, as is
the usual habit, and the result attributes v3's conclusions to v1's evidence
— with nothing anywhere to flag it, because both sources are authentic. The
author list differs too, which is the cheapest available check: v1 has six
authors, v3 has nine.

**Then the paper itself turns out to be two papers.** Its abstract, and the
line everyone will cite, is that SimpleStories improves sample efficiency
over TinyStories. The experiment behind that compares models differing in
**dataset, tokenizer and architecture simultaneously**. Meanwhile the paper
contains one fully controlled comparison — same data, same architecture,
tokenizer swapped — and it is not about the dataset at all: **+26.7 coherence
and +27.5 quality** from a 4,096-token vocabulary against GPT-2's 50,257.
That is a larger effect than the architecture change measured in the same
figure, and it is a footnote to the paper's own story.

`DP-010` says the most citable sentence in a paper is often its least
supported one. This is a clean instance, and the authors are not at fault for
it: their Limitations section names the missing arm exactly — a custom
tokenizer trained on TinyStories, which would separate tokenization from
corpus — and they simply did not run it. So the filing splits along the
evidence rather than along the paper's narrative: `SOTA-295` rests on
the corpus-level diversity metrics, which measure the artefact directly and
are confounded by nothing; `SOTA-294` rests on the tokenizer ablation.
Neither rests on the abstract.

**An agreement that is easy to miss because it looks like a disagreement.**
`SOTA-172` says: do not generate synthetic pretraining data from a prompt,
edit human text instead, because the proportion of generated data correlates
negatively with performance — and the mechanism it names is
**over-concentration of n-gram features**. `SOTA-295` says: when you do
generate from a prompt, here is how to do it better — and its evidence is a
measurement of **n-gram over-concentration** in a generated corpus.

Different group, different corpus, same disease. The remedies diverge because
the jobs diverge: one corpus is meant to stand in for real text, the other is
meant to exclude it, and a model organism that faithfully reproduced the web's
distribution would be useless. `SOTA-172`'s `promote_when` had already
anticipated the collision — "what would not move it: a paper generating
better synthetic data and reporting better results" — so the honest outcome
is a boundary written into both, not a promotion and not a contest. Two
practices that point opposite ways and are both right is a thing this scheme
is supposed to be able to represent.

**A near-miss on `SOTA-125` worth writing down.** TinyStories sweeps depth
against width at a tiny scale and finds that factual knowledge tracks the
embedding dimension while context-tracking tracks the number of layers.
`SOTA-125` is a depth-versus-width practice at a tiny scale awaiting
replication. The words line up and the measurements do not: `SOTA-125` trades
depth against **MLP width** in a **hybrid** at **90M** and its condition asks
for another hybrid or another 90M-class ablation; TinyStories trades depth
against **embedding dimension** in **pure attention** at **1–35M** and has no
SSM state dimension at all. Filing it as the replication would have been the
easy error, and the `promote_when` was specific enough to prevent it — which
is the argument for writing those conditions at the level of the measurement
rather than the topic.

**One unbound line, answered rather than argued.** Extending the
`LIT-447 → LIT-448 → LIT-449` lineage with Mixture-of-Transformers in `#230`
left the line with no tag common to all four. Both of `ADR-049`'s readings
were available and only one survives contact: the relation is right — MoT
reproduces the Transfusion setting, which is where `LIT-449`'s design sits —
and `LIT-483` was simply missing `generative-modeling`, a tag that is plainly
true of a paper whose headline small-model result is that a 760M model beats
a 1.4B one at image generation. Added; the line binds. The record is back to
the single pre-existing unbound line that `ADR-050` left open on purpose.

**Passed over: `2503.08827`, thirteen revisits.** It sits at the top of the
revisit ranking and it is a proposal for a "synaptic field theory" of training
dynamics, with no measurement and a stated contribution of suggesting a
pathway. Thirteen sessions totalling **65 seconds** is a bounce, not a
reading, and the ranking cannot tell those apart. Noted here rather than
filed, because "the instrument ranked it first" is not a reason and a
recommendation it could source does not exist yet.
