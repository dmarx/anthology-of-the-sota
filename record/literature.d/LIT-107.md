# LIT-107: MiDaS v3.1 -- A Model Zoo for Robust Monocular Relative Depth Estimation

Birkl et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2307.14460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14460)

## Key takeaways

- **A controlled backbone sweep, not a method.** The MiDaS architecture and
  recipe are held fixed while the image encoder varies across **BEiT, Swin,
  SwinV2, Next-ViT, LeViT** and recent convolutional approaches — where v3.0 used
  vanilla ViT alone
- **The design answers a question the backbone papers cannot:** does
  classification performance transfer to depth estimation? Including
  convolutional approaches "that achieve comparable quality to vision
  transformers in image classification tasks" is the direct test
- **28% depth-quality improvement** from the best backbone; the deliverable is a
  **performance-runtime frontier**, with efficient models "enabling downstream
  tasks requiring high frame rates" — a zoo, not a winner
- Also publishes **the general process for integrating a new backbone**, which is
  what keeps a model zoo alive after the paper

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-047](../notes.d/NOTE-047.md). Nothing is sourced here.

The methodological sibling of `LIT-156` (*Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers*),
read in batch B: both hold a setting fixed and sweep one component fairly, and
both find that a component's reputation elsewhere does not settle its performance
here. Two independent instances of the same lesson — **a component that wins on
one task is not thereby the right choice for another, and only a fair sweep
tells you** — and the general form is stated nowhere in the record.

The takeaways it carried described the MiDaS *line* rather than this release,
whose entire content is the comparison.


**Audited by [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121); unsourced.** R1 — report a frontier rather than
a winner when downstream uses have different cost constraints — is general,
`analysis-and-evaluation`, and the reading notes R2 is the same lesson
[LIT-156](LIT-156.md) teaches. Two in-record sources for a reporting norm is the
threshold this audit used elsewhere, and the reason it is not filed here is
narrower: the record's evaluation practices are about what to measure, and this
is about how to present it. That may be a gap in the topic rather than in the
corpus.
