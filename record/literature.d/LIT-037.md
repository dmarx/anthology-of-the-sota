# LIT-037: On the difficulty of training Recurrent Neural Networks

Pascanu, Mikolov and Bengio (2012) — [ARXIV-1211.5063](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5063)

## Key takeaways

- The vanishing and exploding gradient problems are examined from three
  angles at once — analytic, geometric, and dynamical-systems — and the
  geometric reading is the one that pays: an exploding gradient is what a
  cliff in the error surface looks like locally, so a step sized for the
  smooth region overshoots catastrophically at the wall.
- The remedy that follows is *gradient norm clipping*: when the gradient's
  norm exceeds a threshold, rescale it to that threshold and keep its
  direction. It is a step-size correction, not a change to what is being
  optimized, which is why it costs nothing and does not bias the solution
  away from the cliff.
- A separate soft constraint is proposed for the vanishing direction; it is
  the clipping half that the field kept.
- Framed for recurrent networks, where depth-in-time makes the pathology
  acute, but the argument depends only on the error surface having cliffs —
  which is why the technique transferred to feedforward and transformer
  training unchanged.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-035](../practices.d/SOTA-035.md). It arrived in the record under a fabricated
citation and is filed properly here; the practice itself was never in doubt,
since every large training run in the corpus clips. Its descendants are in
the record too: [SOTA-071](../practices.d/SOTA-071.md) makes the threshold dynamic, and [SOTA-131](../practices.d/SOTA-131.md)'s QK-Clip is
the same move — rescale rather than let a quantity run away — applied to
attention logits through the weights instead of to the gradient.
