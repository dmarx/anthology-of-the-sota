The standing instruction that produced this entry: *when in doubt, carry the
paper, practice or theory; worst case, assign it a provisional state as a clear
signal that we are not sure how valuable the information is.*

My first reaction was that this is `DP-006` restated, and filing an ADR for it
would be noise. That was wrong in a way worth writing down, because the two
halves of the instruction pull in different directions and only the first is
already covered.

## What `DP-006` does and does not reach

`DP-006` is about a claim you **doubt**: weak-but-honest evidence, and the
reflex to say *not yet*. Its answer is that the bar for having a document is
lower than the bar for believing it, so file it and put the belief in `status:`
and the condition in `promote_when:`.

The case in front of me was not that. `LIT-087` (DSIR) is a paper I believe
fine; the hesitation recorded in its note was about whether the **record**
wanted it — 2023, a 0.9% margin, a scale below where its neighbours argue.
That is doubt about *worth*, not about truth, and `DP-006` does not name it.

The second half of the instruction is where the tension is. "Assign it a
provisional state as a signal that we're not sure how valuable it is" asks
`Proposed` to mean something it currently cannot, because `ADR-014` requires
`promote_when:` to name a kind of result and **no result settles whether a
document is worth having**. A status carrying that would be the one status that
never ages — a fact about the curator rather than about the world.

So the ADR exists to take the instruction and land it somewhere `ADR-014`
survives: carry the item, and let writing the condition decide which of the two
existing doubts you actually have. On `LIT-087` it decides immediately — what
is uncertain is whether a 2023 correlation survives at the scale the record's
data practices live at, and that is an evidence condition, which is `DP-006`
unchanged. The "worth" doubt turned out to be an evidence doubt that had not
been made to state itself.

## The bundling that hid it for ten days

The [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121) audit's determination on `LIT-087` gives two reasons, and they are not
the same kind of thing:

> Unfiled because the record's existing selection practices are quality-filter
> shaped and reconciling the two frames is a larger job than this audit.

> Whether the record should carry it is a registry decision: the evidence is
> correlational, from 2023, at a scale below where mixing laws are now fitted.

The first is **scope**, and an audit is entitled to it — a review that files
everything it finds is not a review. The second is a **decision deferred**, and
`DP-006` already answers it.

Written together, the scope reason does the talking. The note reads as though
the work was sized rather than declined, so nothing in it comes due, and the
audit best placed to notice is the one that already looked and passed. That is
`ADR-014`'s complaint in its usual form: the most perishable fact in the
encounter went into a paragraph.

What [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121) did well, and what makes this contribution possible, is that it named
the specific recommendations — R1 and R2, by number, with what each was missing.
I made this ruling from the note without reopening the paper. A decline that
names what would be filed is a different artifact from one that names only the
hesitation, and the ADR says so.

## Two practices, and where the other two recommendations went

[NOTE-031](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-031.md) carries four recommendations. Filing all four would have been the
instruction applied without judgement, which is not what it says.

- **R2 → [SOTA-tmp4fwxw](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-tmp4fwxw.md).** The frame: specify data selection as *matching a
  target you can sample from*, not as scoring quality. This is the one the
  record genuinely lacked. `SOTA-170` — the central filtering practice — tunes
  how aggressive a quality classifier to run, and the DCLM and FineWeb-Edu work
  it argues with differs on the setting, not on the question. The
  distribution-matching frame changes the question: a quality rubric is an
  argument you have to win, a target distribution is an artifact you produce,
  and nobody has made the two frames argue.
- **R1 → [SOTA-tmp2nprq](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-tmp2nprq.md).** The proxy: rank selections by KL reduction in a
  hashed n-gram space before training on them. Filed separately because it is a
  different instruction with a different failure mode, and because it is the
  stage *before* the two practices the record already has — `SOTA-166` fits a
  mixing law on a sample of mixtures and `SOTA-238` runs a proxy model to
  convergence; both cost runs, and both have to choose which candidates deserve
  one.
- **R3 → inside [SOTA-tmp2nprq](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-tmp2nprq.md)**, not its own document. "Use crude features when
  only the ordering matters" is the *justification* for the proxy — 10,000 hash
  buckets cannot represent text and are not claimed to; they are claimed to
  preserve the ordering of candidate selections. Filed alone it would be a
  maxim with nothing to act on.
- **R4 → a condition on [SOTA-tmp4fwxw](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-tmp4fwxw.md).** "Expect the best selection method to
  depend on target breadth" qualifies when the frame pays rather than
  recommending a third thing — top-`k` quality scoring is competitive for
  narrow domain targets, by the paper's own reasoning that diversity matters
  less there.

That last pair is the part of the instruction that is *not* "file everything".
A recommendation that only works as a clause inside another practice is a clause,
and the registry is worse for splitting it out. Carrying the item is the
instruction; carrying it four times is not.
