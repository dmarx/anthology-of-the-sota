nGPT was the highest-revisit paper on the unfiled ranking that the record's
sixteen topics can clearly express, and it had **zero mentions** here. Eight
revisits, a well-known result, and not a word.

## The number and the footnote

The abstract says the normalized Transformer "reduces the number of training
steps required to achieve the same accuracy by a factor of 4 to 20". That is
accurate. Footnote 3, on the same page as Figure 1, says the time per step is
80% higher at 4k context and 60% higher at 8k.

Multiply: 10× in tokens is about 5.5× in wall clock, and 20× is about 12.5×.
Both are large, and neither is what gets quoted.

The paper is not hiding it — the footnote is in the body, the appendix
explains the cause (six normalizations per layer instead of two, unfused),
and the authors say they expect kernel work and depth to narrow it. But
"expect to narrow" is a prediction. The record files the measured figure and
labels the prediction as one, because a practice that is read once and acted
on later gets read at the headline.

This is `DP-005` in a different key. There the warning is that adoption is
not evidence; here it is that the unit a result is reported in is a choice,
and the choice that flatters is the one that travels.

## What the practice should say, versus what the paper is about

The paper's title and its own framing are the variable-metric reading: a
transformer as a multi-step optimizer on the sphere, blocks proposing
directions, learned per-dimension step sizes as the diagonal of a metric,
normalization as a Riemannian retraction. It is a good frame and it earns the
parameters their names.

It is also not evidence. Nothing measured shows the frame is *why* the
training is faster; it is an interpretation laid over a result. `ADR-031`
exists so the record can hold those apart, so `THEORY-tmp99061` records the
thing with measurements behind it instead — that unconstrained matrices come
out badly conditioned, with attention matrices drifting toward rank
deficiency — and marks it `Proposed`, because that too is correlational.

The one detail that makes it an account rather than a restatement:
renormalizing a trained GPT's matrices afterwards *reduces* the condition
numbers without closing the gap to the constrained model. If post-hoc
normalization had closed it, the constraint would only have been changing
what you measure.

## The bundle nobody has taken apart

Two independent things are being recommended at once: put everything on the
sphere, and make each block's residual contribution a learned per-dimension
step size. The second does not require the first. You could add it to an
ordinary pre-norm transformer this afternoon.

No ablation separates them, and the difference matters enormously for
whether anyone should do this: the sphere constraint is what costs 60–80% of
step time, and the learned step size costs nothing. The practice says so
rather than recommending the bundle as though it were atomic.

## Two arrivals, no citation

`SOTA-122` — Falcon's learnable per-row and per-column multipliers — says a
weight matrix's norm should be learned rather than set by the learning rate
and weight decay. nGPT says a weight matrix's norm should be fixed rather
than set by the learning rate and weight decay. Different remedies, same
diagnosis, different continents, and neither paper cites the other.

`DP-007` says agreement has no author. This is the version of that worth
noticing: the *diagnosis* now has two independent sources while each
*remedy* still has one. The record can record the first without promoting
either of the second, and the two practices' `promote_when` conditions both
say that confirming the diagnosis is not the result they need.

## The collision that is not a contradiction

nGPT removes learning-rate warmup and weight decay, which `SOTA-008`,
`SOTA-009` and `SOTA-120` recommend. Nothing was retired. The recommendations
are about architectures where the quantity in question is free to drift, and
in this one it is not.

Worth being exact about the warmup case, because the temptation to write
"nGPT shows warmup is unnecessary" is strong and would be wrong. `SOTA-008`'s
claim is specifically about **large batch size**. nGPT trained at global batch
512 at 1B parameters. That is not the regime warmup was introduced for, so
the removal is not evidence against the practice — it is untested against it.
