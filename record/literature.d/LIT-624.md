# LIT-624: Step-Video-T2V Technical Report: The Practice, Challenges, and Future of Video Foundation Model

Step-Video Team, StepFun (2025) — [ARXIV-2502.10248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10248)

## Key takeaways

- **Compression doubles in every dimension, with no loss on its own
  benchmark.** A dual-path VAE (pixel-unshuffle convolutions plus a
  channel-averaging shortcut) compresses 16×16×8. It reconstructs at SSIM
  0.978, PSNR 39.37 and rFVD 3.61, against HunyuanVideo's 8×8×4 at 0.971,
  39.56 and 4.17. Cosmos, at the same 16×16×8 ratio, gets rFVD 40.33 (Table
  15). Open-Sora 2.0's Table 1 ([LIT-634](LIT-634.md)) measures the same VAE well below
  HunyuanVideo's: LPIPS 0.082 against 0.046, PSNR 28.719 against 30.240. The
  latent is 64 channels against 16 (per [LIT-634](LIT-634.md)), so the information ratio
  is 2×, not 8×. Step-Video's own numbers present it as a counterexample to
  CogVideoX's finding that 16×16×8 is "extremely difficult". Open-Sora's
  measurement and the 4× larger channel count weaken that reading. Wan
  ([LIT-619](LIT-619.md)) also plots this VAE, listed at 64 latent channels, against its
  own, HunyuanVideo's and others on PSNR and speed over 200 videos (Fig. 7). The
  values are not printed, so the plot neither confirms nor refutes Table 15.
- **The model.** A 30B DiT with 48 layers, full 3D attention and
  cross-attention to two text encoders (a CLIP and a bilingual LLM). It uses
  AdaLN-single, 3D RoPE and QK-norm, and trains with flow matching. The
  backbone is DiT's ([LIT-448](LIT-448.md)), which the paper names as its base. It keeps
  DiT's adaLN timestep modulation but drops the class label, shares the
  modulation across layers as AdaLN-single to cut its cost, and adds a
  cross-attention layer for text between self-attention and the FFN
  (§4.3).
- **Video DPO works, modestly.** DPO ([LIT-169](LIT-169.md)) is applied with fixed initial noise, and the DPO model
  scores 55 against 45 over the baseline on 300 prompts (§9.7). This is a
  preference score, with "no preference" worth 0.5 to each side. It is not a
  win rate, and the tie rate is not given. The objective (§4.4, Eq. 8) is
  Diffusion-DPO's ([LIT-tmp4m2nj](LIT-tmp4m2nj.md)), "based on the DiffusionDPO method … with
  slight modifications, extending it to the Flow Matching framework". It
  keeps the pair loss against a frozen reference and shares the initial
  noise and timestep across each pair. Eq. 8 is printed in DPO's
  likelihood-ratio form, so the flow-matching version of the per-sample
  term is not shown. Diffusion-DPO's defaults (β = 5000, lr 1e-8)
  converge too slowly, by the paper's gradient argument (Eq. 9), and a
  smaller β with a higher learning rate fixes that. Flow-DPO ([LIT-tmp3txak](LIT-tmp3txak.md)) is a concrete case of the same direction: β = 500
  and learning rate 5·10⁻⁶ with LoRA rank 64 for one epoch on a 1B video
  flow model, where a constant β beat the exact β(1 − t)² form. The β values
  are not on one scale, because Diffusion-DPO folds the number of timesteps
  into its β. Gains saturate on off-policy pairs, which
  motivates a reward model.
- **Systems.** It reaches 32% MFU with TP8 + SP + ZeRO-1, and pipeline
  parallelism was rejected for robustness. Hardware restarts run at 0.037 per
  1k GPUs per day, against Llama 3.1's 0.422 (Table 5).
- **Where it ranks.** HunyuanVideo ([LIT-620](LIT-620.md)) is its main open baseline.
  Against it on Movie Gen Bench it wins 615, ties 313, loses 361, and the
  three annotators on its own benchmark favour Step-Video too (e.g. 59-22-47;
  Table 7). Frame count and resolution are not matched. Against Movie Gen
  ([LIT-626](LIT-626.md)), on Movie Gen's own benchmark, it wins 485, ties 315, loses 489,
  i.e. parity (Table 14). The paper's reasons for not doing better
  include a third as many high-resolution pre-training videos (27.3M
  against 73.8M), less human-labelled SFT data, and Movie Gen's 720P against
  its 540P. It loses to T2VTopA
  (Table 9).

## Where the hedges are

Per [DP-010](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-10):

- **"State-of-the-art … compared with both open-source and commercial
  engines"** (abstract) is contradicted by §9.3, which ranks "T2VTopA >
  Step-Video-T2V > T2VTopB".
- **"State-of-the-art" reconstruction** is contradicted by Table 15, where
  HunyuanVideo's PSNR is higher. An outside measurement ([LIT-634](LIT-634.md), Table 1)
  puts the gap much wider: PSNR 28.719 against 30.240.
- **Architecture comparisons are early and unquantified.** DiT against MMDiT
  rests on "similar" early curves, and MMDiT was "not train[ed] … for an
  extended period". Full 3D against factorized attention was run at 4B with
  no numbers.
- **The DPO β and learning rate are unreported,** and the effect is a 55/45
  preference score on 300 prompts, with ties split and their rate not
  given.
- **The sample counts disagree.** §9.3 says 25.3M samples at 540P, and Table
  6 totals 27.3M.

## Standing in the anthology

Step-Video is the line's evidence on how far a video latent can be
compressed, and the one report that applies preference optimization to a
video model. It is in this record for the VAE result and the DPO recipe,
not for the "state-of-the-art" in its abstract.

Its limitations section (§§10–11) is unusually candid, and it names the same
failures as Wan's and Movie Gen's: complex actions, rare compositions,
physics. It attributes them to "inherent limitations of diffusion models",
which is asserted, not shown.
