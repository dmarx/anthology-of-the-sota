# LIT-017: An Empirical Model of Large-Batch Training

McCandlish et al. (2018) — [ARXIV-1812.06162](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06162)

## Key takeaways

- Defines the **gradient noise scale** `B_noise = tr(HΣ)/(GᵀHG)`, and the
  Hessian-free estimator actually used, `B_simple = tr(Σ)/|G|²` — the summed
  per-component gradient variance over the squared gradient norm
- Per-step progress depends on batch size **only** through
  `1/(1 + B_noise/B)`. Below the noise scale, doubling the batch nearly
  doubles progress; above it, almost nothing. **At `B = B_noise`, training
  speed is 50% of maximum** — the noise scale is the knee, not the ceiling
- The steps/examples tradeoff is a hyperbola,
  `(S/S_min − 1)(E/E_min − 1) = 1`, and **critical batch size is defined off
  that fit**: `B_crit = E_min/S_min`. The record inherits `B_crit` from here
- Verified on eight tasks across supervised learning, RL and generative
  modelling — MNIST through ImageNet, Billion Word, Atari, Dota, a VAE. The
  noise scale is **independent of dataset size**, which is what lets it
  transfer between domains
- **The noise scale rises as the loss falls**, so the right batch size grows
  during a run; and model size affects it *only* through the loss the model
  reaches, not through parameter count

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-062](../notes.d/NOTE-062.md). Order-of-magnitude prediction, which the paper says
plainly: it tells you whether 10³ or 10⁵ is the ballpark, not whether 8K beats
16K.

**Nothing in the record is sourced to this paper, which is the surprising
part.** The anthology carries the consequences of the noise scale without
carrying its origin — `B_crit` appears in the scaling-law practices as an
inherited quantity, and this is where it is defined and given a measurement
procedure. A practice recommending that the noise scale be measured rather
than the batch size swept is a real candidate and is deliberately not filed
from a note-repair pass; it needs a session that can check what the registry
already says about batch-size selection.
