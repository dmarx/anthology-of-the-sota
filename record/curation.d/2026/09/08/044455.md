First pass at [#85](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/85). Before auditing 118 notes one at a time, two cheap
mechanical queries: what is actually in there, and does any paper appear
twice.

**The inventory.** The issue estimated "roughly 87 pre-migration" of 115
unsourced notes. The real numbers, taking [LIT-001](../../record/literature.d/LIT-001.md)..118 as the inherited set:

| | notes |
|---|---|
| inherited total | 118 |
| sourced by at least one practice | 45 |
| unsourced | 73 |
| unsourced *and* in a topic the SOTA scheme can hold | **37** |

So the audit's actual surface is 37 notes, not 87. The other 36 unsourced
notes sit in `generative-modeling`, `vision-and-graphics`,
`analysis-and-evaluation`, `adaptation-and-tuning` and
`inference-optimization` — five topics `practices.d/tags.yaml` does not
carry, which is [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) working as designed rather than a backlog. Confirming
that reasoning applies is one paragraph, not 36 readings.

**The duplicates.** Grouping all 218 notes by `arxiv:` and by normalized
title found three pairs. One ([LIT-090](../../record/literature.d/LIT-090.md) / [LIT-105](../../record/literature.d/LIT-105.md), Lion) was already
deliberately retired in September. The other two were live:

- **[LIT-006](../../record/literature.d/LIT-006.md) / [LIT-042](../../record/literature.d/LIT-042.md)**, Loshchilov and Hutter's SGDR. [LIT-006](../../record/literature.d/LIT-006.md) read `Active`
  and [LIT-042](../../record/literature.d/LIT-042.md) read `Superseded`, so the record asserted both about one paper.
  Nothing cited [LIT-006](../../record/literature.d/LIT-006.md); everything that mattered pointed at [LIT-042](../../record/literature.d/LIT-042.md), which
  carries the schedule chain. Retired [LIT-006](../../record/literature.d/LIT-006.md).
- **[LIT-029](../../record/literature.d/LIT-029.md) / [LIT-114](../../record/literature.d/LIT-114.md)**, Xiong et al. on layer normalization. Both `Active`.
  Retired [LIT-029](../../record/literature.d/LIT-029.md).

**What the second pair was concealing**, which is the part worth the
contribution. The two notes reached the same paper from opposite directions,
and that is why a year of lints never saw it. [LIT-029](../../record/literature.d/LIT-029.md) was filed with the
*right identifier and the wrong reading* — a single migration-authored bullet
claiming the paper shows "RMSNorm pre-norm permits stable training at scale".
Xiong et al. never discuss RMSNorm; that is Zhang and Sennrich, [LIT-023](../../record/literature.d/LIT-023.md),
which the record already held. [LIT-114](../../record/literature.d/LIT-114.md) was filed with a *fabricated title
against an unrelated arXiv id* and was repaired in September by re-sourcing
it to Xiong et al. Neither repair could see the other, because no check
compares identifiers across notes (luria#165).

The cost was not the extra document. [SOTA-032](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-032.md) sourced [LIT-029](../../record/literature.d/LIT-029.md) and inherited
its conflation: "Use pre-norm (RMSNorm) for transformer layers" — two
independently adoptable choices, one citation, and that citation supports
only the placement half. GPT-2 is Pre-LN with centered LayerNorm; nothing
stops a Post-LN model using RMSNorm. Split into the placement practice
([SOTA-032](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-032.md), now sourced to [LIT-114](../../record/literature.d/LIT-114.md)) and the statistic ([SOTA-tmp5bfj6](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-tmp5bfj6.md), sourced
to [LIT-023](../../record/literature.d/LIT-023.md)).

**What this says about the query, per [DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4).** The duplicate scan is exact
and finds exactly duplicates — identical `arxiv:` or identical title. It
cannot see the same paper filed under two different identifiers, which is
what the [LIT-029](../../record/literature.d/LIT-029.md) / [LIT-114](../../record/literature.d/LIT-114.md) pair *was* until the September repair happened to
converge them. That pair became findable by accident. How many others are
still divergent is not something this query can answer, and a title-similarity
pass is a different contribution.

It also says nothing about correctness. [LIT-029](../../record/literature.d/LIT-029.md)'s bullet was wrong about its
own paper and no check could have caught it; it was found because the
duplicate scan put two readings of one work side by side. That is the second
argument for luria#165 — a duplicate detector is a cheap correctness check
you get for free.
