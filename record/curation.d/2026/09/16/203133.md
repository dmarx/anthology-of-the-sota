InstructGPT was not in this record. Not as a practice, not as a reading, not
as a bibliography line. `2203.02155` appeared nowhere.

What *is* here: DPO, whose title — *your language model is secretly a reward
model* — is a claim about the reward model this paper introduced.
Constitutional AI, which replaces this paper's human preference labels with
model-written ones. `SOTA-183`, which is that replacement filed as a practice.
`SOTA-126`, which tunes DPO's epoch count. Four documents defined by their
relationship to a paper the record did not hold.

That is the third time in two sessions:

1. **Speculative decoding** — six documents referenced it, including EAGLE-3,
   a paper about making its draft model cheaper. Filed an hour ago.
2. **The outer optimizer in local-update training** — `SOTA-155` prescribed
   it, `LIT-373` demonstrated it four years earlier, and the record had them
   filed as rivals rather than as parent and child. Fixed in [#148](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/148).
3. **InstructGPT**, here.

Three is enough to stop calling it a coincidence.

## Why the filing habit produces this

[DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7) already says it: papers are written about what is new, so the filing
habit runs on arrivals. Each of the four documents above arrived with a name
and a claim and correctly became an entry. RLHF-for-instruction-following by
2023 had no arrival. It was the thing the arrivals were *about*.

What I want to add from three instances is that the gap has a **signature you
can query for**, and it is not "what is missing" — you cannot grep for
absence. It is:

> **Which documents are defined by their relationship to something, and is
> that something here?**

A title containing *"instead of"*, *"secretly a"*, *"without"*, *"beyond"*, or
*"scaling up"* is a document naming its parent. A standing section that opens
*"filed because three frontier models do X"* is naming a practice nobody
filed. In all three cases the missing trunk was written down, in the record,
inside a document about its own child.

## What I filed and what I refused to

`LIT-tmp3me1q` and the reading `NOTE-tmp4lt07`. The reading is full: the three
steps, the 1.3B-beats-175B result, the 85% and 73.4% win rates, the alignment
tax with the four datasets it shows up on, PPO-ptx, the per-token KL penalty,
the single 6B reward model, and the forty contractors the paper is careful to
say its notion of "aligned" belongs to.

**No practice.** The reading carries five recommendations and I filed none of
them, which is worth defending because the reflex runs the other way.

R1 is *spend on post-training before parameters*. R2 is *use your own prompt
distribution*. Both are true, both are load-bearing, and both are now so
universal that a document asserting them would say what the corpus already
assumes on every page. Filing them would be answering [DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7)'s complaint with
its own disease — manufacturing entries for agreement, which is not the same
as documenting it.

R3 is the live one: mixing pretraining gradients into the alignment objective
to pay down the tax. Specific, cheap, measured against a named cost, and
genuinely *not* universally done. It is also close to `SOTA-199`, which
regularizes a narrow fine-tune against the pre-fine-tuning model's own
samples — same failure, different mechanism. Whether those are one practice or
two is a real question, and answering it means reading `SOTA-199`'s source
alongside this one. I left it open and said so in the note rather than
guessing, because guessing is what put a 1 Gbps threshold in a practice title
earlier today.

(And for the third time today the lint caught me hand-writing a link to the
design principles instead of leaving the bare code for `luria link --fix`.
Twice is a slip. Three times in one session, each after writing about the
previous one, is a habit that prose cannot fix — the rule needs to be the
thing I reach for, not the thing I remember.)

## What this note does not close

Three more absences in the same lineage, all found while checking this one:
Christiano et al. (2017), which is where RLHF actually comes from and which
this paper says it follows; Stiennon et al. (2020), which applied it to
summarization; and FLAN, which this paper benchmarks against and beats.

InstructGPT is the pivot, not the origin, and a record that holds only the
pivot will eventually attribute the idea to it. Naming the three in the note
is cheaper than the correction would be.
