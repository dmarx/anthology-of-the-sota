EDM2 has been on the `#342` Tier B list for days and was ranked low twice, both
times for the same stated reason: its own contributions are absent from the record
— zero files say "post-hoc EMA" or "magnitude-preserving" — so it "extends a
well-sourced practice rather than correcting one". It succeeds `SOTA-188`'s source
and nothing here contradicts it.

That reading was wrong, and the error is in how an absence was priced.

## The measurement that inverted it

The record holds a dense weight-averaging cluster, most of it filed in the last
three days:

| document | what it says about the averaging length |
| --- | --- |
| `SOTA-408` | average the tail under a cyclical or high constant LR; window = "further epochs" |
| `SOTA-409` | average across a hyperparameter sweep, in validation order |
| `SOTA-415` | uniform-average recent checkpoints — **"it has no hyperparameter"** |
| `SOTA-156` | average the iterates so no stopping time need be fixed |
| `SOTA-217` | align permutations first |
| `SOTA-288` | replicate a subset, train the copies, average back |

Six practices about averaging weights, and **not one of them knew the averaging
length can be chosen after the run.** `SOTA-415` is the sharpest case: it
recommends uniform averaging partly *because* "it has no hyperparameter", and then
spends its next Conditions bullet on a heuristic for tuning a window — with a
measured 4.6-point penalty for getting it wrong early.

**So the absence was not "this paper adds something the record does not discuss".
It was "the record works around this paper's contribution six times."** Those look
identical from a mention count. Telling them apart takes asking what the
neighbouring documents are *doing*, and the question that has been productive all
week — does the record make a claim this paper would correct or source — gets it
right where the count gets it backwards.

That is the second time a `#342` ranking has been wrong for a measurable reason.
The first was Swin, where `grep swin` was matching the word "swings". This one is
subtler and worse: the count was correct and the inference from it was not.

## The number that reaches outside diffusion

Figure 12, reported as an aside about learning rates:

| averaging length | FID across `t_ref ∈ [30k, 160k]` |
| --- | --- |
| fixed at 13% | up to **72%** worse than optimum |
| swept post hoc | all **within 10%** |

A sweet spot in the learning-rate decay still exists. But most of what the first
row measures is the mismatch between a frozen average and the run it was frozen
for, and a reader of that row alone would conclude the decay is a
high-sensitivity hyperparameter. `THEORY-111` is the general form: a
sensitivity sweep attributes interactions to the knob it moves, so published
sensitivity curves are a lower bound on how forgiving a hyperparameter is.

**A claim I had to weaken while writing it.** I first wrote that freezing "can
only add apparent sensitivity, never remove it, because the swept curve is a lower
envelope". The envelope part is right and the conclusion does not follow: a
pointwise lower bound can have a *larger* range if it dips far at one point. What
makes the bias one-directional in practice is that the frozen value is tuned at the
configuration the authors settled on, so the two curves share a minimum and the
frozen one spreads wider. That holds here — 13% is optimal at `t_ref = 70k` — and
it is a fact about how people tune, not a theorem. Filed as the former.

## Why the practice is Active on one paper

`SOTA-432` is `Active`/`unreplicated`, which is an unusual pair for a single
group's single paper, and the reason is that the two halves have different
evidential status. The reconstruction is **arithmetic** — a least-squares fit
between averaging profiles over stored parameter vectors, with error measured at
`O(1/n⁴)` — and nothing in it is specific to diffusion, so "does it work" is not
really an empirical question. What is unreplicated is the **payoff**: that
sweeping the length changes which other hyperparameters matter, measured once, on
ImageNet-512, on FID.

The `promote_when` for the theory names the cheapest possible test, and it is
unusually cheap: because reconstruction works retroactively from stored snapshots,
the 2×2 can be run on training runs that already finished. Storage and evaluation,
no training. The record's own practices name three settings where somebody has the
checkpoints.

## What I did not do

No separate practice for magnitude-preserving layers. The architecture is half the
paper and the better half by FID, but the record makes no claim it corrects — it
is a new design, not a fix to a recommendation here — and filing a practice for it
would be filing on novelty rather than on a defect. Carried in the note and left
there.
