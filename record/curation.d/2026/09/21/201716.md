**Back on [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) after the owner's list cleared.** Next by revisits at six, and
the reconnaissance found the usual thing first: Gemini appears in this record
only as a model other papers are applied to — `LIT-383` evidences
`SOTA-233` on Gemini 1.0 Pro and 1.5 Flash — while the report describing
those models was absent. Third time in [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) that a descendant surfaced an
absent trunk.

**The filing decision that mattered was not to file a second practice.** The
HellaSwag contamination measurement is the best number in the report, and the
reflex was to make it a practice. `SOTA-197` already exists — account for
test-set proximity and state your exposure — `Active` and `converged` on two
sources, with a `consensus_note` reading "what almost nobody does is state
their exposure or ship a way to probe it". So the right move was v2 and a
third source, not a sixth document in the evaluation-methodology cluster. A
record that files a new practice every time a new paper says the same thing
fragments into synonyms.

**What the Gemini number adds to it is the thing it lacked: a price.** A
hundred extra fine-tuning steps on web extracts matching the HellaSwag
training set take Ultra to 96.0% at 1-shot against GPT-4's 92.3%. A hundred
steps is a rounding error. So the distance between a clean and a dirty number
is larger than the distance between two frontier models, and cheaper than
either of them. The practice had the argument and no quantity; now it has one.

**The new practice is narrower and sharper than "report your protocol".** The
non-obvious finding is that applying the *same* procedure to both models is
not sufficient. Uncertainty-routed chain-of-thought is worth 6.0 points to
Gemini Ultra and 3.1 to GPT-4, and the sources differ — GPT-4 takes all of its
gain from plain CoT with routing adding nothing, Gemini takes almost none from
plain CoT and nearly all from the routing. A procedure that is a no-op for one
model and a six-point lift for another is not a harness. It is part of one of
the systems being compared.

**And the running count stopped here, deliberately.** Five documents this
session say a reported number was sensitive to a choice the report did not
state. This is the sixth candidate and it does not belong with them, because
the report states everything: both protocols in the main table, the full
three-procedure sweep in the appendix, the contamination price, and a FLEURS
retraining control it volunteered against its own interest. Complete
disclosure, and the abstract still carried the flattering row. If the remedy
for the other five is "say what you did", the remedy here cannot be — which is
how one finds out that two shapes were being counted as one. `DP-010` covers
this; `DP-009` says the count does not extend.

**Recorded against the temptation to be unkind.** The report ran the routing
procedure on GPT-4 via API rather than quoting a published number against its
own best, labels quoted and measured numbers differently in the same row,
dropped LAMBADA after leak analysis, and built Natural2Code from non-web
sources. It is more careful than most of what this record holds. The criticism
is about one sentence in an abstract, and it is only possible to make because
the appendices were honest.
