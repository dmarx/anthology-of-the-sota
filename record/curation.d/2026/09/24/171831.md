Unit 6 of `#326`, and the last of its worklist. Worth writing down for two
reasons: the defect shape is new, and the ranking failed in a way opposite to
unit 5's.

## The shape

`LIT-388` is HumanEval. It is `Active`, 25 documents name it, six practices
use it, and `SOTA-210` sends readers to it for the `pass@k` estimator. Its
note has a section headed **"What it is insensitive to"**, and that section is
good: single functions from docstrings, not architecture; tests certify
behaviour not quality; 164 problems is small.

Every item in that section is **the paper's own account of its limits**. The
hardest limit anybody has measured is not there, because it was measured by
somebody else, three years later, in a paper about something else entirely.

Li et al. hand-checked fifty HumanEval problems their model solved and found
**30% false positives** — programs that pass every test and are not correct —
on 7.77 tests per problem. It is one table in a paper about competitive
programming, and it is the only measurement of HumanEval's test coverage the
record has ever had access to.

## Why nothing catches it

Not a lint question, and not even close to one.

A note's "what it does not cover" section is written from the paper. That is
the right default — the authors know the limits best and a note that
speculates beyond them is worse than one that does not. But it means **a
note's limits section ages at the rate of its own paper, which is zero.**
Everything learned about a benchmark after publication has to be carried in
by a reader who happens to hold both papers at once.

The record has no mechanism for that and probably cannot have one. There is
no field that says "documents that measured this document", the citation
graph runs the wrong way, and a benchmark's critics rarely name it in their
title. What there is, is the habit that produced this: when reading a paper
for one reason, notice what it measured about things the record already
holds.

This is `#326`'s corrected question — *"does this paper measure something the
record relies on?"* — applied one level further in. The unit was scoped
against `SOTA-210`, and the finding was against `LIT-388`, the document
`SOTA-210` cites.

## The ranking failed the opposite way from unit 5

Both units were mis-ranked and for opposite reasons, which is the useful part.

**Unit 5** was ranked fifth of six on the strength of its *subject* — an
unadopted theory nobody depends on — and the finding came from its
**bibliography**. Ranking could not see it because ranking reads subjects.

**Unit 6** was ranked last on a **count**: 0 files say "AlphaCode", against
48 for Chinchilla in unit 1 and 52 for RoPE in unit 3. The count was correct
and measured the wrong noun. Nothing depends on AlphaCode; 25 documents
depend on the benchmark AlphaCode audited. **A paper's standing in the record
and the standing of what it measured are different quantities**, and the
mention count only sees the first.

So `#326`'s ranking was sound on its own terms in both cases and wrong in
both. The corrected rule, if there is one, is narrow: **a mention count ranks
a candidate by its own name, and a paper whose value is auditing something
else scores zero by construction.** That is the same failure `#326` diagnosed
in `#290` — ranking by the name in the contribution — surviving one round of
correction in a different disguise.

Both units were near the bottom of a six-item list. If the list had been
truncated at four, both would have been lost.

## What was left, and why

Three findings in the paper were recorded in its note and deliberately not
filed, each with its reason stated in the document rather than here:

- **Test-time compute allocation** — log-linear scaling in sample budget,
  training compute and sampling compute, with larger models showing higher
  slopes. Real, and one system on one task family in 2022. The record has no
  test-time-compute cluster for it to join, and a single paper should not be
  made to open one alone.
- **A 12.8× controlled comparison of multi-query attention** — against a
  practice pair the record has already retired. History, filed where it can
  be found.
- **Validation loss diverging from the target metric during fine-tuning** —
  which `SOTA-284`'s `promote_when` rules out by name as "the premise, and it
  is already established". Its only news is that the premise is three years
  older than the record's citation for it.

Declining three candidates and filing two corrections is roughly the right
shape for a unit ranked last. The mistake would have been not reading it.
