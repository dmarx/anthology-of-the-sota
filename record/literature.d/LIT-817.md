# LIT-817: Benchmarking Graph Neural Networks

Dwivedi, Joshi, Luu, Laurent, Bengio, Bresson, NTU, Cambridge, LMU, Mila
and NUS (2020, v5 2022) — [ARXIV-2003.00982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00982)

## Key takeaways

- **The protocol** (§2, App. C): the same Adam schedule for all models,
  halving the learning rate on plateau and stopping at 10⁻⁵ or 12 hours.
  Budgets of 100k and 500k parameters, with depth and width set to fit.
  Four seeds per experiment. No per-model hyperparameter search
- **Graph-agnostic MLPs** (App. D) score "consistently low", for example
  ZINC MAE 0.706 against 0.375–0.475 for message-passing GNNs at 100k
  parameters. The superpixel sets are the exception the text does not
  mention: the MLP's 95.3% on MNIST beats vanilla GCN and MoNet, and its
  56.3% on CIFAR10 beats those two and GIN
- **Anisotropic aggregation** (App. D, E.2): GatedGCN and GAT are usually
  ahead of vanilla GCN and GraphSAGE at 100k. On TSP edge classification,
  adding a joint edge gate to an isotropic GatedGCN lifts F1 from 0.646 to
  0.757, and explicit edge states to 0.791
- **Laplacian PE** (App. E.1, Tables 10–13): add the k smallest non-trivial
  eigenvectors of the normalized Laplacian to node features, and flip their
  signs at random during training. GatedGCN, ZINC MAE 0.354 → 0.214, CSL
  10.0 → 99.8%, CYCLES at 200 training graphs 50.0 → 95.1%. Using absolute
  values or a fixed node ordering does worse on most of them
- **WL-GNNs** (App. D): 3WL-GNN and RingGNN, provably more expressive, do
  not beat message-passing GNNs on these datasets, diverge at 8 layers, and
  run out of memory on COLLAB
- **TU datasets** (App. F, Table 16): on ENZYMES, DD and PROTEINS all
  models are within noise, a second seed with the same folds changes the
  ranking, and on DD and PROTEINS an MLP matches the GNNs

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from the graph-coverage pass** (gap list from the record's own
review, 2026-10-22). Its TU-dataset result is a source of [SOTA-350](../practices.d/SOTA-350.md):
holding splits and hyperparameters fixed and changing only the seed
reorders the models. It replicates [LIT-838](LIT-838.md)'s finding that a
graph-agnostic model matches GNNs on DD and PROTEINS.

**What a budget-matched benchmark can show.** Every model gets the same
schedule and parameter count and no per-model tuning, which removes one
source of unfairness and adds another: whichever model suits the shared
schedule is favored. The anisotropy claim has exceptions the authors list,
and GIN degrades from 4 to 16 layers on ZINC (0.387 to 0.526 MAE).

**Bearing on [SOTA-351](../practices.d/SOTA-351.md).** On COLLAB link prediction, the sum-aggregation
GNNs, GIN and MoNet, score 41.7 and 36.1 Hits@50 and lose to matrix
factorization (44.2), while max-aggregating GraphSAGE reaches 51.6. The
authors read it as degree-invariant aggregation suiting that task. That
is a node-pair task with informative features, which [SOTA-351](../practices.d/SOTA-351.md)'s
conditions already exclude.

**Positional encodings** are the subject of other documents in this pass,
starting from [LIT-833](LIT-833.md). This note records only what this paper
measured.
