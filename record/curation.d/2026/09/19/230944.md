`#154`'s item 1 is done and `#180` is mined. The first produced two practices;
the second produced a method finding and nothing else, which is the more
interesting half.

## Item 1 was the cheap half and it paid

`#154` proposed two shapes of work in order: **extract from what is already
read**, then file the missing trunk. The second half was done days ago
(`SOTA-236`, `SOTA-237`). The first was left, and it cost no new reading.

Three notes sat read and unsourced in one neighbourhood. Two carried
recommendations nobody had extracted:

- **`LIT-113`** → `SOTA-tmprcin9`. Prior 3D GANs render small and upsample in
  2D because volume rendering is too slow at training resolution. A 2D
  upsampler is **multi-view inconsistent by construction**. Making the native
  512x512 path affordable removes the defect rather than mitigating it.
- **`LIT-111`** → `SOTA-tmpwnew0`. Separate what the system knows about a
  specific instance from what it has learned in general: reconstruct the
  instance once, match with a network that never saw it or its category.
  Adding an object becomes a scan, not a training run.

`LIT-107` gets a written determination instead. Its claim is real — a backbone
that wins at classification does not necessarily win at depth — but the
action it implies is "run your own sweep", which the paper already says and
this record cannot improve on.

**The pattern worth naming**: `#121` produced `SOTA-205` by re-reading
unsourced notes and asking what instruction each contained. That is the second
time the same query has paid on the same neighbourhood. Read-and-unsourced is
a much better predictor of extractable content than unread-and-unsourced,
because somebody has already done the expensive part.

## The upsampler rule, and what keeps it from being a platitude

"Make the fast path unnecessary rather than fixing it" is the kind of advice
that sounds wise and decides nothing. What makes `SOTA-tmprcin9` usable is
step 3 of its pattern: **the defect must be structural rather than
statistical.**

A 2D upsampler cannot see the neighbouring view. That is a fact about where
the operator sits, and no amount of training changes it. A stage that is
merely worse on average does not qualify — that is an ordinary quality trade
and mitigating it is the right response.

`SOTA-249` is the mirror image and the practice names it: activation
recomputation is a shortcut the record *recommends*, because recomputation has
no correctness defect at all. Same shape, opposite verdict, and the difference
is entirely in step 3.

## The third worklist ordered by the wrong thing

`#180` asks to mine "highly-revisited" entries from `dmarx/papers`. That repo
does not exist; `dmarx/papers-feed` does, with 187 papers and full metadata.

**There is no revisit count.** The only engagement field is
`total_reading_time_seconds`. Its median is **10 seconds**, it is **zero for
63 of 187**, and its maximum is 308s. The three highest are a statistics
textbook, a social-network paper about romantic partnerships, and a
hep-th paper on scattering amplitudes.

So the ordering is undefined for a third of the corpus and dominated by noise
at the top. Sorting by it produces a worklist with no relationship to what
this record can answer.

That is the third time in one session:

| worklist | ordered by | what it correlated with |
|---|---|---|
| `#137` | citations across twelve swept bibliographies | held at the top, nothing at the tail |
| `#163` | salience — a list written from memory | nine of fifteen items have zero mentions |
| `#180` | dwell time in a reading tracker | nothing |

**The criterion that has worked every time is the same one**: *something in
the record has to be answerable from it.* It is one grep, it is cheap, and it
has now overturned the ordering of three separate worklists. The lesson is not
that the worklists were badly made — they were made from what their author
could see. It is that **a record's needs are not visible from outside the
record**, and any list assembled elsewhere has to be re-ranked against it
before it means anything.

## One candidate checked and declined

`ARXIV-1503.03585`, the founding diffusion paper, is in the feed and absent
here. 62 documents mention diffusion and four practices are about it, so this
looked like the NeRF case from an hour earlier.

It is not. **Zero documents mention it**, and `LIT-036` (DDPM) is held and is
what the practices rest on — `SOTA-188` is sourced to EDM, which "derives what
this paper set by hand". NeRF cleared the bar because `SOTA-205`'s own text
pointed at it and could not cite it. An origin paper nobody refers to is a
different thing from a trunk everybody assumes, and only the second one is a
gap.
