Three consecutive units ended by naming `SOTA-430`'s own oldest outstanding
defect: its headline example "leans on a figure both Adebayo and Sixt amended",
and the fix is "to state that verdict from the SSIM curves". Nothing arriving
would fix it, so this unit went to fix it.

## The defect was smaller than three reports of it

Re-reading the practice: **it was already correctly scoped, in all three places
it states the claim.**

| where | what it says |
| --- | --- |
| `summary:` | "survive the first above the lowest layers" |
| verdict table | "**invariant to higher layers**; changes only with the lowest" |
| negative half | "on a network randomized above its lowest layers" |

`LIT-713`'s footnote 5 withdrew *"entirely invariant"*. The practice never said
that. The narrowed claim is the one three groups now measure, and the practice has
stated the narrowed claim since it was filed.

So there was an improvement available — say it with three sources instead of one
phrase, put the bug caveat where a reader of the practice will see it rather than
only in `LIT-713`, and point at the mechanism now that `THEORY-115` supplies one —
but not the correction I had billed. **Each of those three reports was written
from memory of the document rather than from it.**

Same shape as the three paper-content errors this week, sign reversed. I have now
asserted things papers did not say (the precision/recall feature space, twice; who
argued guided backprop's failure) and asserted a flaw a document did not have.
**The failure mode is not optimism or pessimism about the source. It is writing
about a text without the text open**, and it produces errors in whichever
direction the story wants.

## The real defect, which the re-read found by accident

`SOTA-430`'s summary ended: *"The evidence is image classifiers only, and pass or
fail is read from curves."*

True at v1. **False from v2**, filed this morning, which added `LIT-724` to
`source:` and a Condition reading "the verdict does not transfer across modality,
which is now measured rather than suspected" — on a BERT classifier fine-tuned on
SST-2. The summary also still credited one source where the field had named two
since that amendment.

Five further versions followed today. **None of them read the summary.**

**And `summary:` is the field the index renders.** It is the line a reader meets
in `docs/` before they ever open the document, which makes it simultaneously the
most-read line and the one an amendment never touches — because amending a
practice means arguing with its body, and the summary sits above the `---`, in
the part that feels like metadata.

## The sweep, and its rate

Rather than fix one line, I read all twelve documents amended today against their
summaries.

**One stale out of twelve.** The other eleven summaries are recaps of what a
source did, and amendments to those documents narrowed scope or added provenance
in the body without contradicting the recap. `THEORY-113`'s is the closest call —
it names `LIT-713` as the suspicion and `LIT-724` as the measurement, and v4 added
a 2017 antecedent that precedes both — but it claims no priority, so it is
incomplete rather than wrong, and lengthening a summary to fix incompleteness
makes the index worse.

**The rate is the finding**, not the single fix: an amendment leaves the summary
alone about one time in twelve, and when it does the consequence lands in the most
visible place. That is frequent enough to be worth a check and rare enough that
nobody would notice the pattern from one instance.

## What the tool could do, and the issue filed

`luria lint` already has `check_form_text`, which catches a `summary:` that still
says what the `_template.md` says. The gap next to it is cheap: **warn when a
`history:` entry is added and `summary:` is byte-identical.** The document changed
enough to earn a version and its most-read line was not looked at.

That is not a correctness check — a summary can legitimately survive an amendment,
which is what eleven of twelve did — so it is a warning to be acknowledged, in the
same register as `inactive-ok:`. Filed as `LU-#326`, and it is the same shape as
`LU-#325`: take a check the prose asks a human to remember and give it to the
tool. **Note the collision** — this record's own `#326` is the D-variant re-read
cited three times above, and luria's `#326` is this. The `LU-` prefix is the only
thing distinguishing them, which is a reason to keep writing it.

**Generalising past this record.** The fields most likely to go stale are the ones
that are *derived from* the body by a human rather than by the toolchain. `luria`
derives `published:` from a source and `primary_topic:` from `tags[0]`, and those
cannot drift. `summary:`, `consensus_note:` and `promote_when:` are hand-written
restatements of what the body argues, and every one of them has gone stale in this
record this week — the `promote_when` naming an unread paper, the `consensus_note`
saying "has not read it", and now this. **A hand-maintained derived field is a
cache with no invalidation.**

## Where this leaves the cluster

`SOTA-430` is at v7 and has no outstanding defect I can name, which is a different
claim from having none — the last three times I named one it was a version behind
or overstated. Its verdict table, its scope, its consensus and its sourcing are
all now stated from documents the record holds, and every mechanism in it has an
account.

Eleven papers, three accounts, two practices. Three `#290` reversals remain and
are referent gaps.
