**The first cluster of the [#73](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/73) sweep produced three practices, and the useful
finding is that one of them is the same shape as the one filed an hour
earlier for a completely different subject.**

The hyper-connections trunk was found by grepping [ADR-015](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-015.md) for a refusal.
The matrix-preconditioner trunk was found by asking which optimizer notes no
practice sources. Different subjects, different search methods, identical
defect: **the record had filed the branches and not the thing they fork
from.**

## Why trunks go missing, which is not the same reason branches do

A branch gets filed because somebody published it: a paper arrives, it has a
name, it makes a claim, and the claim becomes a practice. A trunk has no
paper. It is what several papers agree on, and agreement is nobody's
contribution — so there is no arrival event to prompt the filing.

That makes trunks a third category alongside the two the previous entry
named. Refusals leave a sentence to grep for. Too-settled techniques leave
silence. **Trunks leave prose in the notes saying the trunk is the
interesting part**, written by whoever noticed, in a document that cannot
carry a practice's status.

Which suggests the query: *where does a note's Standing section make a claim
about what several papers agree on?* Both of today's trunks would have been
caught by it, and neither was caught by the other two methods.

## A smaller thing, twice

Two directives in this batch were written from memory — `SOTA-131` and
`SOTA-132` acknowledged as `Proposed` when both are `Active` now — and the
lint rejected both with "annotation no longer applies". That is three
instances today of writing a status from recall rather than reading it. The
fix is not to try harder: it is that the acknowledgement is cheap to add and
the lint always checks, so the cost of the mistake is one cycle. Worth
noting only because the same failure would be expensive anywhere the guard
did not exist.
