Seventy-four paper notes arrived from another project — decentralized
training, gossip SGD, Langevin dynamics, mean-field theory — in that project's
own schema, carrying 187 entries under a field its `SCHEMA.md` describes as
*"General-purpose, project-agnostic practitioner advice. Preserved for reuse
beyond this project."* Read as an invitation, that is 68 new notes, 68 new
readings and up to 187 new practices.

**The check ran before the filing decision, and it is what set the shape of
the contribution.** That ordering is the whole entry.

## The query

Every identifier in the batch against the source that can resolve it: 60 arXiv
ids through `export.arxiv.org/api/query`, in batches of twenty, comparing the
note's `title` and its author surnames against the API's; the 14 non-arXiv
papers through the CrossRef work-search API by bibliographic title plus first
author, keeping the DOI only where title, authors, year and container all
matched. Then the 74 `arxiv_id` values against the `arxiv:` field of all 243
notes in `record/literature.d/`.

## What it found

Six of the papers were already here. Sixteen of the sixty arXiv ids
disagreed with the API on title or authors. Twelve of those sixteen were
noise — `et al.` standing in for an author list, `Ré` transliterated to `Re`,
`Gürbüzbalaban` to `Gurbuzbalaban`, "Two-Layer" against arXiv's "Two-Layers",
a `Jr` suffix — and two were papers retitled between arXiv versions, where the
note had the v1 title and arXiv now shows the v2 one.

**Three were the wrong paper.**

| identifier | what the note says it is | what the identifier is |
|---|---|---|
| `1805.01361` | Mei, Montanari and Nguyen, *A Mean Field View of the Landscape of Two-Layer Neural Networks* | Keller and Maier, *Machine learning regression on hyperspectral data to estimate multiple water parameters* |
| `1906.08632` | Goldt et al., *the hidden manifold model* | Goldt et al., *Dynamics of stochastic gradient descent for two-layer neural networks in the teacher-student setup* |
| `2102.11742` | *Classifying Data with Local and Global Affinities using Graph Convolutional Networks*, with an author who is not on it | Refinetti et al., *Classifying high-dimensional Gaussian mixtures: Where kernel methods fail and neural networks succeed* |

Three in sixty, on the one field a machine can check. `1805.01361` is the
worst of them and the cheapest to resolve: the paper that note describes is
`1804.06561`, which arrived in the same batch under its own correct note, so
the identifier is a duplicate pointing at a stranger. It is not filed.
`2102.11742` is the strangest — the note's own `contribution` field opens by
saying, in prose, that the paper it means is three others.

One more, from CrossRef: Wolfowitz's *Products of indecomposable, aperiodic,
stochastic matrices* is placed in the *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* and
is Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society 14(5).

## Why that decided the filing

`luria.yaml` already contains the argument, written beside the NOTE statuses
about two documents this record had to repair:

> The reason this vocabulary exists at all: `LIT-052` and `LIT-025` each
> carried a plausible-looking set of takeaways that turned out not to be about
> their paper, and eight practices were built on them.

That is not an analogy to this batch. It is a description of it, at
twenty-five times the size, measured rather than suspected. So the papers are
filed as `LIT` at `Deferred` with metadata from arXiv and CrossRef; the
imported readings are not filed; the recommendations become an issue; and the
foreign agenda field is dropped. `ADR-tmpdwsi3` is the decision.

## What this pass did not cover, and the part that matters

**The check was on identifiers. It says nothing about the notes' claims.** It
compared titles and author surnames. It did not compare a single entry under
`key_results`, `claims`, `assumptions` or `concepts` against the paper those
fields are about — and there is no reason to expect the error rate to be
*lower* in the fields nothing can check than in the one field something can.
Three in sixty is the floor on this batch's error rate, not an estimate of it.
That is the entire case for `Deferred`, and it is why the recommendations are
a backlog: what a paper recommends is exactly the kind of claim this pass had
no purchase on.

**Two papers are now filed under titles nobody will search for.**
`2410.05985` was *Asynchronous stochastic gradient descent with decoupled
backpropagation and layer-wise updates* in v1 and is *LAYUP* now;
`2506.01260` was *Protocol Models* and is *Subspace Networks* now. The record
takes arXiv's current title, which is right and is also the title neither
paper is known by outside arXiv.

**CrossRef confirms a paper exists; it does not confirm the note is about
it.** For the fourteen non-arXiv papers there is no abstract in the record and
no second source. They are the least verified documents in the import and
carry the same `Deferred` as everything else, which under-reports them.

**The 187 recommendations were read for shape, not for correctness.** Enough
to see that a large share name that project's own system and experiments
despite the schema's claim to be project-agnostic, and another share is advice
to a theorist about proof technique rather than to a practitioner about
training. Which of the remainder are true is the open question, and it is one
paper at a time.

**The six papers already held were not re-examined.** `LIT-027` (ZeRO) and the
others may disagree with the import about what their papers establish. Nothing
compared them.

## The ordering

Yesterday's failure in this record was inferring a paper's independence from
affiliation, senior authorship and framing — three proxies all pointing the
same way — and never running the one-line author-list check that would have
contradicted all three. The correction filed then said to check authorship
when filing into an existing line, and to record the answer rather than the
impression.

This pass ran that check first, on seventy-four documents, before deciding
anything about how to file them. It changed the contribution from *sixty-eight
notes, sixty-eight readings and up to a hundred and eighty-seven practices* to
*sixty-eight unread notes and an issue*. The check cost about four minutes.
