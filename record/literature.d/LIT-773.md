# LIT-773: Improving Video Generation with Human Feedback

Jie Liu, Gongye Liu, Jiajun Liang, Ziyang Yuan, Xiaokun Liu, Mingwu Zheng,
Xiele Wu, Qiulin Wang, Menghan Xia, Xintao Wang, Xiaohong Liu, Fei Yang,
Pengfei Wan, Di Zhang, Kun Gai, Yujiu Yang and Wanli Ouyang, CUHK MMLab,
Tsinghua, Kuaishou (Kling), Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI
Laboratory (2025) — [ARXIV-2501.13918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13918). NeurIPS 2025. Read at v2 (27 Oct
2025), appendices included. v1 (23 Jan 2025) was checked for what changed.
v1 lists an eighteenth author, Wenyu Qin, whom v2 drops. v1 calls the
dataset "200k" in its conclusion, has a human evaluation on TA-Hard
prompts (its Fig. 10) that v2 drops, and says plainly that VideoReward "is
vulnerable to reward hacking". The method, the hyperparameters and the
main tables are the same. The project's name for the whole is VideoAlign.

## Key takeaways

- **The preference data is for current video models** (§3.1, Table 1,
  App. G). 16,000 prompts across eight categories, expanded with GPT-4o
  and rewritten in-house, rendered by 12 systems from Gen2 and SVD to Kling
  1.5 and Gen3, 108k videos, 182k pairs. Each pair has a 1–5 score per
  video and a win, tie or loss on each of visual quality (VQ), motion
  quality (MQ) and text alignment (TA). Three trained annotators per
  sample, with a reviewer settling disagreements. 13,000 pairs whose
  prompts never appear in training are held out.
- **Pairs beat scores, and ties need modelling** (§3.2, Figs. 2–3, Table
  6). Trained on the same annotators' labels, a Bradley–Terry model beats
  pointwise regression at every data fraction. A plain BT model gives tied
  pairs large reward gaps. The tie-aware version (BTT, Eq. 1, θ = 5) keeps
  ties near zero. It matches BT without ties and gains with them: overall
  61.50 against 61.22 ties-included, VQ 60.52 against 52.33.
- **Separate reward tokens per dimension** (§3.2). [VQ] and [MQ] tokens sit
  after the video and before the prompt, so they cannot see the text. [TA]
  sits after the prompt. The aim is that a video's visual score does not
  change with the prompt it is paired with.
- **VideoReward leads on the authors' modern-video benchmark** (Table 2).
  On VideoGen-RewardBench, 26.5k pairs the authors had annotated over
  third-party videos from 12 recent systems, overall accuracy is 61.26
  with ties and 73.59 without. The next best, VisionReward, has 56.77 and
  67.59. On GenAI-Bench, short clips from older models, it is 49.41 and
  72.89 against VisionReward's 51.56 and 72.41.
- **Flow-DPO, and why β must not follow the derivation** (§4.2, Eqs. 4–6,
  Lemma B.1, Fig. 6). Under the straight path, the noise-prediction error is
  (1 − t)² times the velocity error. Substituting into Diffusion-DPO's loss
  gives Flow-DPO with β_t = β(1 − t)², full strength at the data end and
  zero at the noise end (t = 1 is noise here). Trained that way, the model
  "overfit[s] the objective at higher noise levels" and reward-hacks.
  Dropping the factor, so β is constant, "leads to more stable training and
  improved alignment across all reward dimensions". The authors cite the
  analogy of DDPM's unweighted loss, and uneven training across timesteps
  (Min-SNR). Flow-RWR drops the same factor (Eq. 9).
- **The multi-dimensional result** (Table 3), aligning to the mean of the
  three VideoReward scores, win rates against the base model:
  - Constant-β Flow-DPO on VideoGen-Eval prompts: VQ 90.95, MQ 81.01,
    TA 68.26. On TA-Hard: 77.46, 71.43, 73.24.
  - The β(1 − t)² variant: VQ 88.44 and MQ 91.23, but TA 28.14 on
    VideoGen-Eval and 38.03 on TA-Hard, below the base model's 50.
  - SFT on the preferred videos and Flow-RWR land between 46 and 79.
  - VBench total is 83.19 for the base model and 83.41 for Flow-DPO.
    VBench quality falls under every method, from 84.37 to 84.19 for
    Flow-DPO.
  - Humans, two annotators and a third to break disagreement, on
    VideoGen-Eval: Flow-DPO preferred 44.0%, base 27.0%, ties 29.0% (Fig.
    5). On VQ alone 31.8 / 21.0 / 47.2, MQ 35.5 / 32.2 / 32.2, TA 29.5 /
    22.2 / 48.2.
- **Aligning to text alignment alone** (Table 4). Constant-β Flow-DPO wins
  69.09 (VBench prompts), 65.49 (VideoGen-Eval) and 84.51 (TA-Hard). The
  β(1 − t)² variant gets 63.67, 55.95 and 71.83. SFT gets 52.88, 53.81 and
  64.79.
- **Reward guidance at inference needs a reward trained on noisy latents**
  (§4.4, Eq. 11, Table 5, App. E). The KL-regularized optimum is the base
  distribution tilted by exp(w·r). For rectified flow that is a shift of the
  velocity by −w·t/(1 − t)·∇r. The reward is a small model built from the
  generator's own early layers and trained on pairs noised identically.
  With MQ-only guidance on TA-Hard, the noise-trained reward wins 74.6 and
  a clean-trained one 38.6, worse than no guidance. Predicting x₀ and
  scoring that gave "unusable videos". Weights on the three dimensions
  trade them off at inference: all on TA gives TA 70.42 and MQ 46.48, all
  on VQ and MQ gives 86.43 and 93.23 with TA 26.65.
- **The settings** (App. I, App. D). The base is an internal 1B-parameter
  DiT with a 3D VAE and T5, trained by flow matching. Alignment uses LoRA,
  rank 64 and alpha 128 on the attention projections, Adam at 5·10⁻⁶, one
  epoch, batch 64, on 16 A800s, with Flow-DPO β = 500. Full fine-tuning
  "can degrade the model's performance or potentially lead to model
  collapse".

## Where the hedges are

Per [DP-010](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-10):

- **The judge is the teacher.** Training pairs are relabelled by
  VideoReward, and every automatic win rate is VideoReward's, "ensuring
  that models optimised on these synthetic labels can be evaluated fairly
  by the same reward function" (§5.2). That makes the win rates a measure
  of how well each method optimizes VideoReward, not of video quality. The
  independent automatic number, VBench, moves by 0.2 points.
- **"Reward hacking" is one reward dimension falling while others rise.**
  For the β(1 − t)² variant, the evidence is VideoReward's own TA score
  dropping below the base while its VQ and MQ climb. No human evaluation
  of that variant, of SFT or of Flow-RWR is reported. The human study
  covers only constant-β Flow-DPO against the base. The labels themselves
  were filtered by the scalarized reward, so a gain in VQ and MQ at TA's
  expense can satisfy the training pairs.
- **The β comparison sweeps different ranges.** Fig. 6 runs constant β at
  100, 500 and 1000 and the β(1 − t)² variant at 2000 to 8000. Since
  β(1 − t)² ≤ β, a larger nominal β for the decaying variant is a
  reasonable match. But no β is run both ways, and the constant-β curve
  falls at 1000.
- **The quality ceiling is admitted.** "Excessive training with Flow-DPO
  led to a significant deterioration in model quality", which LoRA was used
  to prevent (§7). The run length was therefore chosen. No curve shows the
  rise and fall. VideoReward still assigns high scores where "human
  assessments indicate a marked decrease in video quality".
- **One internal model.** All alignment results are on a 1B research model
  that is not released. One run per setting, no intervals. Human
  evaluation is on VideoGen-Eval's prompts, about 400 of them by v1's
  caption.
- **VideoReward's lead is on its home benchmark.** VideoGen-RewardBench was
  annotated by the authors' process over the same era of models as the
  training set (Fig. 10). On GenAI-Bench, VideoReward is scored by its TA
  output alone (App. H.2), and VisionReward is ahead with ties counted.
  Separate tokens, the third design choice, gain on GenAI-Bench and lose
  slightly on VideoGen-RewardBench overall with ties (61.26 against 61.50,
  Table 6), against the text's "further improves performance".
- **Reward guidance is scored by a different reward.** Guidance uses the
  latent reward model and Table 5's win rates are presumably VideoReward's.
  The paper does not say which model scores Table 5.

## Which comparisons are like for like

- **SFT, Flow-RWR and both Flow-DPO variants (Tables 3–4)** share the base
  model, the relabelled data, LoRA and the optimizer settings. Only the
  loss differs. The β ranges for the two Flow-DPO variants differ (Fig. 6).
- **Human evaluation (Fig. 5)** compares Flow-DPO only with the base, same
  seeds.
- **Reward models (Table 2)** are run by the authors with per-model
  averaging rules. LiFT's discrete scores tie often, and ties were broken at
  random for the ties-excluded accuracy. The baselines' training data are
  older and differ in era.
- **Flow-NRG against an unguided baseline (App. E)** is like for like
  within the ablation. It is not compared with the training-time methods.

## Standing in the anthology

It extends Diffusion-DPO ([LIT-774](LIT-774.md)). Flow-DPO is that loss with the
noise error rewritten as a velocity error. One timestep and one noise are
shared across the pair, and the reference is frozen; the pseudocode in
App. C is Diffusion-DPO's with velocities in place of noise. What it adds
is the finding that the timestep weighting the substitution produces,
(1 − t)², is the wrong one for a rectified-flow video model, and that a
constant weight works. It also turns the KL-regularized optimum both DPO
papers start from into an inference-time guidance rule, which Diffusion-DPO
does not have. Its offline setting inherits Diffusion-DPO's assumption
that preference pairs come from the reference model, and breaks it the same
way: the pairs are videos from 12 other systems.

**The record's flow-RL papers use it.** Flow-GRPO ([LIT-779](LIT-779.md)), from an
overlapping group, cites this paper with Diffusion-DPO for the Flow-DPO
and Flow-RWR baselines it trains online and offline on SD3.5-M. Those
baselines improve with training and stay below Flow-GRPO on GenEval.
Flow-GRPO does not say whether its Flow-DPO keeps the (1 − t)² factor or
drops it as recommended here. Step-Video-T2V ([LIT-624](LIT-624.md)) applies
Diffusion-DPO to a 30B video flow model and reports that β = 5000 with
learning rate 10⁻⁸ converges too slowly. This paper's β = 500 at
5·10⁻⁶ with LoRA is the same direction, as [NOTE-338](../notes.d/NOTE-338.md) advises. The two β
values are not on one scale: Diffusion-DPO folds the number of timesteps
into its β, and this loss weights a velocity error.

[SOTA-442](../practices.d/SOTA-442.md) is the online, policy-gradient route for the same kind of
model. This paper is the offline one, with the KL anchored through β
rather than per step. Its limits point the same way that practice's
conditions do: run length decides quality, and the reward model scores
its own success.
