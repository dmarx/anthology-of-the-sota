Before starting on the pending-decisions backlog I wanted to know whether
[#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114) had actually finished the job or only finished its worklist. [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114) read 23
`LIT` notes and found eight of them materially wrong. If the population it
sampled from was much larger, that defect rate is a standing liability rather
than a closed issue.

So: count. The signature [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114) identified is a `## Key takeaways` section whose
bullets are all short noun phrases with no numbers, no code and no
paper-specific terms — the shape a generated summary takes when nobody read
the paper. Across 224 `LIT` documents, **52 still have that signature and no
reading**.

That number looks alarming until you ask the question that matters, which is
not "how many notes are unread" but **"how many recommendations rest on an
unread note"**. Cross-referencing every `source:` edge in the practice
registry: of those 52, **none** is named as the source of any in-force
practice. Zero.

A zero is exactly the shape of result that has burned me in this repository
before — the arXiv audit's 166 false id mismatches were a zero in disguise —
so I ran it again from the other end: start at every `Active`, `Proposed` or
`Deferred` practice, follow its `source:` edges, and ask whether the paper is
unread and generic. Also zero, out of 150 source edges across 78 cited
papers. Two independent traversals agreeing is worth more than one clean
number.

**The conclusion is that [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114)'s worklist was the complete at-risk set, and
not by luck** — it was built by asking which notes source practices, which is
the same question. What remains is 52 papers in the corpus whose summaries
are decorative: nothing cites them, so nothing is wrong because of them.
Reading them would improve the browsing experience and would not correct a
single recommendation.

Worth writing down because the obvious next move after [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114) is "read the
other 52", and the measurement says that is a low-value use of a session.
The high-value population is the one the lint already names every run: the
pending decisions.

The census scripts were throwaway and deliberately so — the finding is a
snapshot, and it will stop being true the moment someone sources a practice
to one of the 52. If that becomes a recurring question it belongs in
`src/scripts/audit/` as a real check, not re-derived by hand.

## The other trap, for whoever promotes a practice next

Promoting `SOTA-120` from `Deferred` to `Active` broke **eight**
`inactive-ok` directives across five files — every place that had cited it
while acknowledging it was not in force. The lint caught all eight and named
them, which is the net working.

But the directives were the easy half. Each one sat above prose that *also*
asserted the status in words — "`SOTA-120` is `Deferred`, which the reading
does not settle" — and no linter can see that. Deleting the directive and
leaving the sentence would have produced a record that passes clean while
saying something false, which is worse than the finding.

So: **when a status changes, grep for the code, not just for the directive.**
The directive tells you which files to open. The prose next to it is what you
actually have to fix.
