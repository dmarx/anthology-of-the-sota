# LIT-104: ReLoRA: High-Rank Training Through Low-Rank Updates
<!-- inactive-ok-file: SOTA-tmppbzba — Proposed, and sourced to this paper: the standing and summary name it as the practice this paper carries, whose promote_when states what the paper did not ablate -->

Lialin et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2307.05695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05695)

## Key takeaways

- Restarts LoRA repeatedly during **pretraining** — merge the adapter,
  reinitialise it, prune 99% of the Adam state, drop the LR to zero and
  re-warm — so a sequence of low-rank updates sums to a high-rank one
  (`rank(A+B) ≤ rank(A)+rank(B)`, usually strict)
- All three parts are required: restarts alone gain nothing, and restarts
  plus an optimizer reset **without** the jagged schedule **diverge**
- 1.3B on 23.1B tokens: full-rank 16.83 ppl, ReLoRA 17.27, LoRA+warm start
  18.23. **5.5 GB/GPU** saved and **9% wall-clock** at that scale
- **The ablation is the finding.** At 130M the full-rank warm start *alone*
  gives 25.46; adding all of ReLoRA's machinery gives 25.04. The machinery
  *without* a warm start gives 29.77 — worse than the warm start by itself.
  The paper's own table says most of the gain is the ordinary training it
  also requires

## Standing in the anthology

Read — NOTE-024, re-verified against the full text (v4) on 2026-09-25. `Active`.

Worth carrying for the ablation as much as the method. It is the cleanest
example in this corpus of a paper whose headline contribution accounts for a
small fraction of its own measured gain — and it is the *authors* who
measured it, which is why the paper stays in the anthology rather than the
attic. Read alongside LIT-156 on why challengers outperform their reported
baselines.

**Sources SOTA-tmppbzba** — the restart discipline, R1 of the reading: at a
merge-and-reinit, reset the optimizer state *and* re-warm the rate, because
the pair without the re-warm diverges and either alone gains nothing. It was
held back by #121's audit until the record decided whether ReLoRA-style
restarts belong in it at all; ADR-042 settled that, and the practice is filed
`Proposed`, conditional on restarting rather than a recommendation to.

What is **not** filed, deliberately: ReLoRA itself, either way. As a
recommendation (R3) it is too weak — restarts beat a warm-started LoRA by
0.27–0.96 perplexity, 9% wall-clock at 1.3B, and nothing in fine-tuning. As a
`Rejected` practice under ADR-042 it does not clear condition 3: Table 6 is a
decomposition of the gain, not a refutation of it, and the restarts win every
comparison the paper runs against warm-started LoRA. The open question —
whether the restart mechanism, rather than the warm start, is what pays — now
belongs to whichever practice someday recommends restarting.
