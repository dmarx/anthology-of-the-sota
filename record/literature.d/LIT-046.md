# LIT-046: LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models

Hu et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)

## Key takeaways

- The hypothesis is about the *update*, not the weight. A pretrained matrix
  is full-rank and needs to be; the delta a downstream task applies to it,
  the paper argues, has low intrinsic rank. So parametrize the delta as `BA`
  with `r` small, initialize `B` at zero so training starts from the
  pretrained model exactly, and freeze `W`.
- On GPT-3 175B: roughly 10,000× fewer trainable parameters and about 3×
  less GPU memory than full fine-tuning. The saving is in optimizer state
  and gradients, which scale with the trainable count — not in the forward
  activations, which do not.
- Quality is on-par with or better than full fine-tuning on RoBERTa,
  DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3, across the paper's task suite.
- The structural property that made it the default rather than one adapter
  among many: `BA` has the shape of `W`, so it can be folded into the weight
  after training. The served model is an ordinary model — no inserted module,
  no extra forward pass, no added latency. Adapter methods that insert layers
  pay at every token forever.
- A corollary the paper notes and the field then leaned on hard: many small
  adapters over one frozen base are cheap to store and cheap to swap, which
  is a different deployment shape from one full fine-tune per task.
