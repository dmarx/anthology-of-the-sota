# DP-003: Keep what you stopped believing — the contrast is most of the content

"Do this" is advice. "Do this, and here is the thing everybody did until 2021
and why it stopped being right" is knowledge. The second is what a reader
comes for, and it only exists if the abandoned position is still written
down.

There is a sharper reason than pedagogy. A document that silently drops what
it used to say cannot be checked. Every version of it looks equally
authoritative, because the evidence of it having been wrong is removed at the
same time as the wrongness. This is the specific way a "living document" goes
bad: not by being wrong, but by becoming unable to be shown to have been.

Retirement therefore has to be an edit to a status, never a deletion — and
the reason has to render where a reader meets the entry, not sit in a field
that only the file's author ever opens.

Applied here: `Rejected` and `Superseded` are statuses on a document that
keeps its body; retired entries appear in the generated index with their
reason in the status column; and a live practice citing a retired paper is a
lint finding rather than something to be noticed. The migration surfaced a
chain nobody could have read from the source data — a paper superseded by one
that was itself later rejected — which is exactly the shape of thing that
only exists if you kept both.

The corollary people skip: keeping the entry obliges you to keep it
*findable*. A retired document that stays in the record but drops out of the
indexes has been deleted in every sense that matters to a reader; it just
still occupies a file.
