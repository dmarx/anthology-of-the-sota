**One paper arrived and three documents came out of it, which is worth
explaining rather than assuming.** Gan and Isola's *Neural Thickets* is two
things bound together: a measurement of the multi-task loss landscape around
a pretrained checkpoint, and a post-training method built to demonstrate it.
The paper is explicit about the ordering — "our goal is not to promote
RandOpt as superior to alternative methods. Rather, we use it as a probe" —
and the record has had exactly the vocabulary for that distinction since
`ADR-031`. The measurement is a claim about what is true, so it is
`THEORY-006`. The method is a claim about what to do, so it is
`SOTA-212`. Filing them as one document would have meant asserting the
method at whatever confidence the measurement earned, or the reverse.

**The most useful thing the paper did for this record it did as a
bystander.** `SOTA-154` — fine-tune with evolution strategies instead of
policy-gradient RL — has rested since September on `LIT-211` alone, whose
`promote_when` asks for an independent group running ES against a
policy-gradient baseline. This paper runs ES as *its own baseline*, on
different models and a different task suite, and finds it strong enough to
beat the paper's own method in about half the table.

That is not the replication the condition asks for and the status is
unchanged, but the difference is worth being precise about, because it is the
kind of thing that gets rounded off later. A baseline result is run by people
who want it to lose. It is evidence about the method's robustness in
somebody else's hands and it is *not* a second group choosing to test the
claim, which is what `promote_when` is asking for. Both facts are now on
`SOTA-154`'s neighbours rather than in anyone's memory.

**A trap that nearly got written into the practice.** The paper's headline
figure compares RandOpt with a 50-way ensemble against PPO and GRPO answering
with a single sample. It says so — "this setting disadvantages the baseline
but reflects current standard usage" — and it runs the matched arm in §5.4
and Table 4, where ES plus the same 50-way vote takes the best or runner-up
cell about half the time and beats RandOpt outright on GSM8K, MATH-500 and
OlyBench at several scales. A first pass at `SOTA-212` had it beating
`SOTA-154`. **It does not.** What it has over `SOTA-154` is one training step
instead of 167 and no inter-worker communication, and that is what the
practice now claims.

The general form of that trap — an ensembled method compared against a
one-sample baseline is a comparison about ensembling — is filed in
`NOTE-078` as candidate recommendation R2 and **deliberately not as a
practice**. The paper demonstrates it and does not recommend it, and the
distance between those is where a record starts attributing its own opinions
to its sources. Whoever files the practice that does recommend it will find
this paper's table waiting as evidence.

**One tagging decision, made against the reflex.** `LIT-233` wants to be
`adaptation-and-tuning`, because post-training is what a reader would look
for it under, and the first draft gave it both that and
`analysis-and-evaluation`. The `primary_topic` group rejected it, correctly:
`ADR-026`'s rule is that a claim merely *discovered* in a domain still takes
its kind, and the paper's kind is a measurement. The practice drawn from it
carries `adaptation-and-tuning`, which is where the instruction lives and
where a practitioner browsing for one will meet it. The reflex to tag the
paper by where its readers are is how a topic stops meaning anything.

**What this pass did not look at.** The candidate list was one paper, so the
complement is everything. Two specific gaps are worth naming because they are
cheap to run and nobody will be prompted to. PPO has no `LIT` note at all,
despite being a baseline in both papers in this line and the thing `SOTA-129`
and `SOTA-145` are about — an instance of `DP-007` exactly, the trunk nobody
filed because nobody published an argument about it. And the record's
evaluation practices were checked for a test-time-budget rule and found not
to have one; they were not checked for anything else this paper bears on.
