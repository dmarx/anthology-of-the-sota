# NOTE-tmpw04b5: Evolution Strategies at the Hyperscale

## Contribution

Removes the reason ES was impractical at LLM scale, which was never memory
alone. Batched matrix multiplications against unstructured random
perturbations have no data reuse, so accelerators run them far below peak.
Giving every worker a rank-`r` perturbation makes the population's forward
pass an arithmetic-intensive operation, and the paper pairs that engineering
move with a convergence theory saying what it costs — which turns out to be
less than the geometry suggests.

## Key insight

**The rank constraint is per-worker, and the update is per-population.** A
single rank-one perturbation is a severe restriction on where a worker can
look. But the ES update is an average over `N` workers, and an average of `N`
rank-one matrices is generically rank `min(N, m, n)`. So the *search* stays
full-dimensional while each *evaluation* becomes cheap. The saving and the
expressiveness live at different levels, and reading the rank constraint as a
restriction on the optimizer is the mistake the paper exists to prevent.

## Assumptions

- **Assumption 1** — the entries of the two factor matrices `A` and `B` are
  i.i.d. from a zero-mean, symmetric, absolutely continuous distribution with
  finite fourth moments and variance `σ²`. Symmetry is load-bearing: it is
  what produces the faster-than-parametric rate.
- The outer product is scaled by `1/√r` so that the variance of the
  perturbation stays `O(σ²)` as `r` grows, rather than summing `r` terms.
- **Assumption 2** — the fitness `f` is bounded.
- A technical device rather than an assumption: `AB^T` lives on a
  measure-zero manifold of rank-`r` matrices and has no density with respect
  to Lebesgue measure, so the analysis studies `AB^T + δG` with a small
  independent Gaussian `G`. That variable has a density for every `δ > 0`, and
  `δ` can be taken arbitrarily small, so the analysed update differs
  negligibly from the implemented one.
- The LLM experiments assume a recurrent (RWKV-7) architecture, where memory
  not spent on a KV cache funds population members. This is a property of the
  pairing, not of the method.

## Key results

- **Theorem 1 (low-rank ES gradient).** Under Assumption 1 and for finite `r`,
  the low-rank objective's gradient has a score-function form, with the
  perturbation generated by sampling the factors and taking the scaled outer
  product. *Holds when:* the factor entries satisfy Assumption 1.
- **Theorem 2 (consistency with full-rank ES).** Under Assumptions 1 and 2,
  the Frobenius-norm error between the low-rank update's Gaussian score
  approximator and the true full-rank Gaussian ES gradient vanishes as
  dimension grows, **at a rate faster than the `O(1/√r)` a general parametric
  CLT would give.** The improvement comes from the symmetry in Assumption 1,
  via an Edgeworth expansion in which the first correction term cancels.
  *Holds when:* the fitness is bounded and the parameter dimension is large —
  it is a high-dimensional result, and the paper describes the mechanism as a
  *linearising effect*.
- **Throughput.** Auxiliary storage per layer falls from `O(mn)` to
  `O(r(m+n))`; the per-member forward cost falls correspondingly. A
  hundredfold increase in training speed for billion-parameter models at large
  population sizes, reaching **up to 91% of pure batch-inference throughput**.
- **LLM post-training.** RWKV-7 1.5B on Countdown: 35% validation accuracy
  against GRPO's 23% at the same hardware and wall clock, with 1024 parallel
  generations per GPU against GRPO's 32. RWKV-7 7B on GSM8K across 8 GPUs:
  EGGROLL ahead, with 8096 parallel generations against 256.
- **A harder Countdown comparison.** RWKV-7g7B reaches 72.9% against 52.8%
  reported for GRPO and 66.8% for OpenES — from a weaker base model than the
  Qwen2.5-7B those figures come from, which makes it striking and not
  like-for-like.
- **Integer-datatype pretraining.** Stable pretraining of nonlinear recurrent
  language models operating purely in integer datatypes — something
  backpropagation cannot do, and the strongest standalone argument in the
  paper for gradient-free optimization existing at all.
- **Tabula rasa RL.** No performance loss against full-rank ES despite being
  much faster.
- **A negative result.** Large population sizes are necessary for pretraining
  performance, which the authors read as evidence that zeroth-order methods
  are unsuitable for pretraining in general.

## Method

Each worker holds `A ∈ R^{m×r}` and `B ∈ R^{n×r}` drawn i.i.d., and perturbs
a weight matrix by `(1/√r)·AB^T`. Workers are reconstructed from seeds rather
than shipped, so the population shares base weights and the per-member
forward passes batch. Fitnesses are centered per prompt, averaged over
prompts, and standardized by their standard deviation; the reported
implementation evaluates antithetic pairs `+E` and `−E` for each direction.

## Concepts

- **Arithmetic intensity** — FLOPs per byte moved into fast memory. The
  quantity this paper optimizes, and the reason a memory-efficient method can
  still be slow.
- **Low-rank perturbation** — `(1/√r)AB^T`, replacing a dense Gaussian `E` of
  the same covariance.
- **Linearising effect** — the paper's name for the high-dimensional regime in
  which the low-rank and full-rank fields agree.

## Connections

The direct predecessor is Qiu et al.'s full-parameter ES at billion scale, and
the paper's criticism of it is specific: that line uses populations on the
order of a hundred unique perturbations per update and collects hundreds of
rollouts per perturbation to keep GPUs busy, whereas here every generation
uses a different perturbation and the population per update is bounded by the
inference batch size instead. It also distinguishes itself from the ES-over-
LoRA and SVD-basis work by searching the full parameter space. Machine-
readable lineage is on the LIT.

## Recommendations

- **R1** — If you are running ES on a model large enough for the forward pass
  to be matmul-bound, structure the perturbations as low-rank products.
  *Topic:* post-training. *Status:* experimental. *Strength:* strong.
  *Applies when:* billion-parameter models and large populations; the gain
  comes from arithmetic intensity, so it is smallest where the population is
  small.
- **R2** — Size the population by the inference batch size rather than by
  what the perturbation memory allows, once perturbations are cheap to
  generate. *Topic:* post-training. *Status:* experimental. *Strength:*
  moderate.
- **R3** — Do not expect zeroth-order methods to pretrain a large model.
  *Topic:* training. *Status:* experimental. *Strength:* weak — the paper
  infers rather than delimits this.

## Limitations

The authors state the LLM evidence is on RWKV-7 and frame the architecture's
suitability as an advantage rather than a caveat; this reading treats it as
both. Two further limits from this reading: the analysis is asymptotic in
parameter dimension and says nothing about the finite-rank field at a fixed
radius, which is the gap [LIT-tmpcjyw1](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcjyw1.md) walks into; and the technical device
of adding `δG` to obtain a density is clean but means the object analysed is
not literally the object implemented.

## Open questions

- **Does the throughput advantage survive on transformers?** The KV-cache
  argument is why RWKV is favourable. What would close it: the same Countdown
  and GSM8K comparison on a transformer of matched capability.
- **Where is the population-size floor for pretraining?** The paper asserts
  unsuitability from needing large populations. What would close it: a
  measured curve of pretraining loss against population size.
- **Is rank a useful knob at all?** Unaddressed here and answered elsewhere —
  [LIT-tmpcjyw1](../literature.d/LIT-tmpcjyw1.md) finds no reproducible reward advantage for rank eight over
  rank one.
