The session that filed those notes could not reach arxiv.org, huggingface.co
or the model cards — every request returned an egress 403 — so five claims
were written from secondary coverage and labelled as such. This session can
reach all of them. Full HTML for all three arXiv reports, and the Hub for the
Qwen configs.

**All five hold.** That is worth saying plainly, because the interesting
outcome of a verification pass is usually a correction and this one has
almost none. The record was accurate about what it had, and honest about
where it got it.

What changed is the standing of the claims, and in two places their
precision:

- The Qwen3.8-vs-3.6 configuration claim was the weakest of the five, resting
  on somebody else's comparison of two files. Fetching both and diffing them
  makes it the strongest: they differ in exactly one key,
  `transformers_version`, which records the library that serialised the file.
  Every architectural field — `model_type`, all 64 entries of `layer_types`,
  the head counts on both attention paths, `attn_output_gate`, the vision
  block — is identical. "Same configuration" understated it.
- DeepSeek-V4's refusal of QK-Clip was the claim most likely to have been
  garbled in transmission, because it inverts a practice this record holds.
  It is not garbled: the report has a heading for it and one sentence that
  says exactly what the note said it did.

## The one thing that was wrong was a guess about what a paper contains

[LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md)'s *Standing* said the K3 report's "ablation tables are still
unmined". Nobody had looked. The report has five tables — an architectural
comparison with K2, two benchmark tables, an in-house web-development
comparison, and third-party evaluations — and **not one component ablation**.

That matters more than the sentence did. The headline claim is roughly 2.5×
the scaling efficiency of K2, read off fitted scaling-law curves on held-out
out-of-distribution data, and the report credits it to the architectural,
data and training changes *collectively*. There is no number in it that
apportions the gain to Attention Residuals, or the KDA/MLA hybrid, or the
LatentMoE. A future reader tempted to cite K3 as evidence for any one of
those components has to know that, and now the note says it.

The general lesson: "the ablations are unmined" and "there are no ablations"
look identical from outside a paper, and only one of them is a promise the
record can keep.

## A finding that is not this issue's

Reading K3 §3.2 turned up a controlled comparison the record needs to answer
for. The team argues that comparing warmup-stable-decay against cosine under
one hyperparameter set is unfair — the two schedules' optimal peak learning
rates and batch sizes differ substantially even at matched size and budget,
so a shared setting silently favours whichever it suits. They run an
independent scaling-law search per schedule, and under each schedule's own
optimum cosine decay consistently reaches the lower final loss. K3 adopts
cosine.

[SOTA-140](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-140.md) says to use WSD, is Active, and its summary claims WSD is "the
schedule of every 2025–2026 report in the record". A July 2026 frontier
report that ran the fairer comparison and went the other way is exactly what
[ADR-016](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-016.md)'s `contested_by` was added for. The finding is in [LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md) here,
where it belongs; what it costs the practice is a judgement about the state
of the field rather than a fact about a paper, so it is filed on its own.
