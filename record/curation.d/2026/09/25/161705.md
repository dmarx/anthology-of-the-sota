`SOTA-407` was filed this afternoon with a consensus note that named its own
missing piece: the record could not point to an adopter of weight-space
ensembling "outside the authors' own line of work — model soups, which this
record does not yet hold". So this unit filed model soups. It does not fix what
the note wanted fixed, and saying so is the entire point of the unit.

## The dependency resolves to nothing

Mitchell Wortsman is first author of both `LIT-674` and `LIT-tmpay0h1`. The
second paper extends the first: WiSE-FT interpolates two endpoints, model soups
averages many, and the soup paper's whole premise is the single-basin observation
WiSE-FT reported.

`DP-005` says adoption is not evidence, and a group extending its own method is
the cleanest instance of that rule there is. Two papers, one line of work, one
opinion. The consensus stays `emerging`.

What changed is worth having anyway: the note now says *why* the dependency does
not count, rather than naming a paper nobody had read. **A consensus note that
names its own gap is only useful if somebody eventually closes the loop, and
closing it with "still no" is a real outcome.** The alternative — leaving the
sentence in place and quietly hoping — is how `SOTA-373` sat for two days
asserting something false about this record's contents.

This is the fourth unit in two days whose subject was set by a previous unit's own
prose. The mechanism is reliable enough now to state: **write down what you could
not check, precisely enough that the next reading either satisfies it or refutes
it.** Three of the four came back satisfied. This one came back refuted, which is
the more informative result.

## The boundary was a dichotomy and is now a spectrum

The better finding is structural, and it corrects something this record asserted
three hours ago.

`SOTA-217` says to align hidden-unit permutations before averaging weights from
separately trained networks. This afternoon it gained a scope section saying when
the alignment is unnecessary: when the weight vectors share an optimization
trajectory. That was stated as a clean test — *did one weight vector come from
the other* — and model soups shows the test is not clean.

Every ingredient in a soup was fine-tuned from the same pretrained checkpoint.
They all share a trajectory in the relevant sense. And the paper's central method
is **greedy** precisely because uniform averaging fails: some ingredients have to
be rejected, to "avoid adding in models which may lie in a different basin of the
error landscape", which "could happen if, for example, models are fine-tuned with
high learning rates".

So a shared initialization makes averaging *usually* safe. A per-ingredient
held-out check makes it *reliably* safe. Permutation alignment is what is left for
networks that share nothing at all. Three positions on one axis — how much of the
optimization history two weight vectors have in common — and the record now holds
a practice at each. The dichotomy stated this afternoon was a simplification, and
it lasted three hours.

Worth noticing about the sequence rather than the content: the correction came
from filing the *next* paper in the same lineage, not from re-reading anything.
A claim that survives one paper and breaks on its immediate descendant was
under-tested rather than wrong, and the cheapest test available was to keep
reading down the line.

## Two numbers the same sentence reports

> A model soup composed of ViT-G models achieves 90.94% on ImageNet, surpassing
> the previous state of the art of 90.88% attained by the CoAtNet model while
> requiring 25% fewer FLOPs at inference time.

**0.06 pp** and **25%**. The first is the claim the paper is remembered for and
the second is the one that would change a decision. They are in the same
sentence, and the record's job is to keep them in the order that survives —
which is why the note leads on the FLOPs and treats the leaderboard position as
provenance.

The same paper's honest reporting is what makes this readable at all: the
`+0.0, +0.7, +0.0, +0.5` GLUE row is in the paper, under a heading that calls the
experiments preliminary, and the sentence "the improvements are not as pronounced
as in image classification" is theirs. An abstract that says the method "extends
to multiple image classification and natural language processing tasks" and a
table where half the language tasks gain exactly zero are both true; only one of
them travels.
