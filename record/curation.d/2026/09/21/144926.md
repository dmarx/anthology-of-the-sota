NoProp's headline is that a backprop-free method finally matches
backpropagation. On MNIST and CIFAR-10 it does — 99.54 against 99.46, 80.54
against 79.92, with three seeds and five inference runs behind each number.

The train column of the same table says something else.

| | CIFAR-10 train | CIFAR-100 train |
|---|---|---|
| Backprop | 99.98 | 98.63–99.19 |
| NoProp-DT | 95.02–97.23 | 83.25–90.70 |

Matching on test while eight to fifteen points behind on train is not
optimization parity. It is a method that fits the training data substantially
worse and makes it up in generalization — which, on three small image
benchmarks with no augmentation, is free. Backprop is at ceiling on train
there; the test number is decided entirely by what happens after that, and
NoProp's shortfall costs nothing because there was nothing left to gain.

Change the setting to one where fitting the training data is the binding
constraint, and that free lunch is the whole bill. Which is essentially every
setting this record's practices are argued in.

The paper does not mention it. Not in the results discussion, not in the
conclusion — there is no limitations section — and not as a caveat on the
parity claim. The numbers are printed, in the table, next to the ones that are
discussed.

## Why this is the interesting kind of gap

It is not an error and it is not concealment. The table is complete and the
claim made about it ("comparable to or better than back-propagation") is about
test accuracy and is true of test accuracy.

It is that the *decision-relevant* reading of the table is a different column
from the one the claim is about, and nothing in the paper points at it. A
reader who takes the parity claim at face value has taken something true and
will be surprised later for a reason the paper contained.

That is the fourth instance this week of the shape `DP-010` was filed for,
and the first where the unsupported part is not a sentence at all — it is an
absence. The other three had a hedge that did not travel; this one has a
column that was never read aloud.

Worth noting for the principle's next revision: "find the hedge" is the wrong
instruction when there is no hedge. The more general instruction is **read the
table the paper did not narrate**.

## What the practice says instead

`SOTA-tmp8b9jt` is titled as an answer to "which backprop-free method",
because that is the question the evidence settles, and it settles it
decisively — CIFAR-10 test accuracy of about 80 against 69.32 for local greedy
forward gradients and 50.71 for difference target propagation is not a
contest.

What it is not is an answer to "should I stop backpropagating", and the
conditions say so at length: the train gap, the structure-matched baseline
(fair, and matched to a network designed around this method's constraint), the
fact that two of three variants are weak and one scores 6.38 ± 4.9 on
CIFAR-100, and three small image benchmarks with no augmentation.

## A gap the record had not noticed

Before this, the anthology's only answer to "what if not backpropagation" was
`SOTA-154` — evolution strategies. That is a different family: black-box,
perturbation-based, estimating a gradient it never computes. The local-learning
family — forward-forward, target propagation, forward gradients — has been
absent entirely, and all three appear here only as baselines.

So this contribution opens a niche rather than filling one. The three
baselines are now named in the record without being read, which is the
honest state and is also a worklist.
