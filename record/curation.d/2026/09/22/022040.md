**The owner pushed back on one of the two declines** from the previous unit:
*The emergence of clusters in self-attention dynamics* has been cited ~200
times in three years, and the `t → ∞` limit is not a weakness in the
relevance of its contribution to the theoretical literature. That is right,
and reading the paper in full rather than skimming its abstract and table of
contents showed my decline was wrong on more counts than the one raised.

**The stated reason had two halves and both failed.**

The second half — "it underwrites no practice the record holds" — is wrong
against this project's own schema, which I should have known and did not
check. `explains` is optional on a `THEORY` for exactly this case, and the
comment in `luria.yaml` says why in as many words: the two lottery-ticket
documents explain no practice because the record holds no pruning
recommendation for them to explain, and "a finding that underwrites nothing
YET is still a finding, and requiring this would mean filing it nowhere —
which is the state this scheme exists to end." I used as a disqualifier the
precise situation the scheme was built to accommodate.

The first half — "the gap to a trained model is too wide" — survives as a
**condition** and not as a reason to refuse. The record holds `THEORY-009`,
a mean-field gradient flow on an infinite-width limit, at `Active`. An
idealization is something this record files with its assumptions written down.

**And the paper closes part of that gap itself, which a skim could not
see.** Time-independent weights are not only analytic convenience: ALBERT
shares parameters across layers, which is the architecture the assumption
describes literally. The discrete-time forward-Euler analogue is written out
and the proofs are stated to carry through, so "continuous time" is not a
limitation either. Clustering is reported to arrive "after few layers". And
Section 11.2 checks the hypotheses on ALBERT-xlarge-v2's learned weights,
finding heads 5 and 14 satisfy both conditions of Definition 4.1 — while
saying plainly that not all sixteen do.

**The connections I missed are the ones that should have made it obvious.**
The related-work section names Linformer and LoRA as motivated by the
near-low-rank structure of the attention matrix, and observes that in both the
low-rank structure is *imposed rather than extracted from `P` itself* —
which Theorem 2.1 does extract. `SOTA-184` and `SOTA-147` stand on that
premise and the record had no source for it. Separately, the paper refines
Dong et al.'s rank collapse: trivialization into a single cluster is what
happens *without* skip connections, and with them a classification emerges.
`NOTE-105` already uses rank collapse as an established fact, and the record
holds neither Dong et al. nor this.

**`DP-004`, for the third time in this batch, and the third time on my own
work.** The query I ran over these papers was "does this plot a weight
spectrum?" It found the papers that do. It could not see a paper whose
contribution is a classification of what the spectrum of the value matrix does
to representations — nor the two citations in its related-work section that
tie it to practices the record already holds.

**One thing worth separating, because the argument for filing should be the
right argument.** Citation count is adoption, and `DP-005` says adoption is
not evidence: 153 citations on Semantic Scholar does not make the theorems
truer. What the count is good evidence for is that this is a **trunk** in its
literature, and the record files trunks — that is why `LIT-505` was filed
unread. So the count is a reason to look, and the reasons to file are the
ones above.

**Filed `Proposed`, and the reason is not the idealization.** It is one
group, and the bridge to practice is a check on ALBERT's *weights* rather than
on where its *tokens* go. The promotion condition asks for exactly that
missing measurement — token trajectories through a trained model, cluster
counts against each head's value-matrix spectrum, with the heads violating the
condition as the control — and explicitly refuses a wider idealization in its
place. It needs no training run.

**The declines from the previous unit stand as a pair now split.** This one is
reversed. `SpectralFT` is not: three multimodal-sentiment benchmarks against
an incumbent the record already holds is narrow evidence for a recommendation
this record would have to state broadly, and no comparable argument has been
made for it. If one is, it should get the same treatment this did.
