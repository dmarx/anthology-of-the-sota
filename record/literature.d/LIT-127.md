# LIT-127: DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models

Shao et al. (2024) — [ARXIV-2402.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)

## Key takeaways

- DeepSeekMath 7B continues pretraining DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B on 120B
  math-related tokens selected from Common Crawl by an engineered pipeline,
  mixed with natural language and code, and reaches 51.7% on
  competition-level MATH without external tools or voting.
- Group Relative Policy Optimization: a PPO variant that drops the value
  model and estimates the baseline from the scores of a group of outputs
  sampled for the same prompt, which cuts the memory footprint of RL and
  improves mathematical reasoning.
- A unified view of the RL-style methods — rejection-sampling fine-tuning,
  DPO, PPO, GRPO — as variants of one paradigm, used to reason about what
  each is actually optimising.

## Standing in the anthology

Filed for GRPO, which [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) runs on its 0.6B reasoning model and finds
sensitive above all to the learning rate. The data-selection half of the
<!-- inactive-ok: SOTA-124 — a Proposed practice, named as the generalisation on purpose -->
paper is the same idea [SOTA-124](../practices.d/SOTA-124.md) generalises: mine and repeat the
high-quality slice.

GRPO is PPO ([LIT-241](LIT-241.md)) with one term taken out. It keeps the clipped
probability-ratio surrogate and drops the learned value function, taking the
baseline from the scores of a group of outputs sampled for the same prompt.
Dropping the critic is what cuts RL's memory footprint.

It has become the baseline the evolution-strategies line measures itself
against:

- **[LIT-211](LIT-211.md)** ran full-parameter ES against PPO and GRPO with per-experiment
  RL tuning. On Countdown, averaged across models, ES improved over the base
  by 36.4% against 21.3% for GRPO at group size 8.
- **[LIT-237](LIT-237.md)** tried to reproduce that ordering on GSM8K, MATH and
  OlympiadBench at 1–1.5B, and found GRPO still ahead in three of four
  settings. Its GRPO updates were about 95% sparse, against dense ES updates
  with roughly 1000× the drift.
- **[LIT-230](LIT-230.md)** found that GRPO wins average pass@1 but falls below its own base
  model on both pass@16 and pass@32 in 15 of 18 comparisons, while ES raises
  all three.
- **[LIT-235](LIT-235.md)** ran both on Qwen3-4B. ES at 300 iterations had the higher peak
  accuracy on four tasks (Chemistry 76.5% against GRPO's 74.9%), and the two
  solutions are linearly connected despite near-orthogonal update directions.
- **[LIT-233](LIT-233.md)**'s one-step random-perturbation ensemble matched or beat GRPO at
  equal training FLOPs: 87.1% against 83.2% on GSM8K with
  Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, though its answers are majority votes over fifty
  models.
- **[LIT-239](LIT-239.md)** (ESSA) passed 0.80 on GSM8K with Qwen2.5-Math-7B in under 25
  minutes against GRPO's 50 or more, from 100 examples against GRPO's 5,978.
  The two arms also ran on different runtimes.
- **[LIT-240](LIT-240.md)** (ESSAM) tied GRPO on GSM8K across four Qwen2.5 sizes (78.27%
  against 78.34%) at inference-level memory, reported as a 10–18× reduction.
