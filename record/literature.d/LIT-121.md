# LIT-121: Learnable Multipliers: Freeing the Scale of Language Model Matrix Layers

Velikanov et al. (2026) — [ARXIV-2601.04890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04890)

## Key takeaways

- Under weight decay, stochastic gradient noise inflates a weight matrix in
  a Brownian-like way and the decay pulls it back, so the layer settles at a
  norm determined by the learning rate and the decay coefficient. The paper
  treats that equilibrium norm as an artefact of the procedure rather than
  a property of the solution, and shows it is suboptimal.
- The remedy is to give the scale its own parameter: a learnable scalar
  multiplier on each weight matrix, and then learnable per-row and
  per-column multipliers so that individual row and column norms are learned
  as well.
- Freeing the scale yields consistent gains, generalises Maximal Update
  Parametrization ([LIT-148](LIT-148.md)) — the forward multipliers µP fixes by hand become
  learned — and reduces the cost of tuning multipliers by hand.
- The size of the gain: on a 0.5B model trained for 200 GT, against a
  well-tuned Muon baseline, the average over seven benchmarks moves 31.88 to
  32.98, +1.10 points. It is not spread evenly — BBH +3.39 and GSM8K +2.61
  carry most of it, HellaSwag, ARC-C and MMLU-Pro move by fractions of a
  point, and MMLU is flat at 47.98 against 47.96. The Adam gain is the same
  size, +1.21, which is the paper's own framing: the improvement from adding
  learnable multipliers matches the improvement from switching Adam to Muon.
