Unit 2 of `#342`. The defect is the cleanest instance yet of the shape `#290`
named and this record keeps rediscovering: **the floor, not the frontier.**

## The measurement

106 documents in this record name the softmax. 23 practices name it. Before
this unit, **no `LIT` note in the record had softmax in its title**, and
nothing held a claim about the operator itself.

That is not an oversight anyone made. The softmax is not a technique somebody
proposed and others adopted — it is a piece of arithmetic that arrived with
the architecture and has never been the subject of a recommendation. `DP-007`
covers exactly this: what everyone agrees on has no arrival event, so nothing
in the record's usual machinery goes looking for it.

The difference here is that there *is* something to say about it, and it is
not an endorsement. It is a limit.

## Why the gap survives every check

The record's checks all run along declared edges. A practice that names the
softmax in its prose declares no relation to it, because there is nothing to
declare a relation *to*. There is no unbound relation, no retired citation, no
missing `source:`. The component is load-bearing in 23 recommendations and
structurally invisible in all of them.

**A trunk with no document is not a broken edge, it is an absent node**, and
absent nodes do not appear in a report about edges. The only instrument that
finds them is the one `#290` stumbled into: read a catalogue's bibliography
and ask which of these the record leans on without holding.

## What made this one worth the unit

Not the count. The count would have justified a note saying what a softmax is,
which would have been worthless.

What justified it is that the paper **proves something the record's existing
documents are silently assuming the opposite of**. Three documents here are
about attention sharpness — `THEORY-061` bounds entropy below as weights grow,
`THEORY-062` names spectral concentration as a crash predictor, `SOTA-192`
bounds the logits to stop entropy collapsing — and all three treat sharpness
as the dangerous direction. This paper shows sharpness is also the *fragile*
direction: bounded logits plus growing `n` force every coefficient towards
zero, so a head that looks like a robust circuit in-distribution is a head
whose input size has not grown yet.

The two bounds meet on `SOTA-192`. Its Proposition 3.1 names the intervention
in so many words — normalisation before the query-key mechanism clamps the
activation norms — and the `δ` that practice shrinks is the `δ` in the
dispersion cap.

**That is a real interaction and it is not a finding**, which is the line the
unit had to hold. Nobody has measured whether QK-normalised models disperse
sooner. It is written in `SOTA-192` as a consequence of two inequalities with
the arithmetic shown, explicitly not as a cost anyone has paid, and the
recommendation is untouched.

## The candidate that was declined, and why declining was the work

Adaptive temperature is the paper's own mitigation and it is tempting: a
drop-in softmax replacement, streamable, compatible with FlashAttention, with
`p < 0.05` gains from 64 items up.

Then look at the axis. Baseline max retrieval: **98.6% → 12.4%**. With
adaptive temperature: **98.6% → 14.0%**.

A `Proposed` practice recommending this would be technically accurate and
would tell a reader something false about its importance — that there is a
known mitigation for dispersion. There is not. The authors say so
(*"ad-hoc"*, *"warrants further investigation"*), and the honest filing is to
record the technique in the note with the number that decides it, plus what
would change the verdict.

**Filing the strongest thing in a paper is not the same as filing the thing
that is true**, and a 1.6-point patch on an 86-point collapse is the clearest
case of that distinction I have hit in this line of work.

## A gap this opens

The theorem's escapes are three families the record holds **nothing** on:
zero documents name the Differential Transformer, selective attention, sigmoid
attention or stick-breaking attention.

Worth noticing what that does to an existing line. The record's
linear-attention cluster (`#161`) is filed as an efficiency story — fewer
FLOPs, less memory. This paper is an argument for the same family on
**expressivity** grounds: unnormalised attention does not disperse. Same
techniques, entirely different reason to want them, and the record currently
holds only the first reason.

That is a candidate worth ranking, and it is not in `#290`'s queue, so `#342`
has not ranked it either.
