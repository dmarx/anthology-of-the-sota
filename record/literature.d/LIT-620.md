# LIT-620: HunyuanVideo: A Systematic Framework For Large Video Generative Models

Kong et al., Tencent Hunyuan Foundation Model Team (2024) — [ARXIV-2412.03603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03603)

## Key takeaways

- **The line's only video scaling law.** Chinchilla-style envelopes
  ([LIT-068](LIT-068.md)) are fit as N_opt = a·C^b and D_opt = a·C^b, first for text-to-image
  and then for video. The image fit favours parameters (0.563 against 0.433,
  §4.4.1). The video fit, initialized from the image-optimal checkpoints,
  favours data (0.362 against 0.629, §4.4.2). The image proxy family is
  "DiT-T2X" at seven sizes from 92M to 6.6B parameters. The video fit uses
  five sizes, B2 to G2 (Fig. 10d). The token amounts are "only related to
  the first stage of training for images and videos respectively" (§4.4.2).
  The 13B size was chosen from the fits "taking into account the training
  consumption and inference cost", not read off them. No other report in
  the line measures it.
- **Architecture.** 20 dual-stream blocks feed 40 single-stream blocks with
  full attention and 3D RoPE. Text comes from a decoder-only multimodal LLM
  with a bidirectional token refiner, and pooled CLIP-L is added to the
  timestep embedding. Training follows SD3 ([LIT-449](LIT-449.md)): flow matching, with
  t drawn from SD3's logit-normal density (§4.5.1). The dual-to-single
  stream blocks are credited to FLUX, not to SD3's MM-DiT directly.
- **The VAE.** 4×8×8 into 16 channels, causal, trained from scratch on 4:1
  video to image data. It reaches PSNR 35.39 on MCL-JCV against
  CogVideoX-1.5's 33.22 (Table 1). Fine-tuning with tiling randomly on or
  off removes tiling seams. Its causal 3D convolution is MAGVIT-v2's
  ([LIT-623](LIT-623.md)), adopted by name "to handle both videos and images": T+1 frames
  become T/4+1 latent frames, so an image is a one-frame video. Where
  MAGVIT-v2 quantizes with LFQ, HunyuanVideo keeps a continuous latent
  trained with a KL term.
- **Few-step sampling.** A timestep shift s·t/(1+(s−1)t) is used, with s
  raised from 7 at 50 steps to 17 below 20 steps. It is compared against
  Movie Gen's ([LIT-626](LIT-626.md)) linear-quadratic schedule at 10 steps, qualitatively
  only (Fig. 11b): two sample videos, from which the authors read the
  larger shift as the better choice. CFG distillation gives about 1.9× speedup.
- **Evaluation.** 1,533 prompts, each generated once, "avoiding any
  cherry-picking" (§6.1), were rated by 60 professional raters. How many
  ratings each video received is not stated. Overall is 41.3% against 37.7%
  for the best anonymized competitor, and motion quality is the best at 66.5%
  (Table 3).

## Where the hedges are

Per [DP-010](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-10):

- **"Reduce computational resource requirements by up to 5×"** (intro) is
  supported by no experiment in the paper.
- **The scaling law is fit on a different model.** The proxy trains with
  DDPM v-prediction, T5-XXL and cross-attention. The shipped model uses flow
  matching, a multimodal LLM and dual/single streams (§4.4.1). That the fit
  transfers is assumed.
- **No design choice is ablated.** Full attention, the dual-to-single stream,
  the multimodal-LLM encoder and the token refiner are all adopted or
  justified by citation. Data filters were checked by "simple experiments
  using a smaller HunyuanVideo model" whose results are not shown.
- **The headline metric is undefined.** Table 3's "Overall" is not defined.
  On visual quality the model ranks fourth of six (95.7% against 97.7%).

## Standing in the anthology

HunyuanVideo was the open baseline Wan measured itself against, in both its
VAE comparison and its human evaluation. On Wan's own benchmark ([LIT-619](LIT-619.md),
Table 2) the open HunyuanVideo scores 0.673 against Wan 14B's 0.724 and Wan
1.3B's 0.689, and on VBench 83.24 against 86.22 (Table 4). Wan's VAE
reconstructs 2.5× faster than this one on the same hardware, with PSNR
shown only as a plot (Fig. 7). The two reports disagree most
clearly on the text encoder. HunyuanVideo replaces T5 with a multimodal
LLM on the strength of a claim it doesn't test. Wan tests the same question,
finds the LLM ahead by 0.1 FID, and keeps umT5 anyway. The encoder question
is open, and neither report settles it.

It is the main open baseline for two other reports as well. Step-Video
([LIT-624](LIT-624.md)) reports its 16×16×8 VAE level with this 8×8×4 one on its own
benchmark (PSNR 39.37 against 39.56, rFVD 3.61 against 4.17), and wins 615,
ties 313 and loses 361 against HunyuanVideo on Movie Gen Bench (Tables 14
and 15), with frame count and resolution not matched. Open-Sora 2.0
([LIT-634](LIT-634.md)) trains its $200k model on this VAE and measures it at LPIPS 0.046
and PSNR 30.240 (Table 1). That is well ahead of Step-Video's VAE (0.082
and 28.719), and ahead of Open-Sora's own 4×32×32 DC-AE on LPIPS though
slightly behind it on PSNR. Its 100-prompt human evaluation calls
Open-Sora 2.0 "comparable to" HunyuanVideo.

The scaling fit is the most quantitative claim about video compute
allocation in the record. It should be cited with its proxy caveat.

Read in full as [NOTE-344](../notes.d/NOTE-344.md).

Its successor, HunyuanVideo 1.5 ([LIT-tmp1ecle](LIT-tmp1ecle.md)), comes from the same team.
It describes its video data pipeline as built on this report's, and keeps
the multimodal-LLM text encoder with its token refiner and the dual-stream
block. It shrinks the model from 13B to 8.3B and replaces this report's
8×8×4 VAE with a 16×16×4 one. It adds a sparse attention path, SSTA, where
this report used full attention throughout. Like this report, it justifies
its design choices by citation and reports no controlled ablation of the
model. LongCat-Video ([LIT-tmpid6gf](LIT-tmpid6gf.md)) lists this model as a VBench 2.0 row:
total 55.30 against its own 62.11, but Physics 60.20 against its own 59.92.
The row is dated 2025-03 and appears to be a leaderboard entry rather than
a rerun.

Two 4K papers run this model untuned as a baseline. T3 ([LIT-tmpva88i](LIT-tmpva88i.md))
scores it 61.92 VQA at 4K and 41 frames, against 71.72 for its own
fine-tuned Wan derivative. Only T3 was fine-tuned at 4K, so the gap shows
what 4K fine-tuning buys, not anything about this architecture. UltraGen
([LIT-tmp8ew94](LIT-tmp8ew94.md)) runs it natively and with a super-resolution stage at 1080P
and 4K. With super-resolution it is the closest rival on UltraGen's HD-FVD
(238.75 against 214.12 at 1080P, 453.41 against 424.61 at 4K), and it beats
UltraGen on CLIP-L. Native generation took 11 h 36 min at 4K against
UltraGen's 1 h 50 min.

Sliding Tile Attention ([LIT-tmp1yfvi](LIT-tmp1yfvi.md)) measures that this model, trained
with full 3D attention, puts 70% of its attention mass inside a (12,24,24)
window holding 15.52% of the tokens. How local a head is varies by head but
barely across prompts. Training-free STA at 1.89× ties the dense model in
83% of 200 human-rated pairs. Fine-tuned at 91% sparsity it scores 82.62
VBench against 82.71, at 268 s instead of 945 s.

Two training-free sparse attention papers use this model as a dense
reference. Sparse VideoGen2 ([LIT-tmpucn4v](LIT-tmpucn4v.md)) reaches PSNR 30.45 against its
dense output at 25.45% density and 2.30× end to end, and reports that this
model tolerates changes in attention numerics far better than Wan 2.1.
Sol-Attn ([LIT-tmpx3dxt](LIT-tmpx3dxt.md)) runs it at about 85% sparsity: 2.12× end to end,
VBench 76.81 against dense 77.06.
