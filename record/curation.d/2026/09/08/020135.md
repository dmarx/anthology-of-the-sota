**Nothing in the four new principles is new.** Every one of them was argued
in full, at length, inside a devlog entry or a decision written in the last
few days. What they lacked was the one property that makes a principle
useful: being in the place a person looks *before* making the mistake rather
than after.

That is worth noticing about the shape of this record. A journal entry is
indexed by date. A decision is indexed by the choice it settles. Neither is
indexed by the situation a future reader will be in — and "I have a paper
with weak evidence and a real recommendation, what do I do" is a situation,
not a date or a decision.

## The selection criterion, since four were filed and several were not

The test used: **would stating this in advance have prevented a specific
mistake that was actually made this week?** Not "is it true", which too much
is, and not "is it interesting", which is how a principles page fills up with
observations nobody applies.

Filed on that test:

- *Filing is not endorsement* — would have prevented twelve practices being
  absent for months.
- *Agreement has no author* — would have prevented three trunk practices
  going unfiled while their branches were documented.
- *Adoption is not evidence* — would have prevented a support list of four
  model reports under a practice whose own prose called them adoption.
- *A vocabulary is a scope decision* — would have replaced a bad reason for
  declining a vision paper with the real one.

Held back, and worth saying why, because both are true and neither passed:

- **"A pass finds the defect its query is shaped like."** The strongest
  general lesson of the week — it recurred three times, in the source pass,
  the consensus pass, and the refusal grep. It is a principle about *method*,
  not about what belongs in this record, and the four filed here are
  deliberately one subject. It should probably be filed, and as its own
  thing.
- **"Derived state belongs where writes serialize."** Genuinely earned by
  four merge conflicts, and it is luria's decision to make rather than this
  project's — [ADR-018](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-018.md) adopts it wholesale and cites upstream. A principle
  restating a dependency's principle is a copy that will drift.

## The reflexive bit, which is not a joke

The reason these four principles went unwritten is the reason the practices
they are about went unfiled: **nothing prompts you to record what you already
know.** The trunk of a line of work has no arrival event; neither does the
rule you have been following successfully for a week. Both get written down
only if somebody goes looking on purpose.

Which suggests the same defence in both cases — a deliberate pass, run
against a question rather than against a diff — and suggests running it after
any stretch of work where the same correction was made twice.
