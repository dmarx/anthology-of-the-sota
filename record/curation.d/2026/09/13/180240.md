**The query was one paper, and the finding was the shape of its absence.**
Asked whether the record held `ARXIV-1904.10509`. It did not — no `arxiv:`
match, no `first_author: 'Child'`, no mention of the title anywhere in
`record/` or `docs/`. What made it worth a journal entry is what *was* there.

Seven reading notes stand downstream of a sliding window: `LIT-033`
(Longformer), `LIT-191` (attention sinks), `LIT-204` (Griffin), `LIT-176`
(native hybrid attention), `LIT-208` and `LIT-209` (NoPE global layers
interleaved with windowed local ones), `LIT-143` (natively trainable sparse
attention). Five practices use the term and two of them — `SOTA-153` and
`SOTA-176` — recommend designs whose local half is one. In none of the
seventeen documents that say "sliding window" is the window itself a claim;
it is a term the reader is assumed to already have.

This is `DP-007` exactly — *what everyone agrees on has no author, so nothing
prompts anyone to write it down* — and worth noting that the record found it
the way that principle says it will not be found. Nobody reading the
corpus noticed. It took someone arriving with a specific arXiv id.

## The query that would have found it, and the one that would not

Grepping `record/` for "sliding window" returns seventeen documents and
looks like healthy coverage. That query cannot answer the question, because the
documents it returns are exactly the ones that use the term without defining
it — the density of mentions is the *symptom*, not evidence against.

The query that works runs backwards from the term to its first use: for a
mechanism `n` documents assume, is there a document that introduces it? That
is mechanical, it is cheap, and nothing in the record currently runs it.
`SOTA-132`'s "describing a line from its middle" (`2026-09-08`) was the same
defect at the practice layer, found the same way — by someone following a
citation backwards rather than by reading forwards.

## What was not done, and what changed about that

The first pass filed the note and stopped, on the reasoning that the paper's
recommendations were either superseded or already in the record from later
sources. **Both halves of that were wrong, and wrong in the same direction.**

*Superseded is not a reason not to file.* `DP-003` is explicit — "do this,
and here is the thing everybody did until 2021 and why it stopped being
right" is the content, and it only exists if the abandoned position is
written down. The factorization is now `SOTA-tmpu69f8`, `Superseded` by
`SOTA-138`. Filing it cost a page and bought the strongest argument the
corpus has for its own successor: the enwik8 comparison, where a hand-chosen
escape lost to dense attention outright.

*"Already in the record from later sources" is a description of a defect.* If
a practice is sourced to a paper that did not introduce it, the attribution
is wrong — "we have it already" is the sentence that keeps it wrong. `SOTA-087`
was dating a 2019 recommendation to 2022 and `SOTA-060` was naming the
`1/sqrt(2N)` factor in prose with no citation at all.

## The field that did not exist

Fixing those two by reordering `source:` breaks them. `published:` derives
from `source[0]`, so `SOTA-087` would have become a 2019 practice whose body
argues a 2022 mechanism; and `ADR-017`'s retraction test — the primary source
is the one whose removal forces a rewrite — genuinely selects Dao, because
the body is Dao's argument. The two facts are both true and the field could
only hold one.

`ADR-tmpqczy4` adds `introduced_by:`. It is `DP-005`'s cut made a second time:
that principle sent **adoption** out of `source:` and into `consensus:`, and
this sends **origin** out of `source:` into its own field, leaving `source:`
to mean evidence and nothing else.

Filed `Proposed`, and the condition matters. Three cases found by one person
filing one paper in one afternoon argues that the field is needed; it does not
say how much it carries. The promotion test is the backwards query `DP-004`
asks for — over practices whose primary source postdates the technique they
describe — and the record has no view that surfaces those yet.

## What is still not done

No reading note. The paper was read against its method and experiment
sections, not cover to cover; `ADR-025` makes absence the record's word for
unread, and that is the honest value.

The pre-activation residual block is *not* repointed at this paper, and that
is deliberate. Child et al. adopts it from He et al. (2016) and says so;
`SOTA-032` is sourced to Xiong et al., the paper that **argues** it, which is
`DP-005` working correctly. Using a technique is not introducing it — the same
distinction `introduced_by:` exists to hold, applied in the direction that
says no.
