## What was actually missing

Searching the record for `prun`, `influence function`, `TracIn`, `GraNd`,
`coreset`, `datamodel`, `EL2N` and `quality classifier` returns a lot of hits
and almost no signal. Every `prun` hit is **weight** pruning — the lottery
ticket line, `LIT-019`, `LIT-031`, `LIT-039`. Every `influence function` hit
is a flocking paper's φ(r). `GraNd` matches "grand" in prose. This is the
fourth time in this project that a case-insensitive substring has produced a
convincing and entirely false coverage picture, after AIME/"aimed",
VeRA/"average" and RANSAC/"transactions".

With the false positives removed: **data pruning, coresets, example-difficulty
scoring, influence functions, data attribution and datamodels are absent
outright.** Zero documents, not zero practices. The twenty-six `data-pipeline`
practices answer what to include, in what proportion and — since this morning
— in what order, and none of them is about which *examples* are redundant.

## The distinction that made this worth filing

The record's selection material is almost all about **quality**: is this
document good enough to keep. Pruning is about **redundancy**: does this
example teach anything the others do not.

They can point opposite ways on the same object. A well-written document that
says what a thousand others say is exactly what a pruning metric discards and
exactly what a quality classifier keeps. That is stated in `SOTA-tmp70sgi`'s
conditions because the obvious mistake — run a quality filter, then run a
pruner, and assume the composition is fine — is not obviously wrong until
somebody writes down what each is ranking.

## The finding, and the half of it that gets dropped

`LIT-tmpy1abi` (Sorscher et al.) reframes power-law scaling: error falling
slowly in dataset size *is* a measurement of how much the corpus repeats
itself. Given a ranking of which examples teach least, error can fall
exponentially instead.

What gets quoted from this paper is "you can prune a lot of your data". Two
things that change the instruction get dropped:

**The direction inverts.** With abundant data keep the hard examples; with
scarce data keep the easy ones, where keeping the hardest does **worse than
random pruning**. The mechanism is that easy examples locate the target
function coarsely and hard ones refine its boundary, and refinement is
worthless before the coarse fix exists. Theory, then confirmed on
ResNet18/CIFAR-10.

**The fraction is a schedule.** The exponential regime is reached only if the
pruned fraction *grows* with the initial corpus size. Fix the fraction and it
decays back to a power law. A corpus that doubles should be pruned harder, not
proportionally.

## A caveat I did write, this time

`LIT-tmpd1gpw` (Paul et al.) supplies the cheap ranking — EL2N at epoch 20,
averaged over ten initializations — and something that corrects the practice
above: **the top of the difficulty ranking is not safe.** Keeping only the
highest scorers is suboptimal even on clean data, and the excluded window
widens with label corruption, because the hardest examples and the mislabelled
ones are the same examples.

So "keep the hard ones when data is abundant" means keep a *window*, not a
tail, and a corpus's noise rate sets where the upper edge goes. That went into
`SOTA-tmp70sgi`'s conditions rather than into its own practice: it is a
correction to an instruction, and it belongs where that instruction is read.

Same reasoning for two more of the notes' recommendations. Sorscher's R3 (do
not trust a metric across a scale change) is a caution about the practice, not
an independent one. R4 (embedding prototypicality when labels are expensive)
is a real recommendation with one metric on one corpus behind it, and the
instruction that generalises from it — use a cheap proxy that only has to
preserve ordering — is `SOTA-241`, filed this morning from a different
paper in a different domain. Named in the note with the condition that would
file it, which is what [ADR-043](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-043.md) asks a deferral to do.

## The crossover, twice

`SOTA-170` says aggressive filtering wins at 1T tokens and loses at 15T.
`SOTA-tmp70sgi` says keep hard examples when data is abundant and easy ones
when it is scarce. **Both are sign changes in a data-selection trade, driven
by how much data you have relative to what you intend to spend**, found by
different groups four years and one domain apart.

I have deliberately not claimed they are the same claim, and the practice says
so: one ranks documents by quality, the other ranks examples by difficulty,
and no experiment joins them. What the pair is worth is a prior — **a
data-selection recommendation stated without naming a data budget is probably
one regime's answer being quoted in the other.** The open question is on the
note, where a result could come due against it.

## The incumbent, again

`LIT-tmpwhgmr` is DCLM, filed carrying no practice. `SOTA-170` and `LIT-184`
have both been arguing against it since September 8 and the record did not
hold it, so the contrast pointed at a name rather than a document. Under
`ADR-010` it stays out of `source:` — contrast is not support — and
`SOTA-170` goes to v2 to cite it by code.

That is the second incumbent-shaped gap in two days, after DoReMi. The check
that finds them is cheap and worth making standing: **where the record holds a
practice whose body argues against a position, ask whether the record holds
the position.**
