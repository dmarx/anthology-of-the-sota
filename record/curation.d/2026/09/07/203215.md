[ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md) said succession gains a sign and named the mechanism: a second
relation, once a chain's spine could walk more than one. [LU-#211](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/211) shipped that
in 0.11.0, so this is the decision becoming the record's shape.

## What the sign changed, and what it did not

Not the pages. Six literature lines and eight practice lines, every step
nesting exactly where it did before — because `extends:` and `corrects:` are
one spine and the walk cannot tell them apart. That is the correct outcome and
worth stating plainly: the information gained is structural, not visual.

What it did change is what a document can be asked. `LIT-140` now reads
`corrected_by: [LIT-151, LIT-181]` — two independent groups naming defects in
mHC's constraint, legible from mHC itself without opening either. `SOTA-121`
reads `corrected_by: [SOTA-131]`, so "Muon needs QK-Clip at scale" is a fact
about Muon and not only a fact about QK-Clip. Getting the converse for free is
why this was a second relation rather than an attribute on `extends:`.

## Where the sign is still invisible

The lineage page renders code, title and facets, and says nothing about which
relation a step arrived on. So the record knows the difference and the reader
of the generated page does not. Filed upstream as [LU-#213](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/213) rather than worked
around: it
was an open question in [LU-#211](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/211) and the implementation deliberately left it
out, so the walk could land without a rendering decision attached to it.

## The process failure worth keeping

Two findings shipped in [#64](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/pull/64) and survived a merge because the lint output was
being read through `tail -3`. `luria lint` prints its warning *headline* first
and the individual findings under it, so a tail shows the last classes and
hides the first: a code resolving to nothing and a `Proposed` practice cited
unacknowledged were both above the cut, in the same PR that introduced them.

This is the second instance today of the same shape — the first was
`luria index >/dev/null 2>&1` hiding a config error, recorded upstream. The
general rule is the same and now has two witnesses: **do not truncate the
output of a command you are running in order to decide whether something is
clean.** A summary line is not a substitute for the findings, and the cheap
habit that hides them is the one that feels like tidiness.

Both are fixed here rather than filed, because they are small and they are
ours.
