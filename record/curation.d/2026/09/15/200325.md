The sweep prescribed two papers and the record filed them. Both readings
found something the record did not know, and in both cases what they found was
that a document already here was newer than its idea.

**Salimans et al. §3.2 is `THEORY-007`.** That account — filed two hours ago
from a 2026 paper — holds that ES scales because what matters is not the
ambient parameter count but a low-dimensional effective structure. The 2017
paper:

> However, it is important to note that this does not mean that larger neural
> networks will perform worse than smaller networks when optimized using ES:
> what matters is the difficulty, or intrinsic dimension, of the optimization
> problem.

It gives the argument (concatenate a regression's features with themselves:
parameters double, difficulty does not, ES behaves identically given `σ` and
the learning rate halved) and the observation (larger Atari networks did
slightly better). `LIT-236` adds a mechanism, a measurement across scales, and
the connection to rise-then-decay, all of which are real contributions. **But
the record filed that account without knowing its central claim was nine years
old**, and would have gone on presenting it as new.

The same shape for PPO. Three practices here modify it — `SOTA-145` removes
its critic, `SOTA-146` corrects an objective descended from its clipped ratio,
`SOTA-129` makes the family a recipe stage — and none could cite it. Reading
it turns up a detail no descendant mentions: the ES line grid-searches PPO's
KL coefficient `β`, and PPO's own §4 reports the KL-penalized variant as the
**worse** of the two its authors tested.

**The general form, and it is what `DP-007` is about from a new angle.** That
principle says the agreed trunk goes unfiled because agreement has no arrival.
The corollary this pass adds: **while the trunk is missing, its claims get
re-filed as though they were new by whoever restates them.** A record that
holds only the branches cannot tell a contribution from a restatement, and the
error runs in the flattering direction — every paper looks more original than
it is, because the thing it is building on is not here to compare against.

That is a sharper reason to file trunks than pedagogy. It is not that a reader
wants the foundational paper; it is that **without it the record cannot
grade the ones it has.**

## On what counts as evidence for a practice, revisited

Filing Salimans also settles something the record had been asserting on
[LIT-211](../../record/literature.d/LIT-211.md)'s authority. That ES "tolerates long-horizon and delayed rewards"
has been in this record since `SOTA-154` was filed, as a claim about a claim.
The argument is in §3.1 here: the policy-gradient score term is a sum of `T`
uncorrelated pieces so its variance grows nearly linearly with episode length,
while the ES term is independent of `T`. Four of the five arguments `SOTA-154`
makes for itself originate in this paper, and the practice has been making
them second-hand.

`introduced_by:` stays on [LIT-211](../../record/literature.d/LIT-211.md) and the reasoning is worth keeping: this
paper recommends ES over policy gradients for **control tasks trained from
scratch**, and the practice recommends it for **LLM fine-tuning**, which this
paper does not attempt and whose feasibility the field doubted for eight
years. The doubt is the evidence that they are different claims. Filing the
ancestor makes that distinction visible rather than dissolving it.

## The scope question, answered

`ADR-tmpf2vie` records what the sweep forced: benchmarks, model reports and
infrastructure are admissible `LIT` documents. Three things about how that
decision was reached are worth keeping.

**The record was already half-doing it.** [LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md), [LIT-130](../../record/literature.d/LIT-130.md) and [LIT-222](../../record/literature.d/LIT-222.md) are
model reports and a whitepaper, already `Active`. The practice registry counts
model reports as adoption evidence in `consensus:`. So the boundary would have
had to be drawn between things the record already treats identically — which
is [DP-008](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-8)'s warning realized: the scope existed in what the corpus happened
to contain, not in anything anyone decided.

**Nothing in the vocabulary changes**, and that is the test. A benchmark paper
is `analysis-and-evaluation` by that topic's own blurb; a model report takes
the topic of what it reports on; infrastructure is `systems-optimization`.
[DP-008](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-8) says a category added to admit a single document is how a scope
stops being one — the converse also holds, and a scope change needing no new
category is one the vocabulary was already sized for.

**The trigger stays narrow.** Something in the record has to cite it. A
benchmark nothing here is argued on has no standing to record, and the ADR
says so, because the failure mode of this decision is a reading list that is
mostly instruments.

## What this pass did not do

The backlog the ADR creates is named and unfiled: GSM8K (cited by 9 of 12),
the Qwen2.5 technical report (4), MBPP (3), verl (3). The sweep's ranking is
the queue.

And the sweep itself still has the hole its own entry described: **only 45% of
bibliography entries carry an arXiv identifier**, so the classical ES
literature — Rechenberg 1973, Schwefel 1977, Hansen and Ostermeier 2001 — is
invisible to it. Salimans et al. cites all three in its first paragraph on ES.
Filing the trunk has not reached the roots, and the query that would is a
different one.
