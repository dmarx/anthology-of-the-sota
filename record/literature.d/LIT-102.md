# LIT-102: ZeRO++: Extremely Efficient Collective Communication for Giant Model Training

Rajbhandari et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2306.10209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10209)

## Key takeaways

- Treats each of ZeRO's three collectives as a separate problem: **block
  quantization** for the forward weight all-gather, **a secondary redundant
  weight shard** so the backward gather is local, and **`qgZ`**, an all-to-all
  quantized reduction replacing reduce-scatter
- **The reason `qgZ` exists is structural, not empirical.** Reduce-scatter is a
  sequence of add-then-forward hops, so a quantized value is requantized and
  resummed at every hop and error compounds with hop count. An all-to-all
  exchanges data **once**, so each value is quantized once and reduced locally
  in higher precision — which is why INT4 gradients survive here and would not
  survive a compressed reduce-scatter
- **4× less communication, up to 2.16× throughput at 384 GPUs**
- Deliberately re-introduces the redundancy ZeRO exists to remove: optimization
  2 spends memory to buy bandwidth, on the premise that the bottleneck is
  communication — low-bandwidth clusters, or scale that forces the per-GPU
  batch small
- The transferable rule: **when compression fights a collective's structure,
  change the collective, not the precision**

none reasons about **how many times a value is quantized** on its way through a

to restructure the collective so there is only one.
