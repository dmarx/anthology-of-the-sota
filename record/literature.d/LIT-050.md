# LIT-050: Analyzing and Mitigating Data Stalls in DNN Training

Mohan et al., Microsoft Research and UT Austin (2020) — [ARXIV-2007.06775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06775)

## Key takeaways

- The finding that motivates everything else: across nine models, three
  tasks and four datasets on production cluster hardware, DNN training time
  is in many cases *dominated* by data stall time — time the GPU spends
  waiting for items to be fetched from storage and preprocessed — not by the
  step itself. The input pipeline had been the unexamined half of training
  time.
- Knowing that requires measuring it, and measuring it requires a technique:
  DS-Analyzer establishes the stall differentially, by comparing against runs
  with the data path removed, rather than by reading a profiler total. It
  also supports predictive what-if analysis over memory, CPU threads, storage
  device and GPU generation.
- The mitigations land in a data-loading library, CoorDL, and are three
  simple things rather than a redesign — the paper's point being that the
  bottleneck was unmeasured, not intractable.
- Against DALI, the state-of-the-art loader at the time, PyTorch with CoorDL
  cuts training time by as much as 5× on a single server.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of the record's data-pipeline practices: [SOTA-042](../practices.d/SOTA-042.md) (memory-map
large datasets), [SOTA-043](../practices.d/SOTA-043.md), [SOTA-044](../practices.d/SOTA-044.md) (pin memory for host-to-device
transfers) and above all [SOTA-045](../practices.d/SOTA-045.md), profile data loading separately from
training, which is this paper's central claim rather than a tip.

Filed alongside [LIT-053](LIT-053.md), which attacks the same bottleneck from the storage
format end. The two are the record's answer to a failure mode that does not
show up in a loss curve: a correct model, correctly optimized, waiting.
