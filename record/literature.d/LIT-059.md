# LIT-059: CheckFreq: Frequent, Fine-Grained DNN Checkpointing

Mohan, Phanishayee and Chidambaram (2021) — FAST '21,
[usenix.org/conference/fast21/presentation/mohan](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast21/presentation/mohan)

## Key takeaways

- Checkpointing had been epoch-granular and hand-tuned, which makes the
  interval a guess: too rare and a failure costs hours of recomputation, too
  frequent and the write dominates. CheckFreq makes the frequency something
  the system *derives*, at the granularity of iterations, from systematic
  online profiling.
- The interval is then tuned at runtime — adaptive rate tuning — so the
  checkpointing overhead stays inside a stated bound as conditions change,
  rather than being fixed once from an offline measurement.
- Correctness detail most implementations get wrong: resuming must preserve
  the training-data invariant that each item is seen exactly once per epoch,
  which means checkpointing the *data loader's* state, not just the model
  and optimizer. CheckFreq does it with a lightweight resumable iterator.
- Two-phase checkpointing pipelines the snapshot against ongoing compute, so
  the write is largely hidden. Together: recovery time falls from hours to
  seconds with runtime overhead bounded within 3.5%.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-054](../practices.d/SOTA-054.md) through [SOTA-057](../practices.d/SOTA-057.md) — checkpoint frequency as a tuned
quantity, asynchronous writes, and a multi-level strategy. Read the
practices against the paper rather than the reverse on one point: [SOTA-055](../practices.d/SOTA-055.md)
states an epoch-based rule of thumb for optimizer state, while CheckFreq's
argument is precisely that iteration granularity is available and epochs are
the wrong unit. That tension is worth leaving visible until someone re-reads
the practice.

By the same authors as [LIT-050](LIT-050.md), and the same instinct applied one layer
down: measure the thing everyone was estimating.
