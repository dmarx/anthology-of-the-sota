# LIT-018: On the Convergence of Adam and Beyond

Reddi et al. (2018) — [ARXIV-1904.09237](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09237)

## Key takeaways

- Constructs an explicit **one-dimensional convex** problem on which Adam
  converges to `x = +1` when the minimiser is `x = −1` — the opposite end of
  the feasible set — and proves it by induction on the iterates
- Locates the error in the original Adam convergence proof: the quantity
  `Γ_t`, the per-step change in effective learning rate, is assumed to have a
  sign it need not have
- **The cause is the exponential moving average itself.** A large informative
  gradient arriving once every three steps is divided down before it acts, and
  is permanently outvoted by frequent small ones. Any method scaling updates
  by a fixed-size window of past gradients has this failure mode; Adagrad,
  which never forgets, does not
- Proposes **AMSGrad** — keep `v̂_t = max(v̂_{t−1}, v_t)` so the effective
  learning rate is non-increasing — recovering an `O(√T)` regret bound, and
  **AdamNC**, which drives `β₂` toward 1 over training
- The empirical section is, in the authors' own words, **"a preliminary
  empirical study"**: logistic regression, a small net, CIFARNET

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmpz67rs](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpz67rs.md). ICLR 2018 best paper, and almost nobody runs AMSGrad.

**Nothing is sourced to this paper and the reading files no practice** — which
is the right outcome and the interesting one. `SOTA-001` says use Adam by
default; this paper proves Adam can fail on a convex problem. Both stand, and
the gap between them is the anthology's own premise in one line: **what people
do and what is provably correct are different questions, and this record
tracks the first.** Kept visible rather than resolved.

Every result is in online convex optimisation, a setting Adam is not used in,
and the counterexample is adversarial — nobody has shown the mechanism firing
in a real run.
