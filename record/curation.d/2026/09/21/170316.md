**gen2seg filed from the [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) worklist.** The headline is the kind of claim
that should attract suspicion: finetune a generative model on masks of
indoor furniture and cars, and it segments people, animals, luggage x-rays
and impressionist paintings. Plenty of papers report surprising transfer and
most of them are reporting that their evaluation set overlapped their
training distribution more than they thought.

What makes this one different is a baseline that scores **1.4 to 2.4 mIoU**.

`SimpleClick` is a state-of-the-art promptable segmenter. The authors took
it, gave it the **same MAE-B backbone**, finetuned it on the **same data**,
and evaluated it on the same seven splits. It does not degrade gracefully —
it produces essentially nothing. Everything is held constant except the
shape of the model, so the conclusion is not "generative models are good at
this" but something much more specific: *the freshly-initialized mask head is
the component that cannot generalize*, because it has only ever seen the
finetuning categories and has no prior to fall back on.

That is why the practice is phrased as a contrast with attaching a head,
rather than as "use a diffusion prior". The positive instruction and the
negative one are the same instruction.

**A second control does the harder work.** Having shown the head is the
problem, you might conclude the generative *decoder* is the answer. So they
bolt Stable Diffusion's frozen VAE decoder onto DINO's discriminative
features: 14.9 average, against MAE-B's 21.6 and SD's 30.9. Better than a
from-scratch head, worse than a generative encoder. The prior is in both
halves, and the paper reports the intermediate number rather than the
convenient one.

**Then three ablations, each killing a rival explanation.** It is not
internet-scale pretraining: MAE on unlabeled ImageNet-1K alone, no text,
segments x-rays. It is not category diversity: ten classes match
thirty-three, and five still work. It is not clean synthetic labels:
finetuned on COCO's coarse polygonal masks the model loses under five edge-AP
points, which is to say **a model trained on polygons does not predict
polygons**. That last one is the most elegant thing in the paper and it
occupies about four sentences.

**Where it loses, and why that went in the Conditions.** COCO-Small: **8.5
against SAM's 56.9**. The abstract's "closely approach the heavily supervised
SAM" is true of the aggregate and false of small objects by a factor of
nearly seven. The body is honest about it — and for the robotics and
medical-imaging readers the paper names in its conclusion, small objects may
be the entire question. So the practice carries it as a condition rather than
a caveat, and notes that the authors give two causes (pretraining bias toward
large central objects; finetuning at a quarter of SAM's resolution) and
separate neither, when the resolution one is cheap to test.

**The record gains a priced trade rather than a new winner.** `SOTA-186` is
SAM's route: bootstrap an enormous annotation set with the model you are
training. Its cost is 1.1B masks and 256 A100s for 68 hours. This route
reaches most of the same place on **3.7M masks and four GPUs for 29 hours**,
and loses on small objects. Neither refutes the other. Before today the
record only carried one side of that, which meant it could state a practice
but not a decision.

**Why the theory is `Proposed` despite being the best idea here.** The
authors' account is that discriminative self-supervision explicitly rewards
**invariance** — two augmented views must produce the same representation —
while segmentation needs **equivariance**, because deciding where one object
ends is a judgement about the quantities augmentation is designed to
quotient away. It is a genuinely good explanation and it predicts the DINO
control's exact failure signature: activates on objects, cannot separate
instances.

It is still `Proposed`, and the reason is worth stating because it is the
same shape as the reason I gave for `THEORY-050` an hour ago. **At least
three rival accounts predict the same ranking of models.** MAE's decoder
survives into finetuning where DINO's features must be upsampled by an
attached module, so low-level detail retention alone orders them identically.
The generative models operate at input resolution and the discriminative ones
do not, so output resolution does too. And SD is simply larger. Nothing in
the paper measures equivariance or invariance directly; the account is
inferred from which models group. The authors write "we hypothesize" twice in
one paragraph, which is them telling you the same thing.

**One thing cut before filing.** The literature note's first draft ended with
a line claiming this was "a second instance of a pattern `SOTA-262` and
`LIT-449` established: a pretrained generative model carrying structure a
discriminatively trained one does not." That is not what `SOTA-262` says —
it is about per-modality weights and joint attention, and has nothing to do
with generative-versus-discriminative priors. It read well, connected two
parts of the record, and was false. Removed before commit. Worth recording
because it is precisely the failure `DP-010` describes, committed here rather
than found in someone else's paper: the memorable sentence arrives first and
the evidence is checked afterward, if at all.
