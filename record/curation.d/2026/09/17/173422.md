Followed the benchmark filings into the data topics, on the reasoning that
what you filter and mix *for* is decided by what you measure. Three things came
out, and the first is a caveat that turned out to be wrong.

## The bridge I expected, and why I did not build it

Four `data-pipeline` practices argue on a named benchmark, and [SOTA-170](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-170.md) —
the record's central filtering practice — rests on MMLU: "improves MMLU by 5.6
over DCLM" at short horizon, "matches DCLM on MMLU" at long horizon.

I had just filed MMLU's note recording that it is saturated. The obvious move
was to add a bound: *a filtering decision validated on a saturated benchmark is
validated on nothing.*

Then I read the numbers. Those are **8B models at 1T and 15T tokens**, with
five-point MMLU deltas. That is well inside MMLU's discriminating range —
saturation is a frontier-model phenomenon and these ablations are nowhere near
it. Worse, MMLU is a *breadth* instrument, and [SOTA-170](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-170.md)'s claim is that a
less aggressively filtered corpus retains four times the unique tokens while
matching on quality. Breadth is exactly the right instrument for that claim.

So the caveat would have been wrong twice over, and the benchmark note's real
service was letting me check rather than letting me write. **A bound worth
stating and a bound that applies are different things**, and the note is what
makes the difference checkable — which is what [ADR-032](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-032.md)'s "what a result on
it bounds" is for. It is also the third time in two days that a thing I
expected to find wrong was right on inspection.

## The incumbent nobody filed

The record held [SOTA-166](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-166.md) — set data proportions by fitting a mixing law, from
2024 — and did not hold DoReMi, from 2023, which that literature measures
itself against everywhere.

A quieter version of the trunk problem than [DP-007](../../../../principles.d/DP-007.md)'s usual case. Not a
technique nobody wrote down, but one that arrived early enough to become the
baseline everyone assumes, so the *successor* gets filed as news and the
incumbent never does. Worth watching for: where the record holds a method
whose own paper frames it as an improvement, ask what it improves on.

The two are not rivals and the practice says so. A mixing law is a *predictor*
over mixtures — costs runs to fit, and in exchange optimises for any target you
can name. DoReMi is a *producer* of one weighting — costs a reference and a
proxy run, names no target, gives robustness instead of optimality.

## The objective is the paper

DoReMi stated as "Group DRO over domains" does not work, and the paper says
why:

> a naive worst-case approach would upweight the domains with the most noisy
> data, as every domain has a different optimal loss (aka, the entropy)

Worst-case loss selects for the hardest domain, and the hardest domain is
usually the noisiest. Optimising worst-case **excess** loss — the gap against a
reference model — converts "which domain is hard" into "which domain has
headroom". The reference model exists solely to supply that subtraction, which
is why the method has three steps and why skipping the first is not a saving.

The general form carries past data mixing: **a worst-case objective over
heterogeneous groups selects for irreducible difficulty unless you subtract a
per-group baseline.** Any minimax over domains, tasks, languages or users has
the same failure and the same remedy.

## The axis that was missing

Twenty-six practices carry `data-pipeline`. They answer *what to include*
(filtering, deduplication) and *in what proportion* (mixing laws, online
reweighting, DoReMi now). **None answered *in what order*.**

The word "curriculum" appears in five practices and every one of them is about
**training stages** — pretrain, SFT, RL. That is ordering the objectives.
Ordering the data is a different axis, and the vocabulary invites collapsing
them, so the new practice states the three apart explicitly: reweighting
changes how much, ordering changes what comes before what, staging changes
which objective is running.

Filed `Proposed`, because the evidence is in continual-pretraining and
fine-tuning regimes and the money is in pretraining from scratch — and because
identifying skills and their prerequisites is itself work the practice should
not pretend is free.
