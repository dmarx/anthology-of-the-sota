Took [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137), the benchmark backlog. Three things came out of it: the
issue's priority order is wrong in an instructive way, one of the benchmarks
turned out to be a trunk, and the three questions the issue asked to have
decided while filing now have answers.

## The worklist described the sweep, not the corpus

[#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137) ranks candidates by how many of **twelve evolution-strategies papers**
cite each — GSM8K at 9, Qwen2.5 at 4, HybridFlow and MBPP at 3. A sensible
proxy, and the issue says so.

Counting against the record instead gives a different order:

| benchmark | documents | practices |
|---|--:|--:|
| GSM8K | 30 | 6 |
| **MMLU** | 15 | **6** |
| HumanEval | 12 | 5 |
| Countdown | 15 | 3 |
| MBPP | 6 | 3 |

**MMLU ties GSM8K on practice count and does not appear on the issue's list at
all**, because it is not an evolution-strategies benchmark and the sweep could
not see it. The list was a faithful description of its sampling frame and a
misleading description of the corpus — [DP-004](../../../../principles.d/DP-004.md) applied to a worklist rather
than to a defect hunt, which is a use of it I had not met before.

The lesson generalises past this issue: a backlog inherits the shape of
whatever query produced it, and re-deriving it against the current record
before working it costs one script.

## A count I nearly believed

My first pass put AIME top with 40 documents and 11 practices, which would
have reordered everything again. **`-i` plus no word boundary: "AIME" was
matching "aimed".** Case-sensitive, it is 5 and 2.

Third time today — `VeRA` matched "average", `RANSAC` matched
"t*ransac*tions", now this. The pattern is specific enough to name: **short
uppercase acronyms grepped case-insensitively will match inside ordinary
words**, and they are exactly the tokens a corpus audit reaches for.

## The benchmark that was a trunk

[SOTA-210](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-210.md) recommends reporting `pass@k` alongside `pass@1`, and says to
"use an unbiased low-variance estimator … [LIT-234](../../../../literature.d/LIT-234.md) uses **the standard one**".

The standard one is Chen et al.'s, and so is `pass@k`. Section 2 of the Codex
paper opens by "defining the `pass@k` metric", gives
`1 − C(n−c, k) / C(n, k)` over `n ≥ k` samples, devotes an appendix to it, and
makes the variance argument [SOTA-210](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-210.md) had compressed into a clause.

So the record recommended a metric and an estimator whose defining paper it
did not hold, citing instead a paper that *uses* them. That is [DP-007](../../../../principles.d/DP-007.md)'s
trunk in its purest form: the convention is settled enough that nobody was
prompted to file its origin. The practice now carries the citation; the
recommendation is unchanged.

Worth noting this is the second trunk found by filing something that looked
like housekeeping, after InstructGPT. Benchmark backlogs are not low-yield,
they are low-*looking*.

## The three questions [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137) asked to have decided

**Does a benchmark note get a Standing section, or is "cited by X" the whole
standing?** It gets one, and it does different work than a method paper's.
A method paper's Standing argues about influence; a benchmark's records **what
a result on it bounds** — what it measures, what it is insensitive to, and
what a practice resting on its numbers may not conclude. That is the thing the
issue itself identified as unsayable, and it is prose, not a count.

**Do saturated benchmarks get retired?** No. Saturation is a fact *about the
instrument*, not a judgement that the document was wrong, and `Rejected` on a
`LIT` would break the standing of every practice arguing on its numbers.
GSM8K and MMLU are both effectively saturated and both filed `Active`, with
saturation recorded under what they are insensitive to — which is where a
reader needs it, because the question a saturated benchmark raises is "what
does a two-point delta mean now", and that is an insensitivity.

**How do benchmark notes interact with the contamination practices?** The
benchmark note is the right place for *its own* exposure, because contamination
is a property of a dataset and a date. GSM8K's note says it has been published
and quoted since 2021 and is in the training data of anything trained later,
and points at [SOTA-129](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-129.md), [SOTA-164](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-164.md) and [SOTA-197](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-197.md) rather than restating them.

## One filing decision worth defending

Only HumanEval got a `NOTE`. I read its full text, for the estimator section;
GSM8K and MMLU I read as abstracts.

[ADR-032](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-032.md) permits a benchmark with no reading note and [ADR-025](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-025.md) makes absence
mean unread, so filing three notes of visibly uneven depth would have been
claiming three readings and doing one. The asymmetry is the accurate record.
