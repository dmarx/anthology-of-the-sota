# NOTE-063: One Transformer Fits All Distributions in Multi-Modal Diffusion at Scale

## Contribution

A unification with a one-line mechanism. The paper's stated key insight:

> learning diffusion models for marginal, conditional, and joint distributions
> can be unified as predicting the noise in the perturbed data, where the
> perturbation levels (i.e. timesteps) can be different for different
> modalities.

So **the timestep vector is the task specification.** Perturb image and text
both fully and you are learning the joint; perturb the image fully and leave
the text at timestep 0 and you are learning text-to-image; swap them for
captioning. One trained network covers all of it.

The implementation change is correspondingly small: perturb all modalities
rather than one, feed **individual timesteps per modality**, predict noise for
all modalities. A transformer handles the mixed input types.

## Key insight

Conditioning is usually structural — a cross-attention stack, an encoder, a
separate model per direction. Here conditioning is a **value**: setting a
modality's timestep to zero means "this one is given". The set of tasks becomes
a continuous space rather than a list, and nothing about the architecture
changes between them.

The consequence the paper calls out is **classifier-free guidance for free**
(§3.2). CFG normally requires deliberately training an unconditional model
alongside the conditional one, usually by dropping the condition at some rate.
Here the unconditional model is already there — it is the same network at a
different timestep setting — so the guidance term costs nothing extra.

## Assumptions

- **Modalities can be encoded into a common latent space** where Gaussian
  perturbation is meaningful for both. Images and text are both encoded to
  latents first (§4.1); the text side is where this is least comfortable.
- Independent per-modality noise levels are coherent — i.e. the joint model
  learns something sensible at every point in the timestep grid, not just the
  corners the tasks use.
- Image–text pairs at 2023 scale.

## Key results

- **One model, all distributions**: text-to-image, image-to-text, joint and
  unconditional generation, with "minimal modification of the original
  diffusion models".
- **Classifier-free guidance for free**, from the unified parameterisation.
- Competitive with large existing models on image–text data.
- Supports Gibbs sampling between modalities and interpolation between images
  in the wild.
- Points to semi-supervised learning and more modalities as future work.
- **The authors state a negative result plainly:** "the text generated by our
  implementation is not that smooth, mainly because the text data is noisy."

## Method

Encode each modality to a latent. Sample an independent timestep per modality.
Perturb each accordingly. Train one transformer to predict the noise in all
modalities. At inference, set a modality's timestep to 0 to condition on it.

## Concepts

- **Conditioning as a value rather than a structure** — the transferable idea,
  and it is not diffusion-specific.
- **The timestep vector as a task index** — turning a discrete set of tasks
  into a continuous space.
- **Free unconditional model** — the CFG consequence, and a good example of a
  unification paying an unplanned dividend.

## Recommendations

- **R1** — Before building separate models or conditioning machinery per
  direction, check whether the directions differ only by which inputs are
  clean. *Topic:* generative modelling. *Strength:* moderate.
- **R2** — Prefer a conditioning mechanism that is a value in the input over
  one that is a change in the architecture; the task space becomes continuous
  and unplanned capabilities fall out. *Strength:* moderate, and C3 is the
  dividend.
- **R3** — When reporting a weak result, say whether you isolated its cause.
  *Topic:* analysis and evaluation. *Strength:* strong; C5 is the
  counterexample.

## Bearing on the record

**Nothing is sourced to this paper and this reading files no practice.** The
record's generative-modelling practices are single-modality, and the
multimodal-diffusion line is not one the anthology tracks.

Retagged from `model-architecture` to `generative-modeling`: the contribution
is a training-and-conditioning scheme, and the architecture is a stock
transformer by the paper's own account.

The document's takeaways — "universal sequence modeling", "cross-modal
architecture", "unified training approach", "scalable design" — miss the
mechanism entirely. **"Universal sequence modeling" is not what this paper is
about**; it is a diffusion paper, and the unification is over *distributions*
(marginal, conditional, joint), not over sequences.

## Limitations

- 2023, image–text only, and "competitive with" rather than better than the
  large single-purpose models.
- C5 is a self-reported weakness with an unisolated cause.
- The assumption that the model learns something sensible across the whole
  timestep grid, not just at the task corners, is never tested.
- Text-in-a-diffusion-latent is the uncomfortable part and the paper's own weak
  result is on exactly that side.

## Open questions

- Does the interior of the timestep grid mean anything? The tasks use the
  corners; a partially-noised condition is a natural notion of soft
  conditioning and nobody looks at it.
