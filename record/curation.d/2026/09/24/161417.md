Unit 4 of `#326`. The defect is a new shape and the reason it survives every
check is worth writing down.

## The shape

`SOTA-032` recommends Pre-LN. It is `Active`, it is well written, it is at
version 3 with a careful history, and it contains this:

> Pre-norm trades trainability for some final quality: the unnormalised
> residual stream grows in magnitude with depth, and deep pre-norm models can
> see later blocks contributing proportionally less — **the representation
> collapse argument**.

**"The representation collapse argument"** — definite article, no citation,
no number, no document. The practice is telling a reader that the thing it
recommends has a known cost, and pointing at nothing.

Nineteen files in this record discuss Pre-LN. Ten discuss Post-LN. The
argument appears in the prose of the practice that recommends the choice, and
the record held no source for it.

## Why nothing catches this

Every check the record has is about *declared* structure. `source:` is
present and correct — `LIT-114`, Xiong et al., which is the right source for
the recommendation. `explained_by:` points at a real theory. The tags are
legal, the relations bind, no citation is retired.

**The unsourced claim is in prose, and prose is where the record does its
thinking.** A practice's body is exactly where a reader should find the
qualifications and the costs, so the lint cannot object to a body containing
a claim. The failure is not a missing field; it is an assertion that reads
like received knowledge and has never been checked against a paper.

This is the third distinct invisible-to-tooling defect this week, and they
form a set:

| defect | what makes it invisible |
| --- | --- |
| a thin note under a thick cluster (`LIT-068`, unit 1) | the note has every required field and no reading |
| a duplicate recommendation (`SOTA-382`, `#324`) | uniqueness is declared on identifiers, not on claims |
| **an unsourced claim in a body** | prose is where qualifications belong, so its presence proves nothing |

All three are curation questions. None of them is a lint question, and I do
not think any of them can be made into one.

## What the paper supplied, and what it did not

Xie et al. give the argument a rate: `O(1/√k)` per-layer change, `O(1/√N)` for
the output effect of an added block. That is what the practice was missing.

But **reading it also deflated the claim the practice was making.** `O(1/√k)`
is slow. At twenty-four layers the per-layer change is about a fifth of its
first-layer value, not a thousandth. "Collapse" names the direction and
overstates the speed, and the derivation assumes block outputs are
independent — which is the one assumption a trained residual network
obviously violates, since later blocks read what earlier ones wrote.

So the sourcing exercise returned a weaker claim than the unsourced version
implied. **That is the normal outcome and the reason to do it.** An
uncited argument drifts toward whatever strength the citing context needs;
attaching a source fixes it at whatever the source actually shows.

## The second finding, which is about how the first happened

Xie et al.'s Table 5 shows Pre-LN losing **2.84 and 3.36 BLEU** when warm-up
is removed. The paragraph under it says Pre-LN "can train effectively without
it."

`SOTA-032` says, sourcing Xiong et al., that "the warmup stage can be removed
entirely."

**The sentence propagates and the table does not.** Xiong et al. showed
Pre-LN *trains* without warm-up, which is true; the phrase "can be removed
entirely" is a small strengthening; and by the time it reaches a practice
document the qualification has no carrier. Xie et al. reproduce the phrasing
in their own prose while their own table contradicts it.

That is worth separating from ordinary citation error. Nobody misread
anything. **A claim that is true under a test weaker than the one readers
apply will drift, because the prose that travels is the claim and the table
that constrains it is not.** The countermeasure is the one this unit used:
when a practice states a consequence without a number, treat the missing
number as the finding.
