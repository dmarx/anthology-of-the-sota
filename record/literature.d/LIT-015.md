# LIT-015: Understanding Batch Normalization

Bjorck, Gomes, Selman and Weinberger (2018) — [ARXIV-1806.02375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02375)

## Key takeaways

- The practical claim: BN's central benefit is that it **permits much larger
  learning rates than an unnormalised network tolerates**, and most of the
  observed improvement in accuracy and training speed follows from that rather
  than from normalisation as such.
- Without BN, large learning rates at initialisation produce activations and
  gradients that grow with depth, which is what makes those learning rates
  divergent. BN bounds them, so the same rate becomes usable.
- Larger learning rates carry their own regularising effect, which is part of
  why BN is often described as a regulariser.

## Not this paper

The internal-covariate-shift challenge, the loss-landscape smoothing argument
and the Lipschitzness result belong to Santurkar, Tsipras, Ilyas and Madry —
*How Does Batch Normalization Help Optimization?* — filed separately. This
note carried those as its takeaways under this title for as long as the record
has existed.

Worth stating how it survived: the title and the arXiv identifier agreed with
each other, so nothing mechanical could catch it. Only the author field and
the content came from the other paper, and neither is checkable against the
other today.

## Standing in the anthology

The source for [SOTA-020](../practices.d/SOTA-020.md) (use larger learning rates with batch
normalization), which is exactly this paper's claim and is the practice that
was correctly sourced all along.
