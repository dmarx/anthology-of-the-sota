Today's third unit found the same defect as the first two, in a third field, and
this instance is the one that settles what the pattern is: **the record's free
prose asserts things about the record, and nothing checks them.**

## The three

- **`SOTA-365`**, yesterday. Its consensus note made a structural claim about
  papers the record did not hold — that DINO has no predictor — and the claim was
  wrong.
- **`SOTA-373`**, this morning. Its consensus note called BERT one of "the two
  masked-prediction documents it holds". The record held one.
- **`LIT-478`**, this afternoon. Its "Why it's here" said *"The record has
  nothing on weight-space ensembling. No stochastic weight averaging, no model
  soups, no snapshot ensembles, no Polyak averaging."*

The third is the worst of the three and the most instructive, because the other
two were falsified by later reading and this one was **false when written**. On
2026-09-21, the day that sentence was filed, the record held `SOTA-217`
("Align the hidden-unit permutation before averaging weights from separately
trained networks"), `THEORY-010` ("Most of the loss barrier between two
independently trained networks is permutation, not disagreement"), `LIT-333`
(Git Re-Basin) and `LIT-251`. All four were filed **2026-09-15**. Six days.

Nobody had to read a paper to catch it. One grep would have done.

## What the three have in common, and it is a field

Every enforced check in this record is about a *relation between documents*.
`source:` must name a paper. `introduced_by:` must name one. A tag must be in the
vocabulary. A relation must bind on a shared topic. A citation of a retired
document must be acknowledged. The machinery is good and it is why the last five
units found what they found.

None of it looks at prose. And the fields where the record says what it knows
about its own coverage — a `consensus_note`, a "Why it's here", a "Standing in
the anthology" — are exactly the prose fields. So the most confident sentences in
the record sit in the only places nothing verifies.

The failure mode is specific and it is not carelessness: it is **describing a gap
by guessing instead of by counting.** Writing "the record has nothing on X" feels
like reporting and is actually predicting. The three wrong sentences were all
written by somebody who had just read a paper about X and had not run
`grep -rl X record/`.

## The cheap rule

Every claim of the form *this record holds / does not hold / is the only* is a
measurement, and a measurement has to be taken. Two units ago the same rule
produced "89 files say BERT, 51 of them notes, practices or explanations" instead
of the unverified "six documents name BERT as a baseline" that had been sitting in
`LIT-668`. The counted version is shorter to write and impossible to be wrong
about.

Worth considering as a lint: a prose assertion containing "the record has
nothing on" is unverifiable by machine, but the pattern is narrow enough to
flag for a human. Filed as an observation rather than as a proposal, because the
false-positive rate is unknown and this journal is not the place to design it.

## What the gap actually was, which is more interesting than "nothing"

The record held the **hard** case and was missing the **easy** one.

`SOTA-217` and `THEORY-010` are about averaging networks that were trained
separately: the barrier between them is mostly permutation, so align the
hidden units first. That is the subtle, recent, partially-settled corner of
weight averaging.

What was absent is the case where averaging just works because the weight
vectors share an optimization trajectory — SWA along one trajectory, and
interpolating a fine-tuned model with the initialization it was fine-tuned from.
Both are older, both are simpler, and both are in wider use than Git Re-Basin.

`LIT-674` states the boundary between the two halves in one sentence, which
is why it is now quoted inside `SOTA-217`:

> ensembling all layers—as we do when end-to-end fine-tuning—typically fails,
> achieving no better accuracy than a randomly initialized neural network.
> However, as similarly observed by previous work where part of the optimization
> trajectory is shared, we find that the zero-shot and fine-tuned models are
> connected by a linear path in weight-space along which accuracy remains high.

So the test before averaging two weight vectors is **did one come from the
other**, and the record now has both answers filed against it. That pairing only
became visible by getting the coverage claim wrong and then checking it, which is
an argument for checking rather than for guessing well.

## An `ADR-015` corner, on purpose

`SOTA-408` is filed `Proposed` with `consensus: converged`, which looks like
a mistake and is not. SWA is in PyTorch's standard library and appears through
this record as the baseline other methods are measured against — the field agrees.
And every measurement the record holds for it is 2018 convolutional vision
against an SGD baseline, on architectures nobody trains now.

That is precisely the corner `ADR-015` built the two fields to express: the field
has converged and this record has not satisfied itself. `DP-005` is the reason
the two can differ — adoption is not evidence — and here the adoption is real,
wide, and beside the point.
