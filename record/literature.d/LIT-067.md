# LIT-067: Progressive Distillation for Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models

Salimans et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2202.00512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512)

## Key takeaways

- **Progressive distillation**: distil an `N`-step DDIM sampler into a student
  taking `N/2` steps, whose target is two teacher steps; promote the student and
  repeat. **8192 → 4 steps, FID 3.0 on CIFAR-10**, for no more total compute
  than training the original model
- **This is where v-prediction comes from**, and the reason is diagnosed: as
  signal-to-noise → 0, "the effect of small changes in the neural network
  output on the implied prediction in x-space is increasingly amplified", and
  distillation drives evaluation toward exactly that end. The stated criterion
  is that the implied `x̂` must **stay stable as `λ_t = log(α²/σ²)` varies**
- Three parameterisations satisfy it — predict `x`; predict `x` and `ε` on
  separate channels and merge; or predict **`v ≡ α_t·ε − σ_t·x`**. All three
  also work for training an *original* diffusion model, which is how
  v-prediction escaped into general use
- The loss weighting must change too: DDPM's implicit `exp(λ_t)` weighting
  **gives weight zero to zero-SNR data**. Replacements are `max(α²/σ², 1)`
  ("truncated SNR") and `(1 + α²/σ²)`, which is exactly `‖v − v̂‖²`
- **Halving beats quartering** at fixed budget — better to take fewer parameter
  updates per halving than to skip distillation iterations

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-048](../notes.d/NOTE-048.md). Nothing is sourced here, but the reading **identifies the
antecedent of a practice the record does carry.**

`SOTA-188` is sourced to `LIT-075` (EDM), correctly — EDM derives the
preconditioning coefficients from a unit-variance requirement. But the *problem*
`SOTA-188` solves is stated here four months earlier, with a concrete failure
(low-SNR amplification), a concrete criterion (implied `x̂` stable across
log-SNR), and three concrete solutions. **v-prediction is one of them, and the
record mentions it nowhere.**

Whether that means a second `source:` on `SOTA-188` or a v-prediction practice
of its own is a registry decision, deliberately not taken from a note-repair
pass.

Three later papers run it as a baseline. Both distribution-matching
distillers list progressive distillation's one-step ImageNet-64 FID as 15.39,
against 2.62 for DMD ([LIT-643](LIT-643.md)) and 1.51 for DMD2 ([LIT-646](LIT-646.md)). DMD's CIFAR-10
table has it at 8.34 against DMD's 3.77 unconditional. Those baseline rows are
copied, not re-run, and they are not this paper's own numbers either: the
15.39 and the 8.34 are iCT's re-implementation ([LIT-786](LIT-786.md)), starred in its
Tables 2 and 3 as run by its authors. They compare at one step, where halving
has the furthest to go. Hang et al. ([LIT-710](LIT-710.md)) run this paper's
truncated-SNR weighting, `max(SNR, 1)`, as one of four weightings under x0,
ε and v targets on ImageNet-256 latents. Their Min-SNR-5 converges faster in
every cell, and with ε output `max(SNR, 1)` diverges, since it puts weight
`1/SNR` on the ε loss.

The few-step papers filed later carry it too. iCT ([LIT-786](LIT-786.md)) re-ran it
for the rows above and beats it from random weights, with one-step
consistency training at 2.83 on CIFAR-10 and 4.02 on ImageNet-64 (iCT-deep
2.51 and 3.25). sCM ([LIT-790](LIT-790.md)) lists it on ImageNet-64 at 10.70 and
4.70, from a different re-implementation by Heek et al., against one-step
sCT's 2.04. Lee et al. ([LIT-791](LIT-791.md)) copy the 15.39 and two-step 8.95
against their reflowed model's 4.31 and 3.64. Shortcut Models
([LIT-787](LIT-787.md)) is the one controlled rerun. Halving in eight 50K phases from
the same flow-matching teacher on DiT-B, progressive distillation is the best
one-step model in its Table 1, at 14.8 FID on CelebA-HQ-256 and 35.6 on
ImageNet-256, against the shortcut model's 20.5 and 40.3 and one reflow's
23.2 and 44.8. It has no many-step mode, scoring 302.9 and 201.9 at 128
steps.
