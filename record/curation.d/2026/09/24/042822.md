A reader asked whether the record held anything from the Wan family, for
example Wan2.1 I2V. It held nothing. A search of `record/` and `data/` for
"Wan", the arXiv id, "I2V", "image-to-video" and "video generation" found no
generator report. The nearest documents were the DiT backbone (`LIT-448`),
two video-understanding papers (`LIT-215`, `LIT-218`) and two practices that
mention video in passing (`SOTA-187`, `SOTA-335`).

`LIT-218` had already named this gap. Its note ends by asking for "a frontier
video training report with a disclosed recipe", so the missing document was
written down but nothing pointed to a candidate. The Wan report is filed as
that candidate.

## Tags

Six topics, and no new one. I checked whether "video" wanted its own word
and concluded it does not. `generative-modeling` names the kind of claim and
`vision-and-graphics` names the modality, and between them they say what
the document is about. A video topic added for one document is the case
[DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8)'s corollary warns against. The count may change that. If video
reports keep arriving and scatter across these two plus
`representation-and-encoding`, [DP-009](../design-principles.md#dp-9)'s counting test is the one to run.

One thing worth noting for later: `generative-modeling`'s blurb says
"text-to-image" and not "video". It is a gloss, not a boundary, so I left it
alone.

## Reading depth

The note is `Skimmed`, not `Read`. It covers §1–4, §5.1 and §6 of a 60-page
report, and skips the six other application sections. That is enough to
list the hedges and not enough to source a practice. The LIT note lists
three candidate practices. The most transferable is the image-to-video
conditioning recipe. A future pass should read §4 and §5.1 against those
candidates before filing any of them.
