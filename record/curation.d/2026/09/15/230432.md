`SOTA-010` — *skip connections promote training stability by smoothing out the
loss landscape* — is a statement about what is true, filed in the scheme for
statements about what to do. [ADR-031](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-031.md) spotted that, listed it first among eight
practices in the same shape, called it and [SOTA-012](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-012.md) the two clearest
candidates to move, and then did not move it:

> Moving one is not a status change, and "retire by changing status, never by
> deleting" does not cover it: a practice re-filed as a theory leaves a SOTA
> code that is neither `Rejected` nor `Superseded` but simply somewhere else.
> That needs its own decision about what the old code says and what happens to
> links into it.

So the work here was the decision, not the move. The move took ten minutes
once the decision existed.

## What made it non-obvious

`Rejected` means different things in the two schemes, and CLAUDE.md says so
plainly: on a practice it means **do not do this**. The `sota-statuses` attic
blurb even offers *"too theoretical"* as a reason, which reads like an
invitation. It is not — that phrasing belongs to the paper scheme, where a
paper can be too theoretical to carry. A practice whose problem is that it was
never a practice is a different animal, and filing this one `Rejected` would
have published the sentence *do not use skip connections*, which is about the
worst thing this record could say by accident.

`Superseded` carries "replaced, and the record says what" with no instruction
attached to the subject matter. That is the decision.

## The correction worth keeping

I drafted the decision asserting that **no typed field could name a successor
in another scheme**, and that the successor would have to live in prose with a
resolvable code. I wrote a whole numbered point about it.

The lint rejected the first save:

> `record/practices.d/SOTA-010.md`: no `superseded_by:` in frontmatter —
> `superseded_by` names a document in **any** scheme, because
> `status: Superseded`

It already crosses schemes, by an explicit luria decision. The claim in my
draft was invented rather than checked, and it was invented about a field I
could have read in `luria.yaml` in ten seconds. The decision now says so in
place rather than quietly using the field, because this is the second time in
one contribution that a check knew something about the record that I did not —
the first was the same lint refusing an unacknowledged citation of `SOTA-107`
while I was writing a decision *about reading a report*, which is how I
learned that practice was in the attic.

Two for two, in one sitting, on facts I asserted from memory about a record I
was actively reading. The pattern is not "the lint is useful"; it is that I
reach for a confident sentence about the record's own machinery at exactly the
moments I am busy with something else.

## What was not done

**[SOTA-012](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-012.md) stays a practice.** It is the other one [ADR-031](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-031.md) called clearest,
from the same paper and the same figure, and the rule now exists to move it
under. It is left because a rule that has moved one document has one data
point, and the next pass should get to test the rule rather than invent it.
Six of the eight remain besides.

**No practice was filed alongside the theory.** The obvious split — a
`use skip connections` practice plus the explanation — produces an instruction
with no reader, because every model in this record already has them and
[DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7) keeps the agreed trunk unfiled. Worth recording that the split was
considered and is the natural first move, because the next person to reach one
of the eight will consider it too.

**The five practices the account `explains:` were not re-read.** They were
taken from what the old body already claimed it underwrote. That list is
plausible and unverified, and a wrong `explains:` edge is a claim about why a
practice works.
