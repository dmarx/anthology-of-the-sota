# LIT-138: Gated Attention for Large Language Models: Non-linearity, Sparsity, and Attention-Sink-Free

Qiu et al., Qwen Team (2025) — [ARXIV-2505.06708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708)

## Key takeaways

- Gates are everywhere — LSTMs, highway networks, state-space models,
  linear attention, softmax attention — and their specific effects had
  rarely been isolated. The paper does it for softmax attention with a
  controlled comparison over 30 gating variants on 15B mixture-of-experts
  and 1.7B dense models, each trained on 3.5T tokens.
- The finding: a head-specific sigmoid gate applied to the output of the
  scaled dot-product attention, before the output projection, consistently
  improves performance. Two effects are credited: a non-linearity between
  the value projection and the output projection, and input-dependent
  sparsity in the attention output.
- The same gate improves training stability, tolerates larger learning
  rates and improves scaling behaviour — and it removes the attention-sink
  pattern (mass on the first token) and the massive activations that come
  with it.
