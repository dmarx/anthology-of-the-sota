# LIT-096: Segment Anything

Kirillov et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2304.02643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02643)

## Key takeaways

- **The data engine is the transferable part.** Three stages —
  *assisted-manual*, *semi-automatic*, *fully automatic* — with the model
  retrained between them, so each stage's labels train the model producing the
  next stage's
- **1.1B masks** over 11M images, the great majority from the automatic stage;
  each stage materially cheaper per label than the one before
- The model and the dataset are **built together**, which is the method; the
  mask count is the consequence
- Two preconditions the loop needs: the model must be **useful before the
  dataset exists**, and **confidence must be informative** enough to route
  work between model and human
- A promptable formulation is what makes the model useful at stage one — it
  can be steered rather than needing to be right unprompted

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-186](../practices.d/SOTA-186.md).

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-018](../notes.d/NOTE-018.md). The practice is
**confirmed**.

This note's standing has always been that the practice drawn from it is not
about segmentation but about the annotation loop — a `data-pipeline` claim,
under [ADR-026](../decisions.d/ADR-026.md) (which supersedes [ADR-020](../decisions.d/ADR-020.md) and carries that decision
forward). The reading supports that and adds the two preconditions,
which the practice does not state and a reader most needs.

It also names what the record is relying on and the paper does not test: that
the loop **generalises past segmentation**. Masks are unusually cheap to
verify, and a task needing expert judgement per label does not inherit the
economics.
