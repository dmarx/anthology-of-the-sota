Ten papers now. The four added here were all named in the previous pass's
"what this did not cover" section, which is the first time this record has
closed a gap it had written down rather than rediscovering one — and the
readings changed more than the previous three passes combined.

**The record had filed an answer before the question.** `LIT-230`'s second
research question is, in its entirety, a reply to Abdi et al.: does ES's large
parameter drift cause catastrophic forgetting? The record acquired that reply
two passes ago and acquired the paper it replies to today. `LIT-tmpqknia`
(ESSA) is the same failure at the other end — the earliest paper in the whole
line, July 2025, filed last, after everything that came after it.

Both have one cause. Every pass so far started from a paper and followed its
citations *forward*, to what cited it or what it responded to among things
already filed. Nothing ever asked **what does this paper cite that we do not
have**, which is a different query with a different answer, and the two gaps
it left were a paper the record was already arguing with and the paper the
line begins at. [DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4) is about exactly this and did not prevent it, because
knowing that a query has a complement does not tell you to run the complement.
**The cheap fix, written here so the next pass inherits it: after filing a
paper, list its references that the record lacks.** It takes one grep of the
bibliography.

**The most valuable thing in this pass is a table nobody set out to build.**
Sorting all eight ES-versus-RL comparisons by model size:

- GRPO ahead, 1B and 1.5B — Abdi et al.
- Direct ES "highly ineffective", 0.5B and 1.5B — Hyper-ES
- ES ahead in most cells, 1.5B–8B — Neural Thickets
- ES highest on 4 of 4 tasks, 4B — Hoy et al.
- ES ahead on pass@k, ≤7B — Ba et al.
- ES ahead on pass@k, 7B–32B — Hayes et al.

**Every negative is at or below 1.5B. Every positive is at or above it.** Six
groups, mostly not citing each other and none testing `THEORY-006`, recovered
the threshold that account names. That is a real finding and it arrived as a
*side effect of filing*, not from reading any single paper — which is the
argument for a record like this one existing at all.

It is also the most dangerous kind of finding, and both documents say so: it
is read across papers differing in task, tuning and implementation; it is
post-hoc; and it is exactly what an account's author would want to see.
Nobody has run one protocol on both sides of the line, and until someone does
this is suggestive.

**`THEORY-006` has now come close three times and been promoted none.** A
confirmed prediction (population size falling with scale), a mechanism
(functional sparsity), and now the boundary. The condition asks for the
density measurement repeated by an unconnected group on a different family, or
a mechanism predicting where the transition sits, and none of the three is
that. Holding the line a third time is uncomfortable enough that it is worth
naming the discomfort: **either the account is genuinely unconfirmed, or the
condition is asking for an experiment nobody has reason to run while its
consequences keep turning up on their own.** The document takes the first
reading and flags the second, and deliberately does not decide — a condition
rewritten by whoever wants it met is not a condition, and that principle has
been tested twice in this line now.

**Hoy et al. retired an argument rather than winning it.** Three papers here
had measured ES's parameter drift and drawn three conclusions: it causes
forgetting, it is functionally sparse and harmless, it proves the search finds
nothing. The decomposition — an on-manifold component that changes the loss
and an off-manifold random walk that cannot, scaling as `σ²dT/N` — says all
three are describing the same object. The disagreement was never about the
measurement; it was about what each group happened to have measured alongside
it, and over what horizon.

That is the second time in this line that an apparent contradiction turned out
to be two groups measuring different things. The first was Hyper-ES's lemma
about a *direction* against Ba et al.'s sparse *subspace*. **A record that
holds both sides is what makes these visible; a summary of either one alone
would have read as settled.**

**One filing decision worth recording.** ESSA is ES over a few hundred
SFT-initialized singular values. `SOTA-154` recommends ES over the *full*
parameter space in place of policy-gradient RL. Filing ESSA as support for
that practice would have been easy and wrong, and the note says so explicitly
— same method family, different recommendation. What ESSA does instead is turn
the subspace approach into a line: it and Hyper-ES arrived independently,
eighteen months apart, at *seed a low-dimensional subspace with gradients,
then search it gradient-free*. Two independent arrivals is a better argument
for the approach than either paper's numbers, and it means `SOTA-154`'s
`contested` reading is a live methodological disagreement rather than one
group's objection.

**What this pass did not cover.** The line is now filed to the edges this
record can see, but three things are open and cheap. The bibliography sweep
described above has not been run on any of the ten papers. `ARXIV-2605.30148`
(*Overcoming Forgetting in LLM Fine-Tuning with Evolution Strategies*) turned
up in the search for Abdi et al. and is, by title, the remedy paper for the
problem `SOTA-tmpdcmgg` addresses from theory — it was not read, and it is the
obvious next thing. And `LIT-tmp4w505`'s linear-mode-connectivity result, if it
generalizes, is a claim about post-training reaching one basin regardless of
optimizer, which is worth far more than the use this line makes of it and has
no document of its own.
