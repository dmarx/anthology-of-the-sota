**The gap was visible from the practice registry, not from the worklist.**
`ARXIV-2212.07677` is on the refreshed `#180` list at four reading days, which
is how I got to it. But the reason it was worth a unit is that the record held
`SOTA-037` and `SOTA-038` — in-context learning emerges at scale, and permits
few-shot adaptation — plus `THEORY-038` on what a decoder cannot compose in
few layers, and nothing at all about what the forward pass is doing when it
does the thing those practices name. A capability with no mechanism under it.

**Filing the one paper on the worklist would have been a mistake, and I nearly
made it.** The obvious unit was: read `ARXIV-2212.07677`, file the
mesa-optimizer account, done. What stopped it was a search for a rebuttal
before writing anything — a habit this session has had reason to build — which
turned up two, from opposite directions. `ARXIV-2310.17086` attacks the
identification from *inside* the same setup: same task, same GPT-2 backbone,
same training objective, and the layers advance at a second-order rate.
`ARXIV-2310.08540` attacks it from outside: real pretrained models, and a
theorem that needs no weights at all.

**Then the source paper turned out to contain the first refutation.** Four
pages after Proposition 1, von Oswald et al. report that their looped and
unrolled models *do not* match gradient descent — they match GD++, gradient
descent on data transformed by `I − γXXᵀ` with a learning rate and a `γ`
fitted per layer. A first-order step in a data-dependent geometry is a
preconditioned method. The exact result is about one layer, and one layer is
not a transformer. This is in the paper, in its own figures, with its own
name for the variant, and the title does not mention it. `DP-010`, and the
most-cited sentence in the cluster is the one the paper itself qualifies.

**So the unit became two theory documents, because the word is doing two
jobs.** There is a frame — activations carry a model, layers carry updates to
it — which has now been made precise three separate times, by three groups,
for three different algorithms: one gradient step per linear-self-attention
layer, a Sherman–Morrison ridge update in `O(d²)` width, `k` Iterative Newton
steps in `k + 8` layers. And there is an identification, that the algorithm is
gradient descent. The frame is `Active` and the identification is `Rejected`,
and the *reason the frame survives is the same fact that sinks the
identification*: the architecture expresses all three at comparable cost, so
no construction of this kind can pick between them. I put that consequence in
`THEORY-tmpknb4d`'s title, because leaving it in the body would let the
document be read as a vote for the mesa-optimizer picture.

**The relation between the two took a second pass.** I wrote `extends` first —
the frame is the identification widened, which is what `extends` is for — and
then reread the blurb for `corrects`: *"this account replaces the earlier
one's reasoning. What the old account explained may still happen; only the
reason was wrong."* That is the sentence, almost exactly. Transformers do
solve regression from context, do improve monotonically with depth, and do
reach the optimal estimator. The reason offered for it was wrong. `ADR-031`
exists for this and I nearly filed past it, the same way I nearly filed
`extends` where `corrects` belonged three units ago.

**Two practices, from two different papers, and neither is about in-context
learning.** `LIT-tmpvwpn4`'s Hypothesis 1 / Hypothesis 2 distinction — "there
exist weights such that" is not "pretraining produces weights such that" — is
a general warning about a composition that looks reasonable at every step:
observe a capability in the wild, reproduce it in a purpose-built setting,
explain it from the purpose-built setting. `LIT-tmprd6ad`'s rate measurement is
a general method: output similarity cannot separate algorithms that converge to
the same answer, and the paper demonstrates this on itself by reporting that
gradient descent fits well at later layers while being the wrong description.
Both `Proposed`, both with `promote_when` asking for the method to work
somewhere its author was not looking rather than for a second paper agreeing.

**The thing neither side has done is the same thing.** Every result in this
cluster is either a construction or a measurement on a model trained on the
task family it is then tested on. `LIT-tmprd6ad`'s layer-to-iteration probe
would transfer to a model pretrained on text — pick a prompt task with a known
optimal algorithm, measure the rate — and I cannot find anybody who has run
it. That is in both new practices' `promote_when` and in the rejected theory's
closing section, because it is the measurement that would move all three.

**Declined: `ARXIV-2111.02080`.** Xie et al.'s implicit-Bayesian-inference
account is a genuine rival explanation and is not filed here, because it
answers a different question — why the capability emerges from pretraining,
not what the forward pass computes. Putting it in this unit would have made
the dispute look like a four-way disagreement about one question when it is
two questions. It is a unit of its own and the scope note says so rather than
leaving it to look like an oversight.
