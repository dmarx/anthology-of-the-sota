Unit G of `#304`, and the last on its worklist. Ranked seventh of seven, on
honest grounds — word2vec is weak substrate, mentioned three times and only
as a benchmark task. It was expected to be tidying. It was not.

## The thing that was in the literature the whole time

`#304`'s running theme has been that the negative count is not a quality
dial. The record accumulated three independent arguments for it across units
A, B and F:

- `THEORY-085` — the InfoNCE bound cannot certify more than `log N`.
- `LIT-591` (SimCLR) — batch-size gaps "decrease or disappear" with longer
  training.
- `SOTA-tmptiobf` (SigLIP) — performance saturates at 32k, measured to 1M.

Unit G adds a fourth, and it is the oldest and the bluntest. From 2013:

> `k` in the range 5–20 are useful for small training datasets, while for
> large datasets the `k` can be as small as 2–5.

**The number of negatives that helps decreases as the data grows.** Stated
without emphasis, as an implementation note, ten years before the vision
line spent substantial compute establishing that scaling it up stops paying.

Two things follow and they pull in different directions.

**The first is a caution about this record's own framing.** It would be easy
to write "the field believed more negatives were better". The field did not,
uniformly — one half of it said otherwise, early and in plain language. What
happened is closer to two literatures not reading each other, and the
anthology's job is to notice that rather than to smooth it into a consensus
that never existed.

**The second is a caution against overclaiming the agreement.** The
objectives are not the same. Skip-gram's `k` is negatives per positive
against a noise distribution; SigLIP's batch is negatives drawn from the data
itself. Nobody has connected them, and the documents say so. Four arguments
agreeing in *direction* across four literatures is worth recording; four
arguments about the same phenomenon is a claim the record cannot make.

## The sentence that licenses the whole line

Negative sampling descends from NCE, which approximately maximises the log
probability of the softmax. The paper drops that property deliberately:

> we are only concerned with learning high-quality vector representations, so
> we are free to simplify NCE as long as the vector representations retain
> their quality

**Negative sampling is not an estimator of anything.** It is a task chosen
because its by-product is good, and its authors said so in the paragraph that
introduced it.

Every contrastive document in `#304` inherits that posture and almost none
inherits the sentence. `THEORY-085` arrives at the same place from the other
end — the InfoNCE bound is a certificate that saturates while the
representation keeps improving — so the record now has the claim stated twice,
once as a licence taken in 1½ pages of 2013 prose and once as arithmetic on
an inequality. Worth holding together: **in this whole lineage the objective
is a means, and the papers that are clearest about it are the oldest.**

## A measured constant nobody has explained

The noise distribution is the unigram raised to the **3/4** power, which
"outperformed significantly the unigram and the uniform distributions … on
every task we tried". No derivation then, none since, and it is still in
library defaults.

Filed in the practice rather than the note because a practitioner inherits it
silently. It belongs to the same family as the four initialisation lines unit
F catalogued — CLIP's clipped temperature, SwAV's low `ε`, BYOL's weight
decay, SigLIP's bias at −10. **A constant with no derivation is not a
principle, and the record should say which of the numbers it carries are
which.**

## On filing a paper the record does not lean on

`LIT-tmpkfqzo` is filed knowing it is weak substrate, and the note says so in
its own standing section. The precedent is the vision-evidence pass —
`LIT-421`, `LIT-422`, `LIT-424`, `LIT-395` — where the record deliberately
took pre-modern groundwork.

The justification here is narrower and better: the *second* paper carries
negative sampling, which `#304` found the record could not name, and the
first is its antecedent. Filing a weak-substrate paper because a strong one
`extends` it is a different act from filing it because it is famous, and the
note is written so a later reader can tell which happened.
