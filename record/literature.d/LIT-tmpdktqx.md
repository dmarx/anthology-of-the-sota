# LIT-tmpdktqx: Flow-GRPO: Training Flow Matching Models via Online RL

Liu, Liu, Liang, Li, Liu, Wang, Wan, Zhang and Ouyang, CUHK MMLab,
Tsinghua, Kuaishou (Kling), Nanjing University and Shanghai AI Laboratory
(2025) — [ARXIV-2505.05470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05470). NeurIPS 2025. Read at v5 (27 Oct 2025); v1 (8 May
2025) was checked for what changed.

## Key takeaways

- **ODE to SDE without retraining** (§4, Eqs. 7–9, App. A). The marginals
  of dx = v dt are matched by the reverse SDE dx = [v − (σ_t²/2)∇log p_t]dt
  + σ_t dw for any σ_t. For the linear path the score is a function of the
  velocity, ∇log p_t(x) = −x/t − ((1 − t)/t)v(x) (Eq. 27). So the sampler
  needs only the trained v_θ, and Euler–Maruyama gives the update (Eq. 9).
  The schedule is σ_t = a·√(t/(1 − t)) with a = 0.7.
- **The point is a tractable policy.** Each step is an isotropic Gaussian,
  so the PPO-style ratio is a density ratio and the KL to the reference
  model is closed form, a weighted squared difference of velocities (§4).
  The ODE sampler would need divergence estimates for the same ratio, and
  explores nothing beyond its initial noise.
- **Denoising reduction** (§4, §5.3, Fig. 7a, App. D). Rollouts use 10
  sampling steps, evaluation the default 40. This is over 4× faster in
  wall-clock to the same final reward. Five steps is no faster. The 10-step
  SDE samples show visible artifacts — colour drift, blurred detail
  (Fig. 19) — and the relative advantage still carries signal.
- **The headline results** (Tables 1–2), SD3.5-M at 512 px with LoRA rank 32,
  group size 24, KL β 0.04 (0.01 for PickScore), on 24 A800s (App. B):
  - GenEval 0.63 → **0.95**; position 0.24 → 0.99, counting 0.50 → 0.95.
  - Visual text accuracy 0.59 → **0.92**.
  - PickScore 21.72 → **23.31**.
  - On FLUX.1-Dev with PickScore, 22.62 → 23.97 on DrawBench (App. C.4,
    Table 7).
- **The KL term earns its place** (Table 2, Fig. 6, App. C.5). Without it,
  GenEval training takes DrawBench Aesthetic from 5.39 to 4.93, DeQA from
  4.07 to 2.77 and ImageReward from 0.87 to 0.44. With it they are 5.25,
  4.01 and 1.03, at the same 0.95. On PickScore, removing the KL leaves the
  quality metrics intact but collapses diversity: "different seeds
  producing nearly identical results". The KL run reaches the KL-free
  reward, only later. The paper's point is that this is not early stopping.
- **Group size and noise level matter** (Figs. 5, 7b). G = 12 and G = 6
  collapsed on PickScore and G = 24 did not. On OCR, a = 0.1 learns slowly,
  0.7 and 1.0 equally fast, and "too much noise" degrades images to zero
  reward.
- **It beats the alternatives it ran** (Fig. 4, App. C.1, Fig. 8, Tables
  5–6). With the same group size per step it beats SFT on the best sample,
  reward-weighted regression and DPO, each offline and online, on GenEval.
  On PickScore it beats DDPO (run through the same SDE), which "eventually
  collapses", and ReFL. Against ORW its test reward is 29.89 at step 960,
  where ORW has fallen from 29.15 at step 480 to 23.05.

## Where the hedges are

Per [DP-010](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-10):

- **"Outperforming GPT-4o"** on GenEval (§1, Table 1). The training prompts
  are generated by GenEval's own scripts and templates, weighted towards the
  base model's weak categories. The reward is GenEval's detector pipeline.
  The test set is deduplicated against training, but it comes from the same
  distribution. GPT-4o's 0.84 is zero-shot. The out-of-template evidence is
  T2I-CompBench++ (Table 3), where 2D-spatial rises 0.2850 → 0.5447 and
  texture falls 0.7338 → 0.7236. Held-out object classes go 0.64 → 0.90
  (Table 4).
- **"Very little reward hacking"** (abstract) is measured by other reward
  models — Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward, UnifiedReward and PickScore on
  DrawBench. There is no human evaluation, and no diversity metric in the
  main table. Diversity appears only against ORW (Table 6: 0.96 → 1.02 on
  50 prompts × 64 images). On the PickScore task the reward and an
  evaluation metric are the same model, which the paper says may be why
  quality looks intact without KL (§5.3). v1's abstract said "little to no
  reward hacking"; v5 says "very little".
- **v1 tried a multi-reward ensemble and dropped it** (v1 §5.3). Adding
  quality and preference rewards to the task reward "led to local
  blurriness and reduced diversity". v5 no longer mentions it.
- **"The first method to integrate online policy gradient RL into flow
  matching models"** (abstract). The paper cites concurrent GRPO on
  flow-matching speech (F5R-TTS), earlier online reward-weighted fine-tuning
  of flow matching (ORW) and adjoint-matching fine-tuning. The claim is
  true of policy gradient on image flow models, narrowly.
- **The noise schedule is singular at the noise end.** σ_t = a√(t/(1 − t))
  is infinite at t = 1, where sampling starts, and the drift's σ_t²/(2t)
  term goes with it. The paper does not say how the first step is taken.
- **Evaluation samples with the ODE.** Training optimizes the SDE policy
  and the reported images use a 40-step ODE (App. C.7). That is sound only
  because the two share marginals for the fine-tuned model too. Discretized
  at 10 steps they visibly do not (Fig. 19).
- **One run per setting**, no seeds or intervals. Most curves are figures,
  and GenEval training to 0.95 takes several thousand GPU hours (Fig. 7a).

## Which comparisons are like for like

- **SFT, RWR and DPO (Fig. 4, App. C.1)** share the model, the reward and
  the group of samples per step; only the update rule differs. Fig. 8's
  caption says its x-axis counts training prompts because DPO's tuned batch
  size differs. Only online DPO's β is reported as searched.
- **DDPO and ReFL (Fig. 8)** are run by the authors on SD3.5-M with
  PickScore. DDPO goes through Flow-GRPO's own SDE, so this compares the
  update rule rather than DDPO as published. Their hyperparameters are not
  given.
- **ORW (Tables 5–6)** follows Flow-GRPO's PickScore setup, with ORW's step
  frequency chosen from four values.
- **Table 1** is Flow-GRPO trained on GenEval against other models' published
  zero-shot GenEval. Not like for like.

## Standing in the anthology

It extends GRPO ([LIT-127](LIT-127.md)). The group-normalized advantage (Eq. 4), the
clipped ratio and the KL to a reference (Eq. 5) are DeepSeekMath's,
unchanged. What Flow-GRPO adds is a way to make a deterministic generator
into a policy GRPO can use. It is the record's first evidence for [SOTA-145](../practices.d/SOTA-145.md)
outside language: the baseline from a group of samples for the same prompt,
with no critic, carries over to images. The group-size result says the group
has to be large enough — 24 here — for the estimate to hold. The objective
is the published one, with std normalization. [SOTA-146](../practices.d/SOTA-146.md)'s corrections are not
applied; Dr. GRPO's length bias cannot arise here because every trajectory
has the same T, but the std term can.

**On [SOTA-265](../practices.d/SOTA-265.md).** The conversion rests on that practice's identity and puts
it to a second use. Eq. 7 is the family of SDEs the stochastic-interpolant
paper ([LIT-645](LIT-645.md)) derives, which Flow-GRPO cites alongside Score SDE ([LIT-722](LIT-722.md)).
One trained velocity, and therefore one score, gives an SDE for every σ_t
with the same marginals. Flow-GRPO depends on the fact that σ_t is not fixed
by training: it chooses one after training, on a frozen model, with no
retraining. It extends the practice in what σ_t is chosen for. [SOTA-265](../practices.d/SOTA-265.md)
tunes the coefficient for sample quality. Here it is an exploration knob
for RL, set by how fast reward rises (a = 0.7, Fig. 7b), and the sampler it
tunes is used only during training. What it does not do is measure the
quality side: no FID or quality score is reported as a function of a, only
the qualitative statement that 40-step SDE and ODE samples are "visually
indistinguishable" (App. D) and that too much noise breaks generation.
It is a second group using the freedom, not a second measurement of what
tuning it buys.

The base model is SD3.5-Medium from the SD3 line ([LIT-449](LIT-449.md)), whose rectified
flow transformer and published GenEval rows anchor Table 1, beside SDXL
([LIT-566](LIT-566.md)) at 0.55. Its offline and online Flow-DPO baselines apply DPO
([LIT-169](LIT-169.md)) through Diffusion-DPO's form. They improve with training and stay
below Flow-GRPO.

**The record holds none of the RL-for-diffusion papers it builds on.** DDPO,
DPOK, Diffusion-DPO, ReFL and ImageReward are cited here and not filed. The
nearest filed relatives are Step-Video-T2V ([LIT-624](LIT-624.md)), which applies
Diffusion-DPO to a video flow model and finds modest gains, and HyperNoise
([LIT-491](LIT-491.md)), which is what [SOTA-302](../practices.d/SOTA-302.md) rests on. That practice says to steer a
*distilled* generator through its input noise, because the KL anchor that
stops reward hacking is intractable in weight space for a one-step model.
Flow-GRPO is the opposite case, and the comparison sharpens the practice's
condition rather than contesting it. For a multi-step flow sampled
stochastically, each step's KL is closed form, and weight-space LoRA tuning
with that anchor held quality on the reward models it was checked against.
Nothing here tests a distilled model.
