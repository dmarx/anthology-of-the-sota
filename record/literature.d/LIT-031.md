# LIT-031: Pruned Neural Networks are Surprisingly Modular

Filan et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2003.04881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04881)

## Key takeaways

- Makes "is this network modular?" answerable by importing the definition from
  graph clustering — **a module is neurons with strong internal and weak
  external connectivity** — and measuring it by spectral clustering the weight
  graph of an MLP
- **The control is the paper.** Pruned networks are sparse, and sparse graphs
  cluster more easily, so the comparison that matters is against **random
  networks with the same sparse weight distribution** — and trained+pruned MLPs
  are significantly more modular than those too
- **Networks trained with dropout are much more modular** — a regulariser with a
  measurable structural consequence, not only a generalisation one
- Small MLPs on small images, with notable spread in the n-cut values on
  CIFAR-10

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmpqvwzr](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqvwzr.md). The document is `Superseded`, correctly: interpretability
moved to mechanistic circuit-level work on transformers, and a connectivity
statistic on pruned MLPs is not where it went.

**Two of the four takeaways asserted results the paper does not contain.**
"Feature disentanglement" is not measured — the paper measures connectivity, not
features — and "impact on generalization" is not studied at all; no
generalization claim appears anywhere in it.

What survives is the methodological move: **when an intervention produces a
structural property, control against a random baseline matched on the
intervention's incidental statistics**, not only against the pre-intervention
one. Structural claims about networks routinely omit that control.
