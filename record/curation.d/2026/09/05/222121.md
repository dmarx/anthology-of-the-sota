Groups 3, 4 and 5 of [#17](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/17) — 27 notes. The catalogue described them as gaps,
and filling them mostly did what filling gaps does. Three things it did that
a catalogue could not predict.

## The record now holds a disagreement it was only half of

[SOTA-124](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-124.md) says high-quality data can be repeated freely when its epoch
size exceeds the model's memorization window, which is how Falcon-H1-Tiny
repeated Tulu3 a hundred times. It was filed *Proposed* with a note that this
"compares with the standing guidance that repetition past about four epochs
stops paying" — guidance the record did not contain.

It does now, and there are three positions rather than two:

- **Four epochs** ([LIT-166](../../record/literature.d/LIT-166.md)) — up to four epochs of repeated data
  changes loss negligibly against unique data; past that the value of added
  compute decays to zero. 400 runs, up to 900B tokens and 9B parameters.
- **Epoch size, not epoch count** ([SOTA-124](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-124.md)) — one figure and an argument
  its own authors call early.
- **Augmentation** ([LIT-175](../../record/literature.d/LIT-175.md)) — autoregressive pretraining overfits
  severely under repetition, but the overfitting belongs to the *objective*
  and is removable, so hundreds of epochs become productive.

The third is the useful one because it makes the other two falsifiable in
shared terms: if augmentation is what buys productive multi-epoch training,
a recipe repeating a source a hundred times is either augmenting implicitly
or paying an unmeasured cost. And the first two may not conflict at all — one
repeats a whole corpus at fixed compute, the other one source inside a
mixture where a small share means a long delay between repeats. Nobody has
run the experiment that separates them, which is now written down where the
practice is.

## Two model reports explained a design the record had only observed

Kimi K3, Qwen3.8 and DeepSeek-V4 all ship a multi-token-prediction head that
is later fine-tuned into an EAGLE-3-style draft model. Each report states it
as settled fact. The record had recorded the arrangement three times without
holding either paper.

Filed together, they explain it, and the explanation is a coincidence rather
than a design: [LIT-163](../../record/literature.d/LIT-163.md) proposes multi-token prediction as a *training*
objective that improves sample efficiency at no training cost, mentioning
faster inference as a side-effect. [LIT-185](../../record/literature.d/LIT-185.md) designs a draft model whose
structure happens to match what that objective leaves behind. Neither
proposes the combination. The model reports discovered it, and which of the
two motives is doing the work at frontier scale is not isolated by any of
them.

## The residual constraint is now the least defended part of its own practice

[SOTA-136](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-136.md) recommends mHC — widen the residual into several streams, mix them
with doubly stochastic matrices. Within one month two groups disputed the
*constraint*, from opposite directions:

- **oHC** ([LIT-151](../../record/literature.d/LIT-151.md)): the doubly stochastic set is bounded above but not
  below, so mixing can only shrink what distinguishes the streams, and the
  streams homogenize with depth.
- **sHC** ([LIT-181](../../record/literature.d/LIT-181.md)): the same set is degenerate near the identity, and
  its non-negativity forbids subtractive feature disentanglement entirely.

Different arguments, same verdict, opposite remedies — a rotation group
versus a spectral sphere. Meanwhile Qwen's Gated Residual ([LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md)) drops the
mixing matrix altogether and matches.

What all five papers agree on is untouched: widening the stream helps, and
the mixing needs *some* constraint. That agreement is probably the practice
the record should carry, and [SOTA-136](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-136.md) currently carries something narrower
and more contested. Left as a note rather than acted on — rewriting a
practice to be about what its literature agrees on is a bigger move than a
backlog pass.

## My own check caught my own bug

Running `luria lint` over the finished 186-note corpus produced one
`source-mismatch`:

    `arxiv: 2404.19737` resolves to "Better &amp; Faster Large Language
    Models via Multi-token Prediction", not "Better & Faster Large Language
    Models via Multi-token Prediction"

arXiv's Atom feed serves escaped XML and the resolver never unescaped it. A
false positive, and the kind that gets a category switched off — fixed
upstream at the fetch rather than at the comparison, so the lockfile records
a title rather than markup.

Worth recording as a pattern rather than a bug: **every fixture had an ASCII
title.** The check was tested against invented data that shared an
unexamined assumption with the code, and only a real corpus of 186 notes
had an ampersand in it. This is the third time in two days that running
something on the actual record found what the tests could not.

## Costs

The lint now takes about a minute on a contribution that adds 16 notes,
because `network = "auto"` fetches each unknown identifier with a courtesy
delay. That is the design working — the alternative is a freshly typed wrong
citation passing silently — but "bounded by how many citations a
contribution adds" reads differently at 1 than at 27, and a batch this size
is better served by running `luria remotes --resolve` once up front.
