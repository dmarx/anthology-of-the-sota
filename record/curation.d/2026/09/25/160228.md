Every earlier pass over the papers-feed tracker ranked it by **revisits**,
the number of reading sessions. This one ranks it by **cumulative reading
time**, and it is a candidate queue, not a filing backlog: nothing was filed.
The query is now a script, `src/scripts/audit/reading_time.py`, so the next
pass runs the same query rather than a description of it.

**Snapshot of 2026-09-25: 3,920 works, 187 held. 87 unheld works with at
least ten minutes of reading. 14 rows promoted (13 works), 16 held back
as in-scope but not first, 57 declined — 34 of them on scope.**

## What the signal is, measured

The tracker records `duration_seconds` and `idle_seconds` separately, and
`duration_seconds` is already net of idle: in 5,124 of 6,290 sessions it is
less than `total_elapsed_seconds`, and in 105 it is more, which is
unexplained. So the sum is *active* time, not tab-open time. The median session is 20 seconds; the 99th percentile is 835.

`#180` found the feed with 187 papers and a maximum of 308 seconds. It is 20
times larger now and the signal has range: 127 works cross ten minutes, 40 of
them already held.

**The two rankings disagree where the earlier passes never looked.** The
revisit worklist has worked down the high-session tail: only 63 unheld works
have five or more sessions, and most of those are the social-science and
physics clusters `ADR-052` counted. Reading time surfaces works with **one to
four sessions** — 2,696 unheld works have exactly one session, so a long
single read sits in a tie class hundreds of places deep. Of the 14 promoted
rows, 8 rank below 150th by revisits and 3 below 1,000th.

## Three traps, for whoever runs it next

**One paper is filed under several keys.** `2401.00001`, `arxiv.2401.00001`,
a `url.*` hash whose URL is the arXiv PDF, and a bare title are four rows of
one paper. Ranked unmerged, its time splits and it falls. The script merges
by arXiv id (from the key or the URL), then by normalised title.

**"Just a moment..." is a title 169 sessions share.** It is the Cloudflare
interstitial a publisher serves instead of the page. Merged by title, those
169 unrelated papers become the single most-read work in the feed, at 11,905
seconds. So do the URL-hash titles the feed records when it could not read one.
Both are excluded from title-keying, and a test pins it.

**Mentioned is not held.** The first cut marked a paper "in the record" if
its id appeared anywhere in `record/`. That counted `2510.20520` as held
because a journal entry declines it. Held now means a LIT note carries the
id or the title.

## Promoted, by the reason for promoting it

The three reasons are the 2026-09-23 catalogue triage's. They are reused
because they are the right test, not because they worked twice: a reading
feed is an input, which is what `#180` established, and these reasons are
about what the *record* needs, not about whether someone already asked for
the paper.

**Substrate — the record leans on it and does not hold it.**

- **Cramming** (Geiping & Goldstein, ICML 2023, `2212.14034`), 2,440s.
  Filed in the feed under an MLR Press PDF hash, which is why no title-based
  pass could have seen it. It is a BERT-style masked LM trained on one GPU for
  a day, and it asks which of the published improvements survive a fixed
  compute budget. The record filed BERT and RoBERTa this week and has a
  `tiny-models` topic; this is the controlled study both are missing.
- **Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network** (Hinton et al.,
  `1503.02531`), 615s. 110 files mention distillation and none holds the
  paper the word comes from — the same shape as `LIT-439` and diffusion.

**Trunk — the named origin or successor of something held.**

- **On the distance between two neural networks** (Bernstein et al.,
  `2002.03432`), 2,320s. The antecedent of the modular-norm line that
  `THEORY-024` and its practices rest on. The record holds signSGD (`LIT-280`, 2018)
  and Modular Duality (`LIT-438`, 2024), and not the 2020 paper between
  them, which introduced the distance the later line builds on.
- **Revisiting Scaling Laws: the Role of Data Quality** (ACL 2025) and
  **Sub-Scaling Laws** (`2507.10613`) are **one work**: same author list,
  retitled for the ACL version. 1,895s combined. It argues that scaling laws
  bend when data density or repetition is high, which puts it against
  `LIT-028` and the data-constrained note.
- **Reconciling Kaplan and Chinchilla** (`2406.12907`), 840s. It explains the
  gap between two held documents, `LIT-028` and `LIT-068`, by how each counts
  parameters and at what scale each fit.
- **Diffusion Meets Flow Matching** (Gao et al., 2024, a blog post at
  `diffusionflow.github.io`), 1,445s. It argues that Gaussian flow matching
  and diffusion are one family, differing in weighting and network output.
  That joins `LIT-630` and `LIT-036`. It would be a `url:` source, which
  `ADR-009` allows as a last resort.
- **ScheduleFree+** (`2605.19095`), 1,025s. The successor of `LIT-213`.
- **Diffusion Models With Learned Adaptive Noise** (MuLAN), 965s. The
  successor of Variational Diffusion Models, which is held and is the
  sixth-most-read work in the feed.
- **Jadbabaie, Lin & Morse, *Coordination of groups of mobile autonomous
  agents*** (2003), 610s. `NOTE-092` names it as the consensus proof for
  switching networks; the record holds only the later `LIT-304`.

**Claim — carries an instruction or a controlled result a topic can hold.**

- **Model Merging in Pre-training of LLMs** (`2505.12082`), 1,285s. It
  merges checkpoints along a constant-learning-rate run instead of annealing.
  The record has one late-training averaging practice (`SOTA-288`) and nothing
  on this.
- **Recurrent-depth latent reasoning** (Geiping et al., `2502.05171`),
  1,081s, eight sessions. Revisits could have reached it: it is 36th, and 10th
  among unheld works. But no pass gave it a disposition. Test-time compute spent by iterating a block in latent space.
  `model-architecture` has nothing on depth recurrence.
- **Fine-tuning image-conditional diffusion is easier than you think**
  (`2409.11355`), 965s. A negative result of the kind `DP-005` wants:
  Marigold's multi-step advantage came from a scheduler bug, and once that is
  fixed single-step inference matches it.
- **H-Net / Dynamic Chunking** (`2507.07955`), 885s. End-to-end learned
  segmentation instead of a tokenizer. `representation-and-encoding` holds
  nothing tokenizer-free.

**Below the ten-minute line, the substrate test still fires twice.** Lin et
al., *Common Diffusion Noise Schedules and Sample Steps are Flawed*
(`2305.08891`, 400s), is the source of the zero-terminal-SNR rescaling that
`NOTE-351` measures and `NOTE-346` reports, and it is not held. Smith et al.,
*Don't Decay the Learning Rate, Increase the Batch Size* (`1711.00489`,
380s), is in `NOTE-106`'s bibliography and not held. Neither was found by
ranking. They were found by asking the substrate question of the tail, which
is a different query (`DP-004`).

## Held back: in scope, not first

Sixteen works would be on a topic's axis and have no document depending on
them yet. They are ZipNN, the German Commons corpus, ZO-AdaMU, parallel
low-rank adapters (LTE), HyperCloning (Scaling Smart), SPlus, the
condition-number proxy, *Diffusion Models Generate Images Like Painters*,
geometric memorization in diffusion, test-time scaling with flow maps,
outsourced diffusion sampling, the EBM hitchhiker's guide, GFlowNets, Mallat's
group invariant scattering, the statistical comparison of transformers
against feedforward and recurrent networks, and Chen, Su & Xu on Byzantine
distributed learning (the ACM "Just a moment..." entry, 2,660s).

Two of these have a trigger worth stating. **ZO-AdaMU** waits for MeZO, its
trunk: the record holds the evolution-strategies fine-tuning papers, which
are among the most-read works in the feed, and no zeroth-order optimizer. File
MeZO first, and this becomes a successor. **SPlus** becomes a trunk successor
once the record decides whether Shampoo/SOAP get a note of their own. They are
mentioned in 28 and 11 documents.

## Declined, with counts

- **`D-scope`, 34 works, 51,671s — 47% of the ten-minute band's time.**
  Quantum foundations (10), law, politics and security (8), other sciences (5,
  including the single largest total in the feed: an 11,500-second
  planetary-science paper), complex systems and emergence (5), network science
  (3), Bayesian nonparametrics (2) and neuroscience (1).
- **`D-variant`, 18** — a method variant with no held document depending on
  it: Nested Attention, the FFT mixer, MELoRA, Atom of Thoughts, VertexRegen,
  and similar.
- **`D-position`, 3** — Tensor Logic, a retrospective, an annotated
  algorithm: no claim a document could carry.
- **`D-unresolved`, 2** — an OpenReview id (`j4-a3SNyaY`, 1,520s over eight
  sessions) and a Semantic Scholar PDF. Neither resolved from here: OpenReview
  redirects to a login and Semantic Scholar returned 429. Not declined on
  merit. They are the two highest-attention works nobody has read the title
  of.

## The scope finding, stated rather than acted on

`D-scope` is not the same decline as the other three, and `ADR-059` says why.
The topics are an axis, not a scope, so if the anthology wants a document it
cannot place, that is a finding about the vocabulary. Nearly half the time in
this band goes to subjects no topic can hold. Quantum foundations alone takes
9,260 seconds across ten works and network science 8,640 across three. Those
are not stray reads.

`ADR-052` already counted and rejected "the physics of learning" and
"scientific domains". That decision is still the right default and this pass
does not reopen it. But `2510.20520` was declined on 2026-09-21 with the
sentence "a revisit ranking measures interest, which is not the same quantity
as relevance". Reading time is the stronger measure of interest, and it
points at the same clusters at twice the volume. Whether the anthology
**wants** them is the owner's question, and it cannot be answered by a
ranking. What the ranking can do is make sure the question is asked once, in
writing, instead of being answered by a sequence of silent skips.
