# SOTA-445: When the base text-to-image diffusion model is better than the models that generated the preference pairs, train on the pairs with Diffusion-DPO rather than fine-tuning on the preferred images

## Source

Wallace, Dang, Rafailov, Zhou, Lou, Purushwalkam, Ermon, Xiong, Joty and
Naik (2023), [LIT-774](../literature.d/LIT-774.md) — Diffusion-DPO, §5.2, §5.5, Fig. 3, Fig. 7
and Supp. S6.

## What to do

You have a preference dataset of image pairs, preferred and rejected, for
the same prompt, and a base model to improve. If the images were generated
by models weaker than your base:

- Do not fine-tune on the preferred images alone. Imitating them pulls the
  model toward images worse than it already makes.
- Train on the pairs with the Diffusion-DPO loss, against a frozen copy of
  the base as the reference. The signal is which of the two images the
  model should get better at denoising relative to the reference, and that
  can improve a model even when both images are below its level.

When the data are better than the base, the source found SFT helps too, so
the choice is not forced there.

## Evidence

**SFT on the winners hurts the stronger model** ([LIT-774](../literature.d/LIT-774.md), §5.5).
Fine-tuned on the preferred images of Pick-a-Pic, for the same schedule as
DPO at learning rate 10⁻⁹, SD1.5 improves, with a 55.5% win rate over its
base. For SDXL, "any amount of SFT deteriorates the performance … even at
lower learning rates". The authors attribute the contrast to the data:
Pick-a-Pic was generated by SDXL-beta and Dreamlike, which are better than
SD1.5 and worse than SDXL-1.0. No number is given for the SDXL arm.

**DPO on the same pairs helps it** (§5.2, Fig. 3). DPO-SDXL, trained on
Pick-a-Pic v2's 851,293 pairs, is preferred to the untuned SDXL base on
PartiPrompts 70.0% of the time on general preference, by majority vote of
five crowdworkers per comparison. Fig. 7 shows median PickScore rising for
SDXL trained on pairs it did not generate.

**The paper's reading** (Supp. S6) is that this matches the summarization
setting of DPO ([LIT-169](../literature.d/LIT-169.md)), where a model already tuned for the task does not
gain from fine-tuning on preferred outputs.

## Conditions

- **The SDXL SFT arm is a sentence, not a measurement.** The comparison
  that motivates the practice has one number on one side. The SD1.5 SFT
  win rate is reported without saying how it was evaluated.
- **The learning rates differ.** SFT ran at 10⁻⁹, DPO at its β-scaled rate
  (about 8·10⁻⁹ at β = 5000). The text mentions lower rates for SFT, not
  higher ones.
- **One dataset, one strong base.** Every SDXL result is on Pick-a-Pic. The
  claim's condition, base better than data, is inferred from which models
  generated the data and is not measured.
- **DPO's own derivation assumes the pairs come from the reference.** They
  do not here, and the method works anyway; the paper does not say what
  objective it then optimizes. The note's hedges set this out.
- **It is offline.** Online methods that sample from the current model,
  where the data are the model's own, face a different question;
  [SOTA-449](SOTA-449.md) is one of their practices. Flow-GRPO ([LIT-779](../literature.d/LIT-779.md)) finds
  online DPO ahead of offline in its flow-matching comparison, which this
  practice does not address.
- **The language-model recipe runs the other way.** There SFT usually comes
  before preference optimization. The source's reading is that the
  difference is the domain gap: a pretrained text-to-image model is already
  doing the task, so there is nothing for an SFT stage to teach.

## Known implementations

- DPO-SDXL, the source's model. DPO-SD1.5 is trained the same way, but
  its base is weaker than the data, so it is not a case of this practice.
