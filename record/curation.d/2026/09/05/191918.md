[LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md) through [LIT-143](../../record/literature.d/LIT-143.md) were filed in a session with no outbound network.
This entry is what came back when every one of them was checked against
its source.

## The headline is that the notes held up

All twenty-five arXiv identifiers resolve, and every resolved title matches
the recorded title exactly. That is worth stating plainly because the prior
was the opposite: the batch includes five works published after the filing
model's training cutoff, and identifiers are the easiest thing in the world
to confabulate. They were not confabulated. Neither were the hard numbers.
[LIT-134](../../record/literature.d/LIT-134.md)'s Attention Residuals note gives GPQA-Diamond +7.5, Minerva Math
+3.6, HumanEval +3.1, a 1.25x compute advantage and sub-2% latency
overhead; all five are in the paper, verbatim. [LIT-135](../../record/literature.d/LIT-135.md) claims Qwen3.8-27B
carries the same configuration as Qwen3.6-27B — every field of the two
`config.json` files is identical, which is a stronger claim than the note
makes. [LIT-142](../../record/literature.d/LIT-142.md)'s indexer warm-up (1,000 steps, 16 sequences x 128K tokens,
2.1B tokens) is exact. [LIT-139](../../record/literature.d/LIT-139.md)'s whole DeepSeek-V4 paragraph — 33T and 32T
tokens, the 4K/16K/64K/1M ladder, dense warm-up for 1T tokens, sparse
attention introduced at 64K — is exact.

## Where it broke is more interesting than that it broke

Five errors, and four of them share a shape: **the note is precise where
its source is vague.**

- [LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md) said Tulu3 was repeated "on the order of 400 times". The blogpost
  says ">=100 repetitions". The direction of the error is invention of
  precision, not a misreading.
- [LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md) said learnable multipliers give "up to a 20% relative gain on
  MMLU, BBH and GSM8K" at 200 GT. The blogpost quantifies nothing here — it
  shows a plot and says "considerable and consistent". The preprint has the
  numbers, and they do not support the sentence: the average moves +1.10
  points, BBH +3.39 and GSM8K +2.61 carry it, and **MMLU is flat**, 47.98
  against 47.96. A benchmark named as improving was the one benchmark that
  did not move.
- [LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md) gave Kimi K3's context window as "1,048,576 tokens". The report
  says one million. 2^20 is the kind of number that is *right-looking*
  rather than sourced.
- [LIT-140](../../record/literature.d/LIT-140.md) read a single 6.7% overhead figure, stated once at expansion rate
  n = 4, as "6-7% at 3B, 9B and 27B" — the three sizes of a different
  experiment in the same paper, welded onto the one number.

The fifth is a naming error in the same family: [LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md) attributed
"MuonClip" to the K3 report, which never uses the word. It says "the
weight-clipping mechanism introduced in Kimi K2". The substance was right
and the citation was not.

The lesson for the next offline batch is narrow and usable: **prose about
mechanisms survived without a network; digits did not.** Where a note is
vaguer than you expect, that is usually the source being vague, and
sharpening it is where the error enters.

## A hedge that was right, and worth keeping the habit

[LIT-139](../../record/literature.d/LIT-139.md) said DeepSeek-V4 drops QK-Clip and added "the report's own wording
has not been read". The report's wording is "Consequently, we do not employ
the QK-Clip technique in our Muon optimizer", and the reason is an RMSNorm
on queries and compressed KV entries. The guess was correct, and the hedge
still earned its place — it is what made the claim cheap to resolve rather
than something to re-derive. That is the pattern to keep.

## What the network turned up that no amount of care would have

Two papers, both from after the batch was written:

- **oHC** ([LIT-151](../../record/literature.d/LIT-151.md)) argues against [SOTA-136](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-136.md) on its own terms. mHC's doubly
  stochastic constraint bounds the mixing factor at one from above and
  nothing from below, and the paper proves the slack is spent shrinking the
  differences between streams — so the widened residual homogenizes with
  depth, which is the thing widening it was for.
- **The Qwen3.8-Next design report** ([LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md)) runs mHC, Attention
  Residuals and its own Gated Residual in one harness. They come out level.
  [LIT-134](../../record/literature.d/LIT-134.md) and [LIT-140](../../record/literature.d/LIT-140.md) both said, correctly at the time, that no such
  comparison existed.

Neither retires anything. oHC is one model size from one group and says so;
mHC ships in a 1.6T-parameter model. But [SOTA-136](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-136.md)'s *reason* is weaker than
it was this morning, and a reader meeting it should meet both papers.

## A finding outside this batch

The check was run over all 139 arXiv identifiers in the record, not just
the new ones, and 53 of the pre-existing 118 fail. Some are truncations —
[LIT-100](../../record/literature.d/LIT-100.md)'s title is the real paper's title with the subtitle dropped. Many
are not: [LIT-099](../../record/literature.d/LIT-099.md) is filed as the PaLM 2 technical report against an
identifier belonging to a paper on quantum secret sharing. These came in
with the migration and are recorded here rather than fixed, because the
right repair is per-entry and wants its own contribution.
