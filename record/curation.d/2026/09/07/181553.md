The NoPE practice was going to be filed on one laboratory's evidence — Kimi
Linear's ablation and K3's deployment — at `Proposed` and `unreplicated`. It
is filed at `Active` and `emerging` instead, on three, and the difference came
from four lines of a related-work paragraph.

## What happened

Deciding the consensus value meant deciding whether anyone outside Kimi had
done this. Kimi Linear's related work says its findings "align with prior
results [110, 7, 19]" and mentions SwanGPT [76] by name. Resolving five
bracketed numbers against the bibliography took one command and produced:

- **[110]** Yang et al., *Rope to Nope and Back Again* (Cohere, January 2025)
  — the paper this practice most wants, whose title is the lineage.
- **[76]** Puvvada et al., *SWAN-GPT* (NVIDIA, April 2025) — the same layout
  again, independently.
- **[7]** Barbero et al., *Round and Round We Go! What makes Rotary Positional
  Encodings useful?* — not filed, but the obvious next candidate.
- **[19]** DeepSeek-V3, whose decoupled RoPE head is the partial-RoPE version
  of the same idea.
- **[126]** Falcon-H1, already in the record as `LIT-120`, cited here for a
  base frequency near 10^11 that pushes it to a near-NoPE state — a fact the
  record's own Falcon-H1 note does not carry.

## Why the earlier passes missed them

The reference pass in [#40](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/40) worked from the *citation graphs* of five model
reports — what cites what — and these three were sitting in a sentence of
prose that says "others found this too". A citation graph cannot distinguish a
paper cited for its result from one cited in passing, and the numbers it
ranks by are the ones a bundled reference list produces. Reading one
related-work paragraph on a paper the record already holds found more, faster,
than the graph pass did.

## And one of them was a correction

SWAN-GPT converts an existing 8B RoPE model pretrained on 15T tokens to a
NoPE-global architecture by continued pretraining, at parity on short
benchmarks. `SOTA-151` had been asserting that a model which already exists
cannot take the NoPE option, and that assertion was doing real work: it was
the stated reason the two practices are not filed as contesting each other.
The reason survives, weakened — conversion costs a continued-pretraining run,
which most readers will not do — but the claim as written was false, and it
was written twice, in two practices, before anything checked it.

The general shape is worth keeping: a claim about what is *impossible* is the
most expensive kind a record can hold, because nothing routine ever tests it.
The lint checks that citations resolve and that statuses are current. It has
no way to notice a confident negative.
