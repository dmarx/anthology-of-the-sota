This unit went ahead of `#342`'s Tier B because the owner asked whether the
record held DINOv3. It did not, and the measurement that followed found
something worse than a missing successor.

## The measurement

31 documents reference the DINO family. 12 practices touch it. The record held
**DINOv2** (`LIT-599`) and, as of the previous unit, the registers paper. It
held neither the **origin** (`2104.14294`) nor **DINOv3** (`2508.10104`).

So the shape is the one this whole queue keeps finding — the record holds the
middle of a lineage — with one aggravating feature: **the previous unit made it
worse.** `LIT-662`'s headline evidence that DINOv2 regressed object
discovery is *35.3 corloc against DINO's 61.9*. I filed that comparison
yesterday against a paper the record did not hold, and did not notice.

## The defect worth writing down

`SOTA-365` is `Active` and `converged`: *"Learn without negatives by breaking
the symmetry: a predictor on one branch and a stop-gradient on the other."* Its
consensus note reads, in part:

> `LIT-216` (I-JEPA), which this record already held, is built on a predictor
> plus an EMA target; **the `#304` units still to file — DINO and DINOv2 —
> carry the same structure.**

That sentence does three things well and one thing fatally. It names its
evidence, it distinguishes adoption from replication elsewhere in the same
note, and **it says explicitly that the papers are unfiled.** And then it makes
a structural claim about them anyway.

DINO has no predictor. Its own words: *"our method can work with only a
centering and sharpening of the teacher output to avoid collapse."* Centering
and sharpening are two opposing operations on the teacher's output
distribution, and that opposition does the job the predictor does in BYOL and
SimSiam. The DINO line is a **second route** to negative-free learning, not more
adoption of the first.

So `converged` was resting partly on a miscount: two of the descendants it
counted were not in the family.

**The pattern: a gap the record has written down is still a gap.** Naming an
absence does not license reasoning across it, and the note's own honesty about
what it had not read is what makes the error legible now. `#304`'s SSL audit
listed DINO as a unit still to file; the gap was recorded and open for a month.

## What DINOv3 turned out to be worth

Not the model. The finding is that **the two things you measure come apart
under a long schedule**:

- ImageNet linear-probe top-1 **improves monotonically**, ViT-g and ViT-7B.
- Pascal VOC segmentation mIoU **declines after ~200k iterations**, and for the
  7B falls below its early levels.

Their phrase: *"a relative independence between learning strong discriminative
features and maintaining local consistency."* The global metric is improving
while the property people actually use these features for degrades.

This session has now hit that shape three times in different clothes. AlphaCode:
validation loss rises while solve rate keeps improving. `SOTA-284`: KL improves
monotonically while the coverage profile degrades. And here: classification up,
segmentation down. **The metric you watch and the property you need come apart,
and the direction of the divergence is not predictable from either.**

## Two defects in one recipe, on different axes

The interesting result of holding all four papers at once, which none of them
says alone:

| defect | axis | remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| high-norm token artifacts | model **size** — ViT-L and above | registers |
| loss of patch locality | training **duration** — after ~200k iterations | Gram anchoring |

DINOv3 is explicit that these are different: *"with the integration of register
tokens, patch norms remain stable throughout training"*, and locality rots
anyway. That is the same group confirming their own earlier fix worked and did
not cover this.

`LIT-599`'s thesis is that curation plus scale produces general-purpose
features. It does, **on the evaluations run** — and both things that went wrong
were invisible to those evaluations, one needing a larger model to appear and
the other a longer schedule. That qualifier has now been added to that note
twice in two days, by two different papers, and it is starting to look less
like a caveat and more like the finding.

## On going out of queue order

`#342`'s Tier B was next and this was not on it. The argument for jumping: the
previous unit created a dangling comparison against an unheld paper, and a
question from outside surfaced it. That is not a ranking failure so much as a
demonstration that **a queue cannot see the dependencies its own work
creates** — filing the registers paper is what made DINO load-bearing, and no
count taken before that unit would have ranked it.

Worth remembering the next time a queue looks finished: the last unit may have
added to it.
