# LIT-104: ReLoRA: High-Rank Training Through Low-Rank Updates

Lialin et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2307.05695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05695)

## Key takeaways

- Restarts LoRA repeatedly during **pretraining** — merge the adapter,
  reinitialise it, prune 99% of the Adam state, drop the LR to zero and
  re-warm — so a sequence of low-rank updates sums to a high-rank one
  (`rank(A+B) ≤ rank(A)+rank(B)`, usually strict)
- All three parts are required: restarts alone gain nothing, and restarts
  plus an optimizer reset **without** the jagged schedule **diverge**
- 1.3B on 23.1B tokens: full-rank 16.83 ppl, ReLoRA 17.27, LoRA+warm start
  18.23. **5.5 GB/GPU** saved and **9% wall-clock** at that scale
- **The ablation is the finding.** At 130M the full-rank warm start *alone*
  gives 25.46; adding all of ReLoRA's machinery gives 25.04. The machinery
  *without* a warm start gives 29.77 — worse than the warm start by itself.
  The paper's own table says most of the gain is the ordinary training it
  also requires

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-024](../notes.d/NOTE-024.md). `Proposed`, and the note now states the condition:
**evidence that the restart mechanism, rather than the warm start, is what
pays.** Table 6 poses that question and the paper does not answer it.

Worth carrying for the ablation as much as the method. It is the cleanest
example in this corpus of a paper whose headline contribution accounts for a
small fraction of its own measured gain — and it is the *authors* who
measured it, which is why the paper stays in the anthology rather than the
attic. Read alongside [LIT-156](LIT-156.md) on why challengers outperform their reported
baselines.

Nothing is sourced to this paper, and the reading deliberately creates no
practice.
