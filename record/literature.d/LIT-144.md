# LIT-144: oHC: Orthogonal Hyper-Connections on SO(4) via Quaternions

Guo et al., Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Baidu (2026) —
[ARXIV-2609.02672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02672)

## Key takeaways

- The diagnosis is a gap in mHC's ([LIT-140](LIT-140.md)) argument rather than in its
  results. Restricting the mixing matrix to the doubly stochastic matrices
  caps the factor by which a layer can rescale the residual streams at one,
  which is what stops the compounding that destabilises plain
  hyper-connections — but nothing bounds that factor from *below*.
- The paper proves what the slack costs: inside the doubly stochastic set
  the mixing step can reduce the norm of the streams only by shrinking the
  differences between them, leaving their mean untouched. The reduction
  accumulates over layers, so the streams grow more alike and the diversity
  the extra width was bought for is spent with depth.
- oHC constrains the matrix to the rotation group SO(n) instead, which can
  neither amplify nor attenuate in any direction. At the n = 4 of current
  HC models the group has a closed form as a pair of unit quaternions: no
  added parameters, no iterative projection — a fixed pattern of signed
  additions — and construction faster than mHC's Sinkhorn-Knopp.
- Four arms differing only in the mixing set — single-stream residual, HC,
  mHC, and iHC, which pins the matrix to the identity — put oHC ahead of all
  of them on bits-per-byte across the downstream suite.

## Standing in the anthology

Filed for the argument, not yet for a practice. It is the first result in
the record that reads *against* an entry the record already holds: the
