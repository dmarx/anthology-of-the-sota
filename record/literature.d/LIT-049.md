# LIT-049: Learning to Prompt for Vision-Language Models

Zhou et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2109.01134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01134)

## Key takeaways

- Names **prompt engineering as the deployment bottleneck** for CLIP-style
  models — "a slight change in wording could have a huge impact on performance" —
  and replaces it with **continuous context vectors learned end-to-end while
  every pretrained parameter stays frozen**. Two variants: unified context, and
  class-specific context
- **One or two shots beat hand-crafted prompts** by a decent margin; at 16 shots
  the average gain across 11 datasets is about **15%**, the highest over **45%**
- **Domain generalisation is strong** against the zero-shot model with
  hand-crafted prompts — surprising, since a learned prompt is the thing one
  expects to overfit
- **Failures are reported with a diagnosis:** on Food101 both CoOp and linear
  probing underperform, attributed to data with "intense colors and sometimes
  wrong labels", with the learning curves showing "seemingly an overfitting
  problem" and higher weight decay suggested as an unverified remedy

## Standing in the anthology

happens — to `adaptation-and-tuning`. Nothing is sourced here.

The transferable statement the record lacks: **replace hand search over a
discrete artefact with gradient descent over its continuous relaxation.** Prompt
optimisation here, textual inversion in `LIT-078`, and soft prompting in
language models are three instances of one move across two modalities.

The takeaway **"zero-shot capabilities" was misleading** — CoOp is *few-shot*
and needs labels. What it preserves is the zero-shot *model's* generality, which
is a different claim.
