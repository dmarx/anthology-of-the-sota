# LIT-072: Simple Open-Vocabulary Object Detection with Vision Transformers

Minderer et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2205.06230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06230)

## Key takeaways

- Open-vocabulary detection from a contrastively pretrained image-text ViT with
  minimal architectural addition, measured on **LVIS v1.0 val** for its long tail
  of rare categories
- **The transferable finding is about fine-tuning two pretrained encoders.**
  A shared learning rate is "clearly sub-optimal": the text encoder needs one
  about **100× lower**, which the authors suggest prevents catastrophic
  forgetting of what contrastive pretraining gave it. **Freezing it completely
  also fails** — the optimum is interior, not at a corner
- **The two metrics have opposite optima.** *"The optimal recipe for zero-shot
  transfer does not necessarily maximize in-distribution performance"* — the
  same-learning-rate setting that collapses `AP_rare^LVIS` *improves* `AP^OI`.
  One hyperparameter, two measures, opposite gradients

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-027](../notes.d/NOTE-027.md). Nothing is sourced here; open-vocabulary detection is not
a line the anthology tracks.

The reach is in the fine-tuning result. The record's adaptation practices treat
a fine-tune as one operation with one learning rate; this measures that the real
decision is **component-wise**, with a factor of 100 between components and a
non-monotone response. Paired with `LIT-079`'s prior-preservation loss it makes
two different instruments for protecting a pretrained component, neither of
which the record states.

Of the takeaways replaced, **"zero-shot capabilities"** was the third bullet in
this batch to use that phrase in place of saying what the paper does — and here
the paper's finding is that zero-shot capability is in *tension* with
in-distribution accuracy, which is the more interesting claim.
