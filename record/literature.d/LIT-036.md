# LIT-036: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

Ho et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2006.11239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)

## Key takeaways

- The paper that made diffusion produce good samples, via parameterisation
  rather than a new model class: predict the **noise `ε`**, keep the
  **reverse-process variances fixed**, and train on a **simplified, unweighted**
  objective instead of the true variational bound
- Establishes the equivalence that reframed the field — this parameterisation is
  **denoising score matching over multiple noise levels**, with sampling as
  annealed Langevin dynamics
- **Table 2 is the underquoted part.** Predicting `ε` is only *about as good* as
  predicting the posterior mean under the variational bound with fixed
  variances, and **much better** under the simplified objective. Target and
  weighting are not separable choices. Learning the reverse variances "leads to
  unstable training and poorer sample quality"
- **The correct objective is not the best one, and the paper says so:** the true
  variational bound "yields better codelengths… but the latter [simplified
  objective] yields the best sample quality." The field's foundational result is
  a deliberate mis-weighting of a principled loss
- FID **3.17** / IS 9.46 on unconditional CIFAR-10; LSUN 256×256 comparable to
  ProgressiveGAN. §4.3 measures that **most of the model's codelength describes
  imperceptible detail**

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-068](../notes.d/NOTE-068.md). Nothing is sourced here; `SOTA-188` is correctly sourced
to EDM, which derives what this paper set by hand.

The reading supplies provenance the record was missing. **`SOTA-187` rests on
the claim that most of a pixel-space model's capacity goes to perceptually
irrelevant detail**, which `LIT-062`'s reading records as "the paper's premise,
supported by the compression working at all". §4.3 here is the *measurement*,
two years earlier. And `SOTA-188`'s subject — that the loss weighting across
noise levels is a decision — is already visible as the gap between the
variational bound and the simplified objective.

Of the takeaways replaced, "fixed noise schedule" named the *forward* schedule;
the consequential fixed quantity is the **reverse-process variance**, which is
what was ablated and what destabilises training when learned.

**Where the construction comes from.** Sohl-Dickstein et al. ([LIT-439](LIT-439.md))
supplied the model: a slow forward diffusion that destroys structure, and a
learned reverse process that restores it, each step small enough to
approximate simply. This paper keeps that construction and adds the
parameterisation that made it produce good samples: ε-prediction, which
makes it denoising score matching across noise levels; fixed reverse
variances; and the unweighted simplified objective.

**How later papers measured against it.**

- Flow Matching ([LIT-630](LIT-630.md)) trains a DDPM baseline on its own U-Net (Table 1).
  DDPM scores FID 7.48 on CIFAR-10, 6.99 on ImageNet-32 and 17.36 on
  ImageNet-64, against 6.35, 5.02 and 14.45 for the straight OT path. On
  CIFAR-10 it beats flow matching on the diffusion path (8.06). The paper's
  budget statement contradicts itself, and DDPM may have been allowed more
  iterations.
- Rectified Flow ([LIT-636](LIT-636.md)) quotes DDPM at 1,000 steps as FID 3.21 in its
  literature table (Table 1b), where this paper reports 3.17. Against that it
  sets 2.58 for its 1-rectified flow, on a different (DDPM++) architecture. Its
  controlled comparison is against the VP probability-flow ODE, which it
  calls the continuous-time limit of DDIM, derived from DDPM. On the same
  architecture that is 2.58 against 3.93, while the 2,000-step VP SDE, which
  it calls equivalent to DDPM, scores 2.55.
- Stochastic interpolants ([LIT-644](LIT-644.md)) put InterFlow's CIFAR-10 FID of 10.27
  beside this paper's quoted 3.17, with no baseline retrained. On that quoted
  comparison, DDPM's samples are much the better.
- Dhariwal and Nichol ([LIT-699](LIT-699.md)) re-score DDPM's LSUN samples in their own
  evaluation and beat them with ADM at 1,000 steps: FID 1.90 against 4.89 on
  bedrooms and 5.57 against 17.1 on cats.
- Multisample Flow Matching ([LIT-789](LIT-789.md)) reproduces DDPM at its own
  hyperparameters on ImageNet-32 and 64. DDPM needs more evaluations for a
  worse FID than the minibatch-OT flow, 5.72 at 330 NFE against BatchOT's
  4.68 at 146 on ImageNet-32, and falls apart under few-step Euler sampling,
  362 FID at four steps (that paper's Table 7).
- Flow map matching ([LIT-793](LIT-793.md)) quotes those ImageNet-32 rows rather than
  re-running them: DDPM at 63.08 FID with 20 steps and 362.37 with four,
  against its own teacher-free flow map's 9.68 and 16.90.
