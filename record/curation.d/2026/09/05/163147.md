**The Falcon-H1-Tiny blogpost ([LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md)) and what filing it required
([ADR-009](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-009.md)).** The request was to add one
technical blogpost to the corpus. It turned into three pieces of work, and
the order they arrived in is the useful part.

**Getting the text.** The sandbox's egress policy blocks `huggingface.co`,
the Space's `hf.space` subdomain, the Wayback Machine and the authors' own
sites, so the article could not be fetched. The Space's page on
huggingface.co is only a wrapper: it embeds the article in an iframe and
carries none of its text, so pasting that page's HTML yields metadata
(created 2026-01-15, last modified 2026-01-16) and nothing else. A PDF
export of the article, supplied by hand, did work — after two false starts.
`pypdf` and `pdfminer.six` both import the system `cryptography` wheel,
which is broken in this image (`_cffi_backend` missing, a pyo3 panic on
import); `pymupdf` bundles its own renderer and extracted all 71 pages
cleanly. Worth knowing before the next PDF.

**Filing it.** `luria new lit` scaffolds `arxiv: '0000.00000'`, and the
scheme required the field. The blogpost has no arXiv posting — its own
BibTeX is `@misc{falcon_h1_tiny}` with no identifier — and the two ids it
could borrow (the Falcon-H1 report, the learnable-multipliers preprint)
name other documents. The wrong theory, briefly held: that the `arxiv:`
field is what the ARXIV remote resolves, so a wrong id would at least be
caught. It is not; the field is a plain required field, and the remote only
resolves `ARXIV-` codes in prose. A borrowed id would have passed silently.
Luria 0.7 ships `field_groups` with `require = "at-least-one"`, in exactly
this shape in its `knowledge-base` example, so the fix was configuration
plus a decision record rather than code.

**Upgrading.** The workflow pinned luria 0.4.2 in three places (two action
tags and a `pip install`); 0.8.0 and then 0.8.1 were published the same
day as this entry, the second carrying the two fixes below.
The upgrade's one visible effect on the record: `luria lint` now reports a
`status:` that carries a note, and `luria repair` moves the note to
`status_note:` and the successor to `superseded_by:`. It did so for eleven
documents, writing the successor as a one-item list under `superseded_by:`.
A `grep` for the key showed the key alone, I read that as "empty", and the
scalar I filled in by hand sat above the list item — invalid YAML that took
the next `luria index` down. The repair had been right; look at the file,
not the grep. Two other repaired notes ([LIT-031](../../record/literature.d/LIT-031.md), [LIT-042](../../record/literature.d/LIT-042.md)) named their
successor only in prose, which the repair cannot read; 0.8's lint asks for
the code in `superseded_by:`, and they now carry it. Two luria gaps came out of
[LIT-031](../../record/literature.d/LIT-031.md): an arXiv code in `superseded_by:` was truncated to `ARXIV-2110`
before the check saw it, and a `# inactive-ok:` comment in the frontmatter
was not read, so the only acknowledgement that reached the field was
file-scoped. Both are fixed upstream in 0.8.1 (dmarx/luria#163); the note
now names both successors and excuses the rejected one at the field.

**Tracing the references.** The blogpost cites 56 works. Ten of them are
invoked to justify a decision rather than to name a benchmark, a baseline,
a data source or a serving tool, and none of the ten was in the corpus:
the Falcon-H1 report the architecture and µP recipe come from, the
learnable-multipliers preprint, the Muon scaling report, the looping paper
that explains the chain-of-thought filtering, the three FIM papers, GRPO,
Falcon-H1R for the reasoning data, and a tiny-model DPO write-up that is
itself a blog post — the second `url:`-only note, filed a day after the
rule that allows one. Left out at first: Olmo 3, cited only to describe the
pipeline the authors depart from — and that was the wrong cut. An anthology
that keeps what it stopped believing ([DP-003](../design-principles.md#dp-3)) needs the predecessor filed,
not only the successor, or the departure has nothing to be traced back to.
Olmo 3 is now [LIT-130](../../record/literature.d/LIT-130.md), the three-stage recipe it states is [SOTA-129](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-129.md), its
<!-- inactive-ok: SOTA-130 — a Proposed practice, named as part of the chain -->
RL-Zero variation is [SOTA-130](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-130.md), and the anti-curriculum names both. Left out
still: SmolLM2, MobileLLM-R1, Gemma 3 and
Qwen3 as comparison points; FineWeb and FineWeb-Edu as sources; and every
benchmark paper. The abstracts had to come from search snippets — arxiv.org,
its export API and Semantic Scholar are all blocked from the sandbox — so
each note records what the paper establishes at abstract depth, and no
practice is drawn from any of them yet.

**What the blogpost is worth.** Single-run ablations at one size, on
benchmarks the authors call noisy at 90M. Every practice from it is scoped
to tiny models, and two are `Proposed` rather than `Active` — the
learnable multipliers because the larger-scale evidence lives in a preprint
not in the record, and the memorization window because the source itself
says the study is future work. Filing a hedge as a hedge is what the status
field is for.
