Six papers now, read end to end rather than by abstract. Four things the
readings changed that the earlier passes had wrong, missing, or filed on
trust.

**The scale evidence was understated and the independence was overstated —
in the same paper.** `LIT-234` runs evolution strategies at 14B and 32B
against OatZero and SimpleRL-Zoo, which is four times the ceiling `SOTA-154`'s
conditions section claimed. It is also Cognizant AI Lab with Qiu among the
authors, running `LIT-211`'s own method — the case that practice's promotion
condition explicitly excluded. **Both facts are load-bearing and they point
opposite ways**, and the temptation on finding either is to let it settle the
question alone. The practice now carries the 32B number *and* the sentence
"the independent evidence stops at 8B", because a reader needs both to know
what they are looking at.

That is the same error as the last pass in a new costume. Then it was refusing
evidence for a category the condition never named; here it would have been
accepting evidence the condition named and excluded, because the number was
impressive. **A condition is only worth writing if it binds in the direction
you did not want.**

**The dissent and the characterization are arguing about one measurement.**
`LIT-231` proves that random perturbations concentrate near orthogonality
to a useful direction, and that the leftover components accumulate as a random
walk — so ES cannot work at LLM scale. `LIT-230`, four months later,
measures that exact drift, agrees it is 40× GRPO's, and finds it harmless:
the gains survive zeroing every update below a single-step magnitude
threshold, so they live in a sparse coordinate subset — and in a *specific*
one, LayerNorm weights and attention projections, where GRPO's largest updates
land in token embeddings and the language-model head instead.

The gap between them is a line and a subspace. Lemma 1 bounds the angle
between a random vector and a single direction; if the useful set is a
`k`-dimensional subspace, the projection behaves differently and the argument
weakens by an amount nobody has computed. That is written into both readings
as an open question. **It is not written in as a refutation, because working
out which of two papers is right from a third document is how a record starts
believing its own summaries.**

There is a second, sharper tension neither paper notices: Hyper-ES's lemmas
are dimension-asymptotic, so read naively they predict ES gets *worse* with
scale, while every empirical paper in the line reports the opposite and
`THEORY-006` says why it should. Something in that argument does not
track the observation. Saying so is the useful contribution here; saying which
is not available yet.

**A prediction was confirmed and the promotion condition still was not met.**
`THEORY-006` asked for the density measurement repeated by an unconnected
group on a family other than Qwen2.5, or a mechanism predicting where the
transition sits. `LIT-230` cites the account by name, reasons that denser
solutions should mean fewer directions suffice, and measures exactly that at
0.5B, 1.5B and 3B. That is an independent group confirming a *consequence* —
arguably better evidence than a repeat, and **not the evidence the condition
asked for**: different measurement, same family, no prediction of the
threshold. The document now states all of that and stays `Proposed`.

Writing this down is the point. A condition met is a promotion; a condition
approached from an unexpected angle is a judgement call, and the judgement
call is where the bar moves without anyone deciding to move it.

**Two practices came out of readings rather than out of the papers' headlines.**
`SOTA-210` exists because two groups, independently, measured the same
thing the post-training literature does not report — that RLVR raises pass@1
and lowers pass@k, often below the base model. `SOTA-211` exists because
two groups reached one instruction from unrelated arguments: an empirical
variance diagnostic showing the antithetic second evaluation buys nothing when
responses are regenerated, and a derivation showing a leave-one-out baseline
preserves the expected field at half the cost. Neither paper's abstract leads
with the thing that became the practice.

**What this pass did not cover.** The line has at least two more members,
both cited by papers filed here and neither read: **Hoy et al. (2026)** and
**Abdi et al. (2026), *Evolutionary strategies at scale lead to catastrophic
forgetting***. The second is the paper `LIT-230`'s entire RQ2 is a reply
to, and the record currently holds the reply without the claim — which is the
shape `DP-004` warns about, arrived at by following citations forward and
never backward. `LIT-231` also cites **Sun et al. (2026)** for a 10×
space-efficiency result, and `LIT-229` cites **Korotyshova et al. (2025)**
for SFT-then-CMA-ES over SVD bases. Four candidates, named here so the next
pass does not have to rediscover them.

On reading depth: all five notes filed today are `Read` and mean it — full
text including appendices, with two exceptions stated in the notes themselves.
`NOTE-080` takes EGGROLL's Theorem 2 at its stated result and rate
without checking the Edgeworth expansion behind it, and `NOTE-079` takes
`LIT-232`'s resolvent derivation at its stated result without verifying
the conservativity theorem's proof. Both are recorded in the `Limitations`
sections where a reader will meet them.
