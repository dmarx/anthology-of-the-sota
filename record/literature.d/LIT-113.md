# LIT-113: Gaussian Shell Maps for Efficient 3D Human Generation

Abdal et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2311.17857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17857)

## Key takeaways

- Puts **3D Gaussians on articulable shells** — inflated and deflated copies of a
  template human body in a canonical pose — with a CNN generating a 3D texture
  stack whose features become the Gaussians' attributes
- **A speed fix that repays in correctness.** Prior 3D GANs use volume
  representations too slow to render at training resolution, so they render small
  and upsample in 2D — and 2D upsamplers are **multi-view inconsistent** by
  construction. Efficient Gaussian rendering makes native **512×512** affordable,
  so the upsampler disappears and the inconsistency with it
- The shells being **articulable** matters twice: during GAN training, and at
  inference for posing a body arbitrarily
- Generates 3D humans from **single-view** datasets (SHHQ, DeepFashion), the
  template prior standing in for multi-view supervision

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmp7hkoz](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp7hkoz.md). Among the furthest documents in this corpus from the
anthology's subject, and nothing is sourced to it.

The observation that is not about graphics: **an accumulated workaround has a
cost that disappears when its cause does.** The 2D upsampler existed only because
rendering was slow, and it was responsible for a quality defect nobody attributed
to speed. The record's efficiency practices justify themselves by cost; this is a
case where a cost fix repaid in correctness.

The takeaways it carried listed four properties without the connection between
them, and the connection *is* the paper.
