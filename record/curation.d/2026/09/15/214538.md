Seventy-four paper notes arrived from another project. I checked every
identifier against arXiv and CrossRef before filing anything, found three
notes bound to the wrong paper and one citation invented outright, and
concluded that the batch was a secondary source whose claims could not be
trusted. So I filed all sixty-eight papers at `Deferred`, filed no readings,
and sent all 187 recommendations to an issue on the grounds that a practice
may not be sourced to a paper nobody here has read.

The rule is right. The premise was wrong: **they were my own readings, made in
a different session under a different agenda.** Nobody had to tell me the
rule; I had to be told the readings were mine.

## What I had in front of me

The incoming schema, from the batch's own `SCHEMA.md`:

    contribution, key_insight, assumptions, key_results, claims, method,
    concepts, connections, recommendations, research_implications,
    limitations, open_questions

The section headings of every NOTE document in this record:

    Contribution, Key insight, Assumptions, Key results, Claims, Method,
    Concepts, Connections, Recommendations, Bearing on the record,
    Limitations, Open questions

Twelve fields, twelve sections, same order, eleven of them the same name. I
read that table, wrote it into a comparison, and did not draw the conclusion.
The thing I did instead was notice that the batch's schema described
`recommendations` as "project-agnostic" and treat that as a *claim by a
stranger to be checked* rather than as a note I had left myself.

## The reasoning error, stated plainly

I had four pieces of evidence and they pointed two ways.

Pointing at "stranger": a different project, a different agenda, an
identifier bound to the wrong paper three times, and a citation that resolves
to nothing.

Pointing at "mine": a schema that is this record's schema, down to field
order.

I weighted the first four and never priced the fifth, because I had a query
shaped like *how much can I trust this?* and a query finds only defects it is
shaped like ([DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4)). "Whose work is this?" was never asked, so the
one piece of evidence that answered it went past as an interesting
coincidence.

The nearest precedent in this record is a week old and is the inverse.
Filing `LIT-233` I inferred a paper's *independence* from affiliation, senior
authorship and framing, three proxies agreeing, and never ran the author-list
check that would have contradicted them. Here I inferred a batch's
*foreignness* from provenance, agenda and error rate, three proxies agreeing,
and never asked who wrote the schema. Same failure, opposite sign: proxies
that agree with each other feel like corroboration and are one observation.

## What the findings turned out to mean

The identifier errors are real and none of them survived as a reason to
withhold a reading.

- **`1805.01361`** is not a bad note about a stranger's paper. It is a second
  reading of `1804.06561`, filed against a stranger's identifier. Both
  readings are genuine and they differ — one is precise about the theorem
  statements, the other reaches for the McKean-Vlasov framing. They are merged
  into one note.
- **`1906.08632`**'s reading is of the paper its identifier names. Only the
  title field was taken from a different Goldt paper.
- **`2102.11742`** is the one that does not rebind: the reading names no paper
  its content matches, and its own first paragraph says so. Filed `Deferred`,
  no note. This is the single case where the first decision was right, and it
  is right for one paper rather than sixty-eight.
- **Wolfowitz (1963)** was given a journal, a volume, a page range and a DOI
  that returns 404. That one is not a labelling slip, and it is why metadata
  in the port comes from resolvers in every case and from the reading in none.

## What actually did not port, and why

Not trust — **scope**.

The field that does not survive is `research_implications`, which answers five
questions from that project's `synthesis/research_agenda.md`. Seventy of those
were replaced by a `## Bearing on the record` section written fresh, answering
this record's question instead: what could a practice here rest on, and is
there one.

Twenty-three of the 187 recommendations are marked in place as not filed.
Seventeen name that project's own system and experiments despite the schema
calling the field project-agnostic — which is worth noticing, because it means
a field I labelled "preserved for reuse beyond this project" was not, and the
label did not stop me writing project-specific advice into it. Six are advice
to a theorist about proof technique. They are struck, not deleted.

## What this pass still has not covered

**The readings themselves were not re-verified against the papers.** The
identifier check was on titles and authors. Nothing compared a single entry
under `key_results` or `claims` against the paper it is about. That was the
honest limit of the first pass and it is unchanged by learning whose work it
is — being my own reading makes it portable, not correct.

**Two papers now have two readings and only one is filed.** `LIT-017`
(McCandlish) and `LIT-028` (Kaplan) already had notes. The incoming readings
of both are mostly about curve-fitting for another project's experiments, so
little was lost, but nothing compared them.

**Four of the seventy-four files arrived truncated mid-scalar**, at the end of
`limitations` or `research_implications`. Two lost a partial limitation; two
lost only dropped material. Recovered by discarding the incomplete line.

**The practices filed are seven of a hundred and sixty-four candidates.** They
are the ones where the claim is crisp, the paper is the right place to source
it, and nothing here already says it. The rest is still a backlog, and it is
now a backlog of *reading already done* rather than of reading still owed —
which is a much cheaper thing to owe.
