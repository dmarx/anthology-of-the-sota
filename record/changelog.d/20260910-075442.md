### Added

- **`published:` on every practice and every reading note (`#119`).** The
  literature side already had it on all 224. The other two schemes now do:
  **24 practices** and **all 74 notes**, backfilled from the paper rather than
  typed.

  The rule was not invented for this — it was **discovered**, by measuring what
  the 183 practices that already carried the field agreed on. **175 of 183 set
  `published:` to their *primary* source's date**, meaning the first entry in
  `source:` (`ADR-010` makes the list ordered; `ADR-017` makes the first entry
  the one the claim rests on). A note takes the date of the paper in `paper:`.
  The other eight were wrong — see below.

- **`src/scripts/audit/check_published.py`**, wired into CI on both pushes and
  pull requests. Reports every stored date that disagrees with the document it
  was copied from; `--fix` rewrites them. Fired once on a real regression
  before being trusted: reinstating `SOTA-150`'s old value makes it exit 1 and
  name the primary source.

### Fixed

- **Eight practices carried the publication date of a source they no longer
  cite.** Every one is the same accident — the `source:` list was corrected and
  `published:` was left behind:

  | | said | should say | because |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `SOTA-008` | 2017-08-01 | 2017-06-01 | re-sourced to `LIT-007` |
  | `SOTA-025`, `SOTA-026` | 2019-11-01 | 2016-07-01 | re-sourced to `LIT-005` at `#114` |
  | `SOTA-047` | 2020-11-01 | 2020-06-28 | re-sourced to `LIT-219` |
  | `SOTA-048` | 2020-11-01 | 2018-02-15 | re-sourced to `LIT-220` |
  | `SOTA-113` | 2023-09-01 | 2022-07-01 | re-sourced to `LIT-224` (Orca) |
  | `SOTA-122` | 2026-01-15 | 2026-01-01 | re-sourced to `LIT-121` at `#58` |
  | `SOTA-150` | 2024-01-01 | 2017-01-01 | re-sourced to put `LIT-188` first |

  **`SOTA-048`'s date came from `LIT-051`, which is not in its `source:` list
  at all** — the practice was dated by a paper it had stopped citing.

  Recommendations and source lists are unchanged; only the derived date moved.
  Each is at a new version with the reason in its history.

### Found

- **`published:` is derived data stored by hand, and it had already rotted.**
  It is ground truth in exactly one place — a `LIT` note, where it is the
  paper's date and feeds the scheme's alias template. On a practice and on a
  reading note it is a **copy**, and a copy goes stale when its input changes.

  Nothing could catch it. `luria concretize` rewrites references, not values,
  and the lint has no rule relating one document's field to another's. The
  eight above sat undetected across at least three separate re-sourcing passes,
  including two (`#58`, `#114`) whose whole purpose was correcting `source:`.

- **luria has no cross-reference derivation.** `derive` reads a document's own
  fields (`derive = "{tags[0]}"`), so a scheme cannot say "take this from the
  document you point at". Storing and checking is therefore the only shape
  available, which is why this ships a checker rather than a config change.
  Worth revisiting upstream: a `derive` that could follow a reference would
  make 281 hand-maintained values into zero.
