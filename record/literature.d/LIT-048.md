# LIT-048: Train Short, Test Long: Attention with Linear Biases Enables Input Length Extrapolation

Press et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2108.12409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409)

## Key takeaways

- Deletes position embeddings entirely and adds a **fixed, non-learned linear
  penalty** to each attention score, proportional to query–key distance:
  `softmax(q_i·k_j + m·−(i−j))`, with one slope `m` per head
- Slopes are the geometric sequence starting at `2^(−8/n)` — for 8 heads
  `1/2¹ … 1/2⁸` — found by **"a brief manual exploration of around ten slope
  sets"**. The stated rule of thumb: slopes in (0,1), density increasing
  toward 0. **Making the slopes trainable did not extrapolate well and cost 3%
  training speed**
- Sinusoidal embeddings **cannot extrapolate at all** — degrading as soon as
  one token past `L` is added, at both `L = 512` and `L = 1024`. The **T5 bias
  also extrapolates**; Raffel et al. proposed that and never tested it, and
  this paper is where it is tested
- At 1.3B on a 461 GB corpus, ALiBi matches sinusoidal perplexity while
  **running faster and using less memory** — because it can be trained on
  shorter subsequences. The practical argument is training cost, not
  long-context quality
- **The authors' own deflation, Appendix B:** ALiBi's edge is *"largely
  explained by its improved avoidance of the early token curse"*, and future
  work "might achieve further gains by more efficiently exploiting longer
  histories" — i.e. ALiBi is not doing that

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmp3trot](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp3trot.md). Nothing is sourced to this paper; RoPE won, and ALiBi is
carried as the alternative that did not.

**The previous takeaway "theoretical analysis" was false.** There is no
theoretical analysis in this paper: the slopes come from trying about ten sets
by hand, and the mechanism claim is empirical. That bullet attributed to the
paper the one kind of contribution it does not make.

The reading's real value is the **early token curse**, and it belongs to the
record rather than to ALiBi. Every long-context claim in the corpus is a claim
that a model does better with more context — and here the authors of the
technique say most of their own measured improvement was *not* that, but the
easier job of predicting fewer tokens that have little context behind them.
The record states that confound nowhere.
