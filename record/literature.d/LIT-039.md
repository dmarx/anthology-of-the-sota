# LIT-039: Gradient Flow in Sparse Neural Networks and How Lottery Tickets Win

Evci et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2010.03533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03533)

## Key takeaways

- The question is why sparse networks trained from a random initialization do
  worse than the same structure obtained by pruning a dense one. The answer
  offered is gradient flow: sparse networks at initialization have poor flow,
  and that is what training from scratch has to overcome.
- The claim about lottery tickets is deflationary and is the reason to read
  this alongside [LIT-019](LIT-019.md). A rewound ticket re-learns the same solution the
  dense pruning run had already found — it lands in the same basin — rather
  than discovering that the sparse structure was independently a good
  architecture.
- That reframes the hypothesis from "sparse architectures are findable" to
  "the initialization carries information about the solution the dense run
  reached", which is a much weaker and much more specific statement.

## Standing in the anthology

**Carries no practice, deliberately.** It is analysis of a phenomenon the
record holds no recommendation about, and its own conclusion is a reason not
to expect a recipe from that phenomenon.

Filed as a real paper rather than retired because it is what stops [LIT-019](LIT-019.md)
being read as more than it is. A corpus containing the lottery-ticket
hypothesis and not its deflation would be carrying a claim the field has
since qualified, with nothing beside it saying so.

The note also carried the wrong first author for a year — attributed to
Frankle, presumably because it is a lottery-ticket paper and Frankle wrote
the lottery-ticket paper. That is the failure mode of describing a work from
its subject: the description is plausible in every respect except being true.
