# LIT-118: PhotoMaker: Customizing Realistic Human Photos via Stacked ID Embedding

Li et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2312.04461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04461)

## Key takeaways

- Fuses **an arbitrary number** of reference images into one **stacked ID
  embedding**, fused with the embedding of a class word ("man", "woman"), so
  personalization needs **no per-subject optimization at inference** — unlike
  fine-tuning (`LIT-079`) or per-subject embedding search (`LIT-078`)
- Because the identity representation is a **set**, more references give a more
  complete identity, and the paper claims both fidelity and diversity improve —
  unusual, since those normally trade, and unexplained
- **A capability falls out of the representation:** the stack is assembled from
  one identity during training, but nothing requires that at inference, so
  feeding images of *different* people **merges identities**. Reported as the
  side effect it is
- An **ID-oriented data construction pipeline** is presented as part of the
  method rather than as preliminaries

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-038](../notes.d/NOTE-038.md). Retagged to `adaptation-and-tuning`. The furthest paper in
this pass from the record's subject — human-face personalization on a
purpose-built dataset — and nothing is sourced to it.

The durable observation is that identity merging is a **second** instance of a
pattern `LIT-097` also shows: a representation chosen for economy delivering an
unplanned capability. Two independent data points is an argument for preferring
uniform representations over special-cased ones.

Of the takeaways replaced, **"style consistency" is not something this paper
claims**, and none of them mentions that the embedding is a *stack* of arbitrary
size, which is the mechanism.

**Audited by [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121); unsourced.** R1 — prefer a set-valued
conditioning representation when the number of references is not fixed — is the
transferable one, and the record has no practice about the *arity* of a
conditioning signal. Rated moderate by the reading, single-source, and in a
narrow domain; recorded as a candidate rather than filed.
