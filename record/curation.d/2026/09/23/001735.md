`luria` [LU-#311](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/311) turned unbound relations into a lint class. The effect
downstream was one new standing row, on a line nobody had looked at:

```
SOTA-036, SOTA-037, SOTA-038, SOTA-279, SOTA-280, SOTA-281
share no `tags` across the whole line
```

The arithmetic is unambiguous. `in-context-learning` is held by five of the
six; `SOTA-036` — the GPT-3 pretraining recipe — is the sole cause, and drop
it and the line binds.

## The tempting fix, and why it is the failure the alert names

`in-context-learning` on `SOTA-036` closes the row with one tag. The blurb
forbids it: *"getting behaviour out of a **fixed** model by what you put in
the context."* `SOTA-036` is how the model is made. Running it the other way —
`model-architecture` on the three chain-of-thought practices — is worse.

The `topics` vocabulary's `alert` says reaching for the nearest wrong tag is
the failure the closed set exists to prevent. This is what that looks like
from the inside: the wrong tag is *one line*, and the right answer is a
day's work.

## Every edge binds; only the path does not

```
SOTA-037 extends SOTA-036: model-architecture
SOTA-038 extends SOTA-036: model-architecture
SOTA-279 extends SOTA-038: in-context-learning, adaptation-and-tuning
SOTA-280 extends SOTA-279: in-context-learning
```
    [SOTA-281](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-281.md) extends [SOTA-279](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-279.md): in-context-learning

`unbound-relations` reads 0 and always would have. The line is what breaks,
because an intersection only shrinks as the component grows — which the report
has said since it was written and which nobody had had a reason to read.

`SOTA-038` carries `model-architecture` because **a previous pass put it
there**, in v2 on 2026-09-18, to bind that edge. Its own history entry gives
the reason: *"In-context learning is a capability of the decoder-only-at-scale
family [SOTA-036](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-036.md) describes, which is what the relation between them asserts."*
That justifies the **relation**, not the **document** — and the test is the
document's: would someone browsing `model-architecture` be right to expect
"ICL permits few-shot task adaptability"? They would not.

That pass ([#202](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/202)) retagged 49 documents and 27 carry a note like it. Sampled,
the others hold up: warmup really is `model-stability`, micro-batch size
really is `training-optimization`, flash attention really is
`systems-optimization`. `SOTA-038`'s is the one that does not, and the edge
check could not tell the difference. The path check could.

## What the line is actually about

`extends` licenses three senses: *a later version of the same recommendation,
a narrower case of it, or a rule that only exists because the earlier one is
followed.* The first two preserve subject by construction. **The third cannot**
— "only exists because" is exactly the move from one kind of claim to another.

`SOTA-279 → SOTA-038 → SOTA-280/281` are senses one and two.
`SOTA-037 → SOTA-036` and `SOTA-038 → SOTA-036` looked like sense three, and
that reading sent me toward splitting the relation — a new `enabled_by` for
"a rule that only exists because the earlier one is followed."

**That was one step too clever.** Read against the rest of the blurb —
*"could not stand without"* — the two edges are not a different kind of
succession, they are not a succession. `SOTA-036` is a *specific* recipe, and
"ICL permits few-shot task adaptability" does not depend on its corpus or its
context length; it depends on the class of model. And the connection that is
real was already written down: all three name `source: LIT-035`. `extends` was
saying "same paper" a second time, in a vocabulary that means something else,
and the chain read that second saying as a lineage of refinement.

So the fix was the plain one — delete both relations — and it is `ADR-tmpoglw4`.
`SOTA-038` also loses the `model-architecture` tag it was given to bind the
edge that no longer exists.

The general problem is still there: `extends` licenses a third sense that
cannot preserve subject, and an invariant over the chain asserts more than the
relation promises. It just was not this line's problem, and inventing the
relation here would have preserved a lineage that is not one.

## The gap that was actually there

Reading the six for a tag turned up a different thing. `SOTA-037` ("ICL
emerges at scale") and `SOTA-279` ("only once the model is large enough; below
~100B it does nothing or hurts") make a claim with no word, and the sweep for
its siblings found **nineteen documents, every one of them under
`analysis-and-evaluation`, for most of them as the primary**.

A word for methods was holding the field's largest live controversy about
learning dynamics: three mutually incompatible accounts of grokking, an
emergence claim and its published rebuttal, and a formal theory of staged
development. A reader browsing by topic met them in the same list as "state
the token count beside any reconstruction FID".

`capability-thresholds` is the nineteenth topic. It does **not** bind the line
— `SOTA-036`, `SOTA-038`, `SOTA-280` and `SOTA-281` correctly do not take it —
and saying so is the point. The row stays until the relation is fixed.

## What this says about the warn-first bargain

The row had been in `unbound-lineage.md` since the invariant was declared, and
was read the day a lint class put it in front of someone. The report was doing
its job and nobody was reading the report. That is the argument for the class,
and it is also the argument against the reflex it invites: the first thing a
new warning asks for is the cheapest thing that clears it, and here that would
have been one wrong tag on `SOTA-036` and a topic the record would still be
missing.
