# LIT-066: Data Movement Is All You Need: A Case Study on Optimizing Transformers

Ivanov et al., ETH Zurich (2020) — [ARXIV-2007.00072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00072)

## Key takeaways

- The claim that reframes the problem: despite the attention transformer
  optimization has received, existing implementations do not efficiently use
  GPUs, and the reason is that **training has become memory-bound**. Amdahl's
  Law plus enormous gains in compute throughput moved the bottleneck to data
  movement while the mental model stayed on FLOPs.
- Existing frameworks use suboptimal data layouts — a claim about defaults,
  not about a missing feature, which is why the fix is available to anyone
  willing to measure rather than requiring new hardware.
- The method is a recipe for globally optimizing data movement rather than a
  point optimization: classify every operator by whether it is compute- or
  memory-bound, then fuse and re-lay-out accordingly.
- Data movement reduced by up to 22.91%, for a 1.30× speedup over
  state-of-the-art frameworks on a BERT encoder layer and 1.19× on the whole
  of BERT. The authors present the approach as general to deep networks.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-088](../practices.d/SOTA-088.md) (fuse small operations into larger kernels), [SOTA-089](../practices.d/SOTA-089.md)
(align tensor dimensions to hardware boundaries), [SOTA-090](../practices.d/SOTA-090.md) (hardware-specific
memory layouts) and [SOTA-091](../practices.d/SOTA-091.md) (profile and optimize memory access patterns).

It is the empirical companion to [LIT-063](LIT-063.md): TVM builds the machinery for
fusion and layout search, this measures how much of a real workload's time
that machinery is addressing. It is also the argument FlashAttention
([LIT-074](LIT-074.md)) takes to its conclusion two years later — the same observation,
memory movement rather than arithmetic, applied to one operator with an
IO-aware algorithm instead of a compiler pass.
