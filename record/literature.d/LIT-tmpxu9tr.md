# LIT-tmpxu9tr: Automated decision-making as domination

Jenna Burrell (2024), *First Monday (vol. 29, no. 4)* — DOI-10.5210/fm.v29i4.13630

## Key takeaways

- Defining algorithmic injustice as allocative unfairness is a product of computer science's status and funding, not of the harms themselves. Redefining it as domination, following Iris Marion Young, widens the remedies from code fixes to law, participation, resistance and bans.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Burrell argues that ML-ethics research leans heavily toward distributive, computationally tractable definitions of fairness, and she explains the skew sociologically, through how computer science reproduces itself as a field. She holds that allocative fairness is an inherently limited account of justice. She proposes instead Iris Marion Young's account of injustice as domination and oppression, which she argues fits the algorithmic harms the community already acknowledges better. On that account, legislation, participatory assessment, user repurposing and resistance, and activism for outright bans all count as remedies alongside technical fixes.
