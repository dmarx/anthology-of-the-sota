**Filing LLaDA ([#56](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/56), batch B) turned up something about the registry rather
than about diffusion language models.** The paper's argument is that the
capabilities everyone attributes to LLMs are attributed to *autoregressive*
modelling specifically, and that the attribution is unearned: hold the
paradigm fixed, swap only the factorization, and an 8B model is competitive
with LLaMA3 8B. Whether or not that holds up, writing the note required
asking what the record would have to change if it did — and the answer was
not obvious, which is the finding.

## The qualifier nothing needed until now

Every practice here is advice about training an autoregressive transformer.
Not one of them says so, because nothing in the corpus has ever been anything
else. The assumption is not hidden, exactly; it is *ambient*, which is worse,
because an ambient assumption cannot be cited, contested, or scoped.

Sorting the registry against it, the practices fall into two groups that are
easy to name and were not obvious before the sort:

- **Indifferent to the factorization.** AdamW's decoupled decay, µP and
  CompleteP, warmup-stable-decay and the peak-LR power law, MoE routing and
  expert granularity, best-fit packing. These are about optimization and
  capacity. A diffusion language model would want all of them.
- **Load-bearing on it.** Fill-in-the-middle training, multi-token
  prediction, speculative decoding, KV-cache compression, context extension
  by RoPE rescaling. These exist *because* generation is left-to-right and
  cached. Under a masked-diffusion decoder some are unnecessary and some are
  incoherent.

## Why this is not an action item yet

The sort above is a first pass by one reader, and it has the shape of a
thing that will look obvious and be wrong in places — the boundary runs
through the attention material in particular, where a practice can be about
the cache (load-bearing) or about the attention pattern (not). Nothing in the
record currently needs the answer.

What makes it worth a devlog entry rather than nothing is that the cost is
asymmetric. Doing the sort now costs an afternoon with the practices already
in front of us. Doing it the week a frontier diffusion language model ships
means re-reading 153 practices under time pressure, which is exactly the
shape of work this record exists to prevent.

## The smaller lesson, on notes for things that are not practices

Three of batch B's four notes are outside the registry's subject entirely —
a closed video model's emergent capabilities, and two vision
self-supervision papers. The temptation is to skip them: no practice will
ever cite them, so what is a note for?

What a note is for, here, is the boundary. Each of the three says in its
Standing section what the record does *not* hold and what would move the
paper inside. That turns "the anthology has nothing on video" from a silence
— which reads identically to an oversight — into a filed position. Luria has a
design principle for this shape (an absence reads like success); the
anthology's version of it is that a subject nobody filed and a subject
somebody declined to file look the same from the index, and only the second
one is a decision.
