# LIT-103: SARATHI: Efficient LLM Inference by Piggybacking Decodes with Chunked Prefills

Agrawal et al., Microsoft Research India (2023) — [ARXIV-2308.16369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16369)

## Key takeaways

- The asymmetry that drives everything: prefill processes the whole prompt
  and saturates GPU compute even at small batch sizes, while decode emits one
  token per request per step and leaves the machine mostly idle. Serving both
  in the same system means one phase is always wasting the hardware.
- *Chunked prefills* split a prefill request into equal-sized chunks;
  *decode-maximal batching* then builds each batch from one prefill chunk
  plus as many decodes as fit. The chunk saturates compute and the decodes
  piggyback, costing up to an order of magnitude less than they would in a
  decode-only batch.
- Chunking a single prefill into several batches is what makes the coverage
  work — more batches to hide decodes in — and it makes batches uniform in
  cost, which is the second win: under pipeline parallelism the micro-batch
  imbalance between prefill and decode is what creates bubbles.
- LLaMA-13B on an A6000: up to 10× decode throughput and 1.33× end-to-end.
  LLaMA-33B on an A100: 1.25× end-to-end, 4.25× decode. GPT-3 with pipeline
  parallelism: bubbles reduced 6.29×, end-to-end throughput 1.91×.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-115](../practices.d/SOTA-115.md), overlap prefill and decode compute. It reached the
record under a fabricated citation naming CUDA kernels, which is the wrong
level: the win here is in how requests are batched, and it needs no custom
kernel at all.

The record's serving practices now sit on their real sources — continuous
batching on vLLM ([LIT-112](LIT-112.md)), fused attention on FlashAttention ([LIT-074](LIT-074.md)), and
prefill/decode interleaving here.
