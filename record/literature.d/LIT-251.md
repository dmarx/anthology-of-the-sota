# LIT-251: The Role of Permutation Invariance in Linear Mode Connectivity of Neural Networks

Entezari et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2110.06296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06296)

## Key takeaways

States the conjecture (§1; formalized as Conjecture 1, §3.2) that "if
invariances are taken into account, there will likely be no barrier on the linear interpolation of SGD
solutions". The authors call it "bold". The support is a proof in a very
limited setting plus indirect empirical evidence. The paper does not find
permutations that remove the barrier between deep networks. It names the
implications for ensembling and distributed training (§1, §5) but does not
test them.

- **Theorem 3.1 (the only theorem).** Take a fully-connected network with
  one hidden layer of h ReLU units, f(x) = v⊤σ(Ux). Sample U, U′ uniformly
  from [−1/√d, 1/√d] and v, v′ from [−1/√h, 1/√h], i.e. the networks are at
  random initialization, not trained. Fix an input x with ‖x‖₂ = √d. Then
  with probability 1 − δ there is a permutation of the second network such
  that the interpolated network's output at x differs from the interpolation
  of the two outputs by Õ(h^{−1/(2d+4)}).
  *Holds when:* one hidden layer, random initialization, a single fixed
  input. The bound is on network output, not on loss, and the paper's
  gloss calls it "no barrier at random initialization" (§3.3). There is no
  condition on dead neurons. The rate weakens as input dimension d grows.
- **Empirical — barriers without alignment.** Barriers grow quickly with
  depth (Figure 3). For MLPs and Shallow CNNs they first rise and then fall
  with width, which the paper likens to double descent. For VGG and ResNet
  they saturate at a high value and do not change with width (§2.2,
  Figure 2).
  *Holds when:* MLP, Shallow CNN, VGG and ResNet on MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10
  and CIFAR-100 (ImageNet in Figure 4 only). Training-loss barrier, measured
  at α = ½.
- **Empirical — "our model vs real world" (§3.5, §4.1).** Barriers between
  independently trained networks closely match barriers between random
  permutations of one network, before and after search (Figures 5, 7). This
  is the paper's main evidence for the conjecture. It is indirect: a set
  of permuted copies satisfies the conjecture by construction, and the paper
  shows the real set behaves like it. Git Re-Basin, [LIT-333](LIT-333.md), argues in its
  Appendix A.2 that this is also consistent with barriers between all
  solutions.
- **Empirical — permutation search.** Simulated annealing over n = 5
  networks did not reduce pairwise barriers significantly (Figure 6, left).
  Reduced to two networks, it lowered barriers for MLPs and Shallow CNNs on
  MNIST and SVHN. It reached zero barrier for MNIST MLPs at depth 1, and at
  depths 2 and 4 at width 2^10 (Figures 6–7). It gave no significant
  improvement as depth grew. For VGG and ResNet, the search found no
  permutation that reduced the barrier (§4, Appendix E.2).
  *Holds when:* SA with the train error of the averaged model as objective.
  Appendix B finds a greedy functional-difference matching does better
  than SA.

## What the evidence does not cover

- The theorem covers one hidden layer at random initialization, with output
  difference at a single fixed input. It says nothing about trained
  networks, depth, or loss barriers over a dataset. The paper hopes an NTK
  extension is possible and leaves it to future work.
- No permutation that removes the barrier was found for deep networks (VGG,
  ResNet). The paper names search strength as "the biggest limiting factor".
  It also says a failed search cannot separate a weak search from no such
  permutation existing (§3.4).
- Image classification only. The paper names language tasks as future work.
