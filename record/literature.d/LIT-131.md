# LIT-131: Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence

Kimi Team, Moonshot AI (2026) — [ARXIV-2607.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653)

## Key takeaways

- 2.8T total and 104B activated parameters, 16 of 896 routed experts per
  token under what the report calls Stable LatentMoE, native visual
  understanding, and a one-million-token context window. Weights released
  alongside the report.
- The attention stack is a layerwise hybrid: 69 Kimi Delta Attention layers
  ([LIT-133](LIT-133.md)) and 24 gated Multi-Head Latent Attention layers — about the
  3:1 ratio Kimi Linear settled on — so most layers carry a finite-state
  linear-attention memory and a minority keep global attention with a
  compressed KV cache.
- Attention Residuals ([LIT-134](LIT-134.md)) replace the fixed residual sum with learned
  attention over all preceding layers' representations.
- **Stable LatentMoE** is three changes, filed as answers to two failure
  modes that 56× sparsity brings on. LatentMoE separates full model width
  from routed-expert width, so 896 experts with 16 active is affordable —
  but the routed path then composes down-projection, a gated expert FFN and
  up-projection into nearly four consecutive matmuls, which at 2.8T explodes
  the routed branch's activations, and a fixed-step balancing bias stops
  behaving at ~900 experts.
  - *Normalized LatentMoE*: an RMSNorm between expert aggregation and the
    up-projection, so the routed branch's scale stops varying with which
    experts were selected. Reported to improve validation loss and
    downstream benchmarks, not only stability.
  - *SiTU-GLU* replaces SwiGLU ([SOTA-034](../practices.d/SOTA-034.md)). Both of SwiGLU's multiplicative
    factors are unbounded, so coincident large coordinates give activation
    outliers and overflow risk in low precision; the plain sigmoid gate is
    bounded but loses Swish's near-linear positive regime. SiTU-GLU applies
    a scaled `tanh` soft cap to the linear factor of the Swish gate and,
    independently, to the up branch — approximately linear near the origin,
    bounded far from it. K3 uses different cap constants for the two
    branches.
  - *Quantile Balancing*, below.

- **Quantile Balancing** keeps auxiliary-loss-free routing ([SOTA-148](../practices.d/SOTA-148.md)) and
  replaces its update rule. The original adds a fixed step to each expert's
  bias, whose size "trades off slow adaptation against load oscillation";
  with 896 experts per layer that trade stops being acceptable. QB instead
  sets each expert's bias directly from the router-score quantile that
  matches its target load, derived from a single forward pass by taking
  Top-(k+1) so the (k+1)-th entry is the cutoff an expert must beat. The
  quantile spans a global batch of millions of margins, so it is estimated
  from a per-expert histogram — one all-reduce over bin counts, exact up to
  bin width regardless of sharding, a few hundred bins per expert. As in the
  original, the update takes effect only on the next step, so no batch is
  routed with a bias derived from itself; the bias is frozen at inference.
- Table 1's comparison with K2 is the report's one quantitative account of
  what changed: 61 layers to 93, 1.04T to 2.78T total and 32.6B to 104.2B
  active, hidden dimension unchanged at 7,168, routed experts 384 to 896 with
  8 to 16 active and 1 to 2 shared, attention heads 64 to 96, vocabulary
  unchanged at 160K, training context 128K to 1M, MLA to hybrid KDA–MLA,
  SwiGLU to SiTU-GLU. The vision tower is new: 401M parameters, 27 layers,
  patch size 14, 12 heads.
- The headline claim is about the *rate* rather than the size: roughly
  2.5× the scaling efficiency of Kimi K2 ([LIT-132](LIT-132.md)) at matched compute,
  attributed to the three architectural changes together with refined
  training and data recipes.
- Optimization continues the Muon line: Muon on the matrix parameters
  together with what the report calls the weight-clipping mechanism
  introduced in Kimi K2 — QK-Clip ([SOTA-131](../practices.d/SOTA-131.md)) under a different name; the term
  MuonClip does not appear anywhere in the report — and, for the attention
  projections, **Per-Head Muon**, which the report gives its own section
  (§2.5). Instead of applying Newton-Schulz orthogonalization to the whole
  Q, K and V projection matrices, their momentum matrices are partitioned
  along the head dimension and each head's block orthogonalised separately.
  The stated intuition: full-matrix orthogonalization treats all heads as
  one coupled block, so heads with larger gradient or momentum scales
  dominate the shared update direction while smaller-scale heads receive
  insufficiently normalized updates. Claimed effects are more balanced
  learning dynamics across heads and better stability at larger scales, plus
  a small saving — Newton-Schulz on tall per-head blocks is cheaper than on
  the full matrix. No ablation is given for it.
- Long context is reached by extending the window progressively during
  training rather than at once ([SOTA-139](../practices.d/SOTA-139.md)), and by synthesising tasks that can only be
  solved by attending across the whole million-token window.
- **The scaling-law study prefers cosine decay to warmup-stable-decay**, and
  the methodological half is the interesting one. The team argues that
  comparing the two under one hyperparameter set is unfair, because their
  optimal peak learning rates and batch sizes differ substantially even at
  the same model size and token budget, so a shared setting silently favours
  whichever schedule it happens to suit. They therefore run an independent
  scaling-law search per schedule, at a fixed minimum learning rate; under
  each schedule's own optimum, cosine decay consistently reaches a lower
  final loss, and K3 adopts it. This is the opposite conclusion to the one
  [SOTA-140](../practices.d/SOTA-140.md) records, reached by the comparison design that practice's own
  evidence ([LIT-145](LIT-145.md)) argues for.
- A multi-token-prediction layer shaped like a backbone block is trained
  with the model and later fine-tuned into an EAGLE-3-style single-layer
  draft model for speculative decoding — [LIT-163](LIT-163.md) and [LIT-185](LIT-185.md) are the
  two halves that arrangement is assembled from.
- **No positional encoding at all on the MLA layers.** Unlike K2 and K2.5,
  K3 follows Kimi Linear in applying NoPE to every global-attention layer,
  and lets the intervening KDA layers supply position sensitivity and
  recency. The consequence the report names is the one worth carrying:
  extending the context no longer means retuning a RoPE frequency base or
  applying YaRN, because there is no positional parameter in the global
  layers to retune.
- Gated MLA adds "an input-dependent, channel-wise full-rank output gate" —
  [SOTA-134](../practices.d/SOTA-134.md)'s gate at a finer granularity than the head-specific one its
  source ablates.
- The RL infrastructure is reported at a scale worth recording: 51,219,741
  sandboxes across 1,505,678 images created over training and evaluation.

## Standing in the anthology

The current end of two chains the record now holds: the Muon line (Muon →
weight decay and RMS matching in [LIT-122](LIT-122.md) → QK-Clip in [LIT-132](LIT-132.md) → here) and
the hybrid-attention line (Falcon-H1's parallel heads in [LIT-120](LIT-120.md); Kimi
Linear's interleaved layers in [LIT-133](LIT-133.md) → here). No practice is sourced to it
directly — the practices it instantiates ([SOTA-131](../practices.d/SOTA-131.md), [SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md), [SOTA-133](../practices.d/SOTA-133.md)) are
sourced to the papers that introduced each change.

**What this note is read from, and what the report does not contain.** Every
claim above is checked against the report's full text. An earlier version of
this section said its ablation tables were still unmined; that was a guess,
and it was wrong. The report has five tables — an architectural comparison
with K2, two benchmark tables, an in-house web-development comparison, and a
table of third-party evaluations — and **no component ablations at all**.
The headline 2.5× figure is read off fitted scaling-law curves on held-out
out-of-distribution validation data, and the report attributes it to the
architectural, data and training changes *collectively*. So nothing here
apportions the gain between Attention Residuals, the hybrid attention, the
LatentMoE and the data recipe, and no number in the report supports doing
so. The one controlled comparison it does report is between learning-rate
schedules, and it cuts against [SOTA-140](../practices.d/SOTA-140.md); what that costs the practice is a
separate judgement, filed separately.
