The ternary box on [#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87) had nothing behind it — no BitNet, no ternary
anything, and the only `1-bit` matches in the record were 1-bit Adam gradient
compression, which is a different subject wearing the same number.

Filing it turned out to be less about ternary weights than about a shape the
record was missing.

## The record had one branch of a two-branch decision

Every quantization practice here acts on a **finished** model. [SOTA-185](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-185.md)
compensates rounding error into the columns not yet quantized; [SOTA-163](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-163.md)
chooses the block-scaled format to round into. Both assume there is something
to compress.

The other branch — decide the serving format first, train in it, never hold a
high-precision checkpoint — was not represented at all. That is a more
interesting gap than "we are missing BitNet", because it is a gap in the
*shape* of the advice rather than in the list of papers.

## What made it worth filing rather than noting

The two branches would be a matter of taste if the costs were symmetric. They
are not, and the record already held the asymmetry without anyone putting the
two halves together.

[LIT-186](../../../../literature.d/LIT-186.md), Scaling Laws for Precision, reports that post-training
quantization damage **grows with the number of training tokens** — far enough
that additional pretraining data becomes actively harmful for a model destined
to be quantized. [SOTA-160](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-160.md) already encodes that as "treat the token budget
and the quantization plan as one decision".

So the post-training route has a cost that rises exactly along the axis the
field keeps pushing. The from-scratch route does not pay it — there is no
compression step to be damaged by. Neither paper cites the other; the tension
is the record's to notice, which is the kind of thing [DP-007](../../../../principles.d/DP-007.md) says nobody is
prompted to write down.

## The discipline the paper forced

Its strongest claim is that matrix multiplication reduces to integer addition,
which is where the energy argument lives. That is true of the arithmetic and
not of the machines: it assumes kernels and eventually silicon built for
ternary operands, and the paper is candid about it — it "opens the door for
designing specific hardware".

So the practice separates two things the abstract runs together:

- **Measured on hardware you can buy**: weight memory and bandwidth. 2.22GB at
  3B, 3.55×; at 70B on two A100s, batch ceiling 16 → 176 and throughput
  333 → 2977 tokens/s.
- **Argued, conditional on hardware**: the disappearance of multiplication,
  and the energy saving that follows.

Filing the second as though it were the first would have recorded a roadmap as
a result. It is the rare paper here whose headline is a claim about hardware
that does not exist yet, and the note says so rather than letting the number
stand unqualified.

## The condition that is a result, not a caveat

It matches FP16 **from 3B**, and the table says why that is phrased carefully:
700M is 12.87 against 12.33, 1.3B is 11.29 against 11.25, 3B is 9.91 against
10.04. Behind, level, ahead.

A reader taking this to a small model is outside the evidence and the error
does not favour them. That crossover belongs in `## Conditions` as a number,
not in prose as "results may vary" — the difference between the two is most of
what a practice is for.

## Filed Proposed, and the `promote_when:` is the useful part

One group, one architecture, nothing at the frontier trained this way. The
lint refuses a `Proposed` practice with no `promote_when:`, which is a good
refusal: it makes you say in advance what would change your mind.

What would promote it is a second group training at or above 3B in any low-bit
format from scratch and matching full precision at equal tokens — the
architecture need not be ternary, because the claim is about *deciding the
format before the run*. What would not: a better post-training method (the
other branch), a ternary model below 3B (outside the evidence), or ternary
kernels shipping (which strengthens the compute argument without testing the
training claim).
