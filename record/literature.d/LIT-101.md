# LIT-101: AdaNorm: Adaptive Gradient Norm Correction based Optimizer for CNNs

Dubey et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2210.06364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06364)

## Key takeaways

- Keeps an EMA `e_t` of the gradient's **scalar L2 norm** and, when the current
  gradient falls below it, scales the gradient up to meet it:
  `s_t = (e_t/‖g_t‖)·g_t`. A **floor** under the gradient norm
- The correction feeds **the first moment only**. Applying it to the second
  moment is ablated and is worse, because `v_t` sets the step size — the
  method wants a stronger direction signal, not a longer step
- Generic across adaptive optimizers: instantiated on Adam, diffGrad, RAdam
  and AdaBelief
- **Evidence is small-scale CNN vision** — VGG16, ResNet18 and ResNet50 on
  CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet. The title ends "for CNNs". Largest
  reported gain 11.15% (ResNet50/TinyImageNet); `γ = 0.95` default

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmpx9gty](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpx9gty.md). `Proposed` because it is promising and unproven, and
the note now says what would settle it: **any evidence on a transformer, at a
scale where the record's other optimizer claims live.** There is none four
years on, and the absence is itself information — [LIT-156](LIT-156.md) is the paper
that explains why this whole wave of Adam variants did not transfer.

Kept rather than retired because of what reading it surfaced: AdaNorm is the
**exact mirror of [SOTA-035](../practices.d/SOTA-035.md)**. Clipping puts a ceiling on the gradient norm
and this record calls it `Active`; AdaNorm puts a floor under the same
quantity. Nobody has run both, and that question is worth keeping a document
for.

Nothing is sourced to this paper, and the reading deliberately creates no
practice.
