**`LIT-504` filed from [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) — the strongest of the remaining
candidates, and it earns that by asking a question the field had left tacit
and then actually counting.**

A mechanistic explanation has two parts: *what* algorithm, and *where* it sits
in the network. The two dominant strategies find them in opposite orders and
each has a criterion — circuit error for one, interchange-intervention
accuracy for the other. The question nobody had posed: do those criteria pick
out a **unique** explanation?

Four questions, one per component per strategy. All four come back no.

**What makes this a fact rather than a suspicion is the method.** Shrink the
problem until exhaustive enumeration is possible — MLPs of shape `(2, k, k, n)`
trained on two-input logic gates — and then count every circuit and every
mapping. "There are probably other circuits" becomes a number.

The numbers are worse than I expected. Median explanations rise from **38 to
910,000** circuit-first and **8 to 3,700** algorithm-first as width goes from
2 to 5. Under **2%** of networks have exactly one valid minimal mapping. **No
network** has exactly one circuit interpretation.

And both figures are **lower bounds**, stated as such: the enumeration is
capped at sparsity above 0.3 and restricted to two-input/one-output circuits,
and interpretations are counted only for the sparser circuits. So if the
numbers are wrong, they are wrong in the direction that makes the problem
worse. That is an unusually comfortable epistemic position for a negative
result and it is why the practice is `Active` on a single paper: exhaustive
enumeration over an exhaustible space cannot be underpowered.

**The framing I took from it.** Circuit error and IIA are *satisfiability
tests, not selection rules*. They establish admissibility and are silent on
cardinality. The criteria were doing less work than their use implies, and
nobody checked because checking requires a model small enough to exhaust — so
the gap was invisible to every instrument the field had. `DP-004`, again, and
in its purest form yet.

**Two things the paper does that I want to record approvingly.**

It considers sparsity as a tiebreak and rejects it, in one line: "Should we
dismiss an entirely different candidate explanation simply because it involves
one additional node than another?" The obvious fix, taken seriously and
declined.

And §5.1 does not overclaim. The pragmatic position — that an explanation
which predicts and supports intervention may be everything that is owed — is
presented as live rather than defeated, with the interpretability-illusion
debate cited as a case that was resolved exactly that way. The paper's
constructive recommendation is to **say which epistemic goal your explanation
serves**, which costs nothing and is the sentence that makes the finding
actionable rather than merely deflating. Both went into the practice.

**What I did not file.** Training on more tasks significantly reduces the
number of valid abstractions — `p = 0.05`, up to four tasks, then flat. It is
the only lever reported and it is thin, and acting on it means changing how
you train a model *in order to make it easier to explain*. That is a much
bigger decision than the evidence carries, and a practice recommending it
would be smuggling a large claim inside a small number.

**And a count I am still not turning into a principle.** This is the fifth
document today about a reported number being sensitive to something the report
does not state — after reconstruction FID and its token count, generation FID
and its seeds, recall bought with relevance, and the influence metric that
inverts on a working assistant.

Five is more than four and I flagged four this afternoon as a count rather
than a pattern. It is still a count. What would make it a principle is a
mechanism, and I do not have one that survives contact: FID seed variance and
circuit non-identifiability are not the same phenomenon in any sense I could
write down and defend. `DP-009` exists for exactly this temptation, and the
honest thing is to keep the tally in the documents where the instances live
and let a sixth either sharpen it or not.
