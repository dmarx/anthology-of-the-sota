# LIT-079: DreamBooth: Fine Tuning Text-to-Image Diffusion Models for Subject-Driven Generation

Ruiz et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2208.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242)

## Key takeaways

- Binds a **rare token** to a subject from ~3–5 images by fine-tuning the whole
  model, so the identifier then composes with ordinary prompts
- **The interesting part is the regulariser.** Narrow fine-tuning causes
  *language drift* and diversity collapse, and the fix is to **supervise the
  model with its own generated samples**: before fine-tuning, sample the frozen
  model on "a [class noun]" and include those in the objective as a
  class-specific prior-preservation loss. No external data — the model before
  you touched it *is* the regulariser
- **Beats textual inversion** on both subject fidelity (DINO, CLIP-I) and prompt
  fidelity (CLIP-T), by sizeable margins
- **The base model caps the method:** DreamBooth on Imagen beats DreamBooth on
  Stable Diffusion, "approaching the upper bound of subject fidelity for real
  images"
- **The super-resolution stages must be fine-tuned too**, or they hallucinate
  high-frequency detail they have not seen for this subject — 64→256 is
  "essential for most subjects". A pipeline-level finding a
  personalization-is-a-loss-function framing would miss

## Standing in the anthology

The transferable finding is one the record holds only in one domain:
**when narrow fine-tuning risks destroying a general capability, regularise
against the pre-fine-tuning model's own samples.** The post-training practices
treat KL-to-reference as an RL mechanism; prior preservation is the same
instrument in supervised fine-tuning, invented independently, for the same
reason. Stating it once, generally, would cover both.
