Filing the chain-of-thought trunk, the unbound-lineage report flagged
`LIT-035 ↔ LIT-467` as joined by nothing: GPT-3 tagged `model-architecture`,
chain-of-thought tagged `adaptation-and-tuning`. I added
`adaptation-and-tuning` to `LIT-035`, wrote a careful history note defending
the decision, and said in the pull request that "adding a tag to close an
unbound edge is precisely what `ADR-035` forbids".

Challenged on it, I went and read `ADR-035`. It does not say that.

## What it actually says

§3 moves the chain invariant from `primary_topic` to `tags` and states the
consequence plainly: **"A shared tag can now bind a relation, which is the
point and the risk."** The risk it then names is specific —
*"Binding on a real topic is the record saying what two documents have in
common. Binding on a free-form label is the `#101` move."* §4 removes two
`flash-attention` labels for being exactly that, and its decision table
weighs three newly-bound edges and **keeps two**.

Then §5 closes the vocabulary on `SOTA`, `LIT` and `THEORY` — which means the
failure mode §4 is about cannot happen on those three schemes any more. The
only tag you can add is one of the thirteen, each with a label and a blurb and
a decision behind it, and the truth test is the only question left.

And §7 restores eleven documents' old topics as secondaries. Additive
retagging, in response to a review pointing out that documents were not saying
what they were about, is the decision's own central action.

So: the report surfaces candidates, and acting on one by adding a true topic
is the intended response. That is nearly the opposite of what I said.

## Where the wrong rule came from

`CLAUDE.md`:

> Add a second topic when it is genuinely true, and **never to bind a
> relation** — the chain invariant reads the whole tag list now, so an
> *incidental* tag will bind an edge that should have stayed open (`ADR-035`).

The first clause is the real test. The third clause names the real hazard —
an **incidental** tag. The middle clause, read on its own, forbids a
*motivation*: not "don't add a false tag" but "don't add a tag *because* it
binds". That is unfalsifiable from the outside and, taken literally, makes the
unbound report unusable, since any tag you add after reading the report was
added partly because of the report.

I read the middle clause, repeated it, and built a defensive history note
around it.

## What I changed

`CLAUDE.md` now states the truth test and says what the decision rules out:
a label invented to satisfy the check, which the closed vocabulary has already
made structurally unavailable on three of the four schemes.

`ADR-035` gains a short section saying the rule positively. Nothing in the
decision changes — everything the section says was already in §3, §4 and §5,
distributed across a context, a decision and a consequence. That distribution
is how a compression went wrong, and a decision that can only be restated
correctly by someone who reads all 199 lines will be compressed wrongly again.

`LIT-035`'s history note loses its defence and says the plain thing: the
report found a document not saying what it was about, and the fix was to say
it.

## The thing I got right for the wrong reason

I left the `SOTA-036 → 037 → 038 → SOTA-279` line unbound rather than tagging
a prompting practice `model-architecture`. That was correct, and my stated
reason — a prohibition on binding — was not the reason. The reason is simply
that chain-of-thought prompting is not about model architecture. Under the
real rule the question was never "may I close this?" but "is the tag true?",
and the answer is no, so the line stays open and the report keeps reporting
it. Same outcome, and now it rests on something.

## Standing question for the person who was there

`ADR-035` is `Proposed`, and its consequences section says why:

> `exactly-one` was load-bearing for one thing and is no longer … in both the
> argument was about the vocabulary being *shared and small*, not about a
> document having only one subject. Nothing in either turns on the
> cardinality. This decision is filed `Proposed` because that reading should
> be checked by someone who was there.

That is a promotion condition naming a person, not a result, and the review
that prompted this contribution is close to being it. Left `Proposed`; this
contribution corrects a gloss, and promoting the decision is not mine to do.
