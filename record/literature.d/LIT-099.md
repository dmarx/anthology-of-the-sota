# LIT-099: PaLM 2 Technical Report

Anil et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2305.10403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10403)

## Key takeaways

- **Independently re-derives compute-optimal scaling at larger compute than
  Chinchilla and reaches the same answer**: data and parameters should grow
  "roughly 1:1", against the previous trend of scaling the model **3× faster
  than the dataset**. Independent replication at greater scale is the strongest
  evidence a scaling claim can get
- States the inference-economics corollary most papers leave implicit: *"it is
  more beneficial to invest more compute in training a smaller model compared
  to modifying a model's architecture to be more inference-efficient… generally
  more efficient to train a smaller model with more tokens, for a fixed
  inference and training budget"*
- Also checks the scaling fits against **downstream metrics**, not only loss —
  the half nobody replicates, and the half that matters for capabilities with
  thresholds
- **Data mixture is ranked above architecture**: translation pairs were a minor
  part of the mixture and were enough to put the model on par with production
  translation services
- **Memorization measured with designed canaries** — an *interleave* canary
  (two pre-training documents interleaved in batches of 50 tokens, preserving
  some linguistic structure) and a *shuffle* canary — deliberately between
  random outliers and unmodified text
