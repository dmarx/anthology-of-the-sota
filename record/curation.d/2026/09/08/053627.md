---
title: 'Widening the scope, and why the domain axis is a set rather than a scalar'
created: '2026-09-08T05:36:27'
tags: [ontology]
---

The scope was drawn around language-model training, and that turned out to be
wrong in both directions at once.

**Too narrow, already.** [SOTA-113](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-113.md) and [SOTA-115](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-115.md) are `Active` recommendations
about serving, filed under `systems-optimization`, and they had been sitting
outside the written scope for a year with nobody noticing. So had the
quantization practices. [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) asserted "scoped to language-model training by
construction" as a fact about the record; it was a fact about the sentence.

**Too broad, in the sense that mattered.** A scope drawn by domain declines
work whose transferable content is exactly the kind of claim the seven topics
collect. EDM contributes a preconditioning scheme, a noise-level sampling
distribution and a loss weighting. Segment Anything contributes a
model-in-the-loop annotation bootstrap. Those are a `training-optimization`
claim and a `data-pipeline` claim that happen to have been found in image
models, and declining them keeps the record ignorant of its own subject.

**The design fork, and why the obvious move is the wrong one.** The tempting
fix is to add `generative-modeling` and `vision-and-graphics` to the practice
seven. It defeats the purpose. `primary_topic` is `exactly-one` and it is the
browsing axis — a diffusion preconditioning practice tagged
`generative-modeling` renders on the generative page and *not* beside the
normalization practices it is a sibling of. Widening the topic list gives
cross-domain work its own page, which is the opposite of cross-pollination.
A topic spent naming a domain is a topic not spent naming the claim.

So: seven topics unchanged, and domain on its own axis. Same move [ADR-015](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-015.md)
made when `status:` was carrying two facts.

**Why it is many-valued.** `consensus:` is single because consensus is a
scalar — one position on one axis. A domain reading is a set: where the
practice has been shown to hold, accumulating as evidence arrives, which is
`source:`'s shape under [ADR-010](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-010.md) rather than `consensus:`'s. Forcing one value
makes the interesting cases unstatable — LoRA is established in language
modelling *and* in diffusion, and naming either underclaims while
`domain-general` overclaims.

**Two values that do not combine, and no lint can say so.** `unassessed`
means nobody looked, so pairing it with a domain contradicts itself.
`domain-general` is a claim of a different kind from the rest: the others
report where evidence exists, that one says the mechanism does not depend on
modality at all. It is not the union of the list and cannot be reached by
enumerating it — nobody tested Adam in six fields, it simply does not care
what produced the gradients. Both rules are written in `domain.yaml` where
the values are, because luria vocabularies have no mutual-exclusion mechanism
and a coherence rule about what a set would *mean* is not mechanically
checkable. A rule for a reader, not a guard.

**Verified before relying on it.** `many = true` on a vocabulary field is
supported in luria 0.11.0, the version CI pins — checked against the tag, not
the working tree, which is a distinction that has already cost this project
once.

Backfilled eight practices as the demonstration and left the rest
`unassessed`, which is the honest state: a domain reading is a judgement and
most of the corpus has not had one.

**Where domains disagree about a practice.** The axis makes this askable, so
it needed an answer. Mostly it does not arise, because `domain:` *scopes* the
consensus — `universal` with `domain: [language-modeling]` means "universal
within language modelling", which is what anyone means. It bites only when a
practice lists two domains and the field converged in one and not the other,
and the test there is whether the recommendation differs or only its uptake.
Different recommendation: split into separate codes, which was already the
rule for splitting anything. Same recommendation, different adoption: do not
split — one claim across two documents is the failure this record has paid
for twice this week, and `status:` would have to duplicate too, which is
plainly wrong since whether we believe a practice works is not a per-domain
question. The residual case wants a map-valued `consensus:`, which is a luria
feature and is deferred until an actual instance exists.

**What this does to issue [#85](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/85).** Twenty-three inherited notes were set aside
as out of scope by construction. They are ordinary unread notes now, and the
ones with a transferable claim are the most interesting reading left in the
corpus rather than the least.
