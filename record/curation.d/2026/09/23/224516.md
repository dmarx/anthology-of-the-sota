Unit H closed with a hypothesis rather than a finding: **a metric accumulates
critique documents faster than a definition document**, because critique is
what curation rewards — a paper that overturns something is interesting, and
a paper that defines the thing everyone already uses is not. If that is
right, FID was not a one-off and the record should hold opinions about other
measures whose defining papers are missing.

This is the sweep that tested it.

## The query, written down because that is the only way a later reader can
## tell what it could not have found

`DP-004` is explicit that a clean run is evidence about the query. So:

**What was run.** Three passes, deliberately different in shape.

1. **Token frequency over the record's own prose.** Every capitalised token
   of 2–13 characters, ranked by how many documents contain it. This finds
   measures the record names often and I would not have thought of.
2. **A regex for measure-shaped phrases** — `X score`, `X distance`,
   `X divergence`, `X metric`, `pass@k`. This mostly returned noise and is
   recorded as having mostly returned noise.
3. **The `analysis-and-evaluation` topic, read.** All 270 documents listed,
   the 130 practices and theories read for what measure each one names.
   This is the pass that actually worked.

Then, for each candidate measure: is its defining paper in
`record/literature.d/`, by arXiv identifier and by title?

**What it could not have found.** A measure the record uses without naming —
if a practice says "report the distance in CLIP space" and never uses a
proper noun, no pass here sees it. And a measure named only inside a `NOTE`
body, since the third pass read practices and theories. Both are real gaps
and neither was checked.

## What it returned

| measure | record files naming it | defining paper held? |
|---|---|---|
| FID | 90 | yes, since unit H |
| perplexity | 90 | no single defining paper |
| MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K, BIG-bench | 24–46 | **yes**, all four |
| LPIPS | 17 | yes (`LIT-095`) |
| BLEU | 16 | yes |
| tuned lens | — | yes (`LIT-569`) |
| KID | 4 | **no** → filed |
| Inception Score | 3 | **no** → filed |
| ECE / temperature scaling | 4 (probability sense) | **no** → filed |

**The benchmark side was already covered**, which is worth saying plainly
because it is the half I expected to be broken. Whoever filed the LLM
evaluation cluster filed the papers with it.

## Declined, with reasons, so the next sweep does not redo them

- **Improved Precision and Recall (`1904.06991`)** — named twice, both times
  as "the appendix also reports it". Not load-bearing on anything.
- **CLIPScore, BERTScore, MAUVE, nDCG, Chatbot Arena Elo** — the record does
  not name them at all. **An absence is not a defect.** The sweep's criterion
  is a measure the record has an *opinion* about, and it has none about
  these. Whether it should is a different question and a bigger one.
- **Gretton's kernel two-sample test** — reached by searching for MMD, which
  returned 19 hits, **all of them MMDiT**. A pure false positive of the query
  and recorded as one.

## The finding that was not the hypothesis

The hypothesis held — two of three filings are exactly the predicted shape.
But the largest thing the sweep returned had nothing to do with it.

**`#290` declined the KID paper as a `D-variant`, on four record mentions.**
The triage ranked it by its named contribution, which is a metric that is a
variant of one the record already had. What the paper actually carries is a
proof that **no unbiased estimator of FID exists**, and a construction where
50,000 samples reliably return the wrong ordering with a standard deviation
small enough to hide it. Both are about the incumbent.

So: **a candidate ranked by the name in its title gets ranked by the wrong
thing, and a mention count inherits that error.** The four mentions were of
`KID`, and the case for filing is about `FID`. Any triage that counts
occurrences of a paper's own contribution is measuring how often the record
names the thing the paper *made*, not how much the record depends on what it
*showed* — and for a paper whose contribution is a correction, those come
apart completely.

That is a defect in `#290`'s decision rule, not in its execution. Worth a
pass over the other 27 `D-variant` declines under the corrected question:
**not "does the record already have this?" but "what does this paper say
about the thing the record already has?"**

## And one correction that came free

`SOTA-315`'s `consensus_note` claimed nobody else had reported "the
separation or the remedy". The remedy — temperature scaling — is Guo et al.
2017, is nine years older, and is what everyone does. The note was written
while the record held no calibration paper older than 2025, so **a statement
about the field was true only of the record.** That is the specific failure
mode a `consensus` value is supposed to be protected against, arriving by a
route `ADR-011` does not cover: not adoption mistaken for evidence, but the
record's own coverage mistaken for the literature's.
