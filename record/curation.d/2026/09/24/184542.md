Unit 3 of `#342`. The unit found what it was ranked for, and the judgement
that took the time was **not** whether to file the paper — it was what the
paper does to a `promote_when` it very nearly satisfies.

## The situation

`THEORY-032` is `Proposed`. Its `promote_when` read:

> An ablation that uses the condition as a decision rule — spectral updates on
> the blocks that pass it, Euclidean on the blocks that fail — and trains
> faster than applying spectral updates everywhere.

Wang et al. ran a per-block ablation of exactly that shape. Muon on some
blocks, Adam on others, everything else matched. It is the first such
experiment this record has seen.

And it does not meet the condition, for two reasons that are easy to elide:

1. **The decision rule is different.** `THEORY-032` selects blocks by the
   stable rank of their incoming activations. Wang et al. select them by
   architectural role — is this block an associative memory. Nobody has
   checked whether the two rules pick the same blocks.
2. **The outcome is "nearly recovers", not "faster".** VO+FFN tracks full
   Muon closely. It does not beat it. `promote_when` asked for faster, and
   *nearly recovers* means the selective variant is **free**, which is a
   weaker and different claim.

## Why this was worth being careful about

The tempting move is to promote. The experiment is the right *shape*, it is
the only one of its kind, and the record has been waiting for it since
2026-09-20. Promoting would have been defensible in a sentence and wrong.

The reason it is wrong is that `THEORY-032`'s content is the **inequality** —
the spectral bound beats the Euclidean one by the ratio of the gradient's
nuclear rank to the incoming activations' stable rank. An ablation that
selects blocks by a different property tells you nothing about whether that
ratio is the operative quantity. It could be that associative-memory blocks
are exactly the low-stable-rank ones, in which case the two accounts are one
account. It could equally be that they are not, in which case `THEORY-032`
names a quantity that **correlates with the regime Muon is used in** — which
is the failure its own original `promote_when` said it was written to
exclude.

So what the paper actually delivered is a **discriminating experiment**, not a
promotion. The amended `promote_when` says so, and adds the part that was
missing: what a negative result means. Measure the stable rank of incoming
activations for QK, VO and FFN; if the ordering does not match the measured
gains, **the status should fall rather than rise.**

A `promote_when` that only says how to be promoted is half a specification.
This is the first one here that names its own refutation, and it is cheap —
three measurements on a model somebody has already trained.

## What the ablation did establish

The half that was genuinely in doubt. **A per-block split is nearly
costless**, so "apply the spectral update only where it pays" is a real object
rather than a hypothetical, whatever rule ends up selecting the blocks. Before
this, the record had no evidence that a hybrid optimizer configuration was
even viable.

And the localisation is sharp enough to be actionable and surprising: VO and
FFN carry it, QK does not, and **QK and VO are the same size**, which the
authors flag themselves to kill the parameter-counting reading.

## An observation that cuts against the paper's own story

Worth recording because it survived into both new documents rather than being
smoothed over. The paper reports that **Muon also learns more isotropic QK
weights than Adam** — and QK is the block that contributes little to the gain.

So isotropy is general and the benefit is not. Isotropy cannot be the whole
mechanism; the associative-memory structure has to be doing work that isotropy
does not do by itself. The paper does not dwell on this and the account is
weaker for it, which is why the theory is `Proposed` and why that sentence is
in its "why `Proposed`" section rather than in a footnote.

## A shape worth naming: a `Proposed` account becoming load-bearing

The mechanism here assumes the associative-memory reading of FFN layers —
`THEORY-081`, which is `Proposed` on 160 hand-annotated keys of a single
16-layer model. An optimizer result now depends on it.

That **raises the cost of its being wrong without being evidence that it is
right**, and those are different things that feel like the same thing. The
instinct is to treat downstream use as corroboration; it is not. `THEORY-081`
is deliberately left unamended, because its `promote_when` asks for a
replication or a causal edit test, and a downstream user is neither.

But a reader of `THEORY-081` would want to know that something now leans on
it, and the record has no field for *what depends on this account* in the
direction that matters. That is a real gap and not obviously a lint question:
the dependency is in prose, in a different document, and pointing at an
assumption rather than a citation.
