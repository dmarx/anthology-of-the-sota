The instruction was to re-measure the four Tier B items left in `#342` before
picking one. The measurement inverted the ranking, and the paper that had been
**last** of six turned out to be the only one of the four that corrects something
the record asserts.

## What the re-measurement found

The original ranking was mention counts, taken two days and about fourteen
documents ago. Re-run against the question that has actually been productive all
week — *does the record make a claim this paper would correct or source* — the
order reverses.

| paper | by count | re-measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DPM-Solver++ | last, 6 files | **first** |
| EDM2 | first, 39 files | second |
| FLIP | second | third |
| Swin | third, 14 files | fourth |

Two of those movements are worth keeping.

**Swin's count was a substring.** `swin` matches **"swing"** and **"swings"**,
which is what `SOTA-002`, `SOTA-193` and `SOTA-315` actually contain. Real
mentions: `Swin`, `Swin-B` three times, `Swin-S` twice, `Swin-F` once — all
baseline architectures in tables, with `SOTA-320` the only practice among them.
Swin is an instrument this record names, not a subject it reasons about.

**A substring match is not a mention count**, and the ranking that put Swin third
of six was measuring the word "swings". Two days of work were prioritised partly
on that number. The check costs one `grep -o | sort | uniq -c`.

**EDM2 and FLIP are real and unforced.** EDM2 succeeds `SOTA-188`'s source and
its own contributions are absent — zero files say "post-hoc EMA" or
"magnitude-preserving" — so it extends a well-sourced practice rather than
correcting one. FLIP opens a leaf: no practice or theory here concerns masking to
cut contrastive-pretraining cost, in a CLIP cluster twelve practices deep.

## The defect

`SOTA-203` is `Active` with `consensus: universal`, the strongest pair of labels
this record issues. It says: sample a diffusion model with a higher-order ODE
solver on the weights you already trained. It is a good document — it has a
Conditions section covering noise schedules, discrete-time models and the
fidelity/latency trade, and a "What this does not cover" section about
distillation.

It mentions guidance **nowhere**.

DPM-Solver++ exists for that omission, and states the charge in its abstract:
high-order solvers were tested without guidance, "their effectiveness for guided
sampling has not been well-tested before", and under guidance "they even become
slower than DDIM". At scale 8.0 and ten function evaluations the FIDs are
**13.04** at order 1, **114.62** at order 2 and **164.74** at order 3. Those are
not degradations; they are broken images.

The sharpest form of it is that the practice's own punchline inverts. It observes
that DDIM is exactly DPM-Solver-1, "the *first-order* case — the least accurate
member of the family", and uses that to justify stating the recommendation as
"higher-order" rather than as a product name. Under guidance DDIM is the **most**
accurate of them at a small budget. The thing the document tells you to stop
using beats the thing it tells you to use.

## How a practice this careful missed it

Its `implementations` line is the answer, and it is almost funny:

> Default or selectable sampler in essentially every diffusion serving stack.

That is true, and every one of those stacks runs guidance on by default. **The
practice named its deployment setting and did not check the setting's own
defaults.** It cited the regime as evidence of adoption without asking what the
regime does.

Generalising: a `Conditions` section is written by asking *what did the paper
vary*. This gap needed a different question — *what does everybody hold fixed
that the paper also held fixed*. The first question finds the limits the authors
knew about. Only the second finds the ones they did not.

## The half of it the record already had

The better structural finding is that DPM-Solver++ diagnoses **two** failures and
this record was already holding one of them.

*Boundedness.* Large guidance pushes the converged `x₀` outside the data range.
`SOTA-202` has held exactly this since it was filed, from Imagen: training data
lies in `[−1, 1]`, high guidance weights push `x̂₀` out, and because the model is
applied to its own output the excursion compounds. `Active`, `converged`.

*Convergence radius.* Amplified derivatives narrow the radius a high-order method
needs to stay inside. New, and it is what actually bounds `SOTA-203`.

The two are independent and the fixes do not substitute: thresholding fixes the
first and does nothing for the second, multistep fixes the second and does
nothing for the first. A reader who collapses them will expect clamping to rescue
a third-order solver. `THEORY-tmp8i6gq` says so explicitly, because that is the
mistake the pairing invites.

**And the connection runs the other way too.** `SOTA-202` says clamp `x̂₀` — which
assumes the sampler is carrying an `x̂₀`. A high-order solver written on the noise
prediction has intermediate stages where none exists, so the fix this record
already recommends is *unavailable* in the case it was written for. That is why
DPM-Solver++ reparameterizes onto the data prediction, and the reason it gives is
exactly this: solving for `x_θ` is what makes thresholding "further available".

Two documents, filed separately, holding one mechanism from opposite ends —
Imagen found it because images were being destroyed, Lu et al. found it because
an ODE solve was diverging. Neither knew about the other. That convergence is the
strongest support either has, and it only became visible because a practice's
scope was checked rather than assumed.
