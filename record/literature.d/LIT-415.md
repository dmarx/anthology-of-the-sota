# LIT-415: Stream of Search (SoS): Learning to Search in Language

Gandhi et al. (2024) — [ARXIV-2404.03683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03683)

## Key takeaways

- **The method** is to represent a search process as a flattened string — a
  *stream of search* — and pretrain on traces generated by heuristic solvers,
  so a model sees fruitful mistakes rather than only correct next tokens.
  Reported as +25% search accuracy over models trained to predict only the
  solution
- **The task is Countdown**: combine a set of input numbers with arithmetic
  operations to reach a target. The paper presents it as an existing game
  rather than a benchmark it introduces — the numbers round of a British
  television programme — and it is the demonstration vehicle, not the
  contribution
- **What makes Countdown useful to the literature that borrowed it** is
  mechanical: a solution is *verifiable by evaluating it*, so a reward exists
  without a judge, a reference answer or a rubric. That is why it turns up
  wherever a method needs a task it can score a million times cheaply
- **And what that costs**: the search space is small and arithmetic, so the
  task rewards search over a shallow discrete space and says little about
  anything else. A gain on Countdown is a gain at bounded symbolic search

**Twenty-three documents report on Countdown. Zero cite the method.** The
evolution-strategies cluster is measured on it throughout — `SOTA-154`
averages across Qwen2.5 and LLaMA3 on it, `SOTA-212` runs OLMo3-7B on it
across 200 GH200s, `SOTA-213` reads `LIT-237`'s 500-iteration run on it — and
none of them is about learning to search in language. The record borrowed the
benchmark and left the paper behind, which is why this note is about the task.

**The record's own evidence says Countdown behaves unlike its neighbours**, and
that is the thing a citing practice implicitly assumes and cannot currently
look up. `NOTE-082` records that ES reaches peak accuracy quickly on every
task *except* Countdown, and that HellaSwag declines steadily as Countdown
training continues past the point Countdown itself has converged. A benchmark
whose training dynamics differ from the rest of the suite is not
interchangeable with them, and four practices report on it as though it were
one task among several.

Unread — no `NOTE`. `ADR-025` makes that mean what it says; the method is
what a reading would be about, and the method is not what this record uses.
