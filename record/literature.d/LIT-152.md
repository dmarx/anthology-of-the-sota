# LIT-152: On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture: Evaluation, Efficiency, and Training Stability

Qiu et al., Qwen Team, Alibaba (2026) — [ARXIV-2608.30320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320)

## Key takeaways

- The subject is Qwen3.8-Flash-Next: a sparse mixture-of-experts model, 125B
  parameters with 6B activated per token, plus 51B parameters of n-gram
  embedding tables held off the accelerator and prefetched from host memory.
  It leads its 397B-A17B predecessor on eight of fourteen pretraining
  benchmarks and trails on the rest by at most 2.6 points, at a third of the
  activated parameters, a third of the training tokens and roughly a ninth
  of the training FLOPs.
- Token mixing is the same layerwise hybrid the Qwen line has carried since
  Qwen3-Next ([LIT-136](LIT-136.md)): Gated DeltaNet ([LIT-137](LIT-137.md)) with one full-attention layer
  in four ([SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md)). At continued-pretraining time those full-attention
  layers are replaced by Qwen Sparse Attention, which scores context at
  micro-block granularity with a compressed lightweight indexer — a fourth
  entry in the record's sparse-attention line after NSA, DSA and V4's CSA.
- **The comparison the record was missing.** Gated Residual, the paper's own
  design, widens the residual to four branches and reads them through an
  elementwise gate, dropping the stream-mixing matrix altogether. Against
  mHC ([LIT-140](LIT-140.md)) "at this scale the two perform comparably"; against Attention
  Residuals ([LIT-134](LIT-134.md)), at 28 layers, Full AttnRes and GR land level at 1.762
  training loss, with Block AttnRes costing 0.008 at S = 2 and 0.011 at
  S = 4, and the ordering holding at 48 layers. So the three replacements
  for the plain residual that the record carries are, on one team's harness
  at this scale, equivalent in quality and separated by cost: GR's claim is
  that dropping the mixing matrix removes a full read of the residual state
  per block, which is the dominant inference cost of a widened stream.
- The paper names the family explicitly — HC, mHC, VWN and GR — as differing
  in *where the extra expressiveness is spent*, not in whether widening the
  stream helps.
- Method, stated as the point: every candidate change is evaluated on three
  axes at once — loss with downstream benchmarks, the cost in training,
  prefill and decode, and the effect on optimal hyperparameters and
  stability. Loss and downstream accuracy do not always move together;
  enlarging the n-gram vocabulary lowers loss monotonically while downstream
  accuracy saturates.
- Optimization: the architecture and Muon together shift the optimal
  learning rate and batch size upwards and make batch-size warmup
  unnecessary — a retuning consequence of [SOTA-121](../practices.d/SOTA-121.md) that the record's Muon
  chain had not recorded.

## Standing in the anthology

Filed for the comparison. [LIT-134](LIT-134.md) and [LIT-140](LIT-140.md) each said the two residual
replacements had not been compared against each other; this paper compares
both against a third and finds all three level, which is the kind of result
that only shows up when someone runs the arms in one harness. It is a
single team at a single scale, and the losses it separates are thousandths,
so it settles the ranking no more than [LIT-151](LIT-151.md) does — but it does move the
question from "unknown" to "measured once".

It is also the published architecture work behind the generation
[LIT-135](LIT-135.md) records from a model card. The two are different models — a 125B-A6B
MoE here, a 27B dense there — so this is the design lineage, not the recipe
for that checkpoint, which remains unpublished.
