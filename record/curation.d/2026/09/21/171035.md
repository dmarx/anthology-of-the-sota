**PHi filed from the [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) worklist (`LIT-tmpwr4bz`).** The diagnosis is the
best part and it is exactly right: next-token loss cannot tell *hard* from
*interesting*, and it fails at both ends simultaneously. Uniform noise
maximizes the loss and requires no computation, because nothing the model
computes can help. A memorized licence minimizes it and requires none either,
the program having been written into the weights already. The cases anyone
actually cares about sit in the middle, where an intermediate loss is
indistinguishable from a mixture of trivial and impossible tokens.

The fix is a variational bottleneck whose prior is **learned and
autoregressive** rather than fixed — so the quantity being measured is the
information in the hidden state that its own past did not predict, which is
the incremental description length of whatever program the model is
assembling in context. That reframing is what stops the metric being one more
correlate.

**Where the evidence actually is.** The paper has two halves and they are not
equally strong. The LLM half sorts five text types — trivial tasks, licences,
shuffled words, unseen literature, unseen code — into "boring" and
"interesting" *by the authors' judgement*, then shows the metric agrees. That
is suggestive and a short step from circular: the taxonomy and the result
come from the same place.

The automaton half is the one that carries the practice. Complexity there is a
**formula** — bits to encode a probabilistic finite automaton — so the
correlation is against something external. And they do the thing that makes
it count: bin tokens by next-token loss first, *then* stratify by complexity,
so the claim is that PHi explains variance the obvious metric does not. It
holds in every bin, on a Transformer and an LSTM. That is why the promotion
condition insists on an external complexity ground truth rather than another
demonstration.

**And then the number that decided what not to file.** §3.2.3 shows that
picking the higher-PHi-loss rationale from a correct/incorrect pair beats
chance, and the abstract's version is "solutions with high PHi loss have a
significantly increased chance of being correct." True. Also in §3.2.3, one
sentence, unhighlighted: *"when, from all solution pairs, we always pick the
ones with lower mean next token prediction loss, our chance of being correct
is 71%."*

So the baseline everyone already has scores 71%, and the paper reports no
standalone number for PHi at all. What it demonstrates is incremental —
partial correlation with next-token loss controlled, plus an adversarially
constructed subset where the next-token baseline is wrong *by construction*.
None of that is dishonest; the authors put the 71% in the text themselves and
describe the two losses as capturing different aspects. But a reader who takes
the abstract's sentence as "use PHi to pick answers" has been handed the wrong
instruction by a true one.

The practice therefore says **this measures, it does not select**, and the
selection application is not filed. The record is not the place for that gap
to close silently — and in six months "PHi picks better answers" is exactly
the sort of thing that would arrive as settled because the anthology had
carried it.

That is the sixth instance of `DP-010`'s shape today and the second of its
*underreported* direction, after `LIT-487`. The pattern in both: the sentence
that changes what you should do is in the body, unbolded, and the sentence
that travels is in the abstract.

**One thing filed as a condition rather than a finding.** Inserting the
bottleneck in early layers causes posterior collapse — next-token loss
degrades badly *and* the metric drops to nearly zero. That combination is
nasty, because a broken instrument reads as "no interesting computation
here", which is a plausible answer. Layers 18–24 of a 3B Llama are the usable
band and were found by sweeping. It is in the practice's method section
rather than its limitations, because a reader who skips it gets a confident
wrong answer rather than an error.
