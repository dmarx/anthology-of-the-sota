## The triage was a ranking, and four of its sentences did not survive reading

That is the point of `ADR-025`, and it is worth counting honestly, because
the triage was written from titles, abstracts and a grep.

1. **Jadbabaie, Lin & Morse was held all along, as `LIT-285`.** The triage said
   the record held "only the later [LIT-304](../../record/literature.d/LIT-304.md)". The feed's row for it was a URL hash
   pointing at `Consensus2.pdf`, and that PDF is a *different paper*: Tahbaz-Salehi
   and Jadbabaie's *On Consensus Over Random Networks*, the precursor of `LIT-326`,
   which the record also holds. The held-check matched on the feed's title, and
   the feed's title was a hash. The 610 seconds of reading went to a paper the
   record already has in journal form. Reading the actual TAC paper then showed
   `LIT-285` and `NOTE-136` misstating it where it mattered. The imported text
   gave its condition as a *directed spanning tree* over fixed windows. The paper
   is undirected throughout and requires joint connectivity over contiguous
   bounded intervals. The text invented a corollary and said the paper proves
   something about Vicsek it explicitly declines to model. Corrected, with the
   wrong text kept and marked.
2. **Marigold's fix does not make one step match fifty.** The triage wrote
   "once that is fixed single-step inference matches it". Against the 50-step,
   10-member ensemble, the fixed single step is worse on all five depth sets. What
   beats the ensemble is end-to-end fine-tuning, a training change.
3. **Model merging does not replace annealing.** The triage wrote "merges
   checkpoints along a constant-learning-rate run instead of annealing". The one
   fork that tests it has the anneal ahead on MMLU at the end. What survives is
   the proxy use, and that is the practice filed.
4. **Fromage is not where Muon comes from.** The triage called it "the 2020 paper
   between" signSGD and Modular Duality. The order is right and the lineage is
   wrong. Modular Duality does not cite it, and `LIT-437`, which shares an author,
   names its conditioning assumption as the flaw. It is filed as the corrected
   Frobenius antecedent.

**The generalization.** Three of the four errors are the triage adopting a
paper's own framing of itself, and the fourth is a held-check keyed on a
string. That is `DP-010`'s point about the most citable sentence, applied to
the triager. The next triage should say which of its claims came from an
abstract.

## The corrections the readings forced on the record

Six held documents said something a filing proved false. None of them was
a judgement call:

- **`SOTA-140`**: "Nobody has run the two against each other." ScheduleFree+ has.
  WSD lost at every size, against a package that differs in weight decay too.
  The practice stays `contested`.
- **`SOTA-156`**: "no additional hyperparameter". Its own author now reports a
  large-batch cliff and a weight decay that was "compensating".
- **`SOTA-007`**: "every model in this record tokenises this way". Not after H-Net.
- **`SOTA-403`**: "nothing at current scale shares layers at all". Not after
  Huginn.
- **`LIT-028`**: Chinchilla "identified" the schedule as the cause of Kaplan's
  exponent. It *proposed* that, and two reproductions find it is not the main
  cause. `NOTE-017` and `NOTE-072` repeat the attribution and are left for when
  Porian et al. is filed.
- **`THEORY-027`**: the schedule invariance is a property of *scalar* schedules.
  MuLAN's own ablations reproduce it exactly there.

The first four are the same shape: **a universal sentence in prose about the
record's own contents** ("nobody has", "every model", "nothing at"). Like the
consensus notes `SOTA-373` and `SOTA-365` were caught making, they are free text
asserting a fact about the corpus that no check verifies. They go stale the
moment the corpus grows. A grep for `every model in this record`, `nobody has
run`, `nothing at current scale` and similar would be a cheap sweep.

## Masking rates, a third time

Cramming's ICML version adopts 25% masking and says it "provides benefits". Its
reason is a tensor shape: 25% of 128 tokens is 32. Its own appendix still says
larger rates did not help, and its only rate table has no 25% row. After
BERT's 15% and Wettig's 40%, that is the third masking rate this week to be
something other than a measured optimum at the size cited. `SOTA-405`'s
`promote_when` already refuses single-size evidence, and this is why.

## One relation withdrawn on the lint's advice

Hinton et al. was filed with `extended_by: [LIT-441, LIT-664]`. The lint reported
that the line through it shares no topic, because it joined a language-model
data line and a self-supervised vision line at one ancestor. On reading them
again, neither relation was right: [LIT-441](../../record/literature.d/LIT-441.md) runs Kim and Rush's sequence-level
method, and DINO repurposes the temperature for a different problem. Both are
withdrawn to prose. That is `ADR-049`'s second reading of an unbound relation:
the relation was wrong.
