Unit 6 of `#331`. The twenty video papers filed in `#330` and `#332` had LIT
notes written from delegated extractions. This pass read every one in full
and wrote the `NOTE`. Five readers each read four papers end to end, from
the extracted text, and drafted the notes. I checked the corrections that
change a practice or reverse a headline against the paper text before
applying them, and every one I checked held.

## The main finding: the first extraction was not a reading

The `#330` and `#332` LIT notes were careful. Every one had a [DP-010](../design-principles.md#dp-10) hedges
section, and most were written from a reader's full pass over the paper.
The full readings still found **about 55 corrections across nineteen
notes**, and three practices moved. Two things turned out to separate an
extraction from a reading.

**An extraction answers the question it was given.** The first readers
were asked for numbers, evidence type and hedges. They returned the
headline number for each ablation, and many did not record what the
ablation held fixed. Unit 6 asked for the claims table, where each row
needs its support. That question found what the first pass skipped:

- VDM's image arms process more frames per step (20 and 24 against 16).
- Video LDM's pretrained arm gets 73K extra steps and trains only its
  temporal layers.
- CogVideoX's attention result is "in early steps" only.

This is [DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4) at the level of a single paper: a pass finds only the
defects its query is shaped like.

**I added errors while writing from extractions.** Several corrections
reverse sentences I wrote myself:

- the HunyuanVideo exponent comparison, which I had already "corrected"
  once
- AnimateDiff "best user-study ranks"
- "without reflow, one-step FID is 378"
- "the only video report that states the logit-normal"

An extraction lists results. Writing a note means comparing them, and I
made the comparisons without the paper open.

## What moved

**`SOTA-386` from `Active` to `Proposed`.** Its three controlled sources
agree, and none of them isolates the effect. Each gives the image arm
extra compute, or bundles initialization with freezing, or doesn't state
its setup. Two of them use frames of their own videos as the "images". The
practice was filed `Active` hours earlier in `#330`, on the strength of
"three controlled studies". The first readings called them controlled
because they changed one named thing. The full readings found each also
changed something else. [DP-006](../design-principles.md#dp-6) applies: the status says what the record
believes, and the record is not entitled to believe this one yet. The
practice is still filed, because the field does it and the question
matters. `promote_when` names the compute-matched comparison.

**`SOTA-390` and `SOTA-389` were each overstated from one source.**
`SOTA-390` said "with everything else fixed" and "wins on training loss",
and CogVideoX says neither. `SOTA-389` listed CogVideoX as an adopter of
video-native captioning. CogVideoX's own first version says the opposite,
and its third version deleted that sentence. A reading of v3 alone could
not have found it. The reader diffed the versions because the brief
supplied v1.

## Mechanics worth keeping

- **`history:` must end at the current version.** I put new entries at the
  top, and lint rejected three practices. Append; don't prepend.
- **`inactive-ok-file:` written from the report.** Demoting `SOTA-386`, and
  `SOTA-389`/`SOTA-390` being `Proposed`, produced about 100 unacknowledged
  citations in the new notes. Every one is in a "Bearing on the record"
  section, where weighing the practice is the point. The directives were
  generated from `docs/reports/reference-status.md`, restricted to files
  this branch changed. Each names only codes whose presence in that file's
  body was confirmed. Five pre-existing warnings on files outside this
  branch were left alone.
- **A reader was right where my search wasn't.** I doubted LTX-Video trains
  on images, and a grep for "image data" found nothing. §2.5.4 is titled
  "Training on Images". Check the section list before overruling a reader.
