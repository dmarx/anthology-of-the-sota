**ParScale filed from the [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) worklist.** The record already held several
ways of spending parallel computation — self-consistency sampling
(`SOTA-280`), speculative decoding (`SOTA-227`), ensemble-and-distil
(`SOTA-257`) — and this is genuinely a different animal from all three. The
streams share every backbone weight, the input transformation and the
aggregation are *learned*, and the parallelism is present during **training**.
That last one is why the paper can fit a scaling law rather than plot a
benchmark curve, and it is the whole reason it is worth a practice rather
than a note.

**The hinge is a good observation and worth keeping separately from the
result.** Classifier-free guidance runs a second forward pass on a
deliberately *degraded* input and beats the single pass. The degraded stream
carries strictly less information than the clean one. So the benefit cannot
be information — it has to be the computation. Everything else in the paper
follows from taking that seriously: replace the hand-designed bad input with
`P` learned ones, replace the fixed contrastive rule with a learned
aggregator, let `P` be free.

**Then the load-bearing ablation is in an appendix.** Several input
transformations and aggregation rules were tried, including LoRA-style
factorizations, and they "minimally affect model performance; the significant
factor is the number of computations". Without that sentence this is a paper
about prefix tuning. With it, it is a paper about computation as a resource.
It is also the sort of thing that gets skimmed past, and it went into the
literature note's key takeaways for that reason.

**A "law" that is a fit, and the gap is larger than the word suggests.** The
theoretical result (Proposition 1) says the equivalent parameter multiplier
depends on the **diversity** between streams — the correlation between their
residuals. Under independence it is a power law in `P`; under negative
correlation the loss can fall further still; under perfect correlation it
degenerates to a single stream. So the theory constrains almost nothing on
its own. The practical `log P` form was chosen *after* looking at the curve,
because the measured gains from 1→2, 2→4 and 4→8 were about equal, and then
substituted back in. Diversity is never measured.

That is not a criticism of the work, which is honest about the substitution
and names "why is diversity related to `P`" as open. It is a reason the
practice says "fitted over the range tested" rather than "follows a law", and
a reason to distrust `P = 16` — a logarithm is precisely the shape that makes
an unrun extrapolation look safe.

**The best-instrumented part of the paper is the part about cost.** The
inference analysis is denominated in **memory and latency, not FLOPs**, on the
explicit argument that decoding is memory-bound and FLOPs mis-rank it — flash
attention spends more FLOPs for less latency and everyone uses it. Denominated
in FLOPs, ParScale looks `P`× worse; denominated in what the hardware actually
does at batch size 1, it is 22× cheaper in added memory than the parameter
scaling it replaces. Both numbers describe the same system.

And the batch-size sweep is the thing that makes the paper trustworthy on
this: the advantage narrows as batch size rises and decoding shifts to
compute-bound, they show it to batch size 8, and they claim nothing past it.
So the condition went into the practice's **title** rather than its
conditions, because a reader who takes "22× less memory" without "when
inference memory is the binding constraint" will deploy it on a
high-throughput server and be wrong. I considered filing the memory-and-
latency argument as its own methodological practice and did not: the argument
is right and no experiment in the paper isolates it, and inflating an
argument into a practice is the failure `DP-005` and `DP-006` are about.

**The conjecture, which is the sentence that will get cited.** Parameters
carry memorization, computation carries reasoning. The evidence is real and it
is two-way: the fitted coefficient is 0.39 on Stack-V2-Python against 0.33 on
the Pile, and independently, a 1.6B model at `P = 8` reaches the 4.4B baseline
on coding but only the 2.8B baseline on general tasks. A fitted constant and a
benchmark table are different instruments agreeing, which is more than most
conjectures manage.

It is still `Proposed`, and the reason is that **two corpora are not a
variable**. Stack-V2-Python differs from the Pile in more ways than
reasoning-versus-memorization: it is narrower, more repetitive, more
structured, lower-entropy per token. Any of those would raise the return on
ensemble-like diversity without a word of it being about reasoning. The
promotion condition therefore asks for a *within-corpus* test — one model, one
training set, the exchange rate measured against memorization-heavy versus
reasoning-heavy evaluations — because that is the only shape of result that
separates the two stories. The authors call it "an intuitive conjecture"
themselves, which is exactly `DP-010`'s pattern: the most quotable sentence
sitting on the least isolated evidence, flagged honestly by the people who
wrote it and near-certain to be repeated without the flag.
