Unit D of `#304`: SwAV and DINOv2, ranked by the audit as substrate defects
rather than candidates.

## A correction to the audit, made by reading the thing properly

`#304` and `PR #303` both said the case for these two rested on **`SOTA-338`
recommending** measurement "in a non-ImageNet feature space (CLIP, SwAV or
DINOv2)". That was wrong in two ways at once, and both ways understate it.

- **Wrong field.** `SOTA-338` names them in its **`promote_when`** — the
  condition under which a `Proposed` practice would be promoted — not in a
  recommendation.
- **Wrong document.** The practice that actually *recommends* this is
  **`SOTA-337`**, which is `Active`, whose body says to "report a CLIP or
  SwAV Fréchet distance beside FID" when an ImageNet-pretrained network has
  touched a generator's training, and whose measured table carries a
  `SwAV FD` column.

So the real state was worse than reported: an **`Active` practice telling the
reader to use an instrument the record had no note for**, plus a `Proposed`
one that could not have been promoted without a paper the record did not
hold. Both are now held.

**The generalisable bit is about how a coverage audit reads its own
evidence.** The audit found the right two papers from a grep for names, then
attributed the dependency to the first document the grep returned. Name
proximity is enough to *find* a substrate defect and is not enough to
*describe* one — that needs reading which field the name sits in, and whether
the document is asserting or merely conditioning. Same mechanism as unit A's
finding that an audit counting papers cannot see a missing word: the cheap
signal locates, it does not characterise.

`SOTA-337`'s hedge also turned out to be doing real work. It says SwAV "is
partly independent: self-supervised, but on ImageNet images". Reading SwAV
confirms it exactly — the objective uses no labels, the 75.3% headline is
ImageNet-pretrained, and the paper's own experiment on random non-ImageNet
images is a separate and much smaller result (+1.3% over scratch). A
qualification that survives contact with the source is worth noticing, since
most do not.

## A mechanism that prevents collapse, and causes it

SwAV's equipartition constraint is the fifth account in `THEORY-tmpf89jm`,
and it comes with the best single sentence in the whole cluster. The entropy
regularisation `ε` smooths the code assignment, and the paper reports that
"a strong entropy regularization generally leads to a trivial solution where
all samples collapse into an unique representation and are all assigned
uniformly to all prototypes".

**The knob introduced to prevent collapse has a setting at which it causes
it.** Not a different failure, the same one. That is worth holding onto when
reading any of these mechanisms as explanations: a mechanism whose sign flips
with a hyperparameter is not obviously a mechanism at all.

## What a well-resourced group does when there is no theory

The other unit-D addition to the theory is DINOv2, and its contribution is
negative. With five competing accounts of collapse-avoidance in the
literature, DINOv2 **stacks** them: KoLeo (spread the features, in the
variance family) *and* Sinkhorn-Knopp centering borrowed from SwAV (in the
equipartition family) *and* DINO's teacher-student asymmetry. Three ablation
rows, each worth a fraction of a point, no account offered.

That is a reasonable engineering response and it is evidence for nothing.
Recorded because it is the honest reading of the strongest model in the
lineage: **the group with the compute to settle the question did not try to,
and the field's best features came out of additive assembly rather than
understanding.**

## The transplant test, supplied by the introducing paper

`SOTA-tmpoe0zb` (multi-crop) is filed `converged` on grounds the record
rarely gets this early: SwAV applied its own component to **SimCLR,
DeepCluster and DeepCluster-v2** — three objectives, one of them contrastive
with negatives — and gained 2–4 points in each. The introducing paper ran the
replication.

Only one other component in this whole `#304` reading has that property:
VICReg's variance term, which its own paper reports stabilising other
methods. Two components out of roughly a dozen. **When a paper tests its
component outside its own method, the record should weight that far above
the headline number**, because the headline is a composition and the
transplant is the claim.
