# LIT-582: Semi-Supervised Classification with Graph Convolutional Networks

Kipf and Welling, University of Amsterdam (ICLR 2017) — [ARXIV-1609.02907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)

## Key takeaways

- **The layer:** multiply node features by the degree-normalized adjacency
  with self-loops (the "renormalization trick"), then by a weight matrix.
  Each layer mixes one hop of neighborhood, at cost linear in edges
- **Propagation ablation** (Table 3, mean of 100 initializations on the
  Planetoid split): the renormalized form is best on all three citation
  graphs. Chebyshev K = 2 reaches 81.2 on Cora and 73.8 on Pubmed, against
  81.5 and 79.0
- **Results** (Table 2): 70.3 on Citeseer, 81.5 on Cora and 79.0 on Pubmed,
  against Planetoid's 64.7, 75.7 and 77.2
- **Depth** (appendix B): accuracy peaks at 2–3 layers. Beyond about 7,
  training without residual connections becomes difficult

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from `#163`** ("graph representation / gnn") as the reference
architecture the other GNN papers here measure against. GIN
([LIT-580](LIT-580.md)) and Shchur et al. ([LIT-581](LIT-581.md)) are `compared_against` it.

**No practice from it.** Its numbers come from one fixed split, which
Shchur et al. show can reorder models, and later work under fair tuning
finds it a hard baseline to beat. That finding is filed from the papers
that measured it.

**The paper's own table carries the caveat.** The last row of Table 2
re-runs GCN on 10 random splits of the same size: 67.9, 80.1, 78.9 and 58.4,
below the fixed-split headline on every dataset, by 2.4 points on Citeseer
and 7.6 on NELL. The fixed split is the favourable one, which is the effect
[LIT-581](LIT-581.md) measured across architectures and [SOTA-350](../practices.d/SOTA-350.md) turns into a practice.

**The depth appendix is where over-smoothing starts.** Accuracy peaks at 2–3
layers, and the paper attributes the difficulty past about 7 to the growing
receptive field and to overfitting without separating them. Li et al.
([LIT-816](LIT-816.md)) read this layer as Laplacian smoothing and its depth limit
as over-smoothing, and Oono and Suzuki ([LIT-827](LIT-827.md)) prove the
exponential collapse that [THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md) states. Neither settles the
appendix's symptom. Li et al. show the collapse on an untrained network on
the karate club and blame their own trained deep models' drop on
overfitting, and Oono and Suzuki's condition barely holds on sparse
citation graphs.

**As a baseline for a multi-dataset model.** One for All ([LIT-841](LIT-841.md))
reruns GCN ten times on its node and knowledge-graph tasks. GCN stays
ahead of jointly trained OFA on Cora nodes (78.86 against 75.90) and is far
behind on relation classification (WN18RR 67.40, FB15K237 74.20). GCN takes
the native features and OFA text embeddings, so the comparison is not of
architectures alone.

**Retrained as a graph transformer's baseline.** Graphormer ([LIT-843](LIT-843.md))
reruns GCN, with and without a virtual node, on PCQM4M-LSC with OGB's
official hyperparameters: validation MAE 0.1691, or 0.1485 with a virtual
node, against GIN-VN's 0.1395 and Graphormer's 0.1234. Graphormer has
47.1M parameters to GCN's 2.0M.

**The supervised bar for an unsupervised encoder.** Deep Graph Infomax
([LIT-806](LIT-806.md)) trains a one-layer GCN encoder with no labels and fits a
linear classifier on its embeddings: 82.3 on Cora, 71.8 on Citeseer and
76.8 on Pubmed, against this paper's supervised 81.5, 70.3 and 79.0, copied
rather than rerun, all on the one Planetoid split.

**Two follow-ups that rerun it.** GraphSAGE ([LIT-814](LIT-814.md)) makes this
propagation rule inductive as its GraphSAGE-GCN variant, a mean over the
node and its sampled neighbours with no concatenation, and it is the
weakest of its aggregators on unseen nodes (0.772, 0.930 and 0.500
micro-F1). GAT ([LIT-826](LIT-826.md)) reruns a 64-unit GCN over 100 seeds on the
same Planetoid split and is 1.6 points ahead on Cora and Citeseer and level
on Pubmed.
