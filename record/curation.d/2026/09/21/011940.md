Filing the chain-of-thought trunk, the unbound-lineage report flagged
`LIT-035 ↔ LIT-467` as joined by nothing: GPT-3 tagged `model-architecture`,
chain-of-thought tagged `adaptation-and-tuning`. I added
`adaptation-and-tuning` to `LIT-035`, wrote a careful history note defending
the decision, and said in the pull request that "adding a tag to close an
unbound edge is precisely what `ADR-035` forbids".

Challenged on it, I went and read `ADR-035`. It does not say that.

## What it actually says

§3 moves the chain invariant from `primary_topic` to `tags` and states the
consequence plainly: **"A shared tag can now bind a relation, which is the
point and the risk."** The risk it then names is specific —
*"Binding on a real topic is the record saying what two documents have in
common. Binding on a free-form label is the `#101` move."* §4 removes two
`flash-attention` labels for being exactly that, and its decision table
weighs three newly-bound edges and **keeps two**.

Then §5 closes the vocabulary on `SOTA`, `LIT` and `THEORY` — which means the
failure mode §4 is about cannot happen on those three schemes any more. The
only tag you can add is one of the thirteen, each with a label and a blurb and
a decision behind it, and the truth test is the only question left.

And §7 restores eleven documents' old topics as secondaries. Additive
retagging, in response to a review pointing out that documents were not saying
what they were about, is the decision's own central action.

So: the report surfaces candidates, and acting on one by adding a true topic
is the intended response. That is nearly the opposite of what I said.

## Where the wrong rule came from

`CLAUDE.md`:

> Add a second topic when it is genuinely true, and **never to bind a
> relation** — the chain invariant reads the whole tag list now, so an
> *incidental* tag will bind an edge that should have stayed open (`ADR-035`).

The first clause is the real test. The third clause names the real hazard —
an **incidental** tag. The middle clause, read on its own, forbids a
*motivation*: not "don't add a false tag" but "don't add a tag *because* it
binds". That is unfalsifiable from the outside and, taken literally, makes the
unbound report unusable, since any tag you add after reading the report was
added partly because of the report.

I read the middle clause, repeated it, and built a defensive history note
around it.

## What I changed

`CLAUDE.md` now states the truth test and says what the decision rules out.

`ADR-035` is `Active` (v3) and gains a section pointing at the two decisions
that narrowed it. `LIT-035`'s history note loses its defence against the
misread rule and says the plain thing.

## Then I made the same class of mistake again

Having been corrected for quoting a rule I had not read, I promoted `ADR-035`
and went to remove the directives the promotion stripped — and found three of
them on `ADR-046`, `ADR-048` and `ADR-049`. Decisions numbered *after* it.
Which I also had not read.

**`ADR-046`** already did most of what I thought I was doing: *"Tag the
document's subject, and be liberal about it. More than one topic is normal;
three is fine. The test is whether the tag is justifiably appropriate …
`ADR-035` stands unchanged … What this decision says is that the forbidden
case is narrow."* My `ADR-035` v2 section restated that from scratch without
citing it, and my `CLAUDE.md` rewrite cited neither it nor `ADR-049`.

**`ADR-049`** is worse for me, because it names what I had just done. Its
title is *"Unbound is never the resting state"*, and it says an unbound
relation *"is a defect with exactly two readings — the invariant is missing,
or the relation is wrong — and never a third where the answer is to note that
somebody looked."* It took 22 unbound relations and 10 unbound lines to zero,
and rejected an acknowledgement directive for the purpose, saying both earlier
attempts *"treat the report as a noticeboard"*.

One contribution earlier I had written: *"Read, and left."*

So the corrected `CLAUDE.md` rule now carries `ADR-046`'s test and `ADR-049`'s
two readings, and the `ADR-035` section points at both rather than
reconstructing the rule a third time. Four passes have now written down some
version of this. The reason the fourth went wrong is that the operative rule
lived in none of the places a person looks first.

## The thing I got right for the wrong reason

I left the `SOTA-036 → 037 → 038 → SOTA-279` line unbound rather than tagging
a prompting practice `model-architecture`. Not tagging it was right — that tag
fails `ADR-046`'s test. Calling the result a deliberate resting state was not.

## The open line, which is open and not settled

Under `ADR-049` that line is a defect with two readings, and **this
contribution answers neither**. That is a debt, written down as one.

- *The invariant is missing.* `adaptation-and-tuning` is true of `SOTA-037`
  and `SOTA-038` — in-context learning is adaptation without a gradient — and
  not of `SOTA-036`, whose body is four pretraining choices: decoder-only, a
  single next-token objective, a broad filtered web corpus, a fixed context.
  Nobody browsing "taking a trained model somewhere new" should meet that, so
  adding it would fail `ADR-046`'s test.
- *The relation is wrong.* None of the three edges looks wrong. Chain-of-thought
  prompting does refine few-shot prompting.

What the four share is **in-context learning**: the model, the finding that it
emerges, the claim that it permits few-shot adaptation, and how to write the
exemplars. The vocabulary cannot say that, and `CLAUDE.md`'s own rule names
the move — add the word, with a label and a blurb and a decision. That is a
fifteenth topic with a corpus-wide blast radius, and doing it as a side effect
of a gloss fix at the end of a session is how the last two mistakes happened.

Left as the finding it is, with both readings written down and the candidate
answer named. Not "read and left".

## Standing question for the person who was there

`ADR-035` was `Proposed` and its condition named a person rather than a
result — *"that reading should be checked by someone who was there"*. It was
checked in this exchange and is now `Active` (v3). Eighteen `inactive-ok`
directives vouching for it while it was `Proposed` no longer applied and are
removed.
