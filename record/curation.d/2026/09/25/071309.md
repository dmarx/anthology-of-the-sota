The previous unit measured a gap and declined to work it: 89 files saying
"BERT", 51 of them reading notes, practices or explanations, and no BERT in the
record. Working it turned up something better than an absence.

## What the gap was hiding

`SOTA-373` says to set a masking ratio by the signal's redundancy rather than by
the ratio that worked on text. Its argument is a contrast: MAE masks 75% of
image patches, BERT masks 15% of tokens, five times apart, and the difference is
a fact about the signals. Its consensus note called BERT one of "the two
masked-prediction documents it holds".

The record held one. That is the second consensus note in two days to assert
something structural about a paper the record did not have — `SOTA-365`'s was
the first — and the repeat is worth naming as a pattern rather than a slip. **A
consensus note is where the record says what it knows about a field, and it is
the field with the least machinery behind it.** `source:` is linted.
`introduced_by:` is linted. `consensus:` is a closed vocabulary. The *note* is
free prose asserting things about documents, and nothing checks whether the
documents exist.

## And then the larger thing

Filing BERT was supposed to make the contrast true. It made it lopsided instead.

> In all of our experiments, we mask 15% of all WordPiece tokens in each
> sequence at random.

There is no sweep. BERT ablates NSP, bidirectionality, model size, the
feature-based/fine-tuning split, and the 80/10/10 substitution mix. It does not
ablate the fraction of tokens selected. The single appendix table that looks
like it does is headed **"Masking Rates"** and its columns are
`Mask / Same / Rnd` — every row holds the selection rate at 15% and varies what
a selected token is replaced with. A heading that names the quantity the table
does not vary is about as good a trap as a record like this one will find.

RoBERTa, which revisits BERT's recipe in more detail than anyone, varies *when*
the mask is drawn and never how much of the sequence it covers.

So the practice's title — "not the ratio that worked on text" — refers to a
number that was **declared in 2018 and inherited since**. MAE swept its ratio
and calls the optimum "surprisingly high". BERT declared its and nobody in this
record's two sources ever checked.

**The recommendation gets stronger, not weaker.** "Do not inherit the number
from another modality" is *more* forceful once the number turns out never to
have been justified in its own. What dies is the framing where 15% and 75% are
two measured optima whose gap wants an explanation. One optimum is measured. The
other is a convention.

This is the third time this week that reading a source has turned a comparison
the record was making into a different comparison. It keeps happening for one
reason: **a number with a famous paper attached gets treated as a result, and
the check is to open the paper and look for the sweep.**

## The defect worth keeping

RoBERTa's other contribution is methodological and the record now holds it as a
practice.

BERT reported that removing next sentence prediction hurts. RoBERTa:

> It is possible that the original BERT implementation may only have removed the
> loss term while still retaining the segment-pair input format.

An auxiliary loss arrives with a way of building inputs that exists to make it
well-posed. Delete the loss and the pipeline is still paying for a task nobody
is training on, so "remove NSP" was one edit to the code and two to the
experiment. Split apart: keeping the loss and shortening inputs to single
sentences **hurts**; dropping the loss and packing contiguous text **matches or
slightly improves**. The input construction was carrying the result the loss got
credit for.

Filed as `SOTA-tmpvgm7o`, with two sources rather than one, because **the same
defect turned up in this record this morning, in a completely different
literature.** `LIT-667` reports that four grokking papers establish a dataset-size
threshold while every one of them varies the training *fraction of a fixed
universe* — one knob moving how much data there is and what proportion of the
possible examples it covers. Given two knobs and turned separately, size does
nothing.

Encoders and modular arithmetic have nothing in common except the shape of the
mistake: **a single experimental knob that two design decisions are wired to,
and a conclusion that reverses when somebody unwires them.** Neither paper drew
the general lesson, which is why the practice cites both and belongs to neither.

The `promote_when` asks for what the record does not have and would need to
state a rate: a re-run under the rule where the **original conclusion
survives**. Two reversals show the confound can matter. A record that collects
only reversals will believe it always does.

## Three papers that shipped their second-best configuration

Worth recording because it is now three in two units and it bears on how
`implementations:` should be read.

- ALBERT's ablation scores attention-only sharing 1.6 above all-shared. ALBERT
  ships all-shared, because parameter reduction was the point.
- RoBERTa finds `doc-sentences` slightly better than `full-sentences`. It ships
  `full-sentences`, because the better one needs a variable batch size.
- RoBERTa finds byte-level BPE *"slightly worse end-task performance on some
  tasks"* than BERT's character-level BPE. It ships byte-level, because a
  universal encoding never emits an unknown token.

In all three the paper says so plainly. The lesson is not about honesty, it is
about reading: **a paper's shipped configuration is evidence about what its
authors valued, not about what scored best**, and an `implementations:` entry
copied from the abstract can therefore contradict the very ablation the practice
rests on. `SOTA-403` was filed earlier today with `implementations: []` for
exactly this reason and now has company.

## What this unit did not take

`2202.08005` — Wettig et al., *Should You Mask 15% in Masked Language
Modeling?* — sweeps the text side and reports a higher optimum. It is the
obvious next paper and it was deliberately left out: filing it in the same unit
would have let the correction to `SOTA-373` rest on the new answer, when the
finding worth recording is that **the record had no answer and did not know it.**
That distinction survives better as two units than as one.
