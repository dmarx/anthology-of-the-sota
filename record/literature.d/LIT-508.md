# LIT-508: Opening the Black Box of Deep Neural Networks via Information

Shwartz-Ziv and Tishby (2017) — [ARXIV-1703.00810](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00810).

## Standing

**The claim, and the record holds it as the claim rather than as a result.**
Its four stated findings are that most training epochs are spent compressing
the input rather than fitting the labels; that compression begins when
training error becomes small and SGD moves from drift to diffusion; that
converged layers sit on or near the IB bound, by a "generalization through
noise mechanism ... unique to Deep Neural Networks and absent in one layer
networks"; and that adding hidden layers dramatically reduces training time,
so the main advantage of depth is computational.

**Two of those are refuted and one is contested.** [LIT-509](LIT-509.md) shows the
compression phase is a property of the nonlinearity and of the binning scheme,
not of learning; that compression and generalization dissociate in all four
combinations; and that full-batch gradient descent compresses too, so
stochasticity is not the cause. [LIT-507](LIT-507.md) partly defends the phase
claim under better estimators and **agrees** with the rebuttal on
generalization, finding no significant correlation in hidden layers. The
fourth claim — the computational benefit of depth — is addressed by neither
and is left standing here.

**Not read as a `NOTE`.** This record read its experimental setup and its
stated claims directly, which is what the documents around it cite, and did
not work through its theoretical sections. What a full reading would settle
is whether the IB-bound claim (iii) survives the estimator objection, since a
bound stated in units of a quantity that is infinite without an imposed noise
model inherits every problem [LIT-509](LIT-509.md) raises.

**Filed `Active`, as the record files a paper whose claims moved on.** Status
here is standing in the anthology, not whether the claim held — the same
treatment [LIT-470](LIT-470.md) gets after [LIT-471](LIT-471.md).
