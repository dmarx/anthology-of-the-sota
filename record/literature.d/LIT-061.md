# LIT-061: Efficient Large Scale Language Modeling with Mixtures of Experts

Artetxe, Bhosale, Goyal, Mihaylov, Ott et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2112.10684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10684)

## Key takeaways

- The paper is a **comparison study**, not an architecture proposal: how
  autoregressive MoE language models scale against dense ones, measured in-
  and out-of-domain, zero- and few-shot, and under full fine-tuning. Its value
  to this record is the systematic like-for-like measurement rather than a new
  mechanism.
- MoE layers scale efficiently through conditional computation — more
  parameters at roughly constant per-token compute — and the study is about
  where that advantage holds and where it narrows.
- The batch-size observations the record draws on ([SOTA-061](../practices.d/SOTA-061.md), [SOTA-062](../practices.d/SOTA-062.md)) come
  from its training setup: larger batches use the hardware better, smaller
  ones are more sample-efficient, and the two have to be traded rather than
  jointly optimised.

## Not Fedus et al.

This note was attributed to Fedus, who is Switch Transformer's first author —
a different paper about a different contribution. Corrected at version 2.

Filed here because the mistake is instructive: title and arXiv identifier
agreed with each other, and only the author field was wrong, so nothing
mechanical could catch it. That is the second instance in this record, after
[LIT-015](LIT-015.md), and both were found by trying to write down why a practice holds
([#107](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/107)).
