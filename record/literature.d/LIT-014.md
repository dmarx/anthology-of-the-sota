# LIT-014: Visualizing the Loss Landscape of Neural Nets

Li et al. (2017) — [ARXIV-1712.09913](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09913)

## Key takeaways

- **Filter normalization** is the contribution: scale each random plotting
  direction filter-wise to the norm of the trained filter. Without it,
  loss-surface plots ignore batch normalization and the network's rescaling
  symmetries, and their sharpness comparisons "may be misleading"
- **Under that normalization, sharpness correlates with generalization
  error** — "even when making comparisons across disparate network
  architectures and training methods". The conditional is the result
- **The convex-to-chaotic transition**: "when networks become sufficiently
  deep, neural loss landscapes quickly transition from being nearly convex to
  being highly chaotic", coinciding with a drop in generalization and then a
  loss of trainability
- **Skip connections prevent that transition** and "promote flat minimizers",
  which is offered as the explanation for why they are necessary at extreme
  depth
- **Non-convexity is quantified** as the ratio `|λ_min/λ_max|` of the Hessian
  across the filter-normalized surface (Figure 7). For ResNet-56 the negative
  eigenvalues stay under **1% of the positive curvatures** over a large region
- It is affordable: eigenvalues via an **implicitly restarted Lanczos method**
  needing only Hessian-vector products, with no explicit Hessian
- SGD **optimization trajectories lie in an extremely low-dimensional space**
