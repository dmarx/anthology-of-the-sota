# LIT-089: Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

Zhang et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2302.05543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05543)

## Key takeaways

- Adds spatial control — edges, depth, segmentation, pose — to a **locked**
  pretrained diffusion model, reusing its encoding layers as a backbone. The
  trainable branch is a **clone of those layers**, so the adapter starts already
  knowing how to encode images
- **Zero convolutions** are the design: the connections between branch and
  frozen model are convolutions **initialised to zero**, so the adapter is an
  *exact* no-op at initialisation and its parameters "progressively grow from
  zero", ensuring "no harmful noise could affect the finetuning". Zero is not
  small — it is exactly neutral, so the guarantee is structural rather than
  probabilistic
- Works singly and in combination, **with or without text prompts**
- **Robust at both ends of the data range** — under 50k and over 1m examples.
  Adapter methods usually have a small-data floor; this reports not having one

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-071](../notes.d/NOTE-071.md). Retagged to `adaptation-and-tuning`: the generative model
is frozen and untouched.

**The takeaway "zero-shot conditioning" was wrong.** ControlNet trains an
adapter per condition type; nothing about it is zero-shot. The word that belongs
there is *zero-initialized*, which is a different thing and is the actual
mechanism.

Nothing is sourced here, but the mechanism has reach the record does not
capture: **initialise an added branch's output to zero so it is an exact no-op
at the start of training.** That is here, in LoRA's zero-initialised `B`, and in
zero-init residual branches — three independent places, one idea, and
`SOTA-052` carries only the scaling version of the same argument, not the limit.
