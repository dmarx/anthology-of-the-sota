`#290` asked for a triage of four catalogue repositories: resolve every arXiv
identifier their READMEs cite, say what the repository calls each one, and
give every missing identifier a disposition. Explicitly a candidate queue and
not a filing backlog, and explicitly with a declined count, because a triage
that declines nothing did not triage.

**128 distinct identifiers. 22 already held, 106 missing. 25 promoted, 81
declined.**

## What the pass was expecting, and what it found

`#289` had established the reading: an adoption index says people converged,
a catalogue says one person found something interesting, and only the first
is evidence about `consensus`. The expectation going in was therefore that a
catalogue is *low yield* — a list of variants nobody runs, which is exactly
the failure `#183` names, 62 unadopted architecture variants filed as
practice candidates.

That expectation was right about the variants and wrong about the yield. The
two strongest findings of the whole pass are:

- **`2010.11929` (ViT) is not in the record, and 47 held documents say
  "ViT".**
- **`2103.00020` (CLIP) is not in the record, and 45 held documents say
  "CLIP".**

Neither is a practice candidate. Neither would ever appear in a consensus
sweep, because neither is a technique anybody would file a recommendation
about at this point — they are the *background* that later documents assume.
And that is precisely why no previous audit found them. `#202` looks for
practices filed under a topic no source holds; `#289` looks for techniques
several libraries ship. A substrate paper supports no practice of its own and
is shipped by nothing, so it is invisible to both.

Three more of the same kind turned up once the test was made mechanical —
grep the record for the paper's distinctive name and count the documents that
lean on it without holding it:

| identifier | held documents that depend on it |
| --- | --- |
| `2509.26030` — Muon vs Adam on tail-end associative memory | 59 say "Muon" |
| `1909.11942` — ALBERT | 12 run experiments on it; `THEORY-065` leans on its layer sharing |
| `2103.14030` — Swin | 9 use it as an experimental backbone |

**The generalisation worth keeping: a catalogue's value to this record is not
the frontier it lists, it is the floor it stands on.** The variants at the
top of a lucidrains README are a reading list. The papers in the bibliography
that everybody already assumes are the ones the record is quietly missing.

## The decision rules, so the next pass does not re-derive them

Promoted for one of three reasons: the record already depends on it and does
not hold it (substrate); it is a negative or controlled result, which is what
`DP-005` and `DP-009` say this anthology is for; or it is the named origin or
successor of something already held — `2112.05682` before FlashAttention,
`2010.04245` before `LIT-088`'s QK-norm, `2211.01095` after `LIT-076`.

Declined under four rules, with counts:

- **`D-variant`, 27** — an architecture variant with a feature section and
  runnable code, where no held document depends on it and the incumbent
  practice is already filed. This is `#183`'s warning made operational, and
  it is the largest decline bucket for the reason `#183` predicted.
- **`D-biblio`, 27** — cited in the bibliography and never given a feature
  section. One person's reading, mostly 2025–2026 preprints. `#289` measured
  that a catalogue is not consensus evidence, and this is where that bites
  hardest.
- **`D-cluster`, 18** — a method that only means something once a cluster the
  record has not opened lands. Fourteen of these are captum's attribution
  methods, declined *behind* the two promoted from the same list: file
  `1703.01365` and `1810.03292` first, and revisit the rest only if that
  opens.
- **`D-roster`, 7** and **`D-self`, 2** — a checkpoint row or dataset card,
  which is not a technique claim at all, and the repository's own paper.

A decline is a decline of the *candidacy*, not of the paper. Each carries the
trigger that would reverse it.

## captum stays a catalogue; open_clip is a third thing and was dropped

`#290` asked whether these two belong in the catalogue class at all, and said
to decide before spending the pass. The pass is what decided it, which is the
right way round but worth saying plainly.

**captum is a catalogue, and its being Meta's production library does not
make it an adoption index.** Its `References of Algorithms` section is the
cleanest of the four — one line per public class, naming the paper. The
argument that settles the class is in the README itself: it links
`1810.03292` (*Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps*) and `2106.07475`
(*Investigating sanity checks…*), both of which examine methods it ships
anyway. **A list that carries its own critiques is enumerating a literature,
not reporting a convergence.** Completeness is the point, so it is a reading
list with an API.

**open_clip is neither, and is now deliberately absent from the manifest.**
Nine of its fifteen missing identifiers are checkpoint rows in the pretrained
table or dataset cards — SigLIP2, DFN, PE, LAION-400M, LAION-5B, DataComp —
and two more are the repository's own papers. The `arxiv` extractor cannot
tell a checkpoint from a technique, so what it reads there is a model roster
with a bibliography attached: a third kind of index, and not one `#183` has a
reading for. A roster is worth one person's eye once and is a bad thing to
scrape every run, because every new checkpoint grows it and none of the
growth is a technique. The one-off pass took what it had — CLIP, WiSE-FT,
SigLIP, FLIP — and the manifest header now says why it will not be back.

## Two traps for the next person

**The heading a catalogue files a citation under names the feature it
supports, not the paper.** Twenty-three x-transformers identifiers had no
bibtex entry, so the first pass labelled them by the nearest `###` heading.
Four of the twenty-three came out materially wrong: `2405.15071` is *Grokked
Transformers are Implicit Reasoners* filed under "Weight-tied Layers",
`2410.01104` is *Softmax is not Enough* under "Explicit Sparse Transformer",
`2305.19268` is *Intriguing Properties of Quantization at Scale* under "QK
RMSNorm", and `2111.05498` is *Attention Approximates Sparse Distributed
Memory* under "Cosine Sim Attention". Two of those four were promoted after
resolution and would have been declined on the heading. **Resolve the title
before ranking. A heading is a claim about the library, not about the paper.**

**arXiv's 406 is throttling, and I nearly filed a luria bug saying otherwise
for the second time.** The previous entry recorded that `luria remotes
--resolve` gets HTTP 406 where a plain request gets 200, and read that as a
difference between luria's client and curl. It is not. A plain `urllib`
request got 406 immediately after a `curl` for the same endpoint got 200, and
a 429 followed on the next attempt. Resolving all 106 titles took a curl loop
with a 5-second gap between batches and exponential backoff on failure — and
luria's own output had already said **`throttled — HTTP 406`** the whole
time. Twice now the diagnosis has been rediscovered the hard way from
luria's correct message. The rate limit is the finding; there is no bug.
