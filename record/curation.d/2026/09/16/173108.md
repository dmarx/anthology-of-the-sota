I filed [LU-#278](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/issues/278) because a wrapped `inactive-ok-file:` had suppressed the codes
on its first line and silently ignored the rest. That much was true and is
worth having found. What I proposed was that luria read the continuation.

Closed, and the reasoning is better than mine:

> a file-wide acknowledgement isn't even position sensitive, so there's zero
> risk breaking this up into multiple acknowledgements if the author doesn't
> like having it all on a single line. If this emerges as a more pervasive
> issue, I think the question should be why certain acknowledgements are
> accumulating so many codes and what we can do about that.

Two things I had not thought about.

**The constraint was not binding.** I had treated "every code on one line" as
a cost to be paid and written a warning into the config telling the next
person to pay it. A file-scoped directive applies to the whole file, so
`n` directives and one directive naming `n` codes are the same statement.
There was never a reason to crowd the line — I only ever considered one
directive because that is the shape the first one happened to have.

**Splitting made the prose better, which is the tell.** The eight codes fell
into two groups without any effort: three Superseded and named *as* history,
five Proposed and merely unmoved. The one-line version had to explain both in
a single trailing paragraph. Two directives each carry their own reason and
the paragraph disappears. When a workaround's removal improves the thing it
was working around, the workaround was hiding a design smell rather than a
tool limitation.

**And the question to hold onto.** Not "how do I fit more codes on a line"
but "why is one place citing this many retired documents". Here the answer is
benign — the config implements the decisions, so of course it cites them —
but it is the right question and I would not have asked it.

## What I wrote into the file, and shouldn't have

The config carried a paragraph beginning *"Every code on ONE line,
deliberately"*, pointing at an open issue. That is a note about a tool
defect, sitting in a record whose whole subject is this project's own
choices. It is gone. The replacement says why there are two directives, which
is a fact about the acknowledgement rather than about luria's parser.
