# LIT-117: Efficient Online Data Mixing For Language Model Pre-Training

Albalak et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2312.02406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02406)

## Key takeaways

- **The problem is that mixing proportions are fixed before training** and so
  cannot adapt to training dynamics, while data *selection* methods that could
  adapt are too slow to run online
- **ODM** combines the two: group data into domains as mixing does, but choose
  the proportions online as selection would, formulated as a multi-armed
  bandit over the domains
- **The reward is the per-domain training loss**, computed on the batches the
  run is already taking — "fortuitously requires minimal overhead". The
  argument for it is information-theoretic: perplexity measures expected
  information gain, so sample most from the domain with most left to learn
- **The algorithm is Exp3.** Its policy is a Gibbs distribution over the
  importance-weighted rewards, mixed with a uniform distribution for
  exploration at rate `ℰₜ`:
  `πₜ(Dᵢ) = (1 − K·ℰₜ)·exp(ℰₜ₋₁·R̂ᵢ)/Σⱼexp(ℰₜ₋₁·R̂ⱼ) + ℰₜ`
- **Results**: 1B parameters, 50B tokens, 22 domains of The Pile. Reaches the
  next best method's final perplexity in **19% fewer iterations**, and
  improves 5-shot MMLU by **1.9% relative accuracy**, at negligible
  wall-clock cost
