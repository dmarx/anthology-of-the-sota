Took [#154](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/154), the geometry line split out of [#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87). Two things to record: what the
gap actually was, and that the issue's own first step was wrong because I
wrote it without opening the documents it named.

## The gap was a dependency, not a topic

The record recommends how to **represent** a reconstructed scene — [SOTA-205](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-205.md),
from Instant-NGP, K-Planes, 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeuS2 — and held nothing
about how the scene got reconstructed. So a reader arriving with a pile of
uncalibrated images found four papers that assume the camera poses they came
to ask about.

What makes the filing interesting is that the missing half is not a parallel
topic. It is a **dependency order**. Classical multi-view reconstruction needs
calibration and pose first because triangulation is *defined* in terms of
them — DUSt3R's words: "mandatory to triangulate corresponding pixels in 3D
space, which is the core of all best performing MVS algorithms". The stages
are not a decomposition anyone chose; each exists because the next cannot
start without it.

So the practice is an inversion rather than an alternative: regress the
structure, and recover matches and cameras *from* it. Two things fall out that
the classical framing cannot express — monocular and multi-view become one
problem with different amounts of evidence, and the projective camera model
stops being a constraint to satisfy and becomes a property the answer has.

## The first step of my own issue was wrong

[#154](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/154) proposed, as its cheapest work, extracting practices from [LIT-111](../../../../literature.d/LIT-111.md),
[LIT-107](../../../../literature.d/LIT-107.md) and [LIT-113](../../../../literature.d/LIT-113.md) — three vision notes that are read and source nothing.

All three had already been audited by [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121) and deliberately left unsourced,
each with its reason written into the note:

- [LIT-111](../../../../literature.d/LIT-111.md) — its transferable claim is "the same decision [SOTA-205](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-205.md) already
  files from four sources, arrived at for pose rather than for rendering."
- [LIT-113](../../../../literature.d/LIT-113.md) — adoption rather than an independent result, so [ADR-017](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) puts it in
  `consensus_note` and not `source:`.
- [LIT-107](../../../../literature.d/LIT-107.md) — declined on a narrow ground worth quoting: "the record's
  evaluation practices are about what to measure, and this is about how to
  present it. That may be a gap in the topic rather than in the corpus."

I wrote that step from the *query* — read, unsourced, therefore unextracted —
without opening the standing sections. It is the same error as the one on
2026-09-16, when I read a bucket of unsourced notes as a backlog and found
every one correctly filed. **Twice now, "unsourced" has looked like a worklist
and been a set of decisions.**

The difference this time is that I wrote the mistake into a GitHub issue,
where it would have sent the next person to re-derive three determinations
that were already made and recorded. Corrected on the issue rather than
quietly dropped.

One of the three leaves a real candidate behind, and it is worth keeping
visible: [LIT-107](../../../../literature.d/LIT-107.md)'s "report a frontier, not a winner" was declined for a
*topic* reason, not an evidence reason, and [LIT-156](../../../../literature.d/LIT-156.md) is a second in-record
source for the same lesson. Two sources and a stated blocker is a different
thing from a gap.

## What I did not file

The matching line — SuperGlue, LoFTR, SuperPoint — which is the other half of
the classical pipeline and is still absent. It is a coherent follow-up rather
than part of this claim: DUSt3R and VGGT make matching an *output*, so the
papers that made it a better *input* belong to the line they superseded, and
filing them well means saying what they were for.

Also not filed: the architectures, and VGGT's "under one second". The first
dates immediately and the second is hardware-bound. What the practice records
is the choice available to somebody holding uncalibrated images, which is what
survives the specific network.

## The condition that took the most thought

`emerging`, not `converged`, and the reason is not caution about the
measurements. A triangulated point carries a geometric justification and a
residual you can inspect; a regressed pointmap carries neither. Wherever a
reconstruction has to be **certified** rather than merely used — survey,
metrology, anything with a tolerance — that difference is the entire question,
and neither paper addresses it. A practice that said "predict it directly"
without that condition would be right about benchmarks and wrong about the
jobs where the classical pipeline is load-bearing.
