# LIT-003: Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units

Sennrich et al. (2015) — [ARXIV-1508.07909](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.07909)

## Key takeaways

- **Translation is an open-vocabulary problem and NMT models were not**,
  backing off to a dictionary for out-of-vocabulary words — handling them
  outside the model rather than in it
- The fix is to encode rare and unknown words as **sequences of subword
  units**, which makes the model itself open-vocabulary
- **Byte pair encoding, adapted from compression to segmentation**: start
  from characters, repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair, stop at
  a chosen number of merges
- Frequent words survive as single units, rare ones fragment, and **the
  character level is always reachable** — so nothing is unrepresentable
- The merge table is **fit to a corpus**, which is where the permanent cost of
  an unrepresentative fitting distribution comes from

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-007](../practices.d/SOTA-007.md).

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-010](../notes.d/NOTE-010.md). The practice is
**confirmed**.

One framing the reading adds: the contribution is **open-vocabulary
modelling**, and BPE is the mechanism reached for. Stated as a recommendation
*for BPE*, the practice makes the algorithm the point — and the field has
since replaced the algorithm repeatedly while keeping the property.

The first tokenizer-free model in the record was measured against BPE
directly. Hwang, Wang and Gu's H-Net (LIT-679) uses a GPT-2 BPE Transformer
as its tokenized baseline, matched in bytes per batch and FLOPs per byte at
up to 1.3B-equivalent FLOPs, one run each. One stage of learned chunking only
ties it (0.728 against 0.730 bits/byte at XL); two stages beat it (0.715, and
58.2 against 55.5 on seven zero-shot tasks). The largest gains are on
Chinese, where a merge table fit elsewhere compresses least: 3.62 bytes per
token for Llama-3's BPE against 5.81 for learned chunking. The paper says
its own bits/byte for the BPE baseline is overestimated, which favours H-Net.
