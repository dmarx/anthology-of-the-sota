Earlier today I ran a census on the generic-bullet `LIT` corpus, verified from
both directions that **none of the 52 sourced an in-force practice**, and
recommended skipping them: reading them "would correct no recommendation".

dmarx said read them. The measurement was correct and the recommendation drawn
from it was wrong, and the gap between those two is the thing worth writing
down.

## What the measurement missed

I measured the right quantity — *does a wrong note make a wrong
recommendation?* — and treated it as the only quantity. It is not. A note also:

- **states things that are false**, which is a defect whether or not anything
  cites it. Nine documents asserted something their paper does not contain, and
  six of those stated its inverse. `LIT-111` said "category-level pose
  estimation" about a paper whose contribution is needing **no** category-level
  training. `LIT-108` said "dynamic scene optimization" about a static-scene
  paper. `LIT-038`'s bullets were about a different paper entirely.
- **is a place where connections between practices become visible.** Reading
  `LIT-067` produced the antecedent of `SOTA-188`; reading `LIT-017` beside
  `LIT-065` produced the reason 3D parallelism exists; reading `LIT-036`
  produced the measurement behind `SOTA-187`'s premise. None of those is a
  correction. All of them are things the record did not contain and now does.

The census answered "is anything broken?" and the answer was no. It could not
answer "is anything missing?", and I read the first answer as though it settled
both.

## The generalisable version

**A measurement of harm is not a measurement of value.** I checked that these
documents were not causing errors, concluded they were inert, and skipped the
question of what reading them would *produce*. The second question has no cheap
proxy — you find out by doing the work — which is exactly why it is easy to let
the first stand in for it.

Where this bites: any time a cheap check comes back clean and the expensive work
is then declined on the strength of it. The clean check is evidence about one
failure mode.

## What I would keep from the census anyway

The census was not wasted and the finding in it is still true: `#114`'s worklist
was the complete at-risk set, and no recommendation in this record rested on an
unread note. That is worth knowing and it is worth having verified from both
directions. What it did not license was the conclusion I drew from it.

## Practical notes for the next pass

- **`ar5iv` renders 48 of 49 arXiv papers here.** One (`arXiv:1908.08681`) has
  no rendering and became `Skimmed`, the same route EDM took. The fetch script
  and text extractor are in the session scratchpad and were disposable by
  design; if this recurs they belong in `src/scripts/`.
- **`Skimmed` earned a second meaning this pass.** It had been "the full text
  was unavailable". `LIT-026` is `Skimmed` because reading 66,000 words of
  federated-learning open problems, to correct four bullets on a document
  nothing cites, is not a good use of a session. That is a judgement about
  **proportion**, and the status carries it fine.
- **`luria link --fix` moves lines, so place `inactive-ok` directives after
  running it, not before.** I placed several against pre-fix line numbers and
  the lint correctly reported them as applying to nothing. The directive covers
  its own line and the one below; anchor it to the paragraph's first line.
- **Body headings must match `title:` exactly.** I shortened five and the lint
  caught all five. Scaffold with the full title and leave the heading alone.
