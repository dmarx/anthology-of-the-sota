[#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87) is a checklist of fifteen subjects the record might not cover. Before
filing anything I ran the obvious query — does a document mention this? — and
the answer reorganised the list.

Several items were stale: paged attention is `SOTA-105`, continuous batching
is `SOTA-113`, multi-token prediction is `SOTA-162`, LoRA is `SOTA-184`,
post-training quantization is `SOTA-185`. Several were total: **zero
documents** for ternary quantization, self-correction, test-time training,
QLoRA, LyCORIS, SLAM or structure-from-motion, and — this one is a surprise —
for InstructGPT, which is not in the record even as a `LIT` note.

And one was neither, which is why it is the one I took.

## The shape of the gap

**Speculative decoding is referenced by six documents here and filed by
none.**

`LIT-185` is EAGLE-3 — a paper whose entire subject is making the *draft
model* cheaper. `SOTA-162` recommends multi-token-prediction heads and argues
for them partly because "the extra heads are a draft model for speculative
decoding you did not have to train separately", citing a 3x decode speedup.
Three model reports name the technique. `LIT-217` mentions it in passing.

The thing all six take for granted had no document. Leviathan et al. and Chen
et al. were not in the record in any form.

That is [DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7)'s second failure mode, stated in the principle as *"the
refinement filed before the thing it refines"*, and it is worth noticing how
it happened, because nobody was careless. Every one of those six documents was
filed correctly. Each arrived with a name and a claim; each became an entry.
Speculative decoding by 2024 had no arrival — it was what the arrivals
assumed. [DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7)'s warning is that agreement generates no argument and
therefore no prompt, and here the record was one query away from saying so out
loud and nobody had run the query.

## What made it worth filing rather than assuming

Two things, and neither is in the summaries.

**The guarantee is exact, not approximate.** The draft does not approximate
the target's output; it *proposes*, and the target *decides*, under a rule
constructed so the composite sampler is unbiased. Leviathan measures against
identical outputs. Chen is more careful still — "within hardware numerics" —
and that qualifier is the honest version, which is the one the practice
carries.

**The economics are computable in advance.** `alpha = E(min(p, q))` is the
acceptance rate; `c` the draft's relative cost; the walltime improvement is
`(1 - alpha^(gamma+1)) / ((1 - alpha)(gamma*c + 1))`. So "is this draft good
enough" and "how deep should I speculate" are measurements, not habits. A
practice that said only "use a small model to guess" would have dropped the
part that makes it actionable.

## One thing I did not repair

Counting adopters for `consensus_note`, `LIT-185`'s standing says `LIT-135`
and `LIT-139` "ship the same arrangement". `LIT-135`'s note bears it out.
`LIT-139`'s does not — it records multi-token prediction carried over from its
predecessor and says nothing about a draft or about speculative decoding.

I checked rather than copied, which is the whole lesson of the last two
sessions, and then left it.

While writing *this* entry I hand-wrote two links to the design principles
instead of leaving bare codes for `luria link --fix`, and the lint caught
them — the identical defect I fixed in the 19:20 entry ninety minutes ago and
wrote a commit message about. A rule you have just enforced on someone else is
not a rule you have internalised. Repairing it means reading DeepSeek-V4's report,
and repairing it *without* reading would be committing the same error one
document further along. It is written into the practice as a finding for
[#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87)'s next pass.

## The rest of the checklist

Thirteen items remain and are now specified rather than vague. The audit query
was "does any document mention this", which finds absence and cannot find
*thinness* — a subject with one passing mention scores the same as one with a
practice. [DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4) says what a clean result on that query is evidence about, and
the next pass over [#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87) should run the opposite one: which `inference-optimization`
and `adaptation-and-tuning` papers here support no practice at all.
