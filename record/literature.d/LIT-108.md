# LIT-108: 3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering

Kerbl et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2308.04079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04079)

## Key takeaways

- Three elements, all ablated separately: **3D Gaussians initialised from the
  sparse SfM points produced during camera calibration**, **interleaved
  optimization and density control** including **anisotropic covariance**, and a
  **visibility-aware rasterizer** that accelerates training as well as rendering
- **The key property is that one primitive has both:** Gaussians are "a
  differentiable volumetric representation, but they can also be rasterized very
  efficiently by projecting them to 2D, and applying standard α-blending, using
  an equivalent image formation model as NeRF". Prior work had to choose between
  optimisable and fast; this does not trade
- **First real-time (≥30 fps) radiance-field rendering at 1080p** for unbounded,
  complete scenes, at quality matching the expensive methods and training times
  competitive with the fast ones
- **Static scenes.** The optimisation referred to is *density control* during
  fitting; dynamic Gaussian work is later and separate

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-045](../notes.d/NOTE-045.md).

A source of [SOTA-tmpjpcc8](../practices.d/SOTA-tmpjpcc8.md), supplying the Gaussian primitives and the
practice's most portable corollary: **initialise from structure you already
computed.** The SfM initialisation is ablated here (C2), which is the record's
only ablation of that claim.

**The takeaway "dynamic scene optimization" was wrong.** This paper is about
static scenes — the abstract's scope is "unbounded and complete scenes (rather
than isolated objects)", and the optimisation is density control of Gaussians
during fitting. Dynamic 3D Gaussian work came afterwards. A reader taking that
bullet at face value would cite this for something it does not do.
