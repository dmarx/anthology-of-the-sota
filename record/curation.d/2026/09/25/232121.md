Two things, and the second is process.

## Six documents about a trade, and no account of why the trade exists

Over four units the record built out guidance: `SOTA-424` (the CFG recipe),
`SOTA-425` (report precision and recall because FID's optimum is interior),
`THEORY-109` (the adversarial-metric suspicion), `THEORY-110` (FID prefers
coverage), and the scale-dependents `SOTA-202`, `SOTA-203`, `SOTA-410`. Every one
of them takes the fidelity-for-diversity trade as a **given**. The closest thing to
a mechanism the record held was `THEORY-104` — what a large guidance scale does to
a solver's convergence radius — which is a consequence of guidance, not a reason
for it.

`2406.02507` supplies the reason, and it is not the one the cluster assumed.
Guidance adds a force along `∇ₓ log[p₁/p₀]`. The reference `p₀` is not merely
*unconditional*, it is **worse** — "a more difficult task of the two … and typically
only a small slice of the training budget". A looser density falls off more slowly
away from the data, so the ratio decreases with distance from the manifold and its
gradient points inward. The image-quality gain is that inward pull. The class
emphasis is a separate effect, and it is the one that costs diversity.

The measured asymmetry is large and nobody prints it: EDM2-S on ImageNet-512 is
FID **2.56** conditional against **11.67** unconditional, and the paper's aside is
the sharpest line in it — "the unconditional case tends to work so poorly that the
corresponding quantitative numbers are hardly ever reported." Every CFG deployment
is correcting a good model with a model that bad.

**What makes this an account rather than a story is the negative control**, and
this is the part the record should want copied. Construct `D₁` and `D₀` by applying
*different degrees of the same* synthetic corruption to one base model and
autoguidance undoes the damage: dropout 5%/10% recovers FID 2.55 against the
undamaged 2.56; input noise +10%/+20% recovers 2.56. Then cross the corruptions —
dropout on one, input noise on the other — and **guidance stops helping at any
weight**, the optimum falling back to `w = 1`. A generic-sharpening story predicts
the cross arm works. The density-ratio story predicts it fails. It fails.

I have written a lot of `promote_when` clauses this week asking for exactly this
shape and rarely getting it. Worth naming the form: **a mechanism claim earns its
status when the experiment includes an arm the mechanism says must fail.**

## Two Conditions that were about a reference model, not about guidance

`SOTA-424` carried:

- *"Conditional generation only. There has to be a condition to drop."*
- *"Diversity is what is being spent."*

Both true of the recipe. Neither true of guidance. Autoguidance takes unconditional
EDM2-S from 11.67 to 3.86 — the regime the first condition declares empty — and
keeps the quality gain without the variety loss, which is the second condition's
subject matter. v3 says which of the two each belongs to rather than deleting them,
because for a reader who is in fact training a CFG model both still hold.

**The generalisable bit**: a practice's Conditions section is written from the
source's experiments, and a source that varied only one reference model cannot
distinguish "this costs diversity" from "guidance costs diversity". The tell is a
Condition stated about the *category* when the paper only ever tested one *member*.
That is the third variant of scope-defect this journal has recorded in three days —
after a `universal` practice silent about its default regime (`SOTA-203`) and a
title narrower than its own body (`SOTA-337`).

## The check I said was missing, run this time

Yesterday's duplicate happened because I measured the gap before branching and
never re-measured before committing, so a paper filed on another branch in the
meantime was invisible. This unit ran the full version first:

    grep -rl "2406.02507" record/literature.d/          # empty
    grep -rln "autoguidance" record/                    # empty
    gh-equivalent: list open PRs                        # none
    git ls-remote --heads origin | grep -v main         # stale branches, no open PRs

The third line is the new one and it is the one that mattered. **A grep over the
working tree and a grep over `main` are the same check twice**; neither sees a
branch in flight. Listing open pull requests is a different observation, and it is
the cheap one — the uniqueness constraint will catch the collision eventually, but
only after both branches have merged, which is too late to avoid the cleanup.

Not added to `CLAUDE.md` unasked; the paragraph there covers the concretization
window, which is a different hazard, and whether to extend it is the owner's call.

## Still open, and two papers have now asked for it

The source's own future work: "it could also be interesting to further isolate the
origin of the improvement using alternative metrics, such as precision and recall,
Human Preference Score, or PickScore." That is `SOTA-425`'s recommendation and half
of `THEORY-109`'s `promote_when`, requested by the authors and not run. Every number
in this paper is FID or `FD_DINOv2` — both Fréchet distances that mix fidelity and
coverage — so "quality without losing variation", the headline, is currently a claim
that a mixed metric improved.

That is now **two papers in this cluster asking for a coverage measurement on
guided samples and neither taking it**, which is a better argument for `SOTA-425`
than anything in `SOTA-425`.
