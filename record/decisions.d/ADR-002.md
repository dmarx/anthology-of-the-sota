# ADR-002: Separate papers from practices, so each can have its own status

## Context

In the old schema a paper carried `attic`, `experimental`, `sota_maybe` and
`sota` on the same object. The first three are judgements about the *paper*;
the fourth is a list of judgements about *practice*. The builder resolved the
mismatch by projecting paper-level flags onto every recommendation the paper
produced.

That projection is wrong in both directions, and the corpus contains both:

- **Batch Normalization** is foundational and stays worth reading, while one
  of the recommendations drawn from it is "consider alternatives like
  LayerNorm for transformers" — the paper is current, the advice has moved.
- **Papers retired to the attic** can be the source of practice that still
  holds. Under the projection, atticking the paper would have deprecated
  every recommendation from it, and keeping the recommendations meant the
  paper could not be atticked.

The projection also had a bug that hid the whole problem: because the
`experimental` and `superseded_by` branches read `paper.sota`, and no paper
with those flags had a `sota` list, they never fired. Every recommendation
came out `standard`, so the collision never produced a visibly wrong record —
just a uniform one.

## Decision

Two schemes.

`LIT` holds one note per paper. Its status answers *what is this paper's
standing in the anthology* — `Active` for worth reading, `Rejected` for the
attic, `Superseded` when later work covers it better.

`SOTA` holds one document per recommendation. Its status answers *should you
do this* — and it is allowed to disagree with the status of the paper it
cites. `SOTA` documents are required to name a `source:`, which is enforced
in `luria.toml`; a recommendation with no paper behind it is an opinion.

Each scheme declares its own `statuses.yaml`, because the five words mean
different things in the two of them.

## Alternatives considered

- **One scheme with a `kind: paper | practice` field.** Fewer moving parts,
  and it reintroduces exactly the problem: one status field for two kinds of
  claim. The tag groups and the `requires` rules would also have to become
  conditional on `kind`, which is a scheme in a trenchcoat.
- **Papers stay data, practices become documents.** Genuinely tempting — the
  bibliography is machine-generated metadata and wants to stay structured.
  It loses the thing that makes the attic work: `attic.reason` is editorial
  prose, and the attic is only checkable if a retired paper is a document
  something can cite and the lint can find citations of.
- **Status quo.** The collision is invisible today only because a bug is
  suppressing it. Fixing the bug without splitting the schemes would make the
  wrong projection start firing.

## Consequences

A practice and its source can now disagree, which is the point, and also a
new way to be wrong: nothing forces a practice sourced from a `Rejected`
paper to be reconsidered. `luria lint` reports those as `retired-citations`
rather than failing on them, and an `inactive-ok:` comment at the citing site
is how a deliberate one is vouched for.

The migration produced 118 notes and 120 practices from 118 papers and 119
recommendations. The extra practice is the `sota_maybe` hedge, which had no
home in the old schema and is now a single `Deferred` document.
