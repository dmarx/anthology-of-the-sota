Three times today a reading has ended the same way: the paper's *baselines*
turned out to be a body of work the record does not hold.

- **NoProp** brought in forward-forward, difference target propagation and
  forward gradients. The record's only answer to "what if not
  backpropagation" was evolution strategies, a different family entirely.
- **Late-phase weights** brought in stochastic weight averaging, BatchEnsemble
  and deep ensembles. The record has nothing on weight-space ensembling at
  all.
- **The Diffusion Duality** brings in MDLM and SEDD — the two strongest
  discrete-diffusion language models, one of which is the direct source of the
  masked-diffusion practice this record already recommends.

That last one is the sharpest. `SOTA-157` and `SOTA-254` say to train a
language model as a masked diffusion model. Neither is sourced to MDLM or
SEDD. The record recommends the family and holds neither of its leading
papers.

## Why this keeps happening, and why it is not a filing error

Working a ranked worklist by revisits finds the papers somebody read. A
paper's baselines are the papers they did not need to re-read, because the
field already agrees on them. So a corpus assembled by "what did I open
recently" is systematically missing exactly the settled work — and the
settled work is what a practice should be sourced to.

`DP-004` again: a pass finds only defects its query is shaped like. The query
here is a reading history, and a reading history is biased toward the
unsettled.

The cheap partial fix is what happened three times today: name the baselines
in the reading, so they exist in the record as names attached to numbers
rather than as absences nobody has noticed. That is not coverage. It is a
worklist that did not exist an hour ago.

## What this particular paper settles

Not that uniform-state diffusion is better — masked diffusion wins 6 of 7
zero-shot datasets in this paper's own tables, and nothing here moves
`SOTA-157` or `SOTA-254`.

What it settles is a **crossover**, and the reason for it. Below about 32
sampling steps, uniform-state beats masked; above, masked wins. The mechanism
is that masked diffusion emits many tokens independently and cannot revise
one once emitted, while uniform-state diffusion can overwrite an earlier
error. That is a property of the processes, not of a hyperparameter, which is
why it is worth a practice rather than a note.

The record previously could not express that, because it held one of the two
families and so had no axis to put the second on.

## And the theorem is worth less than it looks, by the authors' own count

The duality is elegant — `argmax` of a Gaussian diffusion *is* a uniform-state
discrete diffusion, marginals and dynamics both — and it is the paper's title.

The ablation splits the three-point perplexity improvement roughly evenly
between a Rao-Blackwellized ELBO and the duality-derived curriculum. So the
theorem accounts for about 1.3 of 3 points, and the other 1.7 is a
variance-reduced estimator with no connection to Gaussian diffusion at all.

The paper reports this. It is the second contribution today where the
ablation quietly reassigns credit away from the headline — the other being
late-phase weights, whose own hypernetwork variant lost to doing nothing. Both
times the number was in the paper and not in the abstract, which is `DP-010`'s
territory with the sign reversed: not an unsupported claim, but a supported
one whose support belongs elsewhere.
