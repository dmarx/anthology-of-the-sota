# LIT-044: Contrastive Learning with Hard Negative Samples

Robinson et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2010.04592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04592)

## Key takeaways

- Samples hard negatives **without labels** by reweighting the contrastive
  objective rather than changing the sampling procedure — "only two extra lines
  of code" and **no computational overhead**, via importance sampling plus a
  positive-unlabeled correction for false negatives
- **The hardness knob is forced by a real tension**, stated precisely: the
  hardest negatives are the points closest to the anchor, which are exactly the
  ones most likely to share its label — so the residual error of an approximate
  false-negative correction grows with hardness. Hardness trades learning signal
  against contamination
- The family's extremes are legible: hardness fully down recovers the earlier
  debiased-contrastive method; pushed up it tightly clusters each class and
  maximally separates classes
- Margins are small — MoCo-v2 on CIFAR-10 linear readout **88.08% → 88.47%**

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-tmpspi68](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpspi68.md). The document is `Rejected` and the reading does not argue
with that; the CIFAR-10 margin here is 0.39%.

Two things transfer, neither about contrastive learning. **Reweighting and
resampling are interchangeable when the objective is an expectation, and
reweighting is free** — which applies to data mixing, curriculum ordering, and
anywhere the record recommends changing what a model sees. And **where "harder
examples" is proposed, ask what makes them hard and whether that also makes them
wrong**; the record has several practices recommending more of something
difficult and no statement of why such advice needs a ceiling.

Of the takeaways replaced, **"mining strategies comparison" is wrong** — this
paper proposes one method and derives a family around it; it compares nothing.
