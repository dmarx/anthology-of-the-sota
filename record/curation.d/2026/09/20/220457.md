Back to the reading feed after a rejected proposal. Picked the representation
cluster and found the topic was half-empty in a way its own name makes
obvious in hindsight.

## The gap

`representation-and-encoding` holds fourteen `LIT` notes. All fourteen are
about **encoding**: subword units, RoPE, ALiBi, context extension, positional
encoding's effect on length generalisation, the inner lexicon. Not one is
about **representation** — what one is, what models converge to, how concepts
sit in the space.

That is not a subtle absence. It is the second word of the topic's own label,
and no query anybody has run would have surfaced it, because nothing in the
record complains about it. The topic looks full. It has fourteen documents
and an active line of work running through them.

Found by arriving papers, again. Third time this week.

## What went in

Three hypotheses about what representations are:

- **Platonic** — they converge, across architectures, objectives and
  modalities, toward a model of whatever generated the data.
- **Lattice** — a concept direction plus a threshold is a half-space;
  intersecting half-spaces gives a concept lattice, so refinement and
  generalisation are geometric operations.
- **Contextures** — a representation is the association between the input and
  a context variable, and the useful contexts are the ones whose association
  is neither too strong nor too weak.

## The trunk I did not fill

All three lean on the **Linear Representation Hypothesis**. The Lattice paper
is explicitly an elaboration of it. And the record has nothing — no document
for the hypothesis, none for steering vectors, none for concept directions at
all.

So I have filed two elaborations of an absent foundation, which is backwards,
and I said so in both theory documents rather than letting it pass. It is
named rather than closed because closing it is a unit of its own: the
hypothesis has a real literature, a causal-inner-product formulation, and
live disputes, and a single note asserting "concepts are directions" would be
exactly the thin filing this record exists to prevent.

## Keeping two claims apart

The Platonic paper measures one thing and conjectures another, and the
temptation is to write them as one finding.

**Measured**: vision and language models agree increasingly about pairwise
distances as they scale, across architectures and objectives.
**Conjectured**: the endpoint is a representation of the world that generated
the data — with a proof holding only for discrete worlds with *bijective*
observation functions, which real observation is not.

[THEORY-tmpwwu1v](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-tmpwwu1v.md) separates them and is `Proposed` on account of the second,
even though the first would carry `Active` on its own. A document that merged
them would be claiming a conjecture is measured.

What made me trust the paper more than I expected: they probe their own weak
premise rather than around it. Bijectivity predicts a *more informative*
observation should align better, so they vary caption density on fixed images
— and denser captions do align better. That is the experiment that could have
embarrassed them.

## A skim, marked as one

[LIT-tmpguznl](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmpguznl.md) is a 313,000-character dissertation. I read the abstract, the
introduction, the stated implications, the conclusions and the sections
naming its two objectives. I did not read the theorems, the statistical
learning bounds or the semi-supervised extension.

[NOTE-tmpmg0fm](../../../../notes.d/NOTE-tmpmg0fm.md) is `Skimmed` and says all of that in its first section, and
every claim table entry is marked weak or moderate accordingly. [ADR-025](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-025.md) says
`Skimmed` is respectable and is not enough to source a practice from alone —
so [SOTA-tmpne4bq](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-tmpne4bq.md) exists at `Proposed` with `consensus: unassessed` and
carries the debt in its conditions rather than pretending it away.

The alternative was to skip the paper. Its framing — objectives differ by
context variable, and the useful contexts have a measurable sweet spot — is
the first thing in the record proposing a *criterion* for pretraining
objectives rather than a case, and the registry files every objective on its
own evidence with nothing saying what they share. Worth having at a stated
discount.

## The one I filed no practice from

[LIT-tmpnglrb](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmpnglrb.md) is elegant and I filed nothing actionable from it. It
establishes a structure on WordNet and demonstrates that structure on WordNet
— a hand-built ontology with exactly the hierarchical shape the hypothesis
predicts. Recovering a lattice from concepts somebody constructed as a
lattice is not strong evidence the lattice is in the embeddings.

The obvious practice — build a steering vector for a compound concept by meet
or join instead of searching for one — is *available* from the construction
and is never run as an intervention. Filing it would have been inventing a
recommendation the paper does not make. [THEORY-tmpbqmje](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-tmpbqmje.md) has `explains:`
empty and a `promote_when:` naming the severe test, which is cheap: cluster a
model's own embeddings and look for the structure where nobody put it.
