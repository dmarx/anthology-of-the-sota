**A scope question got answered.** RNA-FM was passed over earlier today with a
reason that ended in a question for the owner: the eighteen topics *can*
express its claim, but it is foundational, the record carries it across eighty
practices that touch pretraining, and its own trunk — protein and biological
sequence models — was absent, so filing the descendant would repeat what
`#243` was opened about. The answer was yes, file it.

**So the trunk came first, deliberately.** `LIT-tmp5vl15` (ESM-1b) is filed
unread and says so in its Standing, which is an unusual thing for this record
to do and the right thing here: without it, RNA-FM is a paper with an
`extends:` it cannot declare. The relation is not a judgement call — RNA-FM
cites it as reference 66 and copies its ResNet32 head verbatim.

**The reading changed what the unit was about.** Going in, the expected
outcome was either no practice (the foundational-claim objection stands) or a
narrow one: *keep the published task model and swap its one-hot input for
pretrained embeddings*. That narrow version looked strong, because Table 5 is
a genuine controlled experiment — somebody else's architecture, found by
somebody else's grid search, inputs replaced and nothing else touched. That is
rarer than it should be and it deserved to support something.

**Then the numbers came out.** On Human7600 — the real-human set the library
authors provide *as the generalization check* — one-hot sequence gives
`R²` 0.814 and the 640-dimensional embedding gives 0.816. A 16-dimensional
predicted secondary structure gives 0.820. And `Seq + SS + RNA-FM` gives
**0.811**, below plain sequence, with the worst MSE in the table. The clean
control does not carry the claim, and the row that contradicts it is the one
combining the two feature sets.

**The generalizable lesson is about where to look.** Every other comparison in
the paper is RNA-FM's pipeline against somebody else's pipeline, so
architecture, training data and features all move at once, and those are the
comparisons the abstract is built from. The single table that isolates the
variable is the one nobody quotes. `DP-004` again, and in its constructive
form: the query that found this was "which experiment here changes exactly one
thing", not "what does the paper claim".

**Credit where it is due, twice.** The abstract volunteers that the margin
falls from ~30% to 4–7.5% under redundancy reduction. The Discussion
volunteers that the functional gains are "more slight" than the structural
ones and offers a hypothesis. Both are the kind of sentence most papers omit,
and holding the paper to its own honest disclosures is only possible because
it made them.

**One thing found and not filed.** Nothing in the paper checks the 23.7
million-sequence pretraining corpus against the evaluation sets, and the
corpus name gives the cut-off away: `RNAcentral100` means cd-hit-est at 100%,
so exact duplicates were collapsed and nothing else. That is the same *shape*
as the practice filed from `#241` an hour ago — a reported margin sharing
something with its own evaluation — and it is a different mechanism. Counted
in the note, promoted to nothing. `DP-009`.
