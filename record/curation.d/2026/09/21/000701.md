Went looking for what the record holds on the limits of transformers and
found that every hit for "expressive" was an architecture paper using the
word as a selling point. Nothing on lower bounds. Nothing on what the
architecture cannot do at any size.

That is a structural absence rather than a missing paper. `SOTA` says what to
do, `THEORY` says why it works, `LIT` says where it came from — three schemes
all pointed at things that work. A claim of the form "no amount of tuning
buys you this" has nowhere obvious to live, and so nothing of that shape had
ever been filed.

## Two kinds of limit, and the cluster is the contrast

The papers that surfaced split cleanly into a limit that is proved and limits
that were measured, and the contrast is the unit.

**Chen et al. (`2412.02975`, 13 revisits)** prove the first unconditional
lower bound for a decoder-only transformer of more than one layer. What makes
it possible is worth recording: there was a published argument that
unconditional bounds here would require breakthrough circuit lower bounds,
and this sidesteps it rather than breaking it, by using the one structural
feature an encoder lacks. Causal masking means a position cannot see ahead
and does not retain what it forwarded. Model that as forgetful players on a
line, one epoch per attention layer, and the bottleneck becomes provable.

Three separations fall out, all exponential and all the same fact from
different angles: depth buys what width cannot, an encoder does it
exponentially smaller, and one layer plus chain of thought suffices.

**The other three papers are about limits that were measured**, and whether
the measurement was of the model.

## The dispute, and what I decided not to decide

`The Illusion of Thinking` reports that reasoning models collapse to zero
accuracy past a complexity threshold while spending *fewer* thinking tokens.
A comment argues the collapse is the evaluation: output caps, a scorer that
cannot distinguish refusal from failure, and — the sharp one — River Crossing
instances that are mathematically unsolvable yet scored as failures.

The tempting move is to file the comment as a debunking. Three things
stopped it.

**The Illusion paper is NeurIPS 2025**, camera-ready, last revised November
2025 — five months *after* the comment. It went through peer review with the
objection already public. That does not make it right and it does rule out
the reading a person would reach if they met only the comment.

**The comment is weaker than its rhetoric.** Single author, who states that
Claude Opus wrote the bulk of it and was removed as co-author under arXiv
policy. Its central empirical rebuttal — ask for a generating function and
accuracy returns — is explicitly underpowered, with full validation named as
future work. §4 is a published self-correction: an earlier version assumed
the evaluation required intermediate states, commenters showed otherwise, the
section was rewritten. And its reference list cites `ARXIV-2501.12948` for
Shojaee et al., which is DeepSeek-R1. The arXiv metadata names the right
target, so this is a bibliography error and not a confusion about what is
being commented on — I checked before writing it down, which is the kind of
thing this cluster is about.

**And they agree on the fact.** Both report the collapse happening *below*
the token limit — the Illusion paper says models are "well below their
generation length limits with ample inference budget available"; the comment
computes the same thing and concludes models "may be poorly calibrated about
their own context length capabilities". The disagreement is entirely about
what that shared observation means. Reading either paper alone, you would not
know that.

One claim in the comment does not depend on any of this: the
Missionaries–Cannibals family has no solution past five pairs with a boat of
three, and instances like that were scored as failures. That is checkable and
it lands.

So: both `Active`, `corrects` declared because that is what the document *is*,
and neither treated as having won.

## The practice came from the boring paper

`SOTA-278` is not sourced to the dispute. It is sourced to Hu and Frank,
COLM 2024, who measured the same phenomenon properly: four capacities, each
asked a high-demand and a low-demand way, 13 models across five families, and
a significant size × method interaction in three of four domains. The gap
narrows as models get larger and as one model trains longer.

The interaction is the part with teeth. If the demand were a constant offset
it would cancel in a comparison. It is not — the weaker model pays more of it
— so comparing a small and a large model on one demanding evaluation counts
the tax as capability.

Sourcing the practice to the systematic study and using the dispute as the
worked example is what lets the practice be `Active` while the dispute stays
open. The vivid case makes the check memorable; it is not what makes it true.

## What I did not file

**No chain-of-thought practice**, and finding out why was the surprise of the
session. The record holds *no* practice recommending chain of thought.
`SOTA-127`, which says to filter chain-of-thought traces out of tiny models'
training data, is the entire CoT presence in a corpus of 278 practices.

Corollary 1.4 would be a tempting way to fix that — the first provable
benefit of CoT, no complexity conjecture needed. But it is a representability
result: a one-layer decoder *with* CoT can express a composition that a
constant-depth one cannot. It says nothing about whether a trained model uses
the room, or whether emitting steps helps on a task anyone runs. Sourcing
"use chain of thought" to it would source a recommendation about inference to
an existence proof. `ADR-017`'s question — what could this claim not survive
losing — answers itself.

So the gap is named in three places and left open. It is now the largest
uncovered trunk I know of in the record.

## An observation about the feed I have been skipping silently

The top of the revisit ranking has been the same paper for several passes:
`2411.01852`, an audit of political exposure bias on Twitter/X, at 22 revisits
— nearly double the next item. Below it sit `Industry Influence in
High-Profile Social Media Research`, `Introducing AI to an Online Petition
Platform`, and `The unintended consequences of large language models as a
labor-augmenting technology in science`.

I have skipped these every pass without writing down why, which is worse than
either filing them or deciding against them. They are a social-science
cluster: studies of deployed systems and their effects on people, not claims
the thirteen topics can express. `ADR-026` scopes by the kind of claim, and
"algorithmic amplification changed political exposure" is not a
recommendation, an explanation of a technique, or a paper's standing in a
practice lineage.

That is a judgement and it should be visible rather than enacted by silence
every pass. If it is wrong, the fix is a topic with a label and a blurb and a
decision, which is what `CLAUDE.md` says to do when the vocabulary cannot say
what a document needs.
