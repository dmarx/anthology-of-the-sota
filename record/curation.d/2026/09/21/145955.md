Most of this session's readings have involved finding the caveat the paper
did not state — a footnote that halves a headline, a column nobody narrated,
an argument with no experiment. `DP-010` exists because it happened four
times.

This one is the other kind, and worth recording for contrast.

The headline is that a cheap ensemble improves generalization: CIFAR-100
WRN 28-10 goes 81.35 → 82.87. Fine. Then the paper runs three ablations, each
of which makes its own method sound less general, and reports all three:

1. **Start the procedure at initialization instead of late** and it *fails to
   reach the base model*. Not "helps less" — fails. The method's name is
   doing real work.
2. **Replicate every weight instead of a few** and you land between the base
   model and the low-dimensional version. Their cheap trick beats their
   expensive one.
3. **Use the expressive variant** — hypernetwork weight embeddings, the
   general case that subsumes the others — and on CIFAR-100 under SWA you get
   82.01 against a base of 82.46. Worse than doing nothing.

Each of those is a result the paper could have omitted. The first two are
ablations nobody would have demanded. The third is a negative result about
the authors' own most sophisticated contribution.

The practice filed here is better for all three, and specifically it can say
*what to do* rather than *what was done*: replicate BatchNorm's scale and
shift, do it late, and do not reach for the general version.

## The comparison that decides adoption is the one that varies

Stochastic weight averaging is the simple incumbent. Against it:

- CIFAR-10: 96.48 → 96.81. CIFAR-100: 82.46 → 83.06.
- enwik8 LSTM: 1.626 → 1.615 BPC.

The CIFAR gaps are 3 to 7 times the reported standard deviations. The LSTM gap
is 0.011 where the same comparison without SWA gave 0.062 — so on that problem
SWA absorbed most of what late-phase weights were providing.

The paper states this accurately and in passing: SWA "substantially improves
all scores, with smaller gains on the models with multiplicative weights". It
is easy to read past, and it is the sentence a practitioner needs, so the
practice says "try SWA first" in as many words.

## And a gap the reading made visible rather than fixed

The anthology has no weight-space ensembling at all. Not Polyak averaging, not
SWA, not snapshot ensembles, not model soups. Several of them are baselines in
this very paper — SWA, BatchEnsemble, deep ensembles — so they now appear in
the record as names attached to numbers, read by nobody.

That is the second family this session to enter that way; the first was
backprop-free local learning, where forward-forward, difference target
propagation and forward gradients arrived as NoProp's baselines. Both are
worklist items now rather than absences nobody had noticed, which is the
cheapest form of progress available and is not the same as coverage.
