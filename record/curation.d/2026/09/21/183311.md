**`LIT-500` — ViT-VQGAN, the paper LlamaGen credits for the codebook
design — is filed, and `SOTA-306` needed correcting in three places as a
result.**

I argued for doing this on the grounds that it would turn "two independent
measurements of somebody else's recommendation" into a practice with its
source. It did, and it also showed that the practice I wrote two hours ago
stated the instruction wrongly, mischaracterized the evidence for one of its
components, and named the wrong thing as the mechanism. All three came from
reading the origin rather than the papers citing it.

**One: the instruction.** v1 said "make the code vectors low-dimensional",
which is how LlamaGen and GaussianToken both describe it. ViT-VQGAN's actual
recommendation is to **factorize** — project the encoder output down to a
low-dimensional lookup space, find the nearest entry *there*, then project
that entry back up to a wide embedding. Its own phrase is "decoupling code
lookup and code embedding".

That is a better idea than the one I wrote down, and the reason is
statable: nearest-neighbour search wants low dimension because distances
concentrate in high ones and a single entry wins nearly every query; the
embedding the decoder consumes wants high dimension because that is where the
capacity lives. Cramming both into one space is what kills codebooks. The
descendants' framing gets the effect and conceals the why.

**Two: `ℓ₂`-normalization.** v1 carried it as something "reported as part of
the same design and not separately ablated in either paper here". Accurate
about the two papers the record held. The origin ablates it, and it is the
**largest single effect in the table** — dropping it at lookup dimension 32
takes usage to 2% and FID to 5.44, worse than leaving the lookup at 256. I had
it filed as a footnote to the main instruction when it is at least as large as
the main instruction.

**Three: what utilization buys.** v1 said utilization is "the mechanism". The
origin's dimension-4 row: **96% codebook usage — the best number in the
table — and 3.68 FID, tied for worst** among normalized rows. Every code gets
used and none of them can say enough. Utilization is a necessary condition you
can satisfy while quality collapses, which is a materially different claim
from the one I filed, and it changes what someone tuning a tokenizer should do
when they see a high number.

**None of these were visible from the two citing papers.** Both report the
effect; neither reports the mechanism, neither ablates the normalization, and
neither has a row where usage and quality come apart. This is `DP-004` in its
constructive form — a pass finds only defects its query is shaped like, and
"read the papers that cite the idea" is a query shaped like the descendants.

**Consensus went `emerging` to `converged`, and I want the reason on the
record rather than implied.** Two measurements of somebody else's
recommendation is corroboration. A recommendation plus two independent
confirmations, one of which cites nobody, is the field having settled. The
difference is whether an origin exists to have been confirmed, and this
morning the record could not tell — which is the same blindness `#243` was
about, one level up.

**One thing I found and did not file.** ViT-VQGAN says VQGAN relied on top-`k`
and top-`p` sampling heuristics *because* most of its codebook was dead, and
demonstrates the point by sampling at temperature 1.0 with neither, at a
codebook eight times larger. As a diagnostic — check how many codes are alive
before reaching for sampling tricks — that is genuinely useful.

It is also half an experiment. The paper shows a fixed codebook not needing
heuristics; it never shows heuristics failing to help a fixed codebook, or
helping a broken one more than fixing it would. "Unnecessary" and "not tried"
are not distinguished, and the comparison is one table nobody has run. Into
the reading's open questions, not into a practice.

**And `β = 0.25` has now passed through three papers in this record
untested.** VQ-VAE reported it insensitive from 0.1 to 2.0 at `K = 512`, in
2017. ViT-VQGAN uses it "in all our experiments" at `K = 8192`. LlamaGen
carries it too. `NOTE-245` asked whether that insensitivity survives a
codebook thirty-two times larger; filing the origin made the question sharper
and answered none of it. Three papers agreeing on an inherited constant is not
three papers having checked it — `DP-005` — and it is a genuinely cheap sweep
for anyone with a tokenizer already training.
