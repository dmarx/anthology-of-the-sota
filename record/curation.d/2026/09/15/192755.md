Eleven papers. This one was named in the previous pass's uncovered list and
read for that reason, and it moved two documents that were still sitting
unmerged in the same pull request.

**A practice was wrong and got corrected before anyone could act on it.**
`SOTA-tmpdcmgg` was filed an hour earlier as a stopping rule: stop ES
post-training when the target task converges, because drift keeps accumulating
and the gains do not. The reasoning was sound given what the record held.
`LIT-tmppbfp5` ran Countdown to 500 iterations, the task converged near 200,
and *averaged* prior-task accuracy fell across the whole run. Drift is linear
in steps. Therefore stop.

`LIT-tmpphacm` tracks the prior tasks **individually rather than averaged** and
the picture inverts: HellaSwag drops about 8% over the first 300 iterations and
**returns to its original level by the final iteration**. MMLU-Pro and
ARC-Challenge do the same. ProofWriter runs it upside down, improving and then
settling back. A stopping rule fitted to the average would stop at the bottom
of the dip — the single worst point on the curve.

The lesson is not about evolution strategies. **An average over a set of
measurements can invert the sign of the effect you are inferring from it**, and
a practice derived from one is only as good as the disaggregation nobody ran.
The durable half of the original claim — spend budget on population rather
than steps — survived and is now confirmed directly, which is why this was a
rewrite rather than a retirement.

It was also caught by luck. The paper was in the uncovered list because a
search for something else returned it, and it was read because the previous
pass wrote down what it had not covered. **Writing down the gap is what made
the correction possible**, which is the one process claim these entries can
actually support.

**On correcting in place rather than retiring.** The record's rule is to
retire by changing status and never by deleting, and this practice was
rewritten with its title changed rather than marked `Rejected` with a
successor. The distinction is that it had not landed: it exists only in an
open pull request, and the claim was never one the record made. Filing a
`Rejected` document for a recommendation that was never published would put
something in the attic that nobody ever took out of it. The wrong version is
in this entry and in the changelog instead, which is where a reader looking
for the reasoning will find it.

That reasoning does not extend to anything already on `main`. Once a document
has merged, the rule applies without exception.

**`THEORY-tmpt76ks` is `Active`, and it is the first promotion in this line
that was clean.** Its condition asked for the `σ²dT/N` scaling measured by an
independent group, on a transformer at a different scale, with the
population-size dependence tested directly rather than inferred. That is
Table 1 and Figure 6 of this paper: no shared authors, Qwen2.5-3B rather than
Qwen3-4B, population 30 → 128 halving the update norm — which is what an
inverse dependence of the squared norm on `N` predicts for a 4.3× increase —
and prior-task degradation falling monotonically across three population
sizes.

Worth contrasting with `THEORY-006`, which has now been approached three times
and promoted none. The difference is not that one account is better supported
in some vague sense; it is that **`THEORY-tmpt76ks`'s condition named a
quantity and an operation** — raise `N`, watch drift fall — and `THEORY-006`'s
named a measurement that nobody has reason to repeat. A condition that
specifies an experiment someone would run anyway gets met; one that specifies
an experiment only a partisan would run does not. That is a claim about how to
write `promote_when`, and it is the most transferable thing in this entry.

**One refinement that improves the account it confirms.** Even at population
256 or with an anchor penalty, ES update norms stay an order of magnitude
above GRPO's — while the prior-task KL divergence becomes comparable. So the
harm is not carried by the size of the walk: "it is the randomness of the
drift unconstrained by the target task that leads to prior task forgetting for
ES, rather than the magnitude of updates alone." Three papers in this record
had reached for update norm or update sparsity as the explanatory variable.
None of them was the right one.

**What this pass did not cover.** One new name: **Liang et al. (2026)**, cited
here for low-dimensional curvature of fine-tuning landscapes as the reason
very small populations suffice at billion scale. That is `THEORY-006`'s
subject approached from a third direction and it is not in the record. It is
the only outstanding reference from this paper.

Also still unrun: the bibliography sweep the previous entry prescribed —
after filing a paper, list its references the record lacks. It was prescribed
and then not performed on any of the eleven papers, which is its own small
demonstration that a process note in a journal entry is not a process.
