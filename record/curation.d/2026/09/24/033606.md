Unit 1 of `#326`. Gopher was declined by `#290` as a catalogue variant, under
a README heading that named a different paper. What the re-read expected to
find was a substrate defect of the shape unit E and unit H had already
established. What it found was that shape at the deepest the record has yet
measured.

## The measurement

`LIT-068` holds Chinchilla. **48 files mention Chinchilla.** Twelve practices
and theories cite `LIT-068`, among them `SOTA-096`, whose entire content is
the ratio `num_tokens ≈ 20 × num_params`.

Gopher is 280B parameters trained on 300B tokens. **1.07 tokens per
parameter.** The record recommends twenty.

So the record held: the rule, the paper that established the rule, twelve
documents depending on the rule — and not the run at whose expense the rule
was demonstrated. Chinchilla's own comparison is 70B against Gopher's 280B at
matched compute; that comparison is the reason `SOTA-096` is believed, and
one side of it was missing.

## What makes this deeper than unit E or unit H

Both of those were one document away from the gap. `LIT-611` was the
definition FID's critics assumed; `LIT-601` was the loser of a comparison
`LIT-216` named. Here the chain is longer:

    Gopher (absent) -> Chinchilla ([LIT-068](../../record/literature.d/LIT-068.md)) -> [SOTA-096](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-096.md) -> 11 more documents

**A dependency can be load-bearing at a distance, and distance is exactly
what a mention count cannot see.** Counting "Gopher" returns 3. Counting
"Chinchilla" returns 48. The number that matters — how much of the record
rests on a comparison whose other side is unfiled — is not a count of
anything, and no query in this record's history would have returned it.

`#326`'s corrected question gets close: *what does this paper say about the
thing the record already has?* It found Gopher because Chinchilla is one hop
away. It would not have found a paper three hops away, and that limit is
worth writing down rather than discovering later.

## A second thing, which is the reason to read rather than file

`LIT-068` is a **migration-era note**. Its whole body is six bullet points —
"Compute-optimal scaling laws. Data/model size relationships. Training
efficiency analysis. Resource allocation strategies." Twelve documents depend
on it and it contains no reading.

The record has been treating that note as though it carried Chinchilla's
argument. It does not; it carries Chinchilla's *topic*. The practices built on
it are better than it is — `SOTA-096`'s body has the 400-run fit and the
`LIT-028` contrast that the note lacks — which means the substance migrated
into the practices and the note stayed a stub nobody re-read.

**A thin note under a thick cluster is invisible to every check the record
has.** It has a `source`, its identifier resolves, its tags are legal, nothing
cites it improperly. The lint has no opinion about whether a note was read.
That is the class this entry exists to name, and Gopher is how it surfaced:
going to check what the record knew about the Gopher–Chinchilla comparison
turned up that it knew almost nothing about Chinchilla either.

## Recorded and not filed

The conclusion offers an account of why scale helps knowledge and not
reasoning — *"it is hard to compress mathematics and easier to learn many
associative facts about the world"* — and then names its own alternative, that
reasoning may emerge past this scale. It sits beside `THEORY-050`.

It is **not** filed as a theory and not filed as evidence for one. `THEORY-050`'s
`promote_when` asks for a within-corpus exchange-rate measurement, and a
conjecture in a discussion section is not that. Writing this down is the
cheap part; the discipline is in not letting a well-phrased conjecture enter
as an account because it agrees with one the record already holds.
