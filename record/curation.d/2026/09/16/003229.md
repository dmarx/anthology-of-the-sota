Closing the unbound-lineage pass I wrote that eleven of its fifteen findings
had one cause: **a claim inherits the topic of the document it was extracted
from.** It is a good sentence. It reads like a rule, and the obvious next
question was whether luria could enforce it from config, without an upstream
feature.

It cannot, and it should not, and the most useful part of finding that out was
discovering the sentence is mostly wrong.

## What the measurements said

**76%.** Of 220 practices naming a source, 167 already share their source's
topic. That is not a defect signal; it is what a correctly filed practice
looks like. An Adam practice sourced to the Adam paper is `training-optimization`
at both ends, and should be.

**Four of six.** Of the practices that pass retagged, four had inherited their
source's topic — `SOTA-060`, `SOTA-092`, `SOTA-093`, `SOTA-094`. The other two
had not: `SOTA-098` and `SOTA-099` were `training-optimization` against
sources in `model-architecture` and `model-stability`, and were wrong in the
opposite direction from the rule I had just written. I generalised from the
majority of a set of six and did not look at the other two.

**Already answered — and that is where I stopped thinking.** The check is
declarable in config, and `luria/invariants.py` had measured it on this record
before the mechanism shipped: 48 of 235 edges, "every one a cross-domain
citation the record was changed to permit", concluding that "a check that
fires on a project for doing the thing it decided to do is worse than no
check". I re-measured — 73 edges, 64 practices — grouped them by topic pair,
saw the biggest bucket was the evolution-strategies line, and wrote the whole
thing off.

**A histogram is not a reading.** Told so in review, I went back and read the
73 one at a time. They are four kinds and only one is settled:

| kind | n | |
|---|--:|---|
| multi-topic source | 16 | a model report sourcing practices across three or more topics — [ADR-032](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-032.md) admitted these deliberately |
| analysis paper as evidence | 14 | the kind-not-subject fact the lineage pass already documents |
| [ADR-026](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-026.md)'s rule working | ~10 | the practice takes its kind, the paper takes the domain it was discovered in |
| everything else | ~33 | cross-layer fault lines, and candidates worth a reading |

Two from the last row: `LIT-112` — *Efficient Memory Management for Large
Language Model **Serving*** — is filed `attention-techniques` while all four
practices under it are `inference-optimization`, whose blurb names cache
layout and serving-time decisions. `LIT-059` (CheckFreq) is
`systems-optimization` while its four checkpointing practices are
`distributed-optimization`, the topic whose blurb names checkpointing. Neither
is confirmed — that needs a reading — but both are the shape of finding the
lineage pass acted on, arriving through a second channel.

So the principle stands and I had argued against it on bad evidence: **a
relation asserts its documents have something in common, and the invariant is
the record saying what.** That is as true of a practice and its evidence as of
two practices.

**And it cannot be declared anyway.** Three lines of config, and
`luria index` raises `KeyError: 'LIT-001'` from `chains.lines_of` — the chain
walker indexes only documents in the declared scheme, and `source:` crosses
`SOTA` to `LIT`. The invariant machinery is fine; the walker under it has
never been asked to cross. That is an upstream fix, not a judgement, and it is
the answer I would have reached on day one by *trying it* instead of by
reasoning about whether it was a good idea.


## What I did instead

`lint.fail_on` had been `[]` since the record was built, so every check the
lint runs is advice. One of them — `source-mismatch` — answers the question
*is this document about the paper it names*, which is the question [DP-001](../design-principles.md#dp-1)
rests on, and it is at zero today. It is now a failure.

The reason it is the right one is not general principle. It is that
**`source-mismatch` is the check that would have failed [#138](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/138)**, whose three

Verified rather than assumed, on this branch: retitling `LIT-045` to another
paper's title produces *"`arxiv: 2104.09864` resolves to 'RoFormer…', not 'A
Mean Field View…'"* and exit 1. A freshly minted note carrying an identifier
that does not match its title fails the same way, with the identifier resolved
live and pinned in the same run.

## What I proposed and had rejected

The first version of this went further: `source-unchecked` in `fail_on` too,
so a green run would mean every reference was *verified* rather than merely
*remembered*. I wrote the cost into the decision honestly — "a network outage
can now fail a contribution that adds a new paper" — and argued it was narrow
and the remedy was in the author's hands.

Rejected, and the reason is better than my argument. The failure
`source-unchecked` adds is an upstream outage. A contributor who did nothing
wrong cannot act on it, and the only move available to them is to learn that
the dial lies — which takes down the class that does work along with it.

The general form is worth keeping. **A check earns enforcement by failing only
on things that are in the diff.** `source-mismatch` reads the committed
lockfile and fails on a disagreement the author introduced: something somebody
can fix. `source-unchecked` fails on something somebody can only wait out.

Writing the cost down honestly is not the same as pricing it correctly. I did
the first and skipped the second, and "narrow, and the remedy is in the
author's hands" was doing the work of an argument without being one.

## The one it does not cover, and where it already lives

Sixty-seven duplicate notes — the defect [#138](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/138) actually shipped — have correct
titles and correct identifiers. Two documents claiming one identifier is not
in luria's finding vocabulary at all, and no config setting produces it.

It is already filed upstream as `LU-#165`, reached from a two-document
collision rather than a sixty-seven-document one, and it goes further than the
issue I opened here: besides a `duplicate-source` finding it asks for a
`luria merge` that resolves one by rewriting references, appending the loser's
code to `formerly:`, and retiring rather than deleting it — which is exactly
the work I did by hand in [#140](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/140) and would have done by hand again. I filed a
duplicate because I searched the finding *vocabulary* for the check and never
searched the *issues* for somebody having already noticed it was missing.

## The pattern this makes four of

Four times in two days I have asserted something about this record's own
machinery and been corrected — three times by the machinery, once by a reader:

- `SOTA-107` described as a live practice while writing a decision about
  reading a report. It is in the attic, and the lint said so.
- `superseded_by` declared unable to cross schemes, in a decision whose whole
  subject was crossing schemes. It crosses schemes; the lint said so on the
  first save.
- *A claim inherits the topic of its source*, offered as a finding worth
  eleven corrections. 76% base rate; the measurement said so.
- The `source:` invariant argued down on a count. Reading the count's
  contents says otherwise, and the actual blocker — a `KeyError` in the chain
  walker — was one config edit away the whole time.

The first three share a shape: I reach for a confident sentence about the
**tooling** at exactly the moment I am concentrating on the **content**, and
the tooling is what I am least likely to check because it is not what I am
thinking about.

The fourth is different and worse. Nothing about it was inattention — I
measured, I quoted the upstream docstring, I built a table. What I did was
**stop at the number**. 73 was large, the largest bucket had an explanation I
already believed, and that was enough to close the question. Reading the 73
takes about ten minutes and reverses the conclusion.

The rule that would have caught all four is the same one: **when the claim is
about the tooling, run the tooling.** Declaring the chain takes three lines
and fails in four seconds, which is less effort than the paragraph I wrote
arguing it was a bad idea.
