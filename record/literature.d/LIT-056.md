# LIT-056: Deep Gradient Compression: Reducing the Communication Bandwidth for Distributed Training

Lin et al. (2017) — [ARXIV-1712.01887](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01887)

## Key takeaways

- The measurement first: 99.9% of the gradient exchange in distributed SGD
  is redundant. Communication bandwidth is the scaling limit, and almost all
  of what is being sent need not be.
- Sparsifying naively costs accuracy, so the contribution is the four
  corrections that stop it doing so — momentum correction, local gradient
  clipping, momentum factor masking, and warm-up training. The compression is
  the easy half; making it lossless in the sense that matters is the paper.
- Compression ratios of 270× to 600× without accuracy loss, across image
  classification, speech recognition and language modelling: ResNet-50's
  gradient from 97MB to 0.35MB, DeepSpeech's from 488MB to 0.74MB.
- The consequence is economic rather than algorithmic — large-scale
  distributed training on inexpensive 1Gbps Ethernet instead of specialized
  high-bandwidth interconnect, and distributed training on mobile.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-075](../practices.d/SOTA-075.md), use gradient compression when the network is the
constraint. The practice's condition matters and comes from this paper: the
technique buys nothing on a fast interconnect, and the corrections above are
not optional if accuracy is to hold.

Filed next to [LIT-051](LIT-051.md), which takes the other route to the same bottleneck —
use the cluster's spare CPU and bandwidth better rather than send less — and
