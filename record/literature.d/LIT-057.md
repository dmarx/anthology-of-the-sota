# LIT-057: What Makes for Good Views for Contrastive Learning?

Tian et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2005.10243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10243)

## Key takeaways

- Proposes the **InfoMin** criterion for choosing contrastive views: *reduce the
  mutual information between views while keeping task-relevant information
  intact.* Too much shared information makes the task trivially solvable, too
  little destroys the signal — a **sweet spot**, not a direction
- Turns augmentation design from a bag of tricks into a constrained optimisation
  with a stated objective, and derives an augmentation set from it rather than
  collecting one
- **InfoMin Aug** with PIRL's JigSaw reaches **73.0% ImageNet linear readout**
  with ResNet-50, nearly **4% over SimCLR**; transfers to PASCAL VOC detection
  and COCO instance segmentation consistently beat **supervised** ImageNet
  pretraining
- **The unresolved tension:** the criterion requires knowing which information is
  task-relevant, in a setting defined by not having labels

The reason to have read it is the **pairing with `LIT-092`**, also `Rejected`,
also in this batch, and a direct methodological objection to this one: it argues
that reasoning about augmentations and the contrastive objective cannot by itself
predict downstream performance, because the function class and optimiser matter
and are absent from such accounts. InfoMin is exactly an
augmentation-and-objective account. Recording that they disagree is worth more
than either alone.

"Information bottleneck perspective" was also loose: InfoMin concerns mutual
information *between views*, which is not the information-bottleneck objective.
