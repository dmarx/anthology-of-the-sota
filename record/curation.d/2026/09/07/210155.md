[#58](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/58) is done: thirty-two practices examined, seventeen changed, fifteen
correct as filed.

## The shape of what was wrong

Not sloppiness, and not one mistake repeated. Three distinct causes, in
roughly equal measure.

**The schema moved and the documents did not.** Most of it. Every practice
corrected here was filed when `source:` took exactly one code, and each named
the single most defensible one. `SOTA-130`'s body still said Olmo 3 "remains
**the** source", singular, months after [ADR-010](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-010.md) made the field a list.

**The prose already knew.** In nearly every case the missing source was
argued for, at length, in the body of the document that failed to name it.
`SOTA-140`'s Source section named Hägele's controlled comparison from the day
it was filed; the frontmatter named only MiniCPM. `SOTA-138`'s Conditions
counted NSA's from-scratch result as evidence in the same sentence that the
field omitted it. `SOTA-145` enumerated four works and put three in the field.
The pass was mostly transcription, not discovery.

**Adoption was doing a job nothing had assigned it.** `SOTA-132` carried four
production lines in `source:` while reading as unassessed on the consensus
axis — the evidence for "the field does this" filed under "this is why it is
true". That one needed a decision before it could be fixed, which is why
[ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md) came in the middle of the pass rather than before it.

## What the decision was worth, measured

The first thirteen practices took two batches under a rule invented for the
occasion. The last nineteen took two more under [ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md), and the second half
went roughly twice as fast per practice — not because the cases were easier,
but because "did this work run the comparison, or did it ship the design?" is
answerable by reading one paragraph, while "does this feel like support?" is
answerable only by argument.

The decision also made the only *removals* in the pass possible. Every batch
before it could add a source; none could take one out, because nothing said
what did not belong.

## Two the record got right before the rule existed

Worth naming, because they are the argument that the rule was discovered
rather than imposed. `SOTA-151` says of `LIT-210`: it "explains why the
problem exists; it does not evidence that rescaling is the answer, and the
sources of a practice are what its recommendation rests on." `SOTA-152` says
Olmo 3 and DeepSeek-V4 "both cite this work, which is adoption rather than
replication — neither reports what it did for them." Both were written before
[ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md) and both state its two halves exactly.

## What is left

The `consensus` values. `SOTA-132` gained one in this pass because the
evidence moved out of `source:` and needed somewhere to go, but the other
sixteen changed practices had their support lists altered without their
consensus readings being re-derived — and several were set while the list
understated the support. That is a smaller pass than this one and it wants
the group count `LU-#209` asks for, since every consensus value in this record
is a hand-count of laboratories that nothing can recompute.
