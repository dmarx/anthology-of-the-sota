# LIT-120: Falcon-H1: A Family of Hybrid-Head Language Models Redefining Efficiency and Performance

Zuo et al. (2025) — [ARXIV-2507.22448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448)

## Key takeaways

- The mixer block runs Transformer attention heads and Mamba-2 state-space
  heads in parallel rather than interleaving layers of each, so the split
  between the two is a per-block channel allocation that can be tuned, not a
  layer pattern.
- Released at 0.5B, 1.5B, 1.5B-deep, 3B, 7B and 34B in base and
  instruction-tuned form; the 1.5B-deep variant is the first public data
  point for the claim that depth, at fixed parameters, buys performance
  comparable to models several times larger.
- A customised Maximal Update Parametrization ([LIT-148](LIT-148.md), [SOTA-143](../practices.d/SOTA-143.md)): the multipliers for learning
  rate, weight decay and the forward pass are tuned once and transferred,
  which is what lets [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) reuse 35 tuned values at 90M.
- Multi-epoch training on high-quality data, made safe by measuring how long
  the model remembers individual training tokens. The data-rollback
  experiment on FalconMamba-7B — replay tokens seen earlier and read the
  loss gap against fresh ones — shows the gap decaying with how long ago the
  tokens were seen. That figure is the whole empirical basis of the
<!-- inactive-ok: SOTA-124 — the Proposed practice this paper is the evidence for -->
  memorization-window argument in [LIT-119](LIT-119.md) and [SOTA-124](../practices.d/SOTA-124.md).
- The tokenizer splits digits and punctuation and injects common LaTeX
  tokens; the authors attribute part of the math and code results to it.

## Standing in the anthology

transfer and the repetition argument all originate here. Its own claims
about hybrid efficiency at 7B–34B are not yet drawn into a practice — the
record's hybrid-attention practice ([SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md)) is sourced to the interleaved
