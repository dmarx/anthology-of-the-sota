# LIT-147: Straight to Zero: Why Linearly Decaying the Learning Rate to Zero Works Best for LLMs

Bergsma et al., Cerebras (2025) — [ARXIV-2502.15938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15938)

## Key takeaways

- Cosine decay to 10% of the peak — the 10× decay — is the default the
  paper argues against. With the peak learning rate tuned, a linear decay
  to zero consistently outperforms the alternatives at compute-optimal
  dataset sizes, across model sizes, batch sizes, datasets and vocabularies.
- The advantage grows with the token horizon: the further past
  compute-optimal a model is trained, the more decaying to zero helps.
- The explanation reinterprets AdamW as an exponential moving average of
  weight updates. Early training needs to move away from the
  initialisation; late training needs to average over more updates to
  cancel gradient noise. Linear decay to zero balances the two demands, and
  a floor at 10% leaves noise unaveraged.
