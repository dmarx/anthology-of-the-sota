# LIT-052: Scale Efficiently: Insights from Pre-training and Fine-tuning Transformers

Tay et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2109.10686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10686)

## Key takeaways

- Kaplan et al. studied upstream pretraining loss; whether those findings
  transfer to downstream tasks under the pretrain-finetune paradigm was open,
  and this paper is the study of that question
- **Model shape matters for downstream fine-tuning**, not only model size —
  two models with the same parameter count and different depth/width can differ
  substantially after fine-tuning
- Scaling protocols operate differently in different compute regions, so a
  strategy validated at small scale need not transfer upward
- The widely adopted T5-Base and T5-Large configurations are Pareto-inefficient
- **The DeepNarrow strategy**: increase depth preferentially, before any other
  uniform scaling. Small 16L matches T5-Base on downstream quality at 134M
  against 223M parameters, 63.1% of the FLOPs and 40% faster; Base 36L beats
  T5-Large at 16% parameter saving; XL 32L is a third of XXL's size at ~44% of
  its FLOPs and about 3× faster
- The strategy's limit is parallelism, not quality: depth is serial and cannot
  be split across devices the way width can, so it holds "within a certain
  hardware limit"
- Checked outside language: the same DeepNarrow comparison holds for Vision
  Transformers on few-shot image recognition
- Over 100 pretrained T5 configurations released
