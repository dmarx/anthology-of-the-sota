# LIT-047: ReZero is All You Need: Fast Convergence at Large Depth

Bachlechner et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2003.04887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04887)

## Key takeaways

- The whole intervention is one parameter per layer: multiply the residual
  branch by a scalar initialized to zero, so at initialization every block is
  exactly the identity and signal propagates through an arbitrarily deep
  stack without attenuation or amplification.
- That satisfies initial dynamical isometry — the condition Pennington et
  al. identified as what makes deep training work — and the paper's claim is
  that this simplest possible change outperforms the more complex
  architectural and initialization schemes built to achieve the same thing.
- The gate is *learned*, so the network decides how much of each branch to
  admit as training proceeds. Zero initialization is a starting point, not a
  constraint.
- Depths reached: thousands of fully connected layers on CIFAR-10, and
  120-layer Transformers trained without the warmup and normalization
  scaffolding that depth usually requires. At 12 layers it converges 56%
  faster on enwiki8.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-051](../practices.d/SOTA-051.md) (initialize the final layer of a residual branch near
zero) and [SOTA-053](../practices.d/SOTA-053.md) (gated architectures want their gate started closed).
Both arrived under a fabricated citation; the advice is real and this is
where it comes from.

It sits at one end of a line the record carries in full. ReZero starts the
branch at zero and learns the scale; DeepNet ([LIT-084](LIT-084.md)) instead derives a
fixed depth-dependent constant for it ([SOTA-052](../practices.d/SOTA-052.md)); Learnable Multipliers
([LIT-121](LIT-121.md)) makes the scale of every matrix learned rather than set by the

LR/WD equilibrium ([SOTA-122](../practices.d/SOTA-122.md)). The question they answer in common is who
picks a layer's scale — the initializer, the schedule, or the data.
