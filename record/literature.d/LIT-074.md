# LIT-074: FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness

Dao et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2205.14135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)

## Key takeaways

- **The bottleneck is HBM traffic, not FLOPs.** Approximate-attention methods
  reduced compute complexity and often failed to produce a wall-clock
  speedup, because the cost model they optimised was the wrong one
- **Tiling to on-chip SRAM**, with an online softmax carrying a running max
  and normaliser, computes **exact** attention without materialising the
  `N × N` matrix
- **Theorem 1**: `O(N²d)` FLOPs — unchanged — and **`O(N)` additional memory**
- **Theorem 2**: standard attention needs `Θ(Nd + N²)` HBM accesses;
  FlashAttention needs `Θ(N²d²M⁻¹)`, for `d ≤ M ≤ Nd`. With `d ∈ [64,128]`
  and `M ≈ 100KB`, `d² ≪ M`
- **Proposition 3**: no algorithm computes exact attention in `o(N²d²M⁻¹)`
  HBM accesses across all `M ∈ [d, Nd]`. It is IO-optimal, not merely faster
- **Block sizes are derived, not tuned**: `B_c = ⌈M/4d⌉`,
  `B_r = min(⌈M/4d⌉, d)`
- **Backward recomputation** rather than storing the attention matrix —
  trading arithmetic for memory traffic, which is the right way round given
  Theorem 2
- 15% over the MLPerf 1.1 BERT-large record, **3× on GPT-2**, 2.4× on LRA;
  and the first Transformers above chance on Path-X (16K) and Path-256 (64K)
