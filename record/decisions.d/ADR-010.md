# ADR-010: A practice names every source it rests on, not one

## Context

`SOTA` required exactly one `source:`, naming one `LIT` note. That was the
shape [ADR-001](ADR-001.md) established and [DP-001](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-1) is built on: a recommendation
with no paper behind it fails the build.

The rule works. The cardinality does not. Practices in this record routinely
rest on several papers, and the schema could only ever record one of them.
[SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md) is the clearest case — the 3:1 interleave of linear and
global attention. Its body cites seven notes: the Kimi Linear paper that ran
the controlled comparison, the two Qwen releases that shipped the same ratio
independently, the Gated DeltaNet paper the linear layer comes from, Kimi K3
and Falcon-H1 for contrast. Its `source:` named one.

So the field was decorative for exactly the practices with the most evidence
behind them, and the note bodies carried the real account. That is
[DP-002](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-2)'s failure — a field that has stopped carrying information —
with a twist that makes it worse than blankness: the field is not empty, it
is *confidently wrong*. A reader querying "what supports [SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md)" gets one
paper and no signal that six more exist.

The cost came due this week. 39 practices — a little over a quarter of the
registry — were sourced to synthetic notes that named no real paper. Every
one of them looked perfectly well-formed, because a well-formed practice is
one with exactly one source, and they each had exactly one. The schema could
not represent "this rests on a single unreplicated claim" as distinct from
"this rests on four independent groups", so nothing could notice that a
quarter of the registry was in the first category.

There is also a rule this project already wrote down and could not enforce.
<!-- inactive-ok: SOTA-122 — a Proposed practice whose promotion rule this decision makes countable -->
[LIT-121](../literature.d/LIT-121.md) says [SOTA-122](../practices.d/SOTA-122.md) "stays *Proposed* until a result from outside the
authors' group is in the record", and [LIT-134](../literature.d/LIT-134.md) said the same of [SOTA-133](../practices.d/SOTA-133.md) —
which has since been promoted on exactly that basis, making the point.
That is a countable condition — how many independent sources does this
practice have? — expressed in prose because the schema offers nowhere to
count.

## Decision

`source:` takes a **list** of `LIT` codes. One entry is still the common
case and still the minimum; the lint's requirement is unchanged in force —
at least one — and changed only in shape.

    source:
    - [LIT-133](../literature.d/LIT-133.md)
    - [LIT-136](../literature.d/LIT-136.md)
    - [LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md)

The order is meaningful: **the first entry is the primary source**, the work
the practice is drawn from, and the rest are what corroborates or extends it.
Generated views cite the primary and count the rest, so the index stays
readable while the evidence stays complete.

What this buys, in order of how soon it pays:

- **A practice's support is countable.** "How many independent sources does
  this rest on?" becomes a query rather than a reading. The promotion rule
  above becomes checkable: a `Proposed` practice with sources from two
  unrelated groups is a candidate for `Active`, and one that has sat at a
  single source for a year is a candidate for the opposite.
- **A single-source practice is visible as such.** Not a finding — plenty of
  good practice rests on one excellent paper — but a fact a curator can sort
  on. Had it been visible, 39 practices sharing eleven fabricated sources
  would have looked strange much earlier.
- **The prose stops being the only true account.** The seven notes in
  [SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md)'s body were already the real answer; now the field agrees with
  them.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep one `source:` and rely on the body.** This is the status quo, and its
defect is not hypothetical: it is the exact configuration in which a quarter
of the registry rested on nothing while every document passed the lint. A
convention that lives only in prose cannot be counted, and the whole argument
of [DP-001](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-1) is that evidence which cannot be counted decays.

**One `source:` plus a separate `corroborated_by:`.** Attractive — it keeps
the primary unambiguous and reads well. Rejected because it asks the filer to
grade evidence at filing time, and the grade is the part they are least
equipped to make: whether a second paper *corroborates* or merely *touches*
the practice is a judgement that often becomes clear only later, when a third
arrives. A list with a meaningful first entry captures the one distinction
that is reliably knowable — which paper this came from — and leaves the rest
flat. The graded version can be layered on later if flatness proves too
coarse; going the other way would mean re-litigating every entry.

**A weight or confidence per source.** Rejected as the same problem with more
decimal places. This record's instrument for confidence is `status:`, and
adding a second one invites them to disagree.

## Consequences

Every existing practice migrates mechanically: a scalar becomes a
single-element list, which changes no meaning and no rendering. The work that
follows is curation rather than migration — going back through practices
whose bodies already name several notes and lifting those into the field.
[SOTA-132](../practices.d/SOTA-132.md) is the first.

This does not weaken [ADR-001](ADR-001.md). A practice with an empty `source:` still fails
the build; the field group's `at-least-one` semantics apply to the list's
length rather than to which of several fields is present.
