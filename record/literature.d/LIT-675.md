# LIT-675: Model soups: averaging weights of multiple fine-tuned models improves accuracy without increasing inference time

<!-- inactive-ok-file: SOTA-217 — Proposed, and cited as the practice this paper's shared-initialization case sits beside. Its being
     open is not what is asserted; where its scope ends is. -->

Wortsman et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2203.05482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)

## Key takeaways

**The observation.** The standard recipe fine-tunes `k` models over a
hyperparameter sweep, keeps the one with the best held-out accuracy, and
discards the rest. Because they were all fine-tuned from the same pretrained
weights, they "often appear to lie in a single low error basin" — so the
discarded models can be averaged into the kept one instead.

**Two recipes, and only one of them is safe.**

- **Uniform soup**: average all `k`. One bad ingredient — a model fine-tuned
  with a learning rate that put it in a different basin — drags the average
  below the best individual model. The paper reports this failure directly.
- **Greedy soup**: sort the models in decreasing held-out accuracy, then add
  each in turn and keep it only if held-out accuracy improves. This is the
  paper's central method, and the reason is a guarantee rather than a
  measurement: it "can be no worse than the best individual model on the
  held-out validation set".

A third *learned* soup optimizes the interpolation weights by gradient descent
and needs every model resident in memory at once, which is what rules it out in
practice.

**Cost.** Every soup is `O(1)` at inference against an ensemble's `O(k)`. The
soup is free *given a sweep you were going to run anyway* — the 32 models per
dataset in the NLP experiments are not free if you were not already sweeping.

**What it buys, in the setting it was built for.** ViT-G pretrained on JFT-3B
and fine-tuned on ImageNet, top-1 and average over distribution shifts:

| | ImageNet | shifts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ViT-G, reevaluated | 90.47 | 82.06 |
| best model in the sweep | 90.78 | 84.68 |
| **greedy soup** | **90.94** | **85.02** |

Over the best sweep member: **+0.16** on ImageNet and **+0.34** under shift. On
CLIP and ALIGN the greedy soup improves over the best sweep member by **+0.7**
and **+0.5 pp**.

**The state-of-the-art claim is the least durable part, and the FLOPs are the
most.** 90.94 surpassed CoAtNet-7's 90.88 — by 0.06 pp — "while requiring 25%
fewer FLOPs at inference time". Read the 25% and not the 0.06.

**The NLP extension is weaker than the abstract makes it sound.** The abstract
says the approach "extends to multiple image classification and natural language
processing tasks". §3.3.3 calls the NLP work "preliminary experiments" and "a
promising initial step", and the BERT row on four GLUE tasks, from a 32-model
random sweep each, reads:

| | MRPC | RTE | CoLA | SST-2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| best individual model | 88.3 | 61.0 | 59.1 | 92.5 |
| greedy soup | 88.3 **(+0.0)** | 61.7 (+0.7) | 59.1 **(+0.0)** | 93.0 (+0.5) |

Two of four gain nothing at all, and the authors say so: "the improvements are
not as pronounced as in image classification". The family now has a non-vision
data point; it does not have a non-vision result.

**And an account, offered analytically and checked.** The paper relates how
closely weight-averaging tracks logit-ensembling to the **flatness of the loss**
and the **confidence of the predictions**, then validates the relation
empirically. That is a claim about when the two operations coincide rather than
about why either works, and the record's flatness cluster — [SOTA-012](../practices.d/SOTA-012.md) — is where
the surrounding argument is kept and hedged.

## Standing in the anthology

Filed because [SOTA-407](../practices.d/SOTA-407.md)'s consensus note named it as unheld, in a sentence
written earlier today: the record could not name an adopter of weight-space
ensembling "outside the authors' own line of work — model soups, which this
record does not yet hold". That is the fourth time in a week that a unit's own
prose has set the next unit's subject.

**It does not do what the note wanted it to do.** Mitchell Wortsman is first
author of both this and [LIT-674](LIT-674.md). `DP-005` says adoption is not evidence,
and a research group extending its own method is the clearest case of that:
this is a second paper, not a second opinion. `SOTA-407` stays `emerging`
and its note now says why rather than leaving the dependency open.

What it does supply: [SOTA-409](../practices.d/SOTA-409.md), the recommendation to soup a sweep rather
than discard it, and a third case for [SOTA-217](../practices.d/SOTA-217.md)'s shared-trajectory scope — with
the sharpest statement of *why* the scope matters, since the greedy recipe
exists precisely to "avoid adding in models which may lie in a different basin
of the error landscape".

**Where it stops.** Image classification is where the numbers are; NLP is
preliminary by the authors' own label. Every ingredient must be fine-tuned from
the same pretrained initialization — the single-basin assumption is what the
whole method rests on, and the greedy recipe is an admission that the assumption
fails often enough to need a guard.
