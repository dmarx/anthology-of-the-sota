Closing the unbound-lineage pass I wrote that eleven of its fifteen findings
had one cause: **a claim inherits the topic of the document it was extracted
from.** It is a good sentence. It reads like a rule, and the obvious next
question was whether luria could enforce it from config, without an upstream
feature.

It cannot, and it should not, and the most useful part of finding that out was
discovering the sentence is mostly wrong.

## What the measurements said

**76%.** Of 220 practices naming a source, 167 already share their source's
topic. That is not a defect signal; it is what a correctly filed practice
looks like. An Adam practice sourced to the Adam paper is `training-optimization`
at both ends, and should be.

**Four of six.** Of the practices that pass retagged, four had inherited their
source's topic — `SOTA-060`, `SOTA-092`, `SOTA-093`, `SOTA-094`. The other two
had not: `SOTA-098` and `SOTA-099` were `training-optimization` against
sources in `model-architecture` and `model-stability`, and were wrong in the
opposite direction from the rule I had just written. I generalised from the
majority of a set of six and did not look at the other two.

**Already answered.** The check is declarable in config — `source:` is a
declared reference, `chains` take an `invariant` — and `luria/invariants.py`
had measured it on this record before the mechanism shipped:

> Measured on the record this was built for, running the check over `source:`
> … makes findings of 48 of 235 edges, and every one of them is a cross-domain
> citation the record was changed to permit. A check that fires on a project
> for doing the thing it decided to do is worse than no check.

**Still true, for a new reason.** That measurement was taken when the two
schemes had different vocabularies; [ADR-026](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-026.md) removed the difference, so the
mechanical cause of those 48 is gone and the check should have improved.
Re-measured: **73 edges, 64 practices.** Worse. The largest crossing is the
evolution-strategies line drawing practices from papers filed under
`analysis-and-evaluation`, which is the structural fact the same pass had
already documented — I found the problem twice, in two shapes, without
noticing they were the same problem until the numbers came back.

## What I did instead

`lint.fail_on` had been `[]` since the record was built, so every check the
lint runs is advice. Two of them — `source-mismatch` and `source-unchecked` —
answer the question *is this document about the paper it names*, which is the
question [DP-001](../design-principles.md#dp-1) rests on, and both are at zero today. They are now failures.

The reason they are the right two is not general principle. It is that
**`source-mismatch` is the check that would have failed [#138](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/138)**, whose three
misbound identifiers were title-versus-identifier disagreements. I found them
with a script written by hand for that contribution while the lint printed a
clean run three feet away.

Verified rather than assumed, on this branch: retitling `LIT-045` to another
paper's title produces *"`arxiv: 2104.09864` resolves to 'RoFormer…', not 'A
Mean Field View…'"* and exit 1. A freshly minted note carrying an identifier
that does not match its title fails the same way, with the identifier resolved
live and pinned in the same run.

## The one it does not cover

Sixty-seven duplicate notes — the defect [#138](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/138) actually shipped — have correct
titles and correct identifiers. Two documents claiming one identifier is not
in luria's finding vocabulary at all, and no config setting produces it. That
is upstream, and is filed as an issue rather than approximated here.

## The pattern this makes three of

Three times in two days I have asserted something about this record's own
machinery and been corrected by the machinery:

- `SOTA-107` described as a live practice while writing a decision about
  reading a report. It is in the attic, and the lint said so.
- `superseded_by` declared unable to cross schemes, in a decision whose whole
  subject was crossing schemes. It crosses schemes; the lint said so on the
  first save.
- *A claim inherits the topic of its source* offered as a finding worth
  eleven corrections. 76% base rate; the measurement said so.

The common shape is not carelessness about the subject matter — the
positional-encoding retags and the misbound identifiers were found by looking
carefully. It is that I reach for a confident sentence about the **tooling**
at exactly the moment I am concentrating on the **content**, and the tooling
is the thing I am least likely to check because it is not what I am thinking
about. All three were a single command away.
