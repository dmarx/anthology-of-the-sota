Back to [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137). Its worklist is tiered by how many of **twelve evolution-strategies
papers** cite each candidate, and the issue is explicit that this is a proxy
for "the record's own practices are argued on this" rather than a quality
ranking. The record now holds 343 literature notes. So the first thing to do
was re-run the count against the corpus instead of the sample.

## What the re-rank changed

| Work | ES sample | Files in the record |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5 | 4 (tier 1) | **45** |
| Stream of Search / Countdown | 2 | **22** |
| ARC | 1 | **22** |
| HellaSwag | 1 | **15** |
| MBPP | 3 (tier 1) | 10 |
| GPQA | 1 | 7 |
| HybridFlow / verl | 3 (tier 1) | **5** |
| PRM800K, MATH-Beyond, DeepSeek LLM, SmolLM, MiniPile, Craftax, NAVIX, JaxMARL | 1 each | **0** |

Two tier-one entries drop — HybridFlow to five files, MBPP to ten — and two
of the "cited once" entries turn out to be among the most-referenced things in
the corpus. Eight candidates have **zero** mentions outside the sweep that
found them, which under the issue's own filing criterion ("something in the
record has to cite it") means they are not candidates at all.

This is `DP-004` arriving on schedule: a pass finds only the defects its query
is shaped like, and a worklist inherits the shape of the query that produced
it. The sweep was right that these papers exist and wrong about which ones the
record leans on, because it was reading twelve papers' bibliographies rather
than the record.

## The finding I did not go looking for

`ARC` at 22 files looked like a straightforward tier promotion. It is not one
benchmark.

Ten documents write `ARC-Easy` or `ARC-Challenge` and mean **Clark et al.'s AI2
Reasoning Challenge** — multiple-choice grade-school science questions.
Twelve write a bare `ARC`, and at least two of those mean **Chollet's
ARC-AGI** — visual grid puzzles built so that every task is structurally
novel. They share four letters and nothing else.

That would be pedantry if it were decorative. It is not: **`SOTA-235` carries a
headline number on it.** "Up to 6× a fine-tuned baseline on ARC, 53.0%" is the
practice's central evidence, and the practice's own argument turns on the
benchmark's construction — *ARC is built to reward search over structurally
novel tasks, which is the regime this method is defined by, so a large gain
there is close to tautological.* That sentence is true of ARC-AGI and false of
AI2 ARC. Read with the other sense, the practice's most important caveat
evaporates.

Meanwhile `THEORY-007` measures across `ARC-C` and `LIT-184` names `ARC`
beside MMLU as a target FineWeb-Edu moves — both the AI2 sense, both correct,
both indistinguishable from the other usage by anything in the record.

So both papers are filed, each stating plainly which one it is and that the
other exists, and `SOTA-235`/`LIT-379` go to v2 saying **ARC-AGI**. This is
exactly the service [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137) says a benchmark note is for: *what each note needs to
earn its place is the thing the citing practice implicitly assumes.* Here the
citing practice assumed a name was unambiguous.

## Two more, for what they bound

`LIT-tmpn6zv1` (HellaSwag) records that its distractors were generated and then
**adversarially filtered against 2019's models** — so its difficulty is
defined relative to a specific generation of systems, and its discriminating
range ages differently from a sampled benchmark's. This record quotes
HellaSwag numbers across a seven-year span with nowhere to say that.

`LIT-tmpx3u6l` (MBPP) is the other half of a pair: `LIT-388` is HumanEval,
ten documents report both, and a code result quoted on one is narrower than it
looks.

## Qwen2.5, and the incumbent shape a third time

Forty-five documents name Qwen2.5. The record held `LIT-182` (Qwen3),
`LIT-135` (Qwen3.8) and `LIT-125` (Qwen2.5-Coder) — two successors and a
sibling — and not the base model they and everyone else measure against.

That is the third instance this week, after DoReMi behind the mixing laws and
DCLM behind the filtering practices. The pattern is stable enough to state:
**a thing that becomes everybody's baseline is cited constantly and filed
never, because nobody ever encounters it as news.** The successor arrives as a
result and gets a document; the incumbent arrives as a column header.

## What is left on [#137](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/137)

`HybridFlow`/verl (5 files) and `GPQA` (7) are real but thin. `Stream of
Search`/Countdown at 22 is the largest remaining and wants care, because the
Countdown *task* and the Stream-of-Search *method* are cited for different
things and I have not checked which the 22 are. The eight zero-mention
candidates should be struck from the list rather than filed — named here so
the next pass does not re-derive the count.
