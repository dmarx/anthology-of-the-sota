The JEPA cluster is read — `LIT-216` (I-JEPA), `LIT-215` (V-JEPA 2),
`LIT-218` (Veo 3 zero-shot). Two practices out of three papers, and the more
useful one is not in the topic anybody would have looked under.

## First, the process failure that produced this pass

I closed `#85` and then found `LIT-215`, `LIT-216` and `LIT-218` still
unread. They are named in item 2 of that issue's body. My closing measurement
was over `LIT-001..118`, which is the population the **title** names — "the
inherited 119 notes" — and not the one the body lists.

Reopened and worked rather than quietly re-closed, because the previous
curation entry's whole argument was that an audit becomes a tracking problem
when it stops at a list. Closing an issue on a convenient population is the
same move wearing a result.

**The generalizable bit:** when an issue has been revised, the scope is
whatever the revision says, and the title is the least reliable part of it.
Both the numbers I got were right; the population was wrong.

## The encoder gap

Twenty-one notes tagged `vision-and-graphics`, and every practice that tag
carried was **downstream of an encoder** — `SOTA-205` on scene
representation, `SOTA-236` and `SOTA-237` on geometry estimation. Nothing
said how the encoder gets trained. `#86`'s trunk detector had already
reported this in `LIT-216`'s own words; this is the pass that acted on it.

`SOTA-250` states it: predict the *representations* of masked regions
from a context block, with an EMA target-encoder, and drop hand-crafted
augmentations. Three details are load-bearing and the paper names all three —
mask the target-encoder's **output** rather than its input, keep target
blocks large enough to be semantic, keep the context block spatially
distributed.

## The finding that was in the wrong topic

`LIT-215` is a video paper. Its most transferable result is not about video:

> 252K iterations at 16 frames and low resolution, then 12K cooldown
> iterations at raised resolution — **up to 8x** less pretraining compute than
> full resolution throughout.

That is a **schedule** claim, and it only makes sense against a schedule with
a distinct decay phase. Which the record already recommends: `SOTA-140`,
warmup-stable-decay. So `SOTA-251` `extends` it, and the dependency runs
the right way — without a decay phase there is no principled place to put the
resolution increase.

`ADR-026` in its purest form. The claim takes its kind, not the domain it was
discovered in, and a reader looking for "how do I make pretraining cheaper"
will now find a video paper's answer without having to be looking at vision.

**Filed `Proposed`, not `Active`**, and the `promote_when:` says why: one
projected 8x, one lab, one axis. The obvious second axis is text sequence
length, where `SOTA-151` already treats context extension as a separate later
stage — whether that is the same rule is genuinely open.

Two things from the same recipe deliberately **not** filed. The gain
attribution (data +1.0, model +1.5, iterations +0.8, resolution +4.0
cumulative) is about the end state rather than about how to reach it cheaply.
And the simplification to fixed teacher EMA and weight decay, reported as
"minimal impact", is a null result about two hyperparameters in one recipe.

## Holding both bets

`LIT-218` gets no practice, and the reasoning is worth writing down because
the paper is genuinely interesting and still fails the entry test. One
proprietary model, no recipe, no ablation of what produced any of it. `DP-006`
asks whether the work contains an instruction; the nearest candidate — "use a
generative video model zero-shot instead of a specialist" — rests entirely on
Veo 3 being available, and `DP-005` puts that in the adoption column.

What it is for is that the record can now say **the field has two live bets**
on general visual understanding from unlabelled video. One predicts
representations and explicitly refuses to generate; the other generates and
gets representations as a side effect. `LIT-216`'s Figure 2 taxonomy draws
exactly that line — joint-embedding-predictive versus generative — two years
before the generative side started looking like it was winning.

A corpus holding only the joint-embedding line would read as though the
question were settled. It is not, and now the record shows that.

## One lab is not two groups

`LIT-215` scales `LIT-216`'s construction and shares authors with it. So it
is named in `SOTA-250`'s `consensus_note` and **not** its `source:`,
per `ADR-017` — the recommendation survives losing it; what it loses is the
knowledge that the construction holds at a billion parameters and a million
hours. `consensus: unreplicated` on both practices, for the same reason.
