# LIT-816: Deeper Insights into Graph Convolutional Networks for Semi-Supervised Learning

Li, Han, Wu, Hong Kong Polytechnic University and ETH Zurich (AAAI 2018) —
[ARXIV-1801.07606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07606)

## Key takeaways

- **The layer is smoothing** (§3): with self-loops, `D̃^{-1/2} Ã D̃^{-1/2} X`
  is Laplacian smoothing with γ = 1 and the symmetric normalized Laplacian.
  Each node's new feature is a weighted average of itself and its
  neighbours
- **Why it works** (Table 1, Cora, 20 labels per class): one-layer FCN
  53.1%, two-layer FCN 55.9%, one-layer GCN 70.8%, two-layer GCN 79.8%.
  The paper credits the gain to the smoothing, which makes features within
  a densely connected cluster alike
- **Why depth fails, in the limit** (Theorem 1): for a graph without
  bipartite components, (I − αL_rw)^m w converges to a combination of the
  component indicator vectors, and (I − αL_sym)^m w to a combination of
  D^{-1/2}·indicators. Self-loops rule out bipartite components, so
  repeated smoothing makes the nodes of a component indistinguishable up
  to degree. The theorem has no weights and no nonlinearity
- **The depth figure** (Figure 2): untrained GCNs with Glorot weights on
  Zachary's karate club (34 nodes, 78 edges, one-hot features). Two layers
  separate the two classes, and three to five mix them
- **Shallow GCNs reach too little** (Tables 3–5): with few labels, a
  two-layer GCN does not propagate label information across the graph.
  Expanding the label set by co-training with a partially absorbing random
  walk, by self-training, or by both ("Union") lifts Cora at a 1% label
  rate from 62.3 (GCN with 500 validation labels) to 69.9, with no
  validation set. At 20 labels per class (Table 6) the methods only match
  GCN

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from the graph-coverage pass** (gap list from the record's own
review, 2026-10-22). It is the paper that named over-smoothing.

**It is `compared_against` GCN** ([LIT-582](LIT-582.md)) directly: every experiment runs
Kipf and Welling's two-layer GCN, with their hyperparameters, as the model
to explain and to beat. It explains GCN's two-layer gain over an MLP as
smoothing, and finds its weak point at label rates of 0.5–3%. At 20 labels
per class, the setting of [LIT-582](LIT-582.md)'s own table, co-training and
self-training only tie it on Cora (79.6–80.5 against 80.3) and fall
0.8–2.2 points short on Pubmed.

**What the paper does not show about depth.** The over-smoothing theorem
is about repeated multiplication by a fixed matrix, and the only depth
picture is an untrained network on a 34-node graph. About its own trained
models it says that 2-layer GCNs did best and deeper ones lost accuracy
"probably due to overfitting". It reads [LIT-582](LIT-582.md)'s appendix-B depth result
through the theorem without testing that reading. Oono and Suzuki
([LIT-827](LIT-827.md)) carry the result to ReLU networks with weights, and Rusch
et al. ([LIT-811](LIT-811.md)) show that keeping features apart is not enough to
make deep GCNs accurate. The account is [THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md).
