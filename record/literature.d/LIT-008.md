# LIT-008: Attention Is All You Need

Vaswani et al. (2017) — [ARXIV-1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)

## Key takeaways

- Relating two positions takes a **constant** number of operations, against
  ConvS2S's linear and ByteNet's logarithmic growth in distance — which is
  what "makes it more difficult to learn dependencies between distant
  positions" in those models
- The cost of that is "reduced effective resolution due to averaging
  attention-weighted positions", and **multi-head attention exists to
  counteract it**
- **The `1/√d_k` scaling has a stated reason.** Unscaled, "the dot products
  grow large in magnitude, pushing the softmax function into regions where it
  has extremely small gradients". The footnote gives the arithmetic: with `q`
  and `k` components independent, mean 0, variance 1, `q·k` has **mean 0 and
  variance `d_k`**
- Dot-product over additive attention is chosen on a **practical** argument —
  "much faster and more space-efficient, since it can be implemented using
  highly optimized matrix multiplication code" — not a quality one
- The paper hedges C3 as "we **suspect**", which is weaker than the flat
  statement the field repeats

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-050](../practices.d/SOTA-050.md).

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-tmpct2nt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpct2nt.md). The practice is
**confirmed**, and gains the *reason* behind the formula it states.

The variance argument is a footnote in the original and is the whole
justification. A reader who knows only `1/√d_k` cannot tell whether it
generalises; a reader who knows that `q·k` has variance `d_k` can.
