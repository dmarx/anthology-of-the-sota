The reverse query, asked of `inference-optimization`: which papers here
support no practice and no theory?

The topic holds nine `LIT` documents and the answer was two. But running the
same query without the tag filter — across all 312 — gives the number that
actually matters:

> **111 of 312 documents, 35%, support no practice and no theory.**

That is the measure of thinness the [#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87) checklist cannot express. [#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87) lists
fifteen *subjects*; the backlog is a third of the *literature*, and the
overlap between those two descriptions is small.

## The two, and one of them was self-diagnosed

`LIT-110`, Pope et al., *Efficiently Scaling Transformer Inference*. Its
standing section opened *"Nothing in the record cites this note"* and then
laid out exactly why something should: it is the analytical backing for
`SOTA-113` (continuous batching), `SOTA-115` (prefill/decode overlap) and
`SOTA-105` (paged attention), all of which source other papers and all of
which answer bottlenecks this one located.

Somebody wrote the diagnosis into the record and nothing acted on it. That is
a fourth instance of [DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7)'s shape in two sessions, with the unusual feature
that the gap was already documented — which says the problem is not noticing,
it is that noticing produces prose and prose produces nothing. A query
produces a list.

`LIT-185`, EAGLE-3. Flagged by `#121` as unsourced and unread, and left that
way, until this query put it next to a practice that now gives it a trunk.

## The part that cost me something

`SOTA-227` — speculative decoding, filed this morning — says the technique is
latency-only and that "at a batch size large enough to saturate the arithmetic
units the trade reverses". It then says, in as many words, that neither source
measures the crossover and **the record holds no measurement of it**.

`LIT-185` measures it. EAGLE-3 improves throughput by **40% at batch size
64** in SGLang, and says outright that speculative sampling "is often thought
to reduce throughput at large batch sizes". That paper was in the record,
`Active`, when I wrote the sentence. I had even read its standing section
that morning — it is where I got the three model reports for `SOTA-227`'s
consensus note.

The claim was wrong in a specific and instructive way. "Neither source
measures X" was true and checkable. "This record holds no measurement of X"
was a claim about the whole corpus, made without querying the corpus, in a
document whose entire subject was a gap found by querying the corpus.

The practice now says to measure the crossover rather than assume its sign,
and records where the counter-measurement came from.

## What the number means for the next pass

35% is not a defect rate. Plenty of those 111 are model reports and
benchmarks, which under `ADR-032` are admissible precisely *because* they feed
`consensus` rather than `source`, and which are correctly unsourced. Filing a
practice from every one of them would be the manufacturing-entries-for-
agreement failure that `LIT-377`'s reading argues against.

What the number is good for is ranking. A method paper that supports nothing
is a candidate; a model report that supports nothing is doing its job. The
query that separates them is the one worth building next, and it is not hard:
the tags already distinguish them, and `ADR-032` already says which is which.

One more thing this pass turned up without looking for it. `LIT-039` —
*Gradient Flow in Sparse Neural Networks and How Lottery Tickets Win* — was
tagged `inference-optimization`, and its only citer is `THEORY-004`, a
lottery-ticket account. A paper about why pruning works is analysis, not
serving advice. Retagged. It was visible only because the tag-filtered query
listed the topic's members, which the unfiltered query would not have done —
so both shapes of the query earned their place, which is not what I expected
when I ran the second one to correct the first.
