**Filing the repetition cluster turned up a defect in a practice the cluster
was not about.** [SOTA-124](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-124.md) said `consensus: unreplicated`, with a note ending
"Nobody has contradicted it either" — and three paragraphs of its own body
argue in detail with [LIT-166](../../record/literature.d/LIT-166.md) and [LIT-175](../../record/literature.d/LIT-175.md), both of which contradict it.

The frontmatter was written when it was true and never re-read when the body
changed. That is the same failure the consensus re-derivation pass found in
`SOTA-141` a day earlier — a value set correctly, then outlived by the prose
below it — and it is worth noticing that the pass which was *designed* to
catch exactly this missed this one, because `SOTA-124`'s support list had not
changed and so it was not on the candidate list.

**A pass keyed to "what changed" cannot see a document whose surroundings
changed instead.** That is a third variant of the shape this journal keeps
recording: a query finds the defect it is shaped like.

## The cluster itself: a fourth trunk, of a different kind

The FIM practice is the fourth case this week of a refinement filed without
the thing it refines. [SOTA-128](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-128.md) — whether to mask the loss on the prefix and
suffix of a FIM sample — has been in the record since the migration. The
practice it is a footnote to, *train with FIM at all*, was filed today.

But the mechanism is not the one the earlier entries named. The
hyper-connections and matrix-preconditioner trunks went missing because
agreement has no arrival event. This one went missing for a different reason:
**the refinement was the part somebody in this project had measured.**
`SOTA-128` is sourced to Falcon-H1-Tiny's two 90M runs. FIM itself was
established in 2022 and simply assumed. The trunk was missing not because
nobody published it but because it was too old to feel like news when the
corpus was being built.

So: agreement has no arrival event, and neither does a four-year-old default.
Both are invisible to a filing habit that runs on arrivals.

## Three positions on one question, and none of them is wrong

The nicest thing in this cluster is that the repetition disagreement is
genuinely unresolved and now legible as such:

- **`SOTA-124`** — repetition is safe when epoch *size* exceeds the
  memorization window. One figure, one scale, authors call it early.
  Falcon-H1-Tiny repeated sources 100×.
- **The four-epoch practice** — four epochs, then the value of added compute
  decays. 400 runs, released models and datasets.
- **The augmentation practice** — repetition overfits severely, and the
  overfitting is a property of the *objective*, removable.

They are not measuring the same quantity: a whole corpus under fixed compute,
against one high-quality source inside a mixture, against the objective
itself. The third makes the first two falsifiable in the same terms, which is
why it is the most useful of the three even though it is the least
established. Filing all three is what lets a reader see that the question is
open rather than picking whichever document they happened to find.
