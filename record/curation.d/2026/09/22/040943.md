**This gap was different from the last four, and worse.** The others were
trunks the record had never reached for. Here the record was *citing the
school's results*: `LIT-476` compares its own local-volume power law against
"Local Learning Coefficient results where `d/2` is a strict upper bound not
seen in practice", and `NOTE-225` lists "the Local Learning Coefficient as the
singular-learning-theory neighbour". A reader following either could not get to
the thing being cited. Zero hits for Watanabe, RLCT, WBIC or algebraic
geometry.

**One question I did not have to reopen.** The curation entry of 21 September
had already run `ADR-050`'s test on "the physics of learning" — 28 documents
mentioning mean-field analysis, free energy, singular learning theory, random
matrix theory and phase transitions — and found 61% already primary-tagged
`analysis-and-evaluation`. The test returned "no gap", loudly, on a candidate
that passed the eyeball check. All four documents here take that primary topic
and I did not propose a tag. Worth noting because the vocabulary debate is the
expensive part of filing a new school, and a past unit had already paid it.

**The theory hits `SOTA-012` harder than I expected.** I went in thinking
singular learning theory would be adjacent to the flatness practice. It is
underneath it. `SOTA-012` is already careful — correlation not causation,
diagnostic not objective, and filter normalisation named as what stopped
earlier sharpness claims being rescaling artefacts. What the trunk adds is a
second objection the normalisation does not touch: in a singular model the
theorems contain the **exponent** of the volume law, and curvature is its
prefactor. It appears in neither `n L_n(w_0) + λ log n` nor `λ/n`. Filter
normalisation makes the measurement honest; it does not make the measured
quantity the right one.

So v3 is a condition and not a correction, and I wrote the history note to say
that explicitly, because the temptation was to write it as a refutation.
`LIT-014`'s correlation is a measurement and it holds. What moves is the
explanation's scope.

**And I nearly repeated a mistake from this morning.** I declared
`compared_against: [SOTA-012]` on the new practice. Nobody has run
filter-normalised sharpness against the local learning coefficient as
predictors of test error on the same models. `ADR-011` says that relation means
somebody ran the comparison; the argument here is theoretical. Removed, and the
practice now says in prose why the relation is absent — which is more useful
than silence, since the absence is the interesting part.

**The best thing in the cluster is a distinction, not a result.**
`LIT-544` separates two events the field calls by one name. A **Bayesian**
phase transition is a change in where the posterior concentrates as sample size
grows; it follows from the free energy formula and is a theorem. A
**dynamical** transition is a plateau-then-drop in a training curve; its
"formal status has remained elusive". And the paper does not just assert the
distinction — it computes both in one model and shows them coming apart: the
`5 → 6` transition is predicted at `n_cr ≈ 600`, observed at `600 ≤ n ≤ 700`,
and **has never been observed as a dynamical transition**.

That matters here specifically. Every grokking mechanism this record filed two
hours ago is dynamical. Everything singular learning theory proves is Bayesian.
Without a document saying so, the record would hold two literatures that use
the same words for different objects and would eventually blur them in its own
prose — which is the failure the two-collapse-accounts precedent and `ADR-031`
exist to prevent. `THEORY-074` is that document, and it is the one I would
keep if I had to drop the rest.

**A finding that reaches sideways into this morning's other unit.**
`LIT-543`'s in-context regression transformer acquires in-context learning
in LR2 and then **loses it** over LR3 and LR4, specializing to its pre-training
task distribution while the loss keeps falling. Every paper in the
mesa-optimization cluster measures a trained model at some unstated point in
that trajectory. Whether von Oswald's single-layer result, Akyürek's depth
phases and Fu et al.'s rate measurement were taken before or after the model
started un-learning in-context behaviour is a question the record can now ask
and cannot answer. It is in the note's open questions rather than asserted as a
problem, because it may well be that their models never reach LR3.

**Declined: `ARXIV-2310.03789`.** Rubin, Seroussi and Ringel map grokking to a
first-order phase transition through the adaptive-kernel approach to feature
learning. Real result, and statistical mechanics rather than singular learning
theory — two machineries, one unit, which is how a cluster stops being legible.
The tempting argument for including it was that it observes grokking as a
function of *sample size* as well as time, which would be a fourth independent
arrival at `THEORY-069`'s regime claim from a school sharing no apparatus
with the other three. That is `DP-009` evidence and it is worth having. It is
worth having as its own unit, with the adaptive-kernel frame filed properly,
rather than smuggled in as a fifth reading here.

**Process note, and it is not the one I expected to write.** This was the first
unit run with `make ready`, which the ADR an hour ago existed to produce. It
found that the ADR was wrong.

`luria repair` is **not** a superset of `luria link --fix`. I asserted it was,
from the two commands' docstrings, and removed `link --fix` from the documented
sequence on that basis. This unit declared two `extends` relations; `repair`
reported nothing to do; the lint said "2 declared relation(s) are held by one
side only"; `luria link --fix` wrote both converses. A docstring describes a
command's purpose, and only running both tells you what each one writes. v2
says so, and adds the inference itself to the alternatives-considered as the
thing that went wrong.

The timing is the useful part. The error was one hour old and it was caught by
the next unit, on a branch, by a check that was already running — rather than
by a contributor six months from now noticing that relations had quietly
stopped being two-sided. That is the argument for the ADR's own remaining
upstream asks, and it is better evidence than the ADR made for itself.

**The other named defect turned up in the same run.** This contribution's prose
cited five temp codes from the two units merged earlier today, all now
concretized on `main`. The lint listed all sixteen sites with the mapping. Every
one was inside backticks, so neither concretization nor the fixer could reach
them — the trap the `LIT-489` fragment warned about and the one I walked into
again a few hours ago. Rewritten by hand, from the lint's own report.

And the rewrite had a bug of its own worth recording, because it nearly did
real damage: applying the mapping across all of `record/` overwrote the
`formerly:` field of the five *concretized* documents — replacing each one's
record of the temporary code it used to carry with its own current number, and
so destroying exactly the provenance that field exists to hold. Caught by reading the diff before
committing, reverted, and worth stating as a rule: **a code rewrite must skip
the document the new code names.** An `ack`-style command that wrote these from
the report would not have made this mistake either, which is the third time
today that particular gap has had a price.
