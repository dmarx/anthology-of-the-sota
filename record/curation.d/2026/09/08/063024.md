The widened scope's first task was re-reading [LIT-090](../../record/literature.d/LIT-090.md), and it paid in a way
worth recording.

**The retirement does not survive the paper.** It was filed at migration as
"a research direction without demonstrated practical impact" — written
against a four-bullet description of the work as automated discovery and
theory. The paper is Chen et al., NeurIPS 2023: 705 citations and 116
influential citations on Semantic Scholar, a concrete optimizer (Lion),
results reported in four domains, and a named production deployment on
Google's search-ads CTR model. `Rejected` is a claim about standing in this
anthology, and leaving it there was asserting something the record does not
believe. Reversed to `Active`.

**And the retirement was over-determined.** Three quarters of this paper's
evidence is vision, vision-language and diffusion. Under the scope
[ADR-tmpqq6xc](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-tmpqq6xc.md) replaces, that material could not have supported a
recommendation here — so a reader following the old rule would have found
little in the paper that counted, and the wrong description had less work to
do than it looks. Two causes, one outcome. The scope decision earned itself
on the first document it touched, which is a better argument for it than the
ones in the ADR.

**The practice, and why Proposed.** Adam carries two state tensors; this
keeps one and replaces the second moment with the sign of an interpolation
between momentum and gradient, so every update has identical magnitude and
the state halves. Two conditions are recipe rather than trivia and the paper
states both: the gain grows with batch size, so a comparison at a convenient
batch size is not a comparison of the method; and the learning rate has to be
smaller than Adam's, because the sign makes the update norm larger — carrying
an Adam learning rate across is the obvious way to conclude it does not work.

Filed Proposed / unreplicated because everything above is one group's report
and the record has just been taught what that is worth: [LIT-156](../../record/literature.d/LIT-156.md) tuned Muon
and AdamW separately and judged at the end of training rather than mid-run,
and the advantage fell from roughly 2x to 1.1x at 1.2B — having found that
ranking optimizers on intermediate checkpoints can reverse the answer
outright. Nobody has done that for Lion in the record's evidence, so the
promotion condition names exactly that methodology.

**Where it sits.** Not a rival version of the live recommendation but a
different branch. [SOTA-165](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-165.md) is the preconditioning line, which [SOTA-121](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-121.md)'s Muon
extends by orthogonalising the momentum; this is entrywise and discards
magnitude rather than reshaping it. Both answer "what instead of Adam?" from
opposite directions and the record holds no comparison between them, so none
is asserted.

**Two mechanical notes.** The lint caught a directive of mine going stale the
moment this reversal landed — an acknowledgement written yesterday vouching
for [LIT-090](../../record/literature.d/LIT-090.md) as retired, reported the instant it went Active. That is the
machinery doing the thing it exists for: an excuse cannot quietly outlive
what it excused.

And this contribution is stacked on the scope decision rather than branched
from the trunk, because it cites a temp code that lives on that branch. Third
time this session I have walked into a cross-branch temp reference; the first
two I fixed by removing the citation, and this time the dependency was real,
so the branch structure had to carry it instead.
