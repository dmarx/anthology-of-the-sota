# LIT-025: Understanding and Improving Layer Normalization

Xu et al. (2019) — [ARXIV-1911.07013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07013)

## Key takeaways

- The received explanation for LayerNorm is **forward normalization** —
  controlling the distribution of layer inputs. The paper's central claim is
  that this is not where the benefit comes from
- What matters instead is the **derivatives of the mean and variance**, which
  re-center and re-scale the backward gradients. The effect is in the backward
  pass, not the forward one
- **The bias and gain increase the risk of over-fitting and do not work in
  most cases** — the paper's own "beyond our expectation" result
- **LayerNorm-simple**, with the bias and gain removed entirely, outperforms
  LayerNorm on four of the datasets tried, and reaches state of the art on
  En-Vi machine translation
- **AdaNorm** is the proposal: replace the bias and gain with a transformation
  function that adapts to the input. Better than LayerNorm on seven of eight
  datasets
