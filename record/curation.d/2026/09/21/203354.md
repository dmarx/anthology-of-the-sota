**Fourth unit since the [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) worklist resumed, and the first in a while that
produced a new practice rather than a version bump.** The two before it went
to `SOTA-197` v2 and `SOTA-205` v2 because the record already said the thing.
Here the grep came back empty in the way that matters: the quantization
cluster has error compensation (`SOTA-185`), block scaling (`SOTA-163`),
quantization-aware training (`SOTA-234`) and a 4-bit base with 16-bit adapters
(`SOTA-230`), and none of them removes magnitude before quantizing.

**Proposition 4.1 turned out to be a map of that cluster.** It bounds a
layer's output error by four quantities — the rounding errors of weights and
activations, and their magnitudes. Read that way, the existing practices act
on different terms of one bound, and they compose rather than compete. The
source demonstrates the composition itself by running GPTQ on its residual
weights. That reframing is worth more to this record than the method is.

**The theory was split out because the practice does not need it to be
followed and does need it to be understood.** `ADR-031`'s test: the practice
says what to do, the theory says what is true. And the true thing is sharp — a
rank-`r` subtraction removes exactly the magnitude held in the first `r`
singular values, so it pays on a steep spectrum and not on a flat one. A
weight matrix is steep; a quantization error, being near-independent across
entries, is flat. That is why the same components in the other order fail, and
LoRC is the published control.

**Filed `Proposed` on a gap that is one line of code wide.** The steep half
is plotted in Figure 5. The flat half is asserted and used — it is the
load-bearing reason the control underperforms — and this record did not find
the spectrum of `W − Q(W)` plotted anywhere. Sound inference, unmeasured
premise, which is exactly what `Proposed` is for rather than a reason to
decline the document.

**The practice states two halves as one instruction, which is unusual here.**
Normally a practice says one thing. This one says decompose *and* fuse,
because the paper measured what happens if you do the first without the
second: **57%** latency overhead, which is more than the 4-bit arithmetic
saves. A reader who takes the clever half and skips the plumbing ends up
slower than where they started, and a practice that let them do that would be
worse than no practice.

**And a result recorded sceptically rather than repeated.** On FLUX.1-dev the
4-bit model beats its own BF16 source on Image Reward, which the paper reads
as stronger human preference. The cheaper explanation is that Image Reward is
a learned model with its own biases; PSNR and LPIPS do not invert. Filing a
paper's number is not the same as filing its reading of it — `DP-006`.
