# LIT-144: MiniCPM: Unveiling the Potential of Small Language Models with Scalable Training Strategies

Hu et al., Tsinghua and ModelBest (2024) — [ARXIV-2404.06395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06395)

## Key takeaways

- Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD): warm up, hold the learning rate at its peak for
  the bulk of training, then decay it sharply — the paper uses about 10% of
  the total tokens for the decay. The loss drops steeply during the decay
  and the final quality matches or beats a cosine schedule of the same
  length.
- The stable stage has no predefined end, so the total token count need
  not be fixed when training starts; any checkpoint from the stable stage
  can be branched into a decay to produce a finished model, which makes
  scaling-law studies and continued pretraining far cheaper than with cosine.
- The decay stage is where the authors mix in high-quality and SFT-style
  data, using it for domain adaptation without a separate fine-tuning run.
- The rest of the paper is a *model wind tunnel*: µP-style scaling
  experiments ([LIT-148](LIT-148.md)) on small proxies to set width, depth, batch size
  and learning rate before the 1.2B and 2.4B runs.
