title: 'A measured number in prose is a maintenance obligation nobody agreed to'
created: '2026-09-18T19:52:05'

---

Two ADRs stop quoting a count. `ADR-018` said the lockfile held 305 entries;
`ADR-044` said 921 lines and 305 entries. Today the file holds 319 entries in
963 lines, and the 305 was wrong on the day it was written.

## Two failures, and only one of them is interesting

The **wrong** number was an ordinary mistake: I counted on a branch that
carried two lockfile entries `main` did not have, and wrote the branch's
figure into a claim about `main`. Caught, offered three times, and the fix is
a subtraction.

The **stale** number is the one worth recording, because nothing was done
wrong to produce it. 921 lines was accurate when written. It stopped being
accurate because the record grew, which is the record working. Every paper
filed this week — and there were a lot — moved it further out, and nothing in
the lint can see it: a number in prose is not a citation, has no code, and
resolves to nothing checkable.

So the document acquired a maintenance obligation at the moment it was
written, and nobody agreed to it. That is the general shape: **a measured
figure in prose is a promise to come back and re-measure, made silently.**

## The test that would have caught it

Neither ADR's argument uses the number. `ADR-044` argues that the lockfile is
a shared artifact every branch rewrites; `ADR-018` argues that being derived
and fragment-fed is not sufficient to avoid that. Both work identically at 30
entries or 3,000. The figures were there as texture — *look how big this
is* — and texture is exactly what ages.

So: **if a number can change without the argument changing, the argument
should not contain the number.** Where the magnitude genuinely carries weight
— GPT-4V's 17.97% on OlympiadBench, or ES beating RL at 32B — the number is
the claim and belongs in the text. Where it is scene-setting, the structural
statement is both truer and permanent: *one file, one entry per cited
identifier, rewritten by every contribution that files a paper.*

## Where the old figures went

Into the `history:` notes, which is what that field is for. The record keeps
what it stopped believing (`DP-003`), and a superseded measurement is exactly
that — the version note now says what the document used to claim, that the
count was wrong when written, and that it drifted afterwards. A reader who
finds the old figure quoted somewhere else can trace it.
