# LIT-304: Consensus Problems in Networks of Agents With Switching Topology and Time-Delays

Olfati-Saber and Murray (2004) — [DOI:10.1109/TAC.2004.834113](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2004.834113)

## Key takeaways

The reference for Laplacian consensus on **directed** networks. The
undirected case was earlier work (the paper cites its own prior result for
undirected graphs with and without delay); this paper extends the analysis to
digraphs with fixed or switching topology, ties convergence speed to the
Fiedler eigenvalue of a "mirror graph", gives an exact bound on tolerable
uniform delay, and adds a max-consensus protocol. Theorem numbers below are
the Caltech preprint's (May 2003), which is what was read; the TAC version's
numbering may differ.

- **Strong connectivity is a rank condition (Theorem 1).** A weighted digraph
  is strongly connected iff rank(L) = n − 1. With Theorem 3 (the limit of
  exp(−Lt) is w_r w_lᵀ for a strongly connected digraph), Corollary 1 gives
  consensus for protocol ẋ = −Lx on any strongly connected digraph — but the
  agreed value is a weighted average set by the left eigenvector, not
  necessarily the average.
- **Average consensus on a digraph needs balance (Theorems 4–6).** For a
  strongly connected digraph the protocol reaches the *average* of the initial
  states iff the graph is balanced (in-degree equals out-degree at every
  node), equivalently 1ᵀL = 0, which makes the average an invariant. A
  three-node connected digraph that fails to average is given as a
  counterexample.
- **Speed is the Fiedler value of the mirror graph (Theorems 7–8).** For a
  balanced graph, (L + Lᵀ)/2 is itself a valid Laplacian, of the "mirror"
  undirected graph Ĝ. For a balanced, strongly connected digraph the
  disagreement vanishes as ‖δ(t)‖ ≤ ‖δ(0)‖·e^{−λ₂(Ĝ)t}, with V(δ) = ½‖δ‖² as
  Lyapunov function.
- **Switching topology (Theorem 9) — every graph balanced and strongly
  connected.** If the topology switches arbitrarily within the set of
  digraphs that are each strongly connected *and* balanced, average consensus
  is reached, because ½‖δ‖² is a common Lyapunov function for every graph in
  the set; the rate is at least min λ₂(Ĝ) over the set. It does **not** prove
  consensus under connectivity of the union over a window — that weaker,
  jointly-connected condition is Jadbabaie et al.'s ([LIT-285](LIT-285.md)) result.
  *Holds when:* each graph in the switching set is individually strongly
  connected and balanced.
- **Uniform delay (Theorem 10).** For an undirected connected graph with the
  same delay τ on every link, the delayed protocol reaches average consensus
  iff τ < π/(2λ_max(L)); at τ = π/(2λ_max) it oscillates at frequency λ_max.
  The bound is on the *largest* Laplacian eigenvalue, so adding links (raising
  λ_max) shrinks the tolerable delay.
- **Max-consensus (Theorem 11).** A discrete-time max protocol with a
  Boolean leader flag reaches the network maximum in at most n − 1 iterations
  on a strongly connected digraph.

## What the evidence does not cover

- Linear averaging dynamics, apart from the max-consensus protocol — no
  nonlinear couplings.
- No noise — stochastic consensus requires separate analysis (Tahbaz-Salehi
  for IID random topology).
- Uniform delay, and undirected graphs, for the delay result —
  heterogeneous delays and delays on digraphs are left open.
- The switching result needs every graph in the set to be connected; it says
  nothing about topologies that are disconnected at some instants.
- Continuous-time throughout for the averaging results; discrete-time
  versions are related only through the Euler-approximation remark.

## Standing in the anthology

It takes [LIT-285](LIT-285.md)'s setting and changes both the graphs and the proof. Read in
the Caltech preprint (April 2003) rather than the TAC version: Remark 6 says
Jadbabaie et al.'s discrete-time heading update is the first-order Euler
approximation of this paper's continuous-time protocol with a particular
choice of weights, and the paper moves the problem from [LIT-285](LIT-285.md)'s undirected
graphs to directed ones, where average consensus holds exactly when the graph
is balanced. Where [LIT-285](LIT-285.md) argues through infinite products of stochastic
matrices, this uses a disagreement function as a common Lyapunov function, so
its switching-topology theorem (Theorem 9 in the preprint) asks that every
graph in the switching set be strongly connected and balanced, a stronger
hypothesis than [LIT-285](LIT-285.md)'s joint connectivity over intervals.
