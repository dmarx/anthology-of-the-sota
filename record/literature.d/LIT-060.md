# LIT-060: Improving Language Models by Retrieving from Trillions of Tokens

Borgeaud et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)

## Key takeaways

- **Comparable to GPT-3 and Jurassic-1 on the Pile with 25× fewer parameters**,
  by cross-attending to chunks retrieved from a **2-trillion-token** database
- **The retriever is a frozen, pretrained BERT** — no retriever training, no
  re-indexing as the model changes. The paper's claim is that this works *at
  scale*, removing the moving part retrieval systems usually break on. Chunked
  cross-attention keeps cost **linear in the amount retrieved**
- **The gain is constant from 150M to 7B** — retrieval is not a small-model
  crutch that scale absorbs
- **Capability increases after training ends:** the model "can be improved at
  evaluation time by increasing the database size and the number of retrieved
  neighbours". No gradient step
- Proposes an **evaluation aware of test/train document proximity**, noting it is
  "relevant for all language models, and especially for retrieval-enhanced models
  since they have direct access to the training dataset during evaluation"

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-060](../notes.d/NOTE-060.md). Nothing is sourced here; retrieval-augmented pretraining is
not a line the anthology tracks.

<!-- inactive-ok-block: SOTA-160 — Proposed, named as an allocation practice this bears on rather than relied on -->
The finding worth recording is an allocation one. Every allocation practice in
the record — the Chinchilla line, `SOTA-160`'s joint token-and-quantization
decision — trades parameters against **tokens seen during training**, and assumes
capability is fixed when training ends. This trades parameters against **tokens
available at inference**, and its evaluation-time improvement is a direct
counterexample to the assumption. The record has no place to put that.

Two of the takeaways replaced were wrong. **"Efficient similarity search" is not
a contribution** — the retriever is an off-the-shelf frozen BERT, and the point
is that none had to be built. **"Memory-efficient architecture" is backwards**:
the 25× parameter reduction is bought with a two-trillion-token database, whose
cost the paper does not price.
