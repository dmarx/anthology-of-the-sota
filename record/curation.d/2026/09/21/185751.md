**`LIT-tmp36izs` filed from [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) in rank order. The headline is a nice
idea; the finding worth keeping is its failure.**

The idea first, because it is genuinely good. You want to fit an interface —
an EMG prosthesis, a gaze tracker, a gesture controller — and you have nothing:
no prior mapping, no labels for what the person meant, no reward for what they
achieved, no list of what they will try to do. The observation that unlocks it
is task-free: *whatever* they are trying to do, an interface they can use
produces commands that explain what happens next. So take the mutual
information between command and induced state transition and use it as the
score.

It works. ρ = 0.43 against real task completion over 540K examples, and
maximizing it with human-in-the-loop RL builds a usable cursor interface from
scratch in half an hour with no feedback from the user at all.

**Then there is the fifth domain.** In the shared-autonomy Lunar Lander data
the one-step version of the objective correlated *negatively* with reward —
strongly. The reason is perfect: the assistant helps by **overriding** the
user to stop them crashing, and overriding commands is exactly what makes
commands uninformative about the next state. Meanwhile it *increases* the
user's influence over everything after that, because they are still in the
air.

So a one-step influence metric punishes an assistant for assisting, and does
it hardest when the assistance is most effective.

That is the sharpest measurement trap I have filed today, and today has had
several. `SOTA-305` says a reconstruction FID is meaningless without its token
count. `SOTA-307` says a generation FID needs an error bar over training
seeds. `SOTA-308` says recall bought with relevance is not an improvement.
This one is worse than all three, because what the measurement choice changes
is the **sign**.

**The fix is honest and it eats the premise.** Generalize to
`I(x_t, (s_t, s_{t+Δ}))` and set `Δ` to the episode length, and the
correlation comes back positive. But — the authors' own words, twice —
"choosing the value of `Δ` requires prior knowledge of the timescale of the
user's desired influence over the system... This is the primary limitation on
the generality of our method."

The abstract calls the objective "completely unsupervised". It is unsupervised
given a hyperparameter that encodes what the user is trying to do. That is
still a large reduction in what you must know and I have said so in the
practice; it is not the same as none.

**And then something I noticed that the paper does not dwell on.** Over twelve
users the learned interface converges to two modes: no perturbation of the
mouse, and **exact inversion of it**. Both are consistent. Both are mappings a
person can learn to use reliably.

The motivating sentence was "when an interface is more intuitive, the user's
commands are less noisy". The quantity being measured is whether the
command-to-outcome relation is *reliable*, and an inverted mouse is perfectly
reliable once you have adapted to it. The objective may be finding
learnability rather than intuitiveness. That would be a different claim and
arguably a stronger one, and nobody has separated them.

**Four instances, and I am not writing the principle.** Four papers today, from
generative-model evaluation, tokenizer benchmarking, graph retrieval and
assistive interfaces, all of the form *the reported number is sensitive to a
choice the report does not state*. That is a tempting shape.

`DP-009` says counting is the antidote and two is not several. Four is more
than two, and four things are not obviously one mechanism — FID's seed
variance and MIMI's horizon inversion have nothing in common but my having
read them the same afternoon. So: counted in the note, counted here, promoted
to nothing. If it is real it will keep showing up and the count will be
waiting.
