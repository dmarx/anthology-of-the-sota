## The part that is mine

`#342`'s body says Tier C is "untouched, as intended". So does a comment on it,
posted at 23:03 on 2026-09-25 — **hours after six of the seven had merged**.
That comment is mine. In the same comment I re-measured Tiers A and B against
`main`, printed a table, and wrote that the issue body "is a 2026-09-24
snapshot and it is out of date."

So: measured two tiers, copied the third, and the copied one was the wrong one.
That is the `#379` defect — a claim read off a stale artefact instead of off
the tree — committed one day after writing that the fix is to re-measure
immediately before committing, in a comment whose entire subject was that
issue bodies go stale.

**The generalisable form is narrower and more useful than "re-measure".** I did
re-measure. What I did not do was re-measure *the thing I was not working on*.
A partial audit inherits the authority of the part it checked: the table of
Tier A and B counts made the sentence about Tier C look measured, because it
sat three lines below numbers that were. **An audit that covers most of a claim
launders the rest of it.** The cheap defence is to state, per assertion, where
the number came from — and the comment does that for A and B and silently does
not for C.

That is the gap-sentence shape this journal has now recorded four times, with

of a practice filed a day earlier.

## What the paper does to the practice

Three things, and none of them is a refutation.

**The failure is attributable.** Integrated Gradients multiplies its path
integral by `(x − x₀)`, which does not depend on the model at all. Drop it and
the map becomes parameter-sensitive: SSIM for the local variant is about half
the global variant's on Inception. `LIT-713` suspected exactly this and wrote
that it "do[es] not measure input multiplier's quantitative impact". So the
new practice step is *decompose the failure before you blame the method*.

**The verdict changes with modality.** On BERT/SST-2, global IG — which fails
on images — passes, because token scores are summed over embedding dimensions
and the multiplier's structure does not survive the sum. `NOTE-365` had asked,
in its open questions, "Do the verdicts hold for transformers and for token
attributions?", in the same document that named this paper as unheld. **The
reading recorded both the question and the answer's location and did not join
them**, which is what an open question next to a bare id is.

**The metric split is independently reproduced.** `SOTA-430`'s step 3 already
told a reader to compare signed rank correlation against perceptual similarity
because "a single metric can hand you either verdict" — from `LIT-713`'s own
data. Appendix A here does it again on a different model: SSIM says global IG
is insensitive to randomization; Spearman says both variants are sensitive.
Same experiment, opposite answers. That is the strongest corroboration any
part of the practice has, and **it corroborates the caveat rather than the
headline** — the third time in a week that the load-bearing half of a
document turned out to be the hedge.

Consensus moves `unassessed` → `contested`, which is what the vocabulary's own
gloss describes: "the fork in a line of work, whose trunk is agreed and whose
branches are not". Both groups run the tests. They differ on what a failure
means.

## A theory, this time

Three of the last four units declined to file one, so the reason this one is
filed is worth stating. The account — the map survives randomization because
of a model-independent factor — has two arms that discriminate, and they point
opposite ways. Remove the multiplier: the map becomes sensitive. Destroy the
multiplier's *structure* without removing it, which is what summing over
embedding dimensions does: the global variant becomes sensitive too. An
explanation that only covered the image result would be indistinguishable from
the bare word "multiplier". This one predicts a sign change across modality and
gets it, which is the same bar autoguidance cleared and Swin did not.

## The reading's own caveat

`LIT-tmp5z3a0` is by captum's maintainers. captum implements Integrated
Gradients; the finding is that IG's most-cited failure is attributable to a
factor captum's API lets you switch off. The local-versus-global arm is a real
control and the measurement stands. But `SOTA-430`'s old `consensus_note`
treated "captum links this critique" as a fact about the field, and it is one
group linking its own paper. Recorded on the note and in the practice.
