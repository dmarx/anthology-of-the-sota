Two notes had already asked this question and left it open on purpose.
[LIT-168](../../record/literature.d/LIT-168.md): *"whether GRPO's successors warrant a practice of their own depends
on whether the anthology wants to carry RL algorithm choice as practice, and
nothing does yet."* [LIT-180](../../record/literature.d/LIT-180.md): *"the RL-algorithm question stays out of the
practice registry until the record decides it wants to carry it."*

The record has decided. The gap those notes were describing is precise:
[SOTA-129](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-129.md) and [SOTA-130](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-130.md) argue about *when* to run RL with verifiable rewards —
three stages, or skip the SFT one — and neither names an algorithm, while
GRPO is cited by five notes and is what all of them actually run.

## Why two documents and not one

Filing "use GRPO" would have asserted something this record's own evidence
contradicts. [LIT-168](../../record/literature.d/LIT-168.md)'s standing section says it outright: *"'we ran GRPO' is
under-determined: three papers within a year each found a different defect in
it worth naming."*

So the evidence was split where it actually splits.

**Agreed:** drop the critic, take the baseline from a group of samples for the
same prompt. [LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md) runs it at 0.6B, and [LIT-167](../../record/literature.d/LIT-167.md), [LIT-168](../../record/literature.d/LIT-168.md) and [LIT-180](../../record/literature.d/LIT-180.md) each
examined this objective closely enough to publish a correction — and not one
of them proposed bringing the critic back. That is what `converged` records:
not that nobody looked, but that people looked hard and changed something
else.

**Unsettled:** the rest of the objective. Dr. GRPO's length bias, DAPO's
decoupled clip and dynamic sampling, GSPO's sequence-level importance ratio.
Three defects, three fixes, no two the same, and no comparison.

## The word that did not fit

The fork looked like `contested` — an agreed trunk with disputed branches is
the phrase in `consensus.yaml` itself. It is not.

`contested` means credible groups publicly disagree, and these three are not
arguing with each other; each found a real defect, published a real fix, and
left the others alone. [ADR-016](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-016.md) is what made the difference legible: it
requires a `contested` practice to name what contests it, and there was
nothing honest to put in the field. Three sources agreeing that a thing is
broken are not evidence against the practice that says so.

`emerging` — several independent groups, moving toward a default without
being there — is the accurate reading, and the guard is what forced looking
for it.

## What would move it

The `promote_when:` asks for a comparison of the three corrections on one base
and one dataset, or a frontier recipe naming which it runs and why —
explicitly not a fourth paper naming a fourth defect. Three already exist, and
that is the problem rather than the evidence.
