`#183` asks the record to learn from what the big repositories implement. Before
working thirteen of them, one — vLLM, as `#287` — to find out what the method
actually yields.

## What the pass does

`raw.githubusercontent.com` is reachable from a session; the GitHub API is 403
through the proxy. So enumeration is fetching known index files. vLLM's
`docs/features/README.md` carries a feature-by-feature compatibility matrix
naming fifteen, and `docs/features/quantization/README.md` names nine more
quantization backends. Twenty-one features, taken as the unit.

Matching them against 351 practices by regex over each practice's title,
summary and body: **fourteen matched, seven did not.**

## The seven gaps are real

None of `AWQ`, `prefix cach`, `RadixAttention`, `expert parallel`, `CUDA graph`
or `GGUF` appears anywhere in `record/practices.d/` **or**
`record/literature.d/`. Checked loosely, not just through the matcher.

The one that says most is **AWQ**. `GPTQ` is in the record three times; its
direct competitor, published a year later and at least as widely served, is in
it zero times. That is not a judgement the record made — nothing weighed the
two — it is a hole that looks like a judgement, which is the failure a coverage
pass exists to find.

`Beam search` and `multimodal serving inputs` are gaps the record may be right
to have: the first is declining rather than emerging, and the second is a
capability rather than a recommendation. A gap is a question, not a worklist
item.

## The fourteen matches are mostly wrong

This is the part worth writing down.

Eighteen matched practices carry `(absent)`, `unassessed` or `unreplicated`
consensus — which looks like a rich seam until the rows are read. `SOTA-143`
matched *FP8* because µP's body mentions FP8 in passing. `SOTA-157` matched
*speculative decoding* because a masked-diffusion practice discusses draft
models. `SOTA-248` matched *INT8* through a sentence about softmax clipping.
None of the three is a thing vLLM implements.

**A practice body mentions a technique for many reasons, and only one of them
is "this practice is about it."** Matching prose finds the mention; the
question is about the subject. So the mapping is a judgement per feature, not
a grep — the grep is a candidate generator whose precision is poor and whose
*recall* is what makes it worth running, because the seven gaps it found are
real and were found in one pass.

## The finding that pays for the exercise

**`SOTA-105` (PagedAttention) and `SOTA-113` (continuous batching) carry no
`consensus` field at all.**

Those are the two most universally adopted serving techniques in the field.
The record has never assessed either. And that is the pattern rather than the
exception — `consensus` is absent on 136 of 351 practices and `unassessed` on
24 more, 46% of the registry.

Neither is moved here. One repository is thin evidence for a claim about what
the field does, and `#289` checks seven more; moving them on this pass would be
the sloppiness `DP-005` is about, committed while citing `DP-005`. The pilot's
job was to find out whether the seam exists. It does.

## What this tells `#288`

The ADR has to decide where library evidence lives. The pilot says prose is
not enough on its own: the interesting output is a **count** — 21 features, 14
matched, 7 absent, 2 headline practices unassessed — and a count that lives in
`consensus_note` sentences cannot be recomputed, diffed or re-run. Whatever
`#288` chooses has to survive `#291` asking the same question again in three
months and getting a different answer.
