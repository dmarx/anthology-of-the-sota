# LIT-393: Understanding Dropout

Baldi and Sadowski (2013) — NIPS 26:2814-2822, <https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2013/hash/71f6278d140af599e06ad9bf1ba03cb0-Abstract.html>

## Key takeaways

- **The averaging theorem is exact for a single logistic unit, and the
  logistic function is why.** Writing `G` for the weighted geometric mean of
  the unit's outputs over dropout configurations and `G'` for that of the
  complements, the normalized weighted geometric mean `NWGM = G/(G+G')`
  satisfies `NWGM = σ(E[S])` exactly — so it is computed by one forward pass
  with weights scaled by their retention probabilities, `σ(Σ wⱼpⱼIⱼ)`. The
  proof turns on the identity `[1−σ(x)]/σ(x) = ce^{−λx}`, and the paper notes
  that **the only functions satisfying `NWGM(f) = f(E)` are the constants and
  the logistic functions**. The test-time trick is a property of the
  nonlinearity, not a generic fact about averaging.

- **In deep networks one approximation remains, and it is named.** The
  averaging properties reduce to three recursive equations: `E(Oᵢ) ≈
  NWGM(Oᵢ)`, `NWGM(Oᵢ) = σᵢ[E(Sᵢ)]`, and `E(Sᵢ) = Σ wᵢⱼpⱼE(Oⱼ)`. The first is
  the only approximation. It is **exact iff the unit's activity is identical
  across all subnetworks** — the paper's notion of a *consistent* neuron —
  and degrades with that activity's variance. Linear units need it at all, so
  regression with linear output layers "shaves off one layer of
  approximations."

- **The approximation is good to second order, and better than the plain
  geometric mean.** The paper bounds `E − NWGM` and shows `NWGM` beats `G`,
  with exactness when activities are consistently low (`0 < Oᵢ ≤ 0.5`) or
  whenever `NWGM ≤ E`. The bounds are corroborated by simulation.

- **The same formalism yields the regularization account.** For a linear unit
  under squared error, the expected dropout gradient is exactly the gradient
  of `E_ENS + ½ Σ wᵢ² Iᵢ² Var(δᵢ)` — ensemble error plus a weight-decay term
  **adaptively scaled by the input magnitudes and by the dropout variance**,
  maximized at `p = 0.5`. For a sigmoidal unit under relative entropy the same
  holds approximately, via a Taylor expansion using the `NWGM` result.

## Standing in the anthology

Two accounts of dropout come out of this one paper, and the record files them
separately because they answer different questions: the averaging result says
why one cheap forward pass is a legitimate stand-in for an ensemble, and the
regularization result says what dropout does to the objective. That the
second is derived here *using* the first is the connection worth keeping —
they are not independent stories, and the theory documents say so.

The regularization half is the weaker of the two: it is derived for a single
linear unit and extended to a sigmoidal one by approximation, and
[LIT-392](LIT-392.md) covers that ground with a sharper
characterization.
