# LIT-084: DeepNet: Scaling Transformers to 1,000 Layers

Wang et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2203.00555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00555)

## Key takeaways

- Post-LN trains well and destabilises with depth; Pre-LN is stable and gives
  up quality. **DeepNorm** takes both, with a *theoretically derived*
  initialization rather than a heuristic
- The residual becomes `LayerNorm(x·α + f(x))`, and residual-branch weights
  are Xavier-initialised with gain `β`. **Query and key projections keep gain
  1** — they are deliberately not downscaled
- `α` and `β` depend only on architecture and depth, and move oppositely: for
  a decoder-only model `α = (2M)^(1/4)` and `β = (8M)^(−1/4)`
- **Model updates are bounded**, which is the analytic claim the constants
  fall out of
- **1,000 layers** (2,500 sublayers) trained "without difficulty", an order of
  magnitude deeper than prior work
- A **200-layer 3.2B model beats a 48-layer 12B model by 5 BLEU** across 7,482
  translation directions — depth substituting for width at a quarter of the
  parameters

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-tmpe03a9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpe03a9.md). The practice is
**confirmed and much vaguer than its source**: "use smaller variance for deep
networks" is a true description of `β` that states neither the exponent, nor
that `α` grows as the same root, nor that query and key projections are
excluded — the last being what a reader would most plausibly get wrong.
