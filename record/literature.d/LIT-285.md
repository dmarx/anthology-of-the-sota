# LIT-285: Coordination of groups of mobile autonomous agents using nearest neighbor rules

Jadbabaie et al. (2003) — [DOI:10.1109/TAC.2003.812781](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2003.812781)

## Key takeaways

**The object.** Vicsek et al.'s (1995) self-propelled particles, with the noise
removed: "The Vicsek system also includes noise input signals, which we ignore
in this paper." Each agent sets its heading to the average of its own and its
neighbours' (within radius `r`). The model is a switched linear system
`θ(t+1) = F_σ(t) θ(t)` over **simple undirected graphs**, with each `F_p`
row-stochastic with a positive diagonal.

**The move that makes it tractable.** The switching really depends on agent
positions, but "it would be difficult to take into account in a convergence
analysis". The paper "ignores how σ depends on the agent positions … and
assumes instead that σ might be any switching signal in some suitably defined
set". Every theorem is about *arbitrary* switching sequences meeting a
connectivity hypothesis. None proves that the Vicsek dynamics produce one.

- **Theorem 1.** Connected at every step → headings converge to a common value.
- **Theorem 2 (the main result).** If there is an infinite sequence of
  *contiguous, nonempty, bounded* intervals across each of which "the n agents
  are linked together" (the union graph is connected), headings converge to a
  common value. The authors call contiguity "more restrictive than one might
  hope for". Lengths may vary and need only be bounded. The proof goes through
  Wolfowitz's theorem on infinite products of ergodic matrices
  ([LIT-301](LIT-301.md)), for which "The finiteness of the set … is crucial".
- **No common quadratic Lyapunov function.** The system is stable and yet has
  none: "a graphic example of a switched linear system which is stable, but for
  which there does not exist a common quadratic Lyapunov function". This is
  established by a semidefinite-programming check on 10-vertex connected graphs
  with 9–10 edges, not proved analytically.
- **Theorem 4 (leader following).** One agent holds a fixed heading. If the
  followers are jointly linked to it across contiguous bounded intervals, they
  all converge to its heading.
- **Theorem 5 (continuous time, with dwell time).** Convergence to the
  leader holds for bounded, non-overlapping intervals (here not required to be
  contiguous), "no matter what the value of τ_D is, so long as τ_D is greater
  than zero".
- **A variant with gain `1/g`, `g > n`,** gives symmetric matrices with a common
  Lyapunov function, at the cost of every agent knowing a bound on group size.
  "A proof … will not be given."

## What the evidence does not cover

- **Whether the Vicsek model generates such sequences.** The paper expects the
  opposite for sparse starts: "with widely distributed initial agent positions
  and r very small, one would expect to see a bifurcation of the group into
  distinct subgroups". It runs no simulations of its own.
- **Any rate.** Convergence is asymptotic, which is why the gossip practices
  that need a rate (spectral gap) do not rest on it.
- **Noise.** "the analysis in this paper is deterministic and does not address
  the noise issue".
- **Directed graphs.** The directed spanning-tree condition the imported text
  attributed to this paper is later work.
- **Headings on the circle.** They are treated as reals in `[0, 2π)`, which the
  paper flags as a defect.
- **Priority.** A 2007 comment by Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis (TAC 52(5), not
  read) says the update is a special case of their 1980s agreement algorithms
  ([LIT-325](LIT-325.md)) and "the main convergence results are essentially the
  same". Do not call this paper "first" without that caveat. A 2003
  correction by the authors (TAC 48(9)) exists and has not been read.

## Standing in the anthology

Read — the reading is [NOTE-136](../notes.d/NOTE-136.md). Arrived in the imported batch, which
brought in the consensus, synchronization and flocking literature that the
decentralized-training results rest on.

**Re-read in full on 2026-09-25, and the imported text was wrong in load-bearing
ways** (see the version note). The reading-time triage of that day proposed it
as unheld. It was held all along. The feed's URL for it was a different paper,
Tahbaz-Salehi and Jadbabaie's *On Consensus Over Random Networks*, the
conference precursor of [LIT-326](LIT-326.md).

No practice or theory cites it. It is background to
[SOTA-223](../practices.d/SOTA-223.md) (resample the gossip topology every round),
whose argument turns on a *sequence* of topologies. This paper is the classical
statement that a sequence of individually disconnected graphs can still agree.
It is not evidence for that practice's rate claim.
