Two findings, and the first was found by accident while collecting data for
the second.

## The duplicates

PR [#138](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/138) went in as two commits. The first filed 68 papers as `Deferred`
literature on the argument that their readings could not be trusted. The
second replaced that decision, filed 70 readings and promoted the papers.

**It did not delete the first commit's files.** The regeneration between the
two ran `git clean -fd record/`, which removes *untracked* files — and the
first commit's 68 were tracked, because they had been committed. So the port
minted a second, separate set of codes for the same 68 papers, and both sets
merged.

Result on `main`: **67 duplicate literature notes**, each a `Deferred` stub
for a paper that also has an `Active` note with a reading attached. The
sixty-eighth is not a duplicate — `2102.11742` is `Deferred` in both passes
and the second one survives.

Nothing cited them. None carried a `NOTE`. They inflated the literature count
from 310 to 376 and the undecided-document count from 54 to 121, and they are
removed rather than retired: *retire by changing status, never by deleting*
protects a decision the record made and later reversed, and these are not a
decision. They are one filing, run twice, by a tool error. Retiring them would
put 67 identical stubs in the attic and make the attic mean less.

**The check that would have caught it costs one line** — group the literature
by `arxiv:`/`doi:`/`url:` and look for a bucket with more than one member —
and it is the same shape as the check that found three misbound identifiers in
the import itself, two commits earlier. I ran that check on the incoming
notes and not on what I had written.

The narrower lesson is about `git clean`: it is not "reset the working tree",
and using it as one between two generations of the same generated corpus is
how the first generation survives. `git rm` was the operation needed, and
nothing in the sequence made its absence visible, because the second
generation's files were all new names.

## The report

`docs/reports/unbound-lineage.md` lists relations whose two documents share no
primary topic. It had 38 relations and 16 lines, generated on every `luria
index` since the chains got their invariant, and had never been read through.

Reading all 38, the prior in its own preamble — *"usually a sign the
vocabulary is short a word"* — turns out to be wrong here. **Not one of the 38
wanted a fourteenth topic.** They were four things:

**Fifteen documents plainly mis-filed (retagged).** Eight positional-encoding
papers split between `model-architecture` and `attention-techniques` because
`representation-and-encoding` did not exist when they were filed and nobody
went back after [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114) added it — its blurb names positional encoding and the
frequency choices that go with it, which is RoPE, ALiBi, YaRN and the rest
verbatim. [LIT-211](../../record/literature.d/LIT-211.md), a paper with *LLM Fine-Tuning* in its title, under
`training-optimization`. Three batch-size claims under `model-architecture`.
An initialization rule under `distributed-optimization`. Two
instability-detection practices under `training-optimization` while the
practices they are compared against — one from the same paper — sat under
`model-stability`.

**Eleven of those fifteen shared one cause**, which is the finding worth more
than the fifteen fixes: a claim extracted from a model report, a Megatron
paper or a systems paper inherits *that document's* topic instead of its own.
[ADR-026](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-026.md)'s filing rule already says not to. This is the first count of how
often it happens.

**Two relations that were analogies, not comparisons (removed).** [ADR-011](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-011.md)
defines `compared_against:` as the work evaluating itself against the other.
Profiling the input pipeline and profiling memory access were never compared;
neither were overlapping communication and pre-fetching batches. The report
calls this case unusual, and it is — two of 38 — but it exists.

**Seven that cross because `analysis-and-evaluation` is a kind, not a
subject.** Every one joins a paper that does something to a paper that
measures it. Twelve topics name a subject; that one names a kind of work, and
it will unbind a lineage every time a line grows an analysis paper. Not one of
those six documents is mis-tagged — [LIT-230](../../record/literature.d/LIT-230.md)'s body argues its own filing and
the argument holds.

**Fifteen genuine fault lines.** A general practice and its domain-specific
instance; rival fixes made at different layers; trunk papers whose
contribution spans two topics. The first shape wants a relation the record
does not have — `specializes:` — which is a scheme change rather than a retag.

One of the fifteen is none of those and was nearly mis-read as the first:
[SOTA-089](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-089.md) against [SOTA-107](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-107.md), where [SOTA-107](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-107.md) is `Rejected` because the 128 in it
was traced to a head dimension rather than a sequence length. The lint caught
it, by refusing an unacknowledged citation of a retired document — which is
the check this project adopted the record to get, firing on a draft of the
decision that was reading the report. An unbound relation with a retired end
is not a filing question at all.

## What this pass did not do

**It did not touch the SSM line.** Mamba, Mamba-2, Mamba-3 and RWKV-7 are
`model-architecture` while Kimi Linear, Gated DeltaNet, GLA, DeltaNet, Griffin
and Monarch Mixer are `attention-techniques`, and there is a rule under the
inconsistency — a model *family* against a *mechanism* — that Mamba satisfies
both halves of. Retagging four papers to bind one line would be the [#101](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/101) move
that `luria.yaml` records as wrong, so it is written down rather than done.

**It did not re-read the bodies of the fifteen retagged documents.** The
retags were argued from title, blurb and the company each document keeps. For
eleven of them the document is a two-line migrated stub with nothing else to
read, but for [LIT-192](../../record/literature.d/LIT-192.md), [LIT-193](../../record/literature.d/LIT-193.md), [LIT-207](../../record/literature.d/LIT-207.md), [LIT-208](../../record/literature.d/LIT-208.md), [LIT-209](../../record/literature.d/LIT-209.md) and [LIT-210](../../record/literature.d/LIT-210.md) there is
a full note and this pass read the summary, not the body.

**It did not check the other generated reports.** `unbound-lineage.md` was
read because it was asked for. `reference-status.md` gets read before every
lint. The rest of `docs/reports/` has had the same amount of attention this
one had, which was none.
