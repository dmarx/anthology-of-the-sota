Went back for the paper I had left at the top of the worklist an hour earlier
with the note "no HTML, abstract only, not filed". That was half right.

## The retrieval was wrong, not the paper

`2603.00541` has no ar5iv HTML — the conversion failed and the fallback page
says so. But the PDF is fine, and the first attempt at it reported seven
pages because the download was truncated. A second fetch gave 76 pages and
187,000 characters, which extracted cleanly once `cffi` was installed to
unbreak `pypdf`'s import of `cryptography`.

The standing debt I wrote an hour ago — "worth a PDF extraction attempt in a
later pass" — was discharged in about four minutes. Worth noting that the
reason it looked unreadable was a truncated transfer reported as a short
document, and nothing in the failure said so. A seven-page muP paper should
have been suspicious on its face and wasn't.

## What the paper is

Zheng et al. extend the spectral condition for muP from width scaling to
joint width-depth scaling, and the result is a unification: Depth-muP and
CompleteP are not rival proposals, they are the `k = 1` and `k ≥ 2` cases of
one condition, where `k` counts the linear transformations inside a residual
branch.

The mechanism is one term. Expand the one-step change in a residual block's
output. With one weight in the branch, every term has exactly one `ΔW`. With
two, there is also the term where both moved — `ΔW⁽²⁾ΔW⁽¹⁾`. Ordinarily that
is second order and negligible; under muP it is not, because muP deliberately
makes every update as large as stability allows, so a product of two updates
is the same order as the updates. Constraining it tightens the residual
multiplier from `1/√L` to `1/L`. That is the whole difference between the two
published parameterizations.

Every Transformer branch holds more than one transformation, so every
Transformer is the tighter case.

## The promote_when

`SOTA-144` was `Proposed`, `unreplicated`, and its `promote_when` read:

> An independent group training under CompleteP itself and reporting depth
> transfer. A result that reaches depth transfer by another mechanism is not
> it — `LIT-153` did that, and two routes to one property is evidence the
> property is reachable, not that this route works.

Zheng et al. are independent — Renmin University and ByteDance Seed, no
overlap with the Cerebras authors. They report depth transfer to 256 layers.
And they are emphatically not "another mechanism": they derive the *same*
scaling, from an independent framework, which is exactly what the field's
second sentence was protecting against the absence of.

So it is tempting to mark it met, and I nearly did. Two things stop it.

**They do not train AdamW.** §5.1: "Since SGD and AdamW have been extensively
studied in prior muP studies under width-depth scaling, we focus on more
recent optimizers." They run Muon-Kimi-AdamW, Muon-AdamW, Shampoo-AdamW and
Sophia. AdamW is the optimizer `LIT-150` defined the depth exponent for.

**The paper's own word is "style".** Condition 3.1 "recovers CompleteP-style
results" — not implements CompleteP. That hedge is theirs, not mine, and
reading past it would be reading past the thing the author was careful about.

What they supply is *stronger* than the literal test in one direction — an
independent derivation and four optimizers rather than one — and not the test
in another. `consensus` goes `unreplicated` → `emerging`; `status` stays
`Proposed`; and a section in `SOTA-144` records the difference rather than
leaving a discharged field with nothing to say why.

## Rewriting the promote_when rather than deleting it

The old field was written about independence because independence was the
visible weakness. It is no longer the weakness. What is left is scale: every
depth sweep in the record, from either group, is 300M tokens. Deep and short.
Nobody has shown that the transferred learning rate is still the right one at
the end of a real training run.

So `promote_when` now asks for that. This is the second time this week a
`promote_when` has needed rewriting rather than satisfying, and the pattern
is the same both times: the field named the weakness that was visible when it
was written, the weakness moved, and the field had to move with it. That is
`ADR-014` working — a field that names a *kind* of result can be re-aimed;
one that named a quantity could not be.

## Two practices the record could not previously state

`SOTA-144` says use CompleteP. It does not say *which architectures need it*,
because `LIT-150` compared CompleteP against muP rather than against
Depth-muP. `SOTA-tmpnjlal` is the missing half: count the transformations in
your residual branch, and that number picks the rule.

`SOTA-tmpr10ct` is the implementation shortcut, and it is the one I expect a
practitioner to actually use. For every modern optimizer they treat except
SGD, width-depth muP is width muP plus `α_l = Θ(1/L)` and nothing else,
because a normalized or preconditioned update has a norm that does not depend
on the residual multiplier. The record recommends Muon (`SOTA-121`) and
matrix preconditioning (`SOTA-165`) and has never said how to muP either. It
turns out the omission was survivable for exactly this reason.

## One thing the authors caught that I would not have

Their §5.4. For Muon-Kimi-AdamW, standard parameterization appears to
transfer across depth reasonably well — which would undercut the whole
argument if taken at face value. They say so themselves, attribute it to
moderate depths and to LayerNorm and QKNorm masking the scaling pathology,
remove LayerNorm, and show SP then breaks.

That is a real finding about how much modern normalization is already
absorbing, and it means the practical margin at the depths people train is
smaller than the asymptotic argument. It is carried into the practice's
conditions and the theory's "what this does not say", because a reader who
adopts this on the strength of the asymptotics and sees no difference at
`L = 32` should find the explanation in the record rather than concluding the
record was wrong.

## Standing debt

- **Islamov et al., `2603.05002`** — "Non-Euclidean gradient descent operates
  at the edge of stability" — still unfiled, and it joins the two halves of
  the previous cluster directly.
- **The Linear Representation Hypothesis**, still named-not-filed.
- **Only one side of `SOTA-tmpnjlal`'s prediction is tested.** The paper shows
  `k ≥ 2` beating `k = 1` on Transformers. Nobody has shown `k = 1` beating
  `k ≥ 2` on a one-transformation architecture, which is what would make
  branch depth an index rather than a preference for the tighter rule. That
  is what its `promote_when` asks for.
