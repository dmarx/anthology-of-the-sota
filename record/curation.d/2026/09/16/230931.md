Auditing the open issues for which could be closed, and [#88](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/88) — *should we
include practices which we know shouldn't be adopted?* — looked like the easy
one. Eighteen practices carry `Rejected`. The record plainly already does
this; write that down and close the discussion.

That was wrong, and reading the eighteen is what showed it.

## All eighteen mean one thing

Every one of them is a migration provenance failure — the source does not
support the claim — and not one is a technique somebody tried and abandoned:

- [SOTA-041](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-041.md) states the *opposite* of its source; Kaplan reports larger models
  need a smaller rate.
- [SOTA-064](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-064.md)'s cited paper does not contain the word "warmup".
- [SOTA-101](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-101.md)'s source performs no filtering; the string "filter" does not appear
  in it.
- [SOTA-107](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-107.md)'s 128 is a head dimension or a block size in every occurrence,
  never a sequence length.

So the category [#88](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/88) asks about — *this was recommended, people did it, it did
not hold* — is *empty*, and the eighteen are not evidence that it is full.
They are eighteen instances of a different thing wearing the same word.

What makes the mistake instructive rather than embarrassing: the count was
right, the status was right, and the conclusion drawn from them was wrong.
Nothing short of opening the documents would have caught it. A status field
tells you a judgement was made and not what the judgement was about.

## The reading list does not have this gap

The same query over `LIT` reads completely differently. [LIT-044](../../../../literature.d/LIT-044.md) is retired
for *"needs per-case tuning, no consistent cross-domain benefit"*; [LIT-041](../../../../literature.d/LIT-041.md)
for *"the result does not look interesting enough to carry"*. Those are
judgements on the merits.

So the record has been judging **papers** by whether they hold up, and has
never once judged a **practice** that way. That asymmetry is the finding, and
it is invisible from either scheme alone — you have to run the same question
over both and notice the answers disagree.

## And something was already stuck on it

[LIT-104](../../../../literature.d/LIT-104.md) is ReLoRA, which is [#88](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/88)'s own worked example. It is `Proposed`,
nothing sources it, and its note says exactly why:

> It is unfiled because the paper's own status is `Proposed`: the record has
> not settled whether ReLoRA-style restarts belong in it at all, and filing
> the restart discipline would answer that question sideways.

A reading was done in [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121), a specific finding came out of it — restarting an
adapter mid-training needs the optimizer state reset **and** the rate
re-warmed, and the pair without the re-warm diverges — and it has been sitting
unfiled waiting for a discussion issue to resolve. The decision is not
housekeeping; it unblocks work already done.

## What the decision turns on

The condition worth arguing with is the third one: **a refutation needs a
citation exactly as a recommendation does.** [DP-001](../../../../principles.d/DP-001.md) makes the citation
structural for a recommendation, and without the same rule for a rejection
the category fills with folklore — "everyone knows that didn't work" is
precisely the claim [DP-007](../../../../principles.d/DP-007.md) says has no author, and filing it would put a
status field on a rumour.

The consequence accepted in exchange: `Rejected` on a practice will now cover
two situations, *never supported* and *supported and overturned*. A new status
word was the obvious fix and was rejected — the vocabulary is closed and read
by the whole toolchain, and `status_note:` already carries the distinction in
prose on all eighteen. A field that said the same thing uniformly would be
[DP-002](../../../../principles.d/DP-002.md)'s comment with a schema.
