Two tags added to `LIT-425`, one to `LIT-426`, and an ADR saying why the
previous contribution got it wrong — which was mine, filed hours earlier.

## What the mistake actually was

I filed T5 with `data-pipeline` alone and wrote the reasoning into the PR as
though it were a considered call: the record holds this paper for C4, so a
`model-architecture` tag would be incidental, and `ADR-035` warns that an
incidental tag binds chain edges.

Two separate errors, and they are worth pulling apart because only one of
them is about tags.

**The record's motive is not the document's subject.** Why this project went
and got a paper is a fact about the project. What the paper is about is a
fact about the paper. Only the second can go in `tags:`, because `tags:` is
what a reader browses by and a reader has no access to the curator's
reasons. T5's text-to-text framing and its transfer-learning ablation are
most of the paper; someone looking for architecture work would have been
right to expect it and would not have found it. The motive already has a home
— the `Standing in the anthology` section, in prose, where a reason with
nuance belongs.

**The guard was read far outside its case.** `ADR-035` forbids adding a tag
*in order to bind a relation*, and the mechanism it is guarding is
`chains.lineage`, which declares `invariant: tags`. But an invariant binds an
edge only where an edge exists — where a document declares `extends`,
`corrects` or `compared_against`. `LIT-425` declares none. There was no edge
for a tag to bind and there never could have been, so the warning I cited had
no purchase on the decision I used it for.

That is the more interesting failure. A correctly-written, narrowly-scoped
guard got read as a general prohibition, and because it named a real
mechanism it *sounded* rigorous. Citing a rule is not the same as checking
that its precondition holds, and prose in a PR describing a tradeoff is
exactly where that check gets skipped — it reads as diligence either way.

## Why the asymmetry settles it

An extra true tag costs one index row. A missing tag makes a document
unfindable to everyone browsing that topic, silently, with nothing in any
report to say so. There is no lint for "this document should have been on
that page". `DP-004` again from a new angle: the defect a pass cannot be
shaped to find is the one that persists, and a missing tag leaves no trace
for any pass to be shaped around.

Which means the two errors are not equally bad, and the filing rule should
not treat them as if they were. "Is this the single best word" is the wrong
test; "would someone browsing this topic be right to expect this document" is
the right one.

## What was not done

No retag sweep. The cautious reading has been in force for a while, so
documents in the record are probably under-tagged — but a sweep is its own
contribution with its own review, and the cost of being late is bounded:
a document shows up on one fewer generated page until someone touches it.
Worth doing, not worth smuggling into a PR about two notes.
