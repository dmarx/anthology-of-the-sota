# LIT-033: Longformer: The Long-Document Transformer

Beltagy et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2004.05150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)

## Key takeaways

- Combines a **sliding window** (linear in sequence length) with **global
  attention at a small set of task-chosen positions** that attend to
  everything and are attended by everything; a **dilated** window variant
  reaches 32K characters in autoregressive language modelling
- **The global tokens are where the task's inductive bias is stated** — the
  paper's own words. Question tokens for QA, `[CLS]` for classification. The
  pattern is a per-task decision rather than an architectural constant, which
  makes it legible in a way learned sparsity is not
- Receptive field comes from **depth × window**: `k` stacked layers of width
  `w` reach `k·w`, and the window is varied per layer rather than fixed
- **LED** is deliberately asymmetric — the encoder uses local+global, the
  decoder keeps *full* attention over encoded tokens and previous outputs,
  because output sequences are short
- Linear complexity, but not a free constant: the dilated pattern needs custom
  8×8 block-sparse kernels

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-059](../notes.d/NOTE-059.md). Nothing is sourced to this paper: the field's answer
became exact attention made fast (`SOTA-086`) rather than approximate attention
made sparse, and the record reflects that correctly.

The reading connects two things the record holds apart. Longformer's
**designated** global tokens and `SOTA-134`'s **emergent** attention sinks are
the same structure — a few positions everything attends to — reached by design
in 2020 and by observation later. That the mechanism appears unbidden in models
nobody designed it into is a stronger argument for its necessity than
Longformer's own results, and the record now has both halves in view.

**Audited by [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121); unsourced, and half of a convergence.** R2 — get
receptive field from depth x window rather than from a wide window — is
[LIT-020](LIT-020.md)'s R3 arrived at independently. See that note for why the pair is
recorded rather than filed.

R1 is the separable claim: **state which positions must see everything as a
task decision rather than learning it.** The record's sparse-attention practices
([SOTA-138](../practices.d/SOTA-138.md)) learn the pattern instead, so this is a genuine
disagreement the record holds only one side of.
