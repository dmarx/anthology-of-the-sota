**The grep-before-filing habit found an absence this time rather than a
duplicate.** Four units running, checking the practices before writing one has
decided the output shape — twice turning a new practice into a version bump,
once confirming a genuinely new one. Here it did the opposite thing: searching
for calibration returned quantization scales, data-mixing temperature
(`SOTA-102`, `Superseded`) and incidental uses of "confidence". Eighty
practices touch training and not one asks whether a model's probabilities mean
anything.

That is a missing subject, not a missing paper, and it is the strongest reason
to spend a full unit on a candidate. `DP-004`: the absence produced no
unresolved code and no unbound relation, so nothing in the machinery would
ever have pointed at it.

**The finding is the kind this record likes: an identity plus an
observation.** A proper loss IS calibration error plus refinement error —
that part is Bröcker, not new. What is new is that the two terms do not bottom
out at the same epoch, so minimizing the sum lands between them. The
validation-loss minimum, which nearly every training loop selects on, is a
compromise point that nobody chose and that is optimal for neither term.

**Temperature scaling turns out to be an instrument, not just a fix.** Because
it rescales logits by a single scalar it cannot reorder predictions, so it
cannot change refinement — only confidence. That makes post-TS validation loss
an estimate of the refinement term alone. The method falls out of the
measurement rather than being bolted on.

**Filed as a practice and a theory, per `ADR-031`.** The instruction stands
without the account, and the account is a claim about what is true during
training with its own evidence and its own weakness. The theory is `Proposed`
for a reason worth stating plainly: the only party to have plotted the two
curves is the party proposing the remedy. Its promote_when asks for one figure
from a run set up for some other purpose, which is about as cheap as a
promotion condition gets.

**The practice is `Active` on one group, and the justification is the same
shape as SVDQuant's earlier today.** The decomposition is arithmetic, the
instruction costs a scalar fit per validation pass, and the study is 196
datasets across three model families plus ten runs per vision dataset. Breadth
is not independence, which is what `unreplicated` says.

**One thing recorded rather than promoted.** `SOTA-196` already says to report
zero-shot and in-distribution performance separately, because one
hyperparameter can move them in opposite directions. This is that shape one
level down: two components of a single loss, moved oppositely by one knob, and
averaged into a number that hides it. Two instances, different mechanisms,
counted in the note. `DP-009`.
