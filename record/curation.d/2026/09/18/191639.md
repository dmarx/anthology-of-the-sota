[#169](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/169) listed four papers, one open question and a broken lockfile. Working it
found that **not one of the four was what the worklist predicted**, and the
misses run in both directions.

## The paper I ranked highest was right, and for a better reason

Li et al. was listed as "the highest-value one, and the only one with a
practice in it", and that held. What I had not expected is *which* practice.

The prediction was that it explained why dropout left convolutional
architectures. It does, and the mechanism is sharper than "they interfere":
dropout changes a unit's variance between train and test — necessarily, that
is what dropout is — while batch normalization applies variance statistics
accumulated over the whole of training and frozen at inference. BN therefore
rescales by a number estimated under a regime that no longer holds.

**The reason to believe it is that the account predicts its own escape
routes.** A theory that only said "these two interfere" would not predict that
*widening the layer* should rescue the combination. Wide ResNet tolerates
rates of 0.3 and 0.5 and beats its baseline where narrower networks at the
same rates do not, because the larger channel dimension keeps the variance
from exploding while the regularization survives. That is the kind of
consequence a post-hoc story does not produce.

`SOTA-tmpjjsir` is also the record's **first practice about an interaction**
rather than about a technique, which is a shape `SOTA` could always express
and had no instance of.

And it does *not* answer `SOTA-240`'s question, which is the discipline worth
recording. Dropout left convnets for an architectural reason. Why it left
language-model pretraining is a different question with a different answer,
and merging them would have been the satisfying move.

## The question I thought needed a survey needed a search

[#169](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/169) framed `SOTA-240`'s open question as work: read pretraining reports for
what they set and why. That was the wrong shape. **Somebody had already run
the experiment** — Liu, Bauer and Manning (2025), whose first move is to note
that dropout vanished from LM pretraining on the reasoning that one epoch
cannot overfit, and that "no thorough empirical investigation has been done".

The answer is stronger than the practice's own hedge. It is not that dropout
is unnecessary in the single-epoch regime; **it is harmful**, monotonically in
the rate, across LM loss, BLiMP, SQuAD and MNLI, on both masked and
autoregressive models. Early dropout does not rescue it. Models trained
without it are additionally more editable under MEND, with a hypothesis
attached — dropout limits co-adaptation indiscriminately, so knowledge ends up
stored as multiple independent copies, and a gradient-based edit has to find
all of them.

**And the paper's limitations section cites this record's own theory
documents.** It declines to make a theoretical claim, notes that the expected
value of dropout converges to the identity (Baldi and Sadowski,
`THEORY-016`), and names the first-order-regularizer lens (Wager et al.,
`THEORY-015`) as the way in. So the record holds both accounts and the paper
that says a treatment through them is the missing work. That is a named open
question rather than a vague one, which is the most a curation pass can hope
to produce.

The lesson for the worklist: **before scheduling a survey, search for the
experiment.** A question phrased as "the field has not written this down" is
often a question somebody has answered directly.

## The paper I expected to be scale evidence is a negative result

I put softpick in [#169](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/169) guessing it "may be the scale evidence the others
lack". It is the opposite, and it is more useful for being so.

Softpick drops the sum-to-one constraint outright. The sink goes to **0.00%**
at 340M and 1.8B. Kurtosis falls from 33511 to 341. Attention matrices become
95-99% exact zeros where softmax's are ~4.5% and only from underflow. As
confirmation of `THEORY-tmp1rmwn` this is about as direct as evidence gets:
remove the normalization, the sink disappears entirely.

**And the model is worse at 1.8B on every benchmark**, with the training-loss
gap widening from 0.004 to 0.12. The authors put "investigate the subpar
performance at 1.8B" in their own contribution list and offer two hypotheses —
underscoring of long contexts, and heads that die and stay dead.

So the record now holds, in one document, the strongest confirmation of its
sink account and the clearest demonstration that acting on it is not free.
`THEORY-tmp1rmwn` already said sinks are not a defect; it said so from
`LIT-191`'s eviction experiment, which shows removing a sink from a model that
has one is catastrophic. This is the harder version: a model built so the
pressure never arises is still worse.

## The one I ranked lowest deserved it

Hara et al. was listed "lowest priority ... worth reading to find out which".
Read: it is a teacher-student soft committee machine, non-ReLU by the authors'
own admission, concluding that dropout is a resampling ensemble and that it
matches an L2 regularizer. Both are already here from sources that reach
further — `THEORY-016` proves the averaging identity exactly for logistic
units, and `THEORY-015` holds the sharper claim that the penalty is
*data-dependent*, which this setting cannot distinguish from plain L2.

It is filed anyway, `Active`, with a standing section saying it adds nothing.
The reason is the one `ADR-025` gives for `Unread`: **a negative determination
is worth being able to point at.** The next person to find it in a dropout
bibliography should be able to see it was read and placed rather than missed.

## The lockfile is not a luria problem

[#169](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/169)'s third item assumed one `luria remotes --resolve` from a better-connected
machine would fix it. Worth recording what the sandbox actually shows: a full
resolve now reports **306 unresolved**, and plain `urllib` against the arXiv
export API returns 406 or 429 under four different user-agent strings, while
`curl` succeeded earlier in the same session. That is arXiv rate-limiting the
egress, not a defect in the tool and not a property of these papers. Every
identifier filed in these passes was verified by hand while the budget allowed.
