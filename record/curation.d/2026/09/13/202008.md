Review on `#129` asked whether `source:` is being abused, and guessed it holds
both *the paper that introduced the practice* and *a paper that uses it*.

**It holds at least three relations, and the guess is right about all of them.**

## The query

Classify every `LIT` note whose title reads as a model or system report — a
technical report, a named model family, an "open bilingual pre-trained model"
— then find the practices citing one in `source:`.

    LIT notes that read as model/system reports:        22 of 225
    Practices whose `source:` includes at least one:    26 of 208
      ... of which ALL sources are reports:             14
      ... that also carry `consensus:`:                 12
      ... that also carry `implementations:`:            7

Spot-checked, and the heuristic is not inventing anything: `SOTA-069` rests on
GLM-130B, `SOTA-094` on PaLM, `SOTA-139` on DeepSeek-V4. **Fourteen practices
whose only cited support is that somebody shipped it.**

`DP-005` says exactly what is wrong with that — *adoption is not evidence* — and
says it about this record, which had already fixed one instance. The principle
was filed and the corpus was never swept.

## The three relations

- **evidence about the claim** — the ablation, the controlled comparison. What
  the field is documented as meaning.
- **origin** — the work that first said to do this. `ADR-tmpqczy4` split this
  one out as `introduced_by:` while filing Child et al.
- **adoption** — this system does it. `consensus:` exists for this and 12 of
  the 26 have both, so the practice is citing its adopters twice and its
  evidence never.

## Why nothing caught it

The definition of `source:` lives in a **TOML comment**. A comment cannot
render in a view, cannot appear in the generated configuration reference,
cannot be quoted by the lint, and is never seen by anyone filing a document.
So every one of the fourteen passes every mechanical check there is, and the
only thing that found them was somebody deciding to ask.

That is `DP-004` in its purest form — *a pass finds only the defects its query
is shaped like* — and worth noting that this query did not come from reading
the record either. It came from a reviewer's suspicion about a field.

Filed upstream as `LU-#254`: relations declared with a description that is
data, the way vocabularies already are. It cannot make the distinction
checkable — no schema tells an ablation from a model report — but it moves the
definition somewhere a reader and a scaffold can reach.

## Not done here, deliberately

**The fourteen are not repointed.** Fixing one means deciding, per practice,
whether a controlled result exists at all — and where none does, the honest
outcome may be `Deferred` with a `promote_when:`, not a better citation. That
is a reading pass over fourteen practices, not a find-and-replace, and doing it
badly would convert a visible defect into an invisible one.

**`source:` is not retired.** The review floated it, and the audit supports
splitting it further — but `source:` is required on 208 practices and
`published:` derives from `source[0]`. Retiring it is a migration with a
decision in front of it, and `ADR-tmpqczy4` is still `Proposed`: the record
should find out whether one new relation carries its weight before minting
three more.

**The counts are a heuristic, not a census.** Title-matching finds model
reports that announce themselves. A systems paper that is really an adoption
report — and the reverse, a model report carrying a genuine ablation — is
invisible to it in both directions. The number is a floor.
