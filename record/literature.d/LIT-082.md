# LIT-082: Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback

Bai et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2212.08073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)

## Key takeaways

- The supervised stage: sample a response to a harmful prompt, ask the model
  to critique it against a principle drawn from an explicit written list,
  ask it to revise accordingly, and fine-tune on the revisions. The model is
  its own labeler, and the label is a rewrite rather than a score.
- The reinforcement stage: have the model choose between pairs of responses
  using the same principles, train a preference model on those comparisons,
  and optimize against it. This is RLHF with the H replaced — the paper's
  own name for it is RLAIF.
- Human labels do not disappear. Helpfulness comparisons stay human; it is
  the harmlessness half, the expensive and unpleasant half, that becomes
  synthetic.
- The reported result is a model that is both more harmless and less evasive
  than the RLHF baseline — it engages with a harmful request and explains its
  objection rather than refusing flatly, which the paper treats as the point
  rather than a side effect.
- The transferable contribution is not the specific principles. It is that
  the supervision becomes a document: readable, versionable, and arguable,
  where a labeling contract's implicit distribution is none of those.
