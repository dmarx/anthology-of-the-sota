Asked to grow the vocabulary. `ADR-024` had already done the analysis — four
seams, four options, decided nothing, and closed by saying the choice "is a
judgement about the subject rather than about the record". Two of its four
options have been taken since: `ADR-035` relaxed `exactly-one`, `ADR-026` and
`ADR-046` supplied the filing rule. Adding a topic never was.

## The instrument had to change

`ADR-024` found its seams in `docs/reports/unbound-lineage.md`. That report
cannot find this kind of gap any more, and the reason is a success rather
than a regression: `ADR-049` drove unbound relations to zero by naming what
documents were already about.

A missing *word* does not produce an unbound edge. It produces a document
filed under whatever topic was nearest, which binds fine and is simply in the
wrong place. The report is a defect detector for relations, not a coverage
detector for subjects.

So this used `DP-009`'s test, which is counting: take the documents about a
candidate subject and measure how concentrated their primary topics are. A
subject the vocabulary can say lands mostly in one. A subject it cannot say
scatters.

| candidate | docs | top topic | share |
|---|--:|---|--:|
| numerics / precision | 48 | `training-optimization` | 25% |
| in-context learning | 20 | `adaptation-and-tuning` | 45% |
| retrieval | 12 | `attention-techniques` | 33% |
| RL post-training | 40 | `adaptation-and-tuning` | 68% |
| scaling laws | 34 | `training-optimization` | 59% |
| mixture-of-experts | 36 | `model-architecture` | 58% |

The bottom three are named — each is written into an existing blurb and holds
a clear plurality. They stay.

## Numerics was not unnamed; it was half-named twice

The thing I expected to find was a subject with no word. What I found was a
subject with two half-words: `systems-optimization`'s blurb ended with
"numerical precision" and `inference-optimization`'s listed "quantization".
Neither topic is *about* numerics. Each carried a fragment as the last item in
a list about something else, so a document had to pick a side.

The split has a history, and it is nobody's mistake. `ADR-021` created
`inference-optimization` for serving and moved the two post-training
quantization practices into it — correctly, because post-training
quantization *is* a serving decision. That left training-time precision
behind, and the subject has been cut in half since.

I nearly reported the opposite. Finding "numerical precision" already in a
blurb, my first conclusion was that this is a filing failure and not a
vocabulary gap — the word exists, use it. What changed my mind is that the
word exists *twice*, in two topics, and in neither is it the subject. A
trailing blurb item is not a home.

## One topic, not two, and the four documents that decide it

The obvious split is train versus serve: loss scaling and FP32 master weights
on one side, GPTQ and microscaling on the other. Different audiences,
different failure modes.

Four documents say no:

- `SOTA-160` — how many tokens a model saw determines how much post-training
  quantization costs it
- `LIT-186` — *Scaling Laws for Precision*: training low reduces effective
  parameter count
- `SOTA-234`, `LIT-380` — train natively in the target low-bit format, which
  collapses the distinction

Under a train/serve split every one of those straddles, which is `ADR-024`'s
own warning about what happens when a subject is divided by provenance. The
interesting recent work in this area *is* the interaction.

## Three probes lied, and all three lied the same way

This is the part worth keeping.

- **"alignment"** returned 42 documents scattered across four topics, which
  looked like a safety gap. Inspected: **two** real ones. The rest are
  gradient alignment, batch gradient alignment, aligned singular vectors —
  the linear-algebra sense, which I had been writing all night in the Muon
  cluster.
- **"retrieval"** returned 12 at 33%. Inspected: **two** — `SOTA-269` and
  RETRO. The rest are *retrieval benchmarks* for sequence models, the
  in-context recall task linear-attention papers report against Mamba2 and
  DeltaNet.
- **"quantization"** would have named the new topic, until `LIT-363` and
  `LIT-341` turned up: *vector* quantization, correctly filed under
  `representation-and-encoding`. Hence `numerics-and-precision`.

Every one was caught by reading the hits rather than by a better regex. A
keyword probe over this corpus is a way to generate candidates and never a way
to settle one, and I would have added a safety topic and a retrieval topic on
the strength of the first numbers.

Retrieval was in the set the owner approved, on my figure of 12. It is two,
and `DP-009` asks for several, so it is not added and the changed figure is
the reason.

## The line that is still unbound

`SOTA-036 → SOTA-037 → SOTA-038 → SOTA-279` has no tag common to every
member, and adding `in-context-learning` did not fix it — `SOTA-037` and
`SOTA-038` take it, `SOTA-279` takes it, and `SOTA-036` is a pretraining
recipe that should not.

Both of `ADR-049`'s readings were tested against the new evidence and both
failed:

- *The invariant is missing.* There is no true tag. `in-context-learning` on
  a four-choice pretraining recipe fails `ADR-046`'s test, and so does
  `model-architecture` on a prompting practice.
- *The relation is wrong.* I expected this one to be the answer, and went to
  drop `SOTA-037 extends SOTA-036` — then read `extends`'s own blurb: "a rule
  that only exists because the earlier one is followed". In-context learning
  emerging is a property of models trained under that frame. The edge
  survives its own definition.

So the line traverses a genuine change of subject with every step correctly
expressed. The report's text allows for exactly this — an intersection "only
shrinks as the component grows", and lines are "the weaker signal of the
two" — but `ADR-049` reversed a previous attempt to call the invariant
over-strong, and I am not going to make that argument again at the end of a
long session on the strength of one case.

Recorded in the decision as an open question with both readings tested, which
is a result rather than the "note that somebody looked" that `ADR-049`
forbids. `#213` carries it.
