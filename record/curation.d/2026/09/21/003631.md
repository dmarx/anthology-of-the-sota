The previous contribution named the chain-of-thought gap and deliberately did
not fill it, because the paper in hand was a representability theorem and the
practice needed an empirical source. This fills it with the right paper.

## How large the gap actually was

The record held 278 practices and not one of them recommended chain of
thought. `SOTA-127` — filter chain-of-thought traces out of a tiny
specialist's training data — was the entire presence of the idea.

That is stranger than a missing paper. `SOTA-130` skips the reasoning SFT
stage and runs RL with verifiable rewards. `LIT-466` evaluates models whose
defining feature is an extended thinking trace. Both stand on something the
record had never written down. The absence was invisible precisely because so
much was built on top of it — nothing complained, because everything assumed
it.

## Why the ablations are the filing

The headline number is that PaLM 540B more than doubles on GSM8K and beats a
fine-tuned model *with a verifier*, from eight exemplars. That is not what
makes this the trunk. What makes it the trunk is that Wei et al. built the
three obvious rival explanations and knocked each down:

- **Equation only.** Maybe chain of thought just gets the model to write the
  equation. Prompted for the equation alone, GSM8K does not improve.
- **Variable compute only.** Maybe it just buys more forward passes. Prompted
  to emit a row of dots as long as the needed equation, performance is at
  baseline.
- **Chain of thought after the answer.** Maybe it activates relevant
  pretraining knowledge. Placed after the answer, performance is at baseline.

A paper that reports a large effect is common. A paper that reports a large
effect and has already built the three experiments a sceptic would ask for is
what a trunk should be.

## Two things that look like a contradiction and are not

The dots ablation says extra tokens buy nothing. `THEORY-038`, filed an hour
ago, says chain of thought gives a shallow decoder extra computation space
and that is a provable benefit. A fast reading has these opposed.

They are not. The theoretical benefit comes from writing intermediate
*results* into the context and reading them back on a later pass. A row of
dots holds no results. One says the room exists; the other says the room only
helps if you put something in it. Written into the reading note because I
nearly filed it as a tension.

## The emergence claim, against a practice filed the same night

Wei et al. report the effect as emergent at roughly 100B parameters. An hour
earlier I filed `SOTA-278` — rule out the evaluation before reporting a
limit — and the record already held `SOTA-200`, check whether an emergent
capability is a metric artefact.

So: this paper's emergence is measured as exact-match accuracy over
multi-step answers. BIG-bench (`LIT-077`), which is `SOTA-200`'s source, says
tasks showing breakthrough behaviour "often involve multiple steps or
components, or brittle metrics". That is a description of this measurement.

The honest reading is narrow and I want it on the record: the **gains** at
540B are enormous and survive three ablations, and nothing about the
metric touches them. The **shape of the curve** — the discontinuity, the
claim that something appears at a threshold — is the shape `SOTA-200` says
to distrust, and nobody has re-run it against a continuous score. Both go in
the practice's conditions.

Schaeffer et al., "Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a
Mirage?" (`ARXIV-2304.15004`), argues this case directly and the record does
not hold it. Named as the obvious next filing.

I also caught myself misattributing that argument to `LIT-085` while writing
the note — `LIT-085` is the grokking progress-measures paper, and the
brittle-metric line is BIG-bench's. Corrected before the commit, and worth
recording because it is the second time this week prose has cited a document
from memory of what it says rather than from reading it.

## Two things broken, found on the way

**A dangling temp code on `main`.** `SOTA-277`, filed in the previous
contribution, cited the temporary code that had already become `SOTA-275` one
contribution earlier. Concretization rewrites a contribution's own codes; it
did not rewrite this cross-reference to an already-concretized one from an
earlier branch. The result was a link resolving to nothing and an `inactive-ok`
directive excusing a document that did not exist, which the lint reported as
"annotation names unknown document(s) … so it excuses nothing". Repointed to
`SOTA-275`.

The general shape: **a branch cut from `main` between a merge and its
concretization commit carries stale temp codes**, and the next contribution
inherits them. I hit this twice tonight — once as this bug, once when my own
new files referenced codes that had concretized while I was writing them. Both
were caught by the lint. Worth an issue.

**`LIT-035` did not say what it was about.** The unbound-lineage report
flagged `LIT-035 ↔ LIT-467` as joined by nothing: GPT-3 tagged
`model-architecture`, chain-of-thought tagged `adaptation-and-tuning`.

The tempting fix is to add a tag until the edge binds, which is exactly what
`ADR-035` forbids. So the question is whether the tag is *true*, and here it
plainly is: the paper is called "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners", its
central claim is reaching a new task from in-context examples with no
gradient update, and `SOTA-038` — derived from it — already carried
`adaptation-and-tuning`. The paper was the one document in the chain not
saying what it was about.

Added, with the reasoning in the history note including the fact that the
report is what surfaced it, so a later reader can audit the motivation rather
than take my word for it. If the tag had been false, the right move would
have been to drop the edge.

## One unbound line, left unbound

`SOTA-036 → 037 → 038 → SOTA-279` now has no tag common to every
member. The first two are `model-architecture`, the last is
`adaptation-and-tuning`.

That is not a missing word. It is the line genuinely changing subject: from
what a decoder-only model does at scale, to how you prompt one. Adding
`model-architecture` to a prompting practice to close the report would be
asserting something false to make a number go to zero — and the report's own
text says lines are the weaker of the two signals and should be read whole
before acting on. Read, and left.

Every previous contribution this week closed at 0/0. This one closes at 0
relations and 1 line, deliberately.
