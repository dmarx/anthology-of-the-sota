**The first batch of [#56](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/56) filed four notes and zero practices, and that ratio is
the finding.** Three of the four are strong results — one won a
third-party competitive benchmark, one reports 500× less communication at
matched quality, one overturns an assumption the field held about parameter
count. None of them became a recommendation, and the reason was different
each time. Writing the three reasons down beside each other makes a rule
visible that none of them shows alone.

## The three refusals

**Schedule-Free AdamW (2405.15682)** — the strongest outside evidence in the
record's schedule material, and it does not transfer. It won the MLCommons
2024 AlgoPerf Self-Tuning track, which is a fixed-rules third-party
benchmark rather than a self-reported sweep. But AlgoPerf's workloads top
out far below the scale at which [LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md) and [LIT-145](../../record/literature.d/LIT-145.md) disagree about
schedules at all. A win under fixed rules at small scale is evidence about
robustness; the record's open question is about 2.8T. *Refused for scale
mismatch with the disagreement it would settle.*

**DiLoCo (2311.08105)** — 8 workers on C4, and the motivation is about
training across data centres. The gap between the result's scale and the
argument's scale is the paper's own. *Refused because the evidence is a
demonstration, not the thing demonstrated.*

**ES at Scale (2509.24372)** — two tasks at 8B and below, and no frontier
deployment. The paper does not claim it should replace RL in a production
recipe. *Refused because the claim a practice would make is not the claim
the paper makes.*

## The rule underneath

A practice in this record says *do this*. It therefore needs two things a
note does not: that the thing works, and that somebody at the relevant scale
is doing it. The three refusals are three ways of failing the second, and
only the second — every one of these papers is good evidence.

That is not a high bar being applied grudgingly. It is what keeps the
practices readable as advice rather than as a reading list, and it is why
`status: Active` on a note and silence in `practices.d/` are a coherent pair
rather than an oversight ([ADR-002](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-002.md)).

**What makes the refusal cheap is writing down what would reverse it.** Each
note ends with the condition — a frontier report training under Schedule-Free;
a schedule comparison with it as a tuned arm; a production recipe that runs
ES. A refusal with a stated condition is a standing query. A refusal without
one is a judgement the next person has to re-derive from scratch, and will
probably re-derive differently.

## A smaller thing, on the new relation

Streaming DiLoCo is the first `corrects:` edge filed on new material rather
than backfilled. It passed [ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md)'s test the easy way: the defect it names
in its parent — DiLoCo left *peak* bandwidth untouched, because every
parameter still crosses every link at each sync — is in its own abstract. The
test is doing what it was meant to do, which is to be answerable from the
child's own text rather than from a curator's reading of both.
