The instruction was to take `1912.09818`, which the previous unit had named as
the cluster's highest-value unread paper and as "the paper that argues" why
guided backprop survives weight randomization.

**The second half of that was wrong, and reading the paper is what found it.**

## The correction

Sixt, Granz and Landgraf measure guided backprop and then hand its explanation
away, in a sentence they did not bury:

> As a ReLU operation is applied to the gradient, the backpropagation is no
> longer a linear function. The ReLU also results in a **different failure** than
> before. (Nie et al. 2018) provides a theoretical analysis for GuidedBP. Our
> results align with them.

Their Theorem 1 is about products of **non-negative matrices**, which requires
the backward pass to be a linear map. Guided backprop's ReLU on the gradient
breaks that, so the theorem does not reach it. The paper that reaches it is Nie,
Zhang and Patel (2018), `1805.07039`, unheld — and that id was resolved against
arXiv rather than recalled, which is the habit the last two units have needed.

**How the error was made, exactly.** `LIT-725` says: "Guided BackProp and Guided
GradCAM, which modify the gradients during back-propagation, do not pass
randomization tests for different reasons (Sixt et al. 2020)." I read that
citation as attributing the *argument* to Sixt. It attributes the *observation*.

**A citation tells you who reported something, not who explained it.** That is
the generalisable form, and it is narrower and more useful than "read the paper".
The sentence in `LIT-725` is accurate. What it supports is "Sixt et al. observed
this", and I wrote "Sixt et al. argue this" into a `Proposed` practice and a
`LIT` note within the same hour. Third instance this week of a claim about a
paper's contents written from its citation context: the precision/recall feature
space twice, and this.

Both documents are corrected in the same contribution that found the error,
which is the only part of this worth being satisfied about.

## What the paper does supply, and it is a real theory

Theorem 1, stated plainly: the `z⁺`-rule backpropagates relevance through a
chain of non-negative matrices, `C_k = ∏ Z⁺_l`, and a product of non-negative
matrices converges to a **rank-1** matrix. Write the converged chain `C = c γᵀ`.
Then for any relevance vector `v`,

    C v = c γᵀ v = λ c

The direction is `c` no matter what `v` is. The output relevance — the thing that
says *which class* — survives only as a scalar, so it can rescale the map and, if
negative, invert it, and do nothing else.

**That collapses two phenomena the record held separately into one.** The same
map for "cat" and for "dog", and the map not changing when the last layers are
randomized, are the same fact viewed from two sides. Nothing in the record had
said that.

The proof is geometric and worth keeping: the first matrix's columns lie in the
positive quadrant, every further multiplication takes non-negative combinations
of columns already in that cone, and the cone shrinks until it is one direction.
Their simulations find it exponential.

## Why it is filed `Active`, where three of the last five theories were declined

Because it clears the bar this journal has been applying, and clears it in both
directions at once.

- **A published exception the account predicts.** DeepLIFT is the one tested
  modified-BP method that does not converge, and the reason is the one the
  account requires: its linear-layer rule keeps and intermixes positive and
  negative contributions, so the chain is not non-negative.
- **An exception built to order.** The authors then construct **DeepLIFT
  Ablation**, removing the intermixing so `W⁺` drives the positive rule and `W⁻`
  the negative. The chains decouple, both become non-negative — a product of two
  non-positive matrices is non-negative — and "as predicted by the theory, it
  converges."

That second arm is the strongest form of the standard: not an arm the mechanism
says must fail among things that already existed, but **a thing made to fail, and
it failed as specified.** Autoguidance cleared the weaker form with its
mismatched-degradation control; Swin did not clear it at all. This is the first
`Active` theory of the run.

## The structural gain

The cluster had one account for a verdict table with three kinds of failure in
it. Now:

| family | mechanism | held |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IG, InputXGradient, DeepLIFT, Gradient SHAP | the model-independent input multiplier | `THEORY-113` |
| DTD, LRP-α1β0, Excitation BP, PatternAttribution | rank-1 convergence | new |
| Guided Backprop, Deconv, RectGrad | a third mechanism | no — `1805.07039` |

Two things this made visible that were not:

**The new paper supports `THEORY-113` from outside its own subject.** Methods
that "rely on the gradient directly (Smilkov et al. 2017; Sundararajan et al.
2017) ... [do] not converge" — so Integrated Gradients' randomization behaviour is
*not* the rank-1 mechanism, which is what leaves the multiplier as the
explanation. A theory can be strengthened by a paper that never mentions it, by
excluding the alternative.

**DeepLIFT sits in the first row and outside the second, and it is not a
contradiction.** It carries an input multiplier and it keeps negative
contributions, so it has one of the two failure modes and escapes the other.
Two properties of one method; the temptation is to read the two accounts as
disagreeing about it.

## And the intuitive story is refuted, by name

> Other than argued in (Gu et al. 2018), the class insensitivity is **not**
> caused by missing ReLU masks and Pooling switches.

That is the inference the previous unit declined to draw from Springenberg's
"the bottom-up signal ... substitutes the switches". Declining it was right, and
now it is better than right: somebody published that story and this paper
refutes it. `LIT-727` v2 records that the switches account is refuted rather than
merely unasserted.

**The general lesson about restraint is not "be cautious".** It is that the
tempting inference and the published-and-wrong inference are often the same one,
so a note that records *why* it stopped short leaves a place to attach the
refutation when it arrives.

## Residue

`1805.07039` is now the cluster's one remaining mechanism gap and the thing that
would let `SOTA-430`'s headline failure be explained rather than only measured.

Four of the fourteen `#290` reversals remain — Grad-CAM, InputXGradient,
plain-gradient saliency, SmoothGrad — all referent gaps, all ranking below it.
`1706.03825` (SmoothGrad) has risen slightly: it is now named by two held
theories, as the kernel-smoothing meta-algorithm in `LIT-725`'s Theorem 4.1 and
as a non-converging gradient method here.

And `SOTA-430`'s headline example rests on a figure that its own authors amended
and that this paper reports differently, having confirmed the bug. The verdict
survives in both papers. The figure does not, and the practice should probably
stop leaning on the visual claim — a v6 that is *not* in this unit, because the
right fix is to state the verdict from the SSIM curves rather than to add a
sixth version in one day.
