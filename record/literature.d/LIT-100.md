# LIT-100: GQA: Training Generalized Multi-Query Transformer Models from Multi-Head Checkpoints

Ainslie et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2305.13245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13245)

## Key takeaways

- Two contributions, and the record only holds one of them
- **Grouped-query attention**: an *intermediate* number of key-value heads —
  more than one, fewer than the query heads — generalising MQA, which is the
  `g = 1` endpoint, and multi-head, which is `g = h`
- **Uptraining**: convert an existing multi-head checkpoint to fewer key-value
  heads for **5% of original pretraining compute**, by mean-pooling the key
  and value projections within each group and continuing briefly
- **Uptrained GQA reaches quality close to multi-head at speed comparable to
  MQA** — the result the record's models are built on
- The motivation is explicitly that MQA costs quality *and* "it may not be
  desirable to train a separate model just for faster inference"

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-109](../practices.d/SOTA-109.md).

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-tmpx1otw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpx1otw.md). The practice is
**confirmed**.

**Uptraining is not represented in the practice registry**, and is arguably
the more portable of the two ideas: an inference-side architectural property
need not be committed to before pretraining, if a cheap conversion exists.
The record holds several pretraining-time commitments where the same question
could be asked and has not been.
