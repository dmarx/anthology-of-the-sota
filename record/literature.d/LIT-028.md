# LIT-028: Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models

Kaplan et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2001.08361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)

## Key takeaways

- **Loss is a power law** in model size, dataset size and compute, over more
  than seven orders of magnitude: `α_N ≈ 0.076`, `α_D ≈ 0.095`,
  `α_C ≈ 0.050`
- **Shape barely matters** — width, depth, heads have "minimal effects within
  a wide range" at fixed non-embedding parameter count. True of *upstream
  loss*; `LIT-052` contradicts it downstream
- **Larger models are significantly more sample-efficient**, so
  compute-optimal training means very large models stopped well short of
  convergence, with data growing only as `D ∼ C^0.27`
- **Compute-optimal allocation** (Eq. 1.7): `N ∝ C^(α_C/α_N)`,
  `B ∝ C^(α_C/α_B)`, `S ∝ C^(α_C/α_S)` — the batch exponent being
  `0.050/0.21 = 0.24`
- **Critical batch size** `B_crit(L) = B*/L^(1/α_B)`, `B* ≈ 2×10⁸` tokens,
  depends on the **loss, not directly on model size**, and roughly doubles
  per 13% decrease in loss
- **On learning rates**, and this is where the record went wrong: the *shape*
  of the schedule is "mostly irrelevant" given warmup and a final decay —
  measured on a **3M-parameter model** — but **"larger models require a
  smaller learning rate to prevent divergence"**, implemented as
  `LR(N) ≈ 0.003239 − 0.0001395·log(N)`, which "breaks down for `N > 10¹⁰`"
- The schedule was **held fixed across runs of different length**, which is
  the methodological choice Chinchilla later identified as the source of the
  wrong allocation exponent

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-040](../practices.d/SOTA-040.md) and, until 2026-09-20, of [SOTA-097](../practices.d/SOTA-097.md).

**The batch-size exponent has been corrected.** Equation 1.7 gives
`B ∝ C^0.24`, and two groups measuring off the Chinchilla line —
[LIT-443](LIT-443.md) and [LIT-445](LIT-445.md) — find that optimal and critical batch
size scale in the token budget `D`, not in compute. The fit is not an
arithmetic error: along that line `C = 6ND` moves with `D`, so a power law in
either describes the data, and equation 1.7 measured a projection. This is
the same failure mode Chinchilla found in the allocation exponent, in a
different coordinate, and it is why the paper stays `Active` — the method
generalized past its own fits in a way its authors could not have checked
with runs only on the compute-optimal line.

Read in full for [#114](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/114); the reading is [NOTE-017](../notes.d/NOTE-017.md).

[SOTA-041](../practices.d/SOTA-041.md) — "lr tuning less important for larger models" — is `Rejected`. It
