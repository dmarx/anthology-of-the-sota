**Three of those counts needed correcting for substring noise**, which is now
the fourth unit in a row where a grep lied. `1311.2901` first returned six, and
three were `occlusion` in the physical sense — a robot arm, a viewpoint, object
permanence. `1412.6806` first returned eight, and one was "all convolutional"
inside "a small convolutional RNN". `1312.6034` kept all five, but one is
`NOTE-356` using gradient saliency to find knot invariants, which is real and
outside this cluster entirely. The pattern is stable enough to state: **a
method name that is also an ordinary English word needs the author's surname in
the pattern.**

The six confirmations are worth their own sentence, because [#290](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/290) declined them
for a reason that was procedural and they now decline for a reason that is
measured. Four of the six are layer- or neuron-level attribution — conductance,
internal influence — which is a cluster the record has not opened and which
nothing in it depends on. Two are not attribution at all: L₀ regularization and
stochastic gates are sparsity methods captum reuses for feature selection. That
is a **catalogue-shape artefact**: captum's reference list is one line per
public class, so a regularizer repurposed as a gate arrives looking like an
interpretability paper.

## The one filed, and why it was first

`1901.09392` is not another method in the verdict table. It is the **instrument
two held documents now report numbers in**. `LIT-724` scores its whole
randomization study with infidelity and max-sensitivity, including the
2.84 → 1.27 × 10⁷ climb that `SOTA-430` carries as a Condition, and neither
document could say what either number was. Exactly the `LIT-703` shape from two
days ago: a metric carrying a quantitative claim with its defining paper
unheld.

Two properties change how the numbers already in the record read:

**Max-sensitivity is minimised by a constant explanation.** The paper says it
outright — the optimum "is simply a constant explanation that just outputs a
(potentially nonsensical) constant value for all possible test inputs." So
`LIT-724`'s "max-sensitivity remains almost unchanged" is a weaker signal than
it reads as, and `LIT-724` v2 now says so.

**Infidelity is relative to a perturbation distribution**, and Propositions
2.2–2.5 show several published methods are each its optimum for some choice —
confirmed by construction, SHAP winning under SHAP's own perturbation. `LIT-724`
happens to have done this right: it names its perturbations. That is the whole
of `SOTA-tmp34edx`, and it costs a sentence.

## The finding worth keeping

`SOTA-430`'s `promote_when` asks for the randomization tests run "where the true
dependence of the output on the input is KNOWN by construction — a synthetic
task or a planted feature".

**This paper builds that setting.** Bird-versus-frog images with a caption in
one half; at noise `p = 0` the model provably uses the image (image-only
accuracy 0.9, caption-only 0.5) and at `p = 0.6` it provably uses the caption
(0.98 against 0.5), both above 0.95 overall. Ground truth by construction, in
the sense the `promote_when` means.

**And it runs the randomization test.** Table 2, ResNet-50, final layer
randomized.

They are different experiments. The planted feature validates *infidelity*
against human judgement; the randomization test runs on ImageNet with no ground
truth. Nobody crossed them.

So the bar is not met, and the reason is now specific rather than "nobody has
done it": **the experiment a `promote_when` asks for can already exist, as two
tables in one paper that were never joined.** A `promote_when` searches the
literature for a paper; sometimes what it should search for is a pair of
sections. The v3 amendment says so, so the next reader looks for the join and
not for a new paper.

## What the re-read also did for the practice

Step 3 of `SOTA-430` — compare signed rank correlation against absolute or
perceptual similarity, because "a single metric can hand you either verdict" —
now has **three groups on three model families**: Adebayo on Inception and
MNIST, Kokhlikyan on Inception and BERT (SSIM against Spearman), and this paper
on ResNet-50 (signed 0.10–0.18 against absolute 0.57–0.62, same explanations,
same randomization).

That is the third consecutive unit where the load-bearing part of a document
turned out to be its caveat rather than its headline. The practice's
recommendation is still `Proposed` on one group; its warning is corroborated by
three. **A caveat can outrun the claim it was attached to, and nothing in the
schema notices** — `consensus` is a field on the document, not on the sentence.

## Deliberately not in this unit

The seven other reversals. They are referent gaps rather than measurement
gaps: `SOTA-430` states each method's verdict correctly, quoting `LIT-713`, so
filing Guided Backprop or Grad-CAM adds a document to point at rather than
correcting anything. Ranked and posted to `#290`; the top of that list is
`1412.6806`, `SOTA-430`'s headline failure case — and a paper whose title,
*Striving for Simplicity: The All Convolutional Net*, is about an architecture.
Guided backprop arrives in its abstract's last sentence, as "a new variant of
the 'deconvolution approach' for visualizing features learned by CNNs", offered
to analyse the network the paper is actually proposing. Its own antecedent,
Zeiler and Fergus's deconvolution (`1311.2901`), is a second reversal on this
list, so that pair should be one unit.
