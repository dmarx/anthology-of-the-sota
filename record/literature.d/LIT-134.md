# LIT-134: Attention Residuals

Kimi Team, Moonshot AI (2026) — [ARXIV-2603.15031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15031)

## Key takeaways

- A residual stream accumulates every earlier layer's output with fixed,
  uniform weight. Attention Residuals let each layer choose: a single
  learned pseudo-query per layer produces content-dependent weights over
  the preceding layers' representations, adding only O(d) parameters per
  layer. The paper frames it as the depth-wise dual of attention over time.
- Selective retrieval of earlier representations mitigates dilution and
  the unbounded growth of the hidden state along depth.
- Block AttnRes partitions layers into compressed blocks so the cross-layer
  attention costs O(Nd) rather than O(Ld) in memory and communication, which
  is what makes it usable at production depth.
- Validated at 48B parameters on 1.4T tokens: a 1.25× compute advantage on
  scaling laws, inference latency overhead below 2%, and the largest gains
  on multi-step reasoning (GPQA-Diamond +7.5, Minerva Math +3.6) and code
  (HumanEval +3.1).

## Standing in the anthology

<!-- inactive-ok: SOTA-133 — the Proposed practice this paper is the source of -->
The source of [SOTA-133](../practices.d/SOTA-133.md), which stays *Proposed*: one group, one paper, and
one production adoption ([LIT-131](LIT-131.md)) so far. The other replacement for the
residual in the record is [LIT-140](LIT-140.md), which DeepSeek-V4 ships. The two have now
been compared once, by a third party running both against its own design
([LIT-145](LIT-145.md)): Full AttnRes and Gated Residual land level at 1.762 training
loss, and mHC and Gated Residual are called comparable at that scale. The
block-summarised variant — the one that makes AttnRes affordable at depth —
gives up 0.008 to 0.011 of that, which is the price of the approximation
this paper's design avoids by dropping the mixing matrix instead.
