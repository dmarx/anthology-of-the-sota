Prompted by a correction: where a paper carries a claim the topic set cannot
express, that is a shortcoming of the topic set, not evidence the literature
is irrelevant.

I had been doing the opposite. The generative and vision pass filed eight
notes as carrying no practice, and four of those declined the paper because
"the seven topics cannot express" its claim — which reads as a verdict on the
work and is exactly backwards. Corrected in that branch; the principle it
violated is now written down as its own document, because it is more general
than one pass.

**The argument for the principle is that the two readings are not
symmetric.** When a document arrives with a real claim and no category, either
the claim is out of scope or the vocabulary is short a category. The first is
cheaper, requires no work, and is self-confirming: decline the document and
the vocabulary still looks complete, because the evidence that would have
shown otherwise has just been turned away. So the default has to be the
second, and the burden sits on an argument about the kind of claim rather than
on a shrug at the absence of a slot.

**And the evidence was already in the record.** Two counts, neither
speculative:

- Seven practices about serving, filed under four different topics —
  model-stability, model-architecture, attention-techniques and
  systems-optimization. Running statistics at inference, multi-query
  attention, PagedAttention, continuous batching, prefill/decode overlap, and
  two on quantization.
- Six about adaptation, across four more. In-context learning, tiny-model
  pretraining, RoPE rescaling, evolution-strategies fine-tuning, low-rank
  adaptation, and the preference-training pair.

A subject scattered across four ill-fitting categories is a category that
exists in the corpus and not in the list. That is a checkable test, and it is
what the decision rests on rather than my judgement about what deserves a
page.

**The words come from the reading list.** The LIT scheme has had
`inference-optimization` and `adaptation-and-tuning` since [ADR-003](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-003.md). Where both
vocabularies name the same kind of claim they should use the same word, and
what stays LIT-only is `generative-modeling` and `vision-and-graphics` —
domains rather than kinds of claim, and [ADR-020](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-020.md)'s argument against domain
topics is untouched.

**What was declined, and why that matters here.** `analysis-and-evaluation`
is the third kind-of-claim word in the LIT extras and it is not being added,
because no practice in the record wants it. Adding a category to hold zero
documents is [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8)'s corollary run backwards, and a principle that says
"expand the vocabulary" would be worthless if it could not also say when not
to. The same holds for a representation or encoding topic: [LIT-064](../../record/literature.d/LIT-064.md), [LIT-086](../../record/literature.d/LIT-086.md)
and [LIT-111](../../record/literature.d/LIT-111.md) all want one, and the record's encoding practices are scattered
the way serving was — but nothing has been *filed* on those yet, so the
retagging evidence does not exist. File one and see where it wants to live.

**A pattern worth naming.** This is the third decision about this vocabulary
in one day: [ADR-003](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-003.md) set it, [ADR-020](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-020.md) stopped it being read as a domain
boundary, this one widens it. That is not coincidence. Seven categories
transcribed from a 2024 document are now governing 185 practices across a
subject that has widened twice since. Expect a fourth, and treat the next
homeless claim as data rather than as a nuisance.
