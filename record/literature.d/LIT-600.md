# LIT-600: Automatic Evaluation of Topic Coherence

Newman, Lau, Grieser and Baldwin, NICTA, UC Irvine and Melbourne (NAACL-HLT
2010) — <https://aclanthology.org/N10-1012/>

## Key takeaways

- **The task** (§1, §5): judge a topic, shown as its top ten words, for
  coherence. The question given to annotators was whether you could imagine
  using it as a subject heading in a search interface. Topics came from LDA
  on 55,000 Gigaword news articles (200 topics) and 12,000 Internet Archive
  books (400 topics). 237 were sampled, and 9 annotators rated each on a
  3-point scale
- **The candidates** (§4): nine WordNet similarity measures, four Wikipedia
  measures (link-based MIW, category-overlap RACO, document cosine DOCSIM,
  and term co-occurrence PMI), and two Google measures (title matches and
  log hit counts). Pairwise scores over the 45 word pairs are combined by
  mean or median
- **PMI** (§4.2): log p(wᵢ, wⱼ) / p(wᵢ)p(wⱼ), with co-occurrence counted in
  a sliding 10-word window over about 1 billion words of English Wikipedia
- **Results** (Tables 2 and 3, Spearman ρ against the human scores):
  - **News:** PMI 0.78 median and 0.77 mean. Google TITLES 0.80. IAA 0.79
    and 0.73
  - **Books:** PMI 0.74 median and 0.77 mean. Google TITLES 0.51. IAA 0.82
    and 0.78
  - **WordNet:** from −0.31 to 0.66, with the best measure different on each
    corpus
- **The authors' reading**: word-pair co-occurrence in a large external
  corpus models topic coherence well, and Wikipedia is the most consistent
  resource

## Standing in the anthology

Filed on request. It `extends` Reading Tea Leaves ([LIT-597](LIT-597.md)), which showed
that topic quality has to be measured with people, and it automates that
measurement. It sources [SOTA-371](../practices.d/SOTA-371.md).

**The comparison to human agreement flatters the method.** The "upper
bound" is the average correlation between one annotator and the mean of the
other eight. The methods are correlated with the mean over annotators, a
less noisy target than any single person. So approaching or passing that
number does not mean matching a human judge. The collections were also
chosen to give topics of "more variable quality", which widens the spread
and inflates rank correlations. And only LDA topics were scored.
