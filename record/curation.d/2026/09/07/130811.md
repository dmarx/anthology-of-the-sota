The last item on [#18](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/18): run mechanically, for the other model reports, the
"which works does this paper lean on to justify its decisions" pass that was
done by hand for [LIT-119](../../record/literature.d/LIT-119.md). Result filed as [#40](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/40). This entry is the method and
the two things worth remembering about it.

## Semantic Scholar was not the way in

[#18](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/18) named `api.semanticscholar.org`, and it is reachable but rate-limits an
unauthenticated caller hard — roughly one request in three succeeded, with
no key available. That turned out not to matter, because the better source
was already local: every one of these reports has an arXiv HTML build, and
the build carries the bibliography as markup. Parsing `li#bib.bibN` out of
it gives titles, arXiv identifiers and — this is the part a citation API
does not have — **the sections each reference is cited from**.

152 entries for K3, 257 for Olmo 3, 125 for Falcon-H1, 103 for DeepSeek-V4.
Subtract the corpus by arXiv id and by normalised title, drop
benchmarks and datasets, and what remains is the gap list.

## The back-references are the whole filter, and two builds do not have them

K3's and V4's builds carry "Cited by: §2.1.1" on each entry, so a reference
can be scored by *where* it is used: a work cited from §2 (Architecture) is
a design justification, and one cited only from §6 (Evaluations) is a
baseline. That is exactly the distinction [#18](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/18) asked for, and it is free.

Falcon-H1's and Olmo 3's builds have none — 0 of 125 and 0 of 257 entries
carry them. The first run reported "0 design-cited references missing" for
both, which read like a clean result and was an artefact of a filter with
nothing to match. Caught by asking why two large bibliographies would have
*exactly* zero, which is the shape a broken filter makes and a real answer
almost never does. Those two were re-run on titles alone and [#40](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/40) says so.

## What the pass is actually good at

Not "find every missing paper" — a title filter cannot tell a method from a
benchmark reliably, and judgement is still required per row. What it is good
at is the **multiply-cited absence**: a work that two independent labs both
reached for, and the record does not have. Six of those, and they are the
top of [#40](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/40)'s list.

The other thing it surfaced is sharper than any single paper. **[SOTA-134](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-134.md)'s
headline benefit is that the gate removes the attention-sink pattern, and
the phrase "attention sink" appears in exactly one file in this record —
[LIT-138](../../record/literature.d/LIT-138.md)'s own summary.** The record recommends removing a phenomenon it has
never described. A reference pass looking for what the corpus leans on found
a hole in what the corpus explains, which is not what it was pointed at.
