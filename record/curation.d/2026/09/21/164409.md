**`DP-010` went to v2 on four more instances ([#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180)).** The principle —
the most citable sentence in a paper is often its least supported one — was
filed yesterday on four papers from one week. Four is a number this record has
opinions about: `DP-009` says counting is the antidote and that two is not
several, and the same standard ought to apply to the record's own principles,
not only to candidate topics.

So the honest thing was to keep counting, and the next four papers read end to
end came off the `#180` revisit ranking for reasons having nothing to do with
this principle — Mixture-of-Transformers, SimpleStories, ParScale, the
Change.org natural experiment. All four have the shape. That is what makes it
a pattern rather than a sample selected to be one, and the v2 body says so
explicitly, because a second batch that was *chosen* for the property would
have proved nothing.

**The fourth one broke the principle open.** `LIT-487`'s abstract reports the
null: the AI tool changed petition text and did not change petition outcomes.
Correct, and not the finding. The finding is that the text features — chosen
*because* they predicted petition success — stopped predicting it afterwards,
and that appears once in an appendix and once in a discussion sentence. It was
not preregistered. Everything that makes the paper general rather than a fact
about petitions rests on it.

That is the same failure and the opposite direction, and v1 could not have
caught it. v1 says: find the hedge, distrust the striking claim. Applied to
`LIT-487` that advice files the abstract's null, correctly labelled, and
loses the sentence that mattered. So v2 adds the second instruction — **look
for the underreported sentence too** — and restates the diagnosis:
compression is not selective for evidence *in either direction*. It promotes
the memorable unsupported sentence and it demotes the unmemorable load-bearing
one, and both are the same fact about what survives a single reading.

Worth saying plainly, since a principle with eight cited instances can start
to read like an accusation: **in all eight, the authors were honest.** The
hedge is in the paper, usually within a page of the claim it qualifies. nGPT's
step-time footnote is on the page with the figure. The coverage paper writes
"somewhat speculatively" itself. SimpleStories names the exact missing
ablation in Limitations. ParScale says "why the diversity is related to `P`
remains an open question". The Change.org authors label their own mechanism a
set of untested hypotheses. Nobody is hiding anything. The hedge just doesn't
travel, and a record that files summaries is a compression step like any
other — which is the reason this is a design principle here and not a
complaint about the literature.

**And one thing deliberately not written.** Across the same four units an
unplanned convergence appeared: `SOTA-172` and `SOTA-295` describe generated
corpora collapsing toward themselves via n-gram over-concentration, and
`SOTA-298`/`THEORY-051` describe the same collapse via embedding similarity in
text that people wrote holding a tool. Two causal routes, one statistical
destination.

It would make a good `THEORY` and I did not file one. It would be sourced to
papers none of which claim it, resting on two metrics that can dissociate —
paraphrase preserves n-gram structure and destroys embedding similarity, or
the reverse — which nobody has run on either corpus. A memorable sentence with
no isolated evidence under it is the exact failure this session spent four
units documenting in other people's work, and the fact that it would be *our*
memorable sentence is not a defence. Filed as `#234` with the measurement that
would settle it, so the next pass has the question and the record has not
asserted the answer.
