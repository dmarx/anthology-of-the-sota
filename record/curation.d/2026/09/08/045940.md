title: 'The tag is not the scope boundary — and I applied that error twice in one hour'
created: '2026-09-08T04:59:40'
tags: [curation]
---

Second pass at [#85](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/85). The issue proposed that the inherited notes tagged
`generative-modeling`, `vision-and-graphics`, `adaptation-and-tuning`,
`inference-optimization` and `analysis-and-evaluation` are unfiled because
`practices.d/tags.yaml` declines those subjects — [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8), a stated scope
decision rather than a backlog. About 36 notes, expected yield zero, one
paragraph of confirmation.

That premise is wrong, and I got it wrong twice on the way to saying so.

**The issue's error.** The record already holds twelve
post-training and quantization practices — [SOTA-126](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-126.md) on DPO epochs, [SOTA-129](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-129.md)
on the pretrain/SFT/RLVR staging, [SOTA-154](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-154.md) on evolution strategies, [SOTA-160](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-160.md)
and [SOTA-163](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-163.md) on quantization — filed under `training-optimization` and
`systems-optimization`. Every one of them sources a *post-migration* note. So
the anthology has not declined to hold fine-tuning, preference training or
quantization. It holds them, under the seven topics.

The `LIT` scheme has twelve topics and the `SOTA` scheme has seven, and the
extra five are not a scope boundary. **The two vocabularies overlap; they do
not nest.** What happened is that post-migration papers on these subjects were
read and filed, and pre-migration ones got a tag outside the practice seven
and were never looked at again. The tag concealed them rather than excluding
them. LoRA — one of the most widely adopted training practices in the corpus
— sat unfiled for a year behind `adaptation-and-tuning`.

**The second error was mine, and it was the opposite one.** Having written
that paragraph, I asserted that the 24 notes tagged `generative-modeling` and
`vision-and-graphics` were "out of scope by construction" — from their tags,
without opening one. Challenged on it, I accepted the challenge, rewrote the
reasoning to say that domain never decides scope, and pushed that.

Then I read the record. [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) says, in as many words, that *the
recommendations are scoped to language-model training by construction* — that
a vision paper can be a note and cannot be a recommendation, "not on
judgement, not on evidence, but because there is no category for it to take."
[ADR-003](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-003.md) made that split deliberately and rejected the single-vocabulary
alternative precisely because it "puts a diffusion sampler paper under
`training-optimization` or `model-architecture` and makes both pages worse."

So the conclusion was right and the reasoning was not, and then I replaced
correct reasoning with wrong reasoning because I did not check. Which is the
generator this whole audit exists to catch: a claim that sounds right,
asserted without opening the source. Three of the migration's errors were
that. So was this.

**What actually survives.** [ADR-003](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-003.md) justifies all five extra `LIT` topics
with one sentence — "the reading list covers ground the practice registry
does not." That is true of `generative-modeling` and `vision-and-graphics`,
and it is *false* of `adaptation-and-tuning` and `inference-optimization`: the
record holds fine-tuning, preference-training and quantization practices under
the seven, several of each. [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) names "vision, generative and theory work"
and never claims otherwise. **Five topics were bundled under a reason that
holds for three of them**, and the bundling is what made LoRA look declined
rather than overlooked.

**The corrected surface.** Of 71 inherited notes that source no practice: 18
carry a written reason, 13 are retired, and 53 are bare. Of the 53, 23 are the
generative and vision corpus, which [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8) settles — they need a written
reason each or, better, one statement in the index, not a reading. The other
30 need reading.

**Two errors, opposite directions, one cause.** The issue over-applied a scope
decision to topics it never covered. I then under-applied it to the topics it
does cover. Neither of us read [ADR-003](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-003.md). A decision that is written down and
not read is, operationally, a decision that was not made — which is [DP-008](../design-principles.md#dp-8)'s
own point about vocabularies, turned on the document that states it.

Filed two practices as the demonstration that the concealment in those two
topics is real rather than theoretical, and left the rest for a pass that
reads them.
