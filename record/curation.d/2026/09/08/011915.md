**The precision cluster produced four practices, and the thing worth writing
down is what these four papers were already doing in the record before
today.** Three of them are what the frontier reports cite for decisions the
record already records:

- Kimi K3 keeps the attention output in FP32 and redesigns its kernel to
  absorb the doubled footprint. [LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md) says so. [LIT-198](../../record/literature.d/LIT-198.md) is the paper it
  is citing, and without it the choice reads as caution rather than as a fix
  for a named failure mode.
- DeepSeek-V4 and Kimi K3 both quantize to "FP4" and "MXFP4". [LIT-197](../../record/literature.d/LIT-197.md) is
  what those words mean — a 4-bit element in a 32-element block with a shared
  power-of-two scale, not a bare 4-bit float.
- [SOTA-034](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-034.md) is `contested` on the strength of [LIT-200](../../record/literature.d/LIT-200.md) and K3's SiTU-GLU.

So the record was already *using* these papers to explain its own contents.
What it could not do was state what a reader should therefore do — because
the statement had nowhere to live except inside a note about a different
paper.

## A fourth category, or the second half of the third

The previous entry named three ways a practice goes missing: refused for
adoption, too settled to discuss, and a trunk nobody publishes. This cluster
adds a variant of the last one, and it is worth distinguishing.

**A paper cited to explain somebody else's decision is not the same as a
paper nobody read.** These four were read closely — the notes are detailed,
they are cross-referenced from the model reports, and they carry the exact
sentences the practices now carry. The failure is one step later: the reading
was filed as *exegesis of a model report* rather than as advice, because the
prompt to write it was "why does K3 do this" rather than "what should I do".

The tell is a note whose Standing section explains another document's
decision. That is a different query again from the previous three, and it
would have found all four of these.

## And the trunk pattern showed up a third time

[LIT-200](../../record/literature.d/LIT-200.md)'s own Standing section had already done the analysis: "Neither
cites a problem with SwiGLU's quality; both object to its **range**." Two
groups, two remedies, no comparison between them — so the record can state
the agreement without picking a function, and could not before, because the
only nodes available were `SOTA-034` (SwiGLU, contested) and nothing.

Three trunks in one day, found by three different methods, in three unrelated
subjects. That is no longer a coincidence about hyper-connections; it is what
this record's filing habit does. A branch has an arrival event and gets
filed. Agreement has no arrival event and does not.

## The acknowledgement mechanic, again, in the other direction

This batch wrote **four** directives vouching for documents that turned out to
be `Active` — no acknowledgement needed — and the lint rejected all four with
"annotation no longer applies". Yesterday's instance was the same mistake;
today's is at four times the rate, because I was writing acknowledgements
defensively rather than from the report.

The fix is mechanical and worth stating: **write the acknowledgement after
running the lint, from the list of findings, not while writing the prose.**
The report says exactly which citations need one. Guessing produces both
kinds of error — a missing vouch and a vouch for nothing — and only the first
is the one anybody worries about.
