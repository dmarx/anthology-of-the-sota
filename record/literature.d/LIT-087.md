# LIT-087: Data Selection for Language Models via Importance Resampling

Xie et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2302.03169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03169)

## Key takeaways

- Frames pretraining data selection as **matching a target distribution you can
  sample from** — not as scoring data for quality — and solves it with classical
  **importance resampling** made tractable by working in a reduced feature space:
  unigrams and bigrams hashed into `m = 10,000` buckets
- **The metric is the bigger result.** *KL reduction* — how much a selection
  reduces distance to the target versus random selection, measured in that
  hashed space — **correlates strongly with downstream performance across
  selection methods, including ones that use no n-grams at all, such as manual
  curation.** A model-free proxy that ranks data selections before any training
- The crude features are deliberate: the space is not claimed to represent text
  well, only to preserve the *ordering* of candidate selections
- **+0.9% over heuristic classification**, comparable to top-`k` heuristic
  classification — which the authors note may suit *domain-specific* selection
  where "diversity may be less important than in the general-domain setting"
- Within-domain F1 82.9% vs cross-domain 81.2%: a 1.7-point average cost to
  selecting for a target in a different domain

The reading puts a cheaper instrument beside one the record carries. `SOTA-166`
says to set data proportions by fitting a mixing law on small runs rather than
by argument — right, and it costs a set of training runs. If KL reduction really
ranks selections without training, it is a pre-run filter that makes searching a
mixing law cheaper. Whether the record should carry it is a registry decision:
the evidence is correlational, from 2023, at a scale below where mixing laws are
now fitted.

Of the takeaways replaced, **"data quality assessment" and "quality-aware data
mixing" describe scoring data for quality**, which this paper deliberately does
not do.
