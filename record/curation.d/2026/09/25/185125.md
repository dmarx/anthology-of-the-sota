The instruction was to take Dhariwal & Nichol, deferred out of the previous unit
on purpose. Two things came back that the deferral is what made visible, and one
that has now happened three times in a row.

## The instrument was already here, under a title that excluded the case

`THEORY-109` was filed this morning. Its `promote_when` asked for

> a metric that uses **no** classifier — a human preference study, or a feature
> distance from a self-supervised encoder

which is a reasonable thing to ask for and also a paraphrase of a practice this
record has held since 2026-09-23. `SOTA-337` says that when an ImageNet network
is in the pipeline, FID's ranking is not to be trusted, and it names **CLIP and
SwAV Fréchet distances** as the check. It has a measured case: a fixed StyleGAN2
whose FID fell 5.30 → 1.78 under resampling while CLIP-FD moved 2.76 → 2.64, and
human raters preferring the model FID ranked worse.

So the theory asked for an experiment the record already recommends, with an
instrument the record already names, and said neither. I wrote both documents; the
gap between them is hours.

**Why it was missable, and this is the useful part.** `SOTA-337`'s v1 title read
*"When ImageNet-pretrained networks take part in training a generator…"*. Its
**body** was wider — it said the authors "suspect the same of classifiers used to
curate the data or placed in the sampling loop" — but the title is what a search
returns and what an index shows. Classifier guidance is a classifier in the
sampling loop. The one case the practice most obviously covers was outside the
sentence that names it.

Two mismatches, and only one is wording. "Training" against "sampling" is the
body-versus-title gap. **"Pretrained" is a category error**: a classifier trained
from scratch on ImageNet labels has the same feature space as a downloaded one,
and the hazard [SOTA-337](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-337.md) identifies is the overlap with FID's features, not the
provenance of the weights. Guidance trains its own classifier, so it failed the
title twice over while being the sharpest instance in existence.

v2 widens the title, says provenance is not the point, and adds the case.

**The shape, for next time:** that is two practices in two days whose *scope*
was narrower than their subject — `SOTA-203`, `universal` and silent about
guidance, and `SOTA-337`, titled for training while suspicious of sampling. The
check that found both is the same one: take the practice's own hazard and ask
what the most widely deployed instance of it is. If the title does not cover that
instance, the title is the defect, and running the check costs one question.

## The order of the dispute is backwards from how it was filed

`LIT-693` reports Ho and Salimans raising a good question about their
predecessor: classifier guidance follows an image classifier's gradient, FID and
IS are computed with an image classifier, so the gains might be adversarial
rather than perceptual. Filed this morning as an open question against the
antecedent.

Reading the antecedent inverts the sequence. Its introduction already says the
gradient scale can be raised

> by an order of magnitude without obtaining adversarial examples

with a citation to Szegedy et al. And **the word "adversarial" appears nowhere
else in the paper.** Not in §4, not in the limitations, not in any of the
thirteen appendices. No adversarial-example test, no held-out classifier, no
non-Inception distance. The support is one figure showing that scale 10.0 gives
"much more class-consistent images" on visual inspection.

The nearest thing to a check is Appendix C, and it is aimed at a different worry —
memorization — tested on nearest neighbours **in InceptionV3 feature space**, for
"a handful of samples". If guidance pushes samples toward ImageNet-class modes,
Inception distance cannot tell you whether those samples came from the training
set. The circularity that makes the adversarial question hard is the same
circularity that makes the memorization check weak, and the paper runs into it
twice without naming it once.

So: **asserted away in 2021, raised in 2022, measured by neither.** The record now
holds both papers and no measurement, which is exactly what `Proposed` is for.

**And this is what the deferral bought.** The previous unit kept these two papers
apart on the reasoning that filing a paper with its named antagonist lets the
sourcing of each rest on the other's summary. That reasoning paid: CFG's account
of its predecessor is accurate and fair, and reading it would have produced
"Dhariwal & Nichol did not consider this", which is false. They considered it in
one clause and dismissed it. Only opening the antecedent gets the order right.

## The pattern that has now recurred three times

| note | the sentence | falsified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `LIT-676` v2 | CFG is "the most-referenced unheld paper in this cluster" | hours later, by `LIT-693` |
| `LIT-693` v2 | "The antecedent is still unheld" | hours later, by `LIT-699` |
| `LIT-676` v3 | "What is still unheld is the antecedent" | same day, same unit |

Three units, three stale gap claims, one note twice. The sentences are not
mistakes — two of these three filings happened *because* a note named the gap,
and that is the mechanism working. What is missing is any check that they are
still true.

**They are checkable.** Every one of these is a bare arXiv id in prose next to a
phrase like "unheld" or "does not hold", and whether the record holds that id is
a lookup. The lint already reports codes that resolve to nothing and temporary
codes this record does not mint; "an identifier in prose that this record now
holds as a document" is the same kind of report. No lint rule exists for it, and
this is the third recurrence, so I am no longer treating the pattern as my
vigilance problem.

## Two smaller things worth keeping

**A paper shipped its second-best configuration twice, and said so twice.**
Increased depth improves FID but "takes longer to reach the same performance as a
wider model", so it is dropped. On head width, from a 14.08 baseline, 32 channels
per head gives −1.36 FID and 64 gives −1.03 — and 64 is the recommendation, for
wall-clock time. That is now the fourth literature in which this record has found
the same thing: ALBERT's all-shared, RoBERTa's `full-sentences` and byte-level
BPE, and here. **An `implementations:` line copied from a paper's final
configuration can disagree with the ablation the practice rests on**, and the
tell is always a sentence about wall-clock time next to a worse number.

**One ablated change made things worse, and it is the one the field had adopted.**
Rescaling residual connections by `1/√2`, following Song et al. and both StyleGAN
papers, cost +0.16/+0.25 FID. Kept in the note because a negative result on a
widely copied trick is the rarer half of an ablation table, and because the
practice registry has no entry for it either way.

## Declined, with the reason

`1904.06991`, the precision/recall definitions, is **not** filed here, and the
new practice says so in its Source section rather than implying otherwise. The
2026-09-23 metric-definition sweep looked at it and declined it as "not
load-bearing on anything" — correct at the time, when it was "named twice, both
times as 'the appendix also reports it'". `SOTA-425` is what makes it
load-bearing. **The decline has expired and the sweep's journal entry cannot
record that**, because curation entries are not versioned and nothing points from
a declined id to the document that later needs it. It is the next thing this line
wants.
