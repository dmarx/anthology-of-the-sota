Asked how the record stood on fine-tuning, and specifically whether it had a
zero-init practice and a LoRA practice. It has both, and both were better than
expected. What the check turned up instead was a defect in one of them and a
mistake of mine in the other direction.

## The zero-init practice states the weaker claim

[SOTA-051](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-051.md) is the zero-init practice, and it is a good document. It
already unifies three things the record holds separately: ReZero's
zero-initialised residual scalar, ControlNet's zero convolutions, and the `B`
matrix of LoRA. The argument it makes is sharp — *a small random
initialisation makes the branch probably harmless; an exact zero makes it
provably the identity* — and it draws the line where it matters, at a branch
attached to a model that is already expensively trained.

Its title was **"Initialize final layer weights near zero"**, and the body says
so itself: *"the distinction worth holding is the one this practice's own
title blurs: zero is not 'near zero'."*

A document that names the defect in its own title and leaves it there is worth
stopping on. The cost is not cosmetic. The registry is browsed by title, so a
reader looking for the exact-identity rule was searching for the one phrase
this practice had been filed under the negation of. Retitled to state the
claim the body argues.

Two smaller things fell out of opening the file, neither of which any check
would have found:

- The `version: 3` history entry consisted of the single word **"The"**. It
  had been truncated at some point and the lint has nothing to say about a
  history note that parses as valid YAML and means nothing. Completed from
  the source comments and the diff it describes.
- The `summary:` was a bare citation line — the field was present, so nothing
  reported it, and a reader of the index got the authors and no claim.

## The convention is in better health than I reported

Yesterday's entry says two of 109 unsourced notes write *"Carries no practice,
deliberately."*, and names [LIT-019](../../../../literature.d/LIT-019.md) and [LIT-039](../../../../literature.d/LIT-039.md) as the two. The count was
right and the identification was wrong, which is the more embarrassing half —
I read the exemplars out of [ADR-032](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-032.md) and asserted they were what my own query
had found, without printing the bucket.

Printing it: the two were [LIT-055](../../../../literature.d/LIT-055.md) and [LIT-095](../../../../literature.d/LIT-095.md), the second on a lowercase
prose variant rather than the convention at all. And [LIT-019](../../../../literature.d/LIT-019.md) and [LIT-039](../../../../literature.d/LIT-039.md)
are not in the unsourced 109, because **both are now sourced — by [THEORY-002](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-002.md)
and [THEORY-004](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-004.md)**.

That reverses the conclusion rather than adjusting it. I read a two-of-109 as a
convention nobody adopted. What it actually shows is the scheme split working:
[ADR-032](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-032.md) let a paper say it carries no practice, [ADR-031](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-031.md) then created
somewhere for such a paper to carry an *explanation*, and two of the three
papers that had declared themselves practice-free turned out to be theory
sources. A paper carrying no practice is not thereby carrying nothing.

The general lesson is narrower than [DP-004](../design-principles.md#dp-4) and worth stating on its own: **a
count is not an identification.** Both times I have been wrong about this
corpus in two days, the number was right and the documents behind it were not
the ones I named.

## What the fine-tuning question actually surfaced

[LIT-046](../../../../literature.d/LIT-046.md)'s practice, [SOTA-184](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-184.md), says to train a low-rank update to *each
weight matrix*. The LoRA paper's experiments adapt the query and value
projections. Those are not the same instruction, and the gap between them
became the industry default without anyone measuring it.

QLoRA measures it, which is the more useful half of a paper filed under a
memory headline: query/value alone does not replicate full fine-tuning on
large base models, the count of adapted matrices is what closes the gap, and
the projection rank — the hyperparameter people search — is flat across the
sweep. The two knobs trade places against the usual habit.

Worth trusting because they re-tuned the baseline too, searching learning rate
1e-6 to 5e-5 and batch 8 to 128 rather than taking the published default. An
undertuned baseline is exactly how a placement default survives unexamined: it
makes the incumbent look adequate by weakening what it is compared against.
