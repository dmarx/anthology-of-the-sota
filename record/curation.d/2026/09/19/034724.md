rather than the fifty-three it was filed at. The one it named as most
valuable — `LIT-004`, gradient checkpointing — is now filed from.

## Why `#121` was right to decline and this is not a reversal

That pass looked at `LIT-004`, found four bullets naming a category —
*"introduces gradient checkpointing"*, *"trade-off between memory and
computation"* — and deliberately filed nothing, on the grounds that filing a
practice from a note like that is the failure two passes had been spent
correcting. That judgement was correct and it still is.

**What changed is the input, not the judgement.** The paper's own abstract
states the algorithm (`O(sqrt(n))` memory for one extra forward pass per
minibatch), the other end of the curve (`O(log n)` for `O(n log n)`), and a
measured before-and-after (1,000-layer ResNet, 48G → 7G, +30% running time).
A practice filed from *that* is filed from what the paper says. A practice
filed from the note would have been filed from what the note said about the
paper, which was the category's name.

The distinction is worth keeping because it is easy to collapse into "we
decided not to, now we decided to". The rule that survives: **read the
source, not the note, and if the note is all you have, say so.** `LIT-004`
stays `Unread` — the takeaways are now the paper's stated results, and the
`sqrt(n)` construction's derivation still is not in the record.

## The gap it sat in, which is ordinary [DP-007](../design-principles.md#dp-7)

`SOTA-087` recommends recomputation **for attention**, and is better
evidenced than the general rule — `LIT-074` argues it from HBM traffic with a
mechanism `LIT-004` does not have. Meanwhile `SOTA-017`, `SOTA-019` and
`SOTA-031` all do memory accounting that assumes activations are recomputed,
and nothing stated the trade.

This is `DP-007` in its ordinary form rather than its dramatic one. The
linear-attention case two contributions ago was twelve documents arguing
about a construction they could not cite. This is quieter: recomputation is
universal enough in large-model training that nobody writes the rule down,
and the record held the narrow case for a year.

**The edge was predicted by the document it attaches to.** `SOTA-087`'s v3
history says it gained `systems-optimization` because "trading recomputation
for stored activations is a memory-access decision, and it is what its line
holds in common". That sentence describes a line whose earliest member did
not exist. Declaring `extends:` now binds on exactly that tag, so the
unbound report stays at 0 and 0 — checked rather than assumed.

## A collision worth naming once

`SOTA-054` is "derive the **checkpoint interval** from online profiling".
`SOTA-249` is about **checkpointing activations**. The first is
fault-tolerance — how often to write model state to durable storage so a
crash costs little. The second never leaves the device. They share a word and
nothing else, and a reader scanning `distributed-optimization` will meet both
within a few rows of each other, so the new practice says so in its body.
