`#288` had a small question: a repository implements a technique — where does
that fact go? The pilot (`#287`) had already ruled out guessing, so this was
meant to be twenty minutes of schema.

It was not. The answer is in `ADR-tmp6q6zy` and the interesting part is
everything that turned up on the way there.

## The field that already exists cannot take it

`implementations:` looked like the obvious home, and reading its values killed
that in one query. **85 distinct values across 84 practices** — near-zero
reuse, which is what a free-text field looks like when nobody can tell what it
is for. And the values are four kinds of thing:

    models       llama2, PaLM, GPT-3, DeepSeek-V3, bert, t5, ViT-22B
    methods      NeRF, CycleGAN, Progressive Distillation, DoReMi, TracIn
    libraries    vLLM, diffusers, bitsandbytes, XGrammar, pyreft
    prose        "the 4-bit path in the major PEFT libraries"

The **methods** row is the one that gives it away. `NeRF` and `CycleGAN` are
techniques with their own notes; calling them implementations *of* a practice
says something the record already says better with a relation. And a sentence
in a list field is a sentence that escaped.

Declared in no scheme, so nothing ever complained. That is `#293`.

## The field the decision needs is undeclared too

`consensus_note:` — 206 practices carry it, no scheme mentions it. Fine while
it was decoration; not fine once a decision makes it the place where a
`consensus` reading's grounds live. So: declare it.

luria refused, and the refusal is interesting. **A field's type comes from the
constraint it carries** — `vocabulary`, `many`, `unique`, `required_when`,
`groups`. An optional prose field carries none of those, so its `blurb` is
unreachable; you cannot say what a field is for without also saying what it is
checked against. `PlainField` carries a `blurb` key precisely to describe
fields like this, and the validator makes it impossible to use. `LU-#319`.

## And the declaration that would work is eight documents away

The honest declaration is `required_when` on every non-default `consensus`:
assert the field has converged and you have to say on what grounds. Measured:
**191 practices assert a non-default reading, 183 carry a note, 8 do not.**

Those eight are not marginal. Four say `universal` — *"not doing it is what
needs justifying"*, the strongest claim the axis makes — on nothing at all:
pre-norm, RMSNorm, LoRA, latent diffusion. All plausible, which is exactly why
they need checking; `SOTA-182` may even be an overclaim as written, since
LayerNorm is still standard in much of vision and the title does not say
otherwise.

Not written here. Eight consensus assessments are editorial judgement about
what the field believes, which is the highest-stakes claim this record makes,
and doing it as a side effect of a schema change at the end of a long session
is how `DP-010` happens. `#294`.

## What the episode is actually about

None of the three was visible from the pilot, which enumerated 21 features
against 351 practices and found a clean coverage gap. They were visible only
from trying to write down where a single sentence belongs.

The pilot asked *what is out there*. The ADR asked *what does this record
already mean*, and that is the question that finds the record's own defects —
which is the argument for `CLAUDE.md`'s rule that the fragment gets filed in
the same contribution as the work, and for writing the decision at all rather
than just making it.
