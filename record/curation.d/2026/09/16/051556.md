luria 0.23.0 made it possible to assert a topic invariant across a scheme
boundary, so this record had six candidate relations and had to pick. The
temptation was to pick by rate: `NOTE.paper` fires on 3% of its edges and
`SOTA.explained_by` on 47%, so take the cheap one.

That reasoning gets the right answer for the wrong reason, and the wrong
reason would have generalised badly. The rate is a consequence. What decides
it is whether the relation's two ends are **the same object** or **two things
this record deliberately files on different axes**.

- A note and its paper are one paper. The scheme exists so a reading can carry
  a status separate from the paper's standing — not so it can be about
  something else. Two documents about one paper that share no topic have
  disagreed, and one is wrong.
- A practice and its source are a claim and a paper, and [ADR-026](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-026.md)'s filing rule
  puts them on different axes on purpose: the practice takes the topic of the
  claim, the paper takes what it is about. Asserting they share a topic
  reports the record for following its own rule.

Once that is the test, the rates stop being surprising and start being
confirmation — which is the right way round. 3% against 20-47% is what you
would predict from the two descriptions above, and if it had come out the
other way that would have been the finding.

## The one I nearly got wrong

`SOTA.contested_by` has the best constructional argument of the five that
lost: a paper cannot contest a practice without being about the same thing.
It fires on 4 of 13. Thirteen edges cannot distinguish a 31% rate from a 15%
one, so the honest answer is "not enough evidence", not "the argument is
wrong" — and the decision says so, with a note to revisit if it grows. It
would have been easy to write the argument up as though the measurement
settled it.

## What I did not do

Four `NOTE.paper` findings landed immediately, two of them plainly right to
fix. I left all four. [ADR-036](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-036.md) is the reason: [#101](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/101) bound a pair by giving both
documents a `flash-attention` tag, which *satisfied the check rather than
answering it*, and retagging a paper inside the commit that turns the check
on is the same move with better manners. Each of the four wants a reading.
