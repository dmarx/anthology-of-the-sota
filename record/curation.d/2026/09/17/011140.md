Self-correction was a box on [#87](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/87) with nothing behind it — the record's only
match was [LIT-082](../../../../literature.d/LIT-082.md) (Constitutional AI) on the string "self-improvement",
which is AI feedback and a different thing. Filing it turned into a small
lesson about what a negative result is for.

## Two papers, filed as a pair because either alone misleads

[LIT-382](../../../../literature.d/LIT-382.md) reports that models cannot self-correct reasoning. Filed
alone it reads as a closed door. [LIT-383](../../../../literature.d/LIT-383.md) (SCoRe) reports that
training produces self-correction. Filed alone it reads as a solved problem
and its numbers lose their meaning, because a 15.6% gain on MATH is only
interesting against a baseline somebody established as not working.

Together they are a trunk and its resolution: *asking* fails, so stop asking
and train. Neither paper disputes the other — SCoRe opens by conceding the
negative result, which is the tell that they belong in the record as one
filing.

## The definition outlives the result

What makes the negative paper worth holding is not the measurement. It is the
term **intrinsic** self-correction: revision on the model's own capabilities,
with no oracle, no verifier, no second model in the loop.

That line is the useful instrument, because a great deal of the literature it
was written against is on the other side of it. If an oracle says "wrong, try
again", the loop contains information the model did not have, and the gain
belongs to that information. So the paper's contribution is a question to ask
of any self-correction claim — *was there external feedback?* — and that
question outlives whether models could self-correct in 2023.

The mechanism, once stated, makes the result unsurprising: with no external
signal a model cannot tell its right answers from its wrong ones, so revision
is about as likely to overwrite a correct answer as an incorrect one. Where
most first attempts are already right, revision has negative expected value.

## The rhyme, and why it is not a merge

[SOTA-130](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-130.md) already holds the R1-Zero line: skip the supervised reasoning
stage, run RL with verifiable rewards on the base model. SCoRe says supervised
fine-tuning on correction traces is insufficient and reaches for multi-turn
online RL. Same shape — a supervised stage failing a reasoning-adjacent
capability, online RL replacing it.

The temptation is to treat that as one practice. It is not, and the difference
is the interesting part: **`SOTA-130` reports that skipping SFT works, and
SCoRe reports why fitting offline traces fails.** Two mechanisms, named and
separated — *distribution mismatch* (the traces carry someone else's
mistakes) and *behaviour collapse* (training settles into a correction mode
that scores on training prompts and does not correct at test time).

A reader with only the first knows that a thing happens. The mechanism is what
transfers to capabilities neither paper studied, and it would have been lost
by merging them.

Worth noting the record could not have seen this rhyme from either side alone.
It needed a paper filed today to make a practice filed weeks ago legible as an
instance of something more general — which is the direction [DP-004](../../../../principles.d/DP-004.md) usually
runs the other way.

## What was deliberately not filed

The two-stage recipe. SCoRe's method is an initial multi-turn RL phase for a
collapse-resistant initialization, then a reward bonus amplifying correction —
reported on two Gemini models by one group. The practice records the **choice
of intervention**, which the evidence supports, and not the schedule, which
would be a recommendation built on a single set of experiments.

Also not filed, because nothing measures it: self-correction spends extra
decoding on every attempt. A capability that buys 15.6% while doubling the
tokens is a different trade from a free one, and neither paper prices it. The
practice says so rather than leaving a reader to assume the gain is free.
