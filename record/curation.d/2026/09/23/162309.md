Unit B of `#304`: SimCLR and MoCo, read together because they are the fork
the field actually faced — large batch with in-batch negatives against small
batch with a queue — and reading either alone makes the other's design look
arbitrary.

## The finding worth extracting: a monitor that moves the wrong way

Both papers independently discovered that batch normalization lets a
contrastive model cheat, within three months of each other, and shipped
different fixes. That is already worth two sources on one practice
(`ADR-010`). What makes it worth an entry is *how* they found it.

The pretext-task accuracy is the obvious thing to watch during contrastive
pretraining. Under this bug it goes **up**, faster, and the representation
gets worse. MoCo's Figure A.1 is the picture: the dictionary-lookup accuracy
tracking high while a kNN monitor on actual classification stalls, and
"training longer without shuffling BN overfits more". SimCLR's phrasing is
the same finding from the other side — the model can "improve prediction
accuracy without improving representations".

So the diagnostic is not that the loss looks wrong. It is that **the loss
looks unusually right.** Both groups got there by noticing that a good-looking
run produced a bad representation, which is a thing you can only notice if
you are measuring something other than the objective.

The generalisation, which is what `SOTA-tmpw2bcy` states: this is a risk
whenever a sample's target can be inferred from which batch it is in — any
in-batch ranking or discrimination loss with a normalization that mixes
samples. The specific fix is less transferable than the diagnosis.

## Solving the task and learning the representation are different events

SimCLR says the same thing in a second, unrelated place, and the repetition
is what makes it a principle rather than an anecdote. On augmentations: no
single transformation suffices *"even though the model can almost perfectly
identify the positive pairs"*. Crops of one image share a colour histogram,
so colour alone identifies the image, and the network takes that route.

Twice in two papers, the pretext-task metric and the representation quality
came apart, in opposite directions from what you would guess. **A
self-supervised objective is a proxy, and a proxy that is too easy to satisfy
is the failure mode, not the success condition.**

## A consensus reading that had to be split

`SOTA-tmpv2jq0` bundles MoCo's two mechanisms because the paper's own argument
bundles them — a queue buys size and is worthless without something buying
consistency. But the two halves had different fates, and averaging them into
one reading would have been false in both directions:

- the **slow key encoder** converged so thoroughly it left contrastive
  learning entirely and became the EMA teacher;
- the **queue** did not — SimCLR dropped it for a large batch, CLIP uses
  in-batch negatives at 32,768, and where memory is plentiful it is often
  skipped.

Filed `emerging` with the split written into the note rather than picked. The
general lesson for the remaining `#304` units: **when a paper's contribution
is two mechanisms it argues are inseparable, the consensus reading is about
each of them, and they can diverge.**

## Smaller things kept

**The negative count is not a quality dial, for two independent reasons.**
`THEORY-085` gives the information-theoretic one — the MI bound is capped at
`log N`. SimCLR gives the empirical one: larger batches help markedly at 100
epochs and the gaps "decrease or disappear" with longer training, so more
negatives buys convergence speed. Two different arguments landing on the same
caution is the kind of corroboration worth noticing, because neither is
downstream of the other.

**A linear probe needs its own hyperparameters.** MoCo reports the optimal
probe learning rate as **30** with weight decay **0**, and says this implies
the feature distribution differs substantially from a supervised model's. A
probe run with supervised-style settings is not measuring what it claims to —
a candidate practice this unit did not file, noted here so the reason is on
the record rather than the omission being silent.

**SimCLR's own summary of its contribution is a warning about ablations:**
"almost all individual components of our framework have appeared in previous
work… The superiority of our framework is not explained by any single design
choice, but by their composition." A paper whose result is a composition is
one the record should resist filing as a single practice, which is why this
unit produced four.
