[#58](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/58) needs a rule sharp enough to apply thirty-two times without drifting.
[ADR-010](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-010.md) says `source:` holds every paper a practice rests on, and `SOTA-132`
supplies the working distinction — *support and corroboration go in `source:`;
contrast and alternatives stay in the body*. That is right and still leaves
the hard cases undecided, because most citations read as vaguely supportive.

The test that worked:

> **If this paper were retracted tomorrow, would the practice need
> rewriting?**

It separates the batch cleanly.

- `SOTA-122` without `LIT-121` has no claim left — the blogpost validated
  something, and the something is in the paper. **Add.**
- `SOTA-124` without `LIT-120` has no memorization window, which is the
  quantity the whole recommendation is stated in terms of. **Add.**
- `SOTA-129` without `LIT-127` says "some other RL algorithm" and carries on.
  **Body.** Without `LIT-119` it loses an arithmetic aside in its conditions.
  **Body.** Without `LIT-172` its third stage has no origin. **Add.**
- `SOTA-124` without `LIT-166` loses the position it argues against, which
  makes the practice *less* interesting and not less supported. **Body** —
  and this is the case the vague version of the rule gets wrong, because a
  paper you spend two paragraphs on feels like a source.

## The one that was already right

`SOTA-128` cites `LIT-124` and `LIT-125` and should not name them. It cites
them for what they do *not* say — whether to mask the loss on a FIM sample's
prefix and suffix is "left unstated in one and only implicit in the other" —
which is the reason the source's own two 90M runs are worth reporting. A
citation can be load-bearing and still not be support.

## What the pass is actually finding

Not sloppiness. Every one of these was filed when `source:` took exactly one
code, and each names the single most defensible one. `SOTA-130`'s body says
it outright — Olmo 3 "remains **the** source" — in the singular, months after
[ADR-010](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-010.md) made the field a list. The schema changed and the documents written
under the old one did not get revisited, which is the ordinary way a record
goes stale: not by being wrong when written, but by being right under rules
that have since moved.

Worth checking after the pass: whether `consensus` values set while the
source list understated the support are still correct. A practice reading
`unreplicated` on a one-entry list that should have held three is not
reporting the field, it is reporting the field it was written next to.
