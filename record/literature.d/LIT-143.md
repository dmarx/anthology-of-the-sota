# LIT-143: Native Sparse Attention: Hardware-Aligned and Natively Trainable Sparse Attention

Yuan et al., DeepSeek-AI (2025) — [ARXIV-2502.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089)

## Key takeaways

- Sparse attention had mostly been an inference-time approximation. NSA is
  *natively trainable*: the sparsity pattern is part of the model from
  pretraining, so the model learns to route information through it rather
  than losing information to it afterwards.
- A dynamic hierarchical strategy with three branches per query:
  coarse-grained compression of token blocks for global awareness,
  fine-grained selection of the most relevant blocks for precision, and a
  sliding window for local context.
- Hardware alignment: block-wise selection and kernels balanced for
  arithmetic intensity, so the sparsity turns into speed — substantial
  speedups over full attention at 64k in decoding, forward and backward.
- A model pretrained with NSA maintains or exceeds full-attention baselines
  on general benchmarks, long-context tasks and instruction-based reasoning.

## Standing in the anthology

The first step of the sparse-attention line (→ [LIT-142](LIT-142.md) → [LIT-139](LIT-139.md)), and the
paper that made trainable sparsity a pretraining choice rather than a
serving trick. Its own three-branch design was not what DeepSeek shipped —
V3.2's indexer is simpler — so the practice ([SOTA-138](../practices.d/SOTA-138.md)) is sourced to V3.2 and
this note carries the origin.
