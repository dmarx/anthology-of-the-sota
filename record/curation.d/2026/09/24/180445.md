Unit 1 of `#342`. I went in expecting the third `introduced_by` repair in
four days and found something better: **the field was wrong and the correct
value is empty.**

## What I expected to find

`#342` ranked this first on a two-line argument: `SOTA-032` is `Active`,
`universal`, and names Xiong et al. (Feb 2020) as `introduced_by`; Nguyen and
Salazar (Oct 2019) state the same headline consequence in their abstract —
*"pre-norm residual connections (PreNorm) and smaller initializations enable
warmup-free… training with large learning rates"*. Four months earlier, same
claim. Repoint and move on.

That is exactly the reasoning that produced the correct repair in `#338`, and
here it would have produced a second wrong answer.

## What the paper actually says

Second paragraph of the introduction:

> **Chen et al. (2018)** found that pre-norm residual units (PreNorm)… were
> instrumental to their model's performance. **Wang et al. (2019)** compare
> the two, showing that PreNorm makes backpropagation more efficient over
> depth…

and a page later:

> This is cited as a stabilizer for Transformer training… and is **already
> implemented in popular toolkits** (Vaswani et al. 2018; Ott et al. 2019;
> Hieber et al. 2018), **though not necessarily used by their default
> recipes.**

Their own description of their contribution is *"Our work demonstrates
**additional consequences** in the base Transformer regime."*

So pre-norm has no origin paper. It was in OpenNMT, tensor2tensor and Sockeye
and was used inside larger systems before anyone published an argument for it.
The two earliest papers that argue for it both attribute it to others.

`introduced_by: []` under `ADR-053`, with a frontmatter comment naming the
four candidates so a reader can refute it by producing an earlier argument.

## The rule this suggests

`#338` established that a mention count ranks by the wrong noun. This adds a
narrower one, about repairs rather than rankings:

**An abstract states the paper's contribution in the strongest form its
authors think defensible. The related-work section states what they think
they owe. A claim about priority has to come from the second.**

The abstract sentence — *"we show that pre-norm… enable warmup-free
training"* — is true and is not an origin claim, and nothing but reading two
paragraphs further distinguishes it from one. Both `#338`'s repair and this
one turned on the same test; the difference is that ViT-22B's note had already
done the reading and this practice never had.

Worth noting where the check came from. I had the answer from the *other*
paper first: Henry et al.'s QK-norm work attributes PreNorm to *"Klein et al.
2017; Domhan 2018; Vaswani et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2018"*, four names none of
which is Nguyen and Salazar. I had opened that paper to check an unrelated
lineage. **The correction to unit 1's premise came from a document filed in a
different unit two days earlier**, which is not a method.

## Two findings the ranking did not predict

Both are in the paper and neither is what it was ranked for.

**A regime where the recommendation loses.** High-resource WMT'14
English-German, base Transformer: post-norm 27.58, pre-norm 26.83. The paper
calls it surprising and reports Wang et al. seeing it too. `SOTA-032` is
`universal` and its note said *"Not doing it is what needs justifying"* with
no scale qualifier. It has one now.

**Most of the instability is an initialization artefact.** Post-norm with
default Xavier init fails to converge on en→vi at 4k and 8k warm-up. Reduce
the attention layers' initialization and it reaches 28.17 against pre-norm's
28.52 — from *fails* to within half a BLEU. The paper's phrasing is exact and
much weaker than the received version: smaller init *"partly reclaims
PostNorm's stability"*, and pre-norm is *"less sensitive to this magnitude"*.

The record had been carrying the dramatic version. What survives it intact is
depth without warm-up, where post-norm fails at five and six layers and
pre-norm does not — categorical, not marginal.

## An error of mine, corrected in the same contribution

`LIT-640` (QK-norm, filed two days ago) said its +0.928 BLEU was measured
*"against state-of-the-art bilingual benchmarks"*. That is the paper's own
abstract phrasing and it is true. It is also the kind of true that misleads:
the baseline row is **Nguyen and Salazar's system**, and the comparison changes
**two things at once** — ScaleNorm to LayerNorm, and scaled dot-product to
QKNorm.

So the number is not QK-norm against a plain Transformer. I took an abstract's
framing into a note without checking the results table, in a note whose whole
point was that a hedge in one document does not travel to the document citing
it. The same failure, one level down, in the repair for it.

Both corrections in this unit have the same cause and it is worth saying
plainly: **an abstract is an argument, and a note built from one inherits the
argument rather than the evidence.**
