# LIT-032: PowerNorm: Rethinking Batch Normalization in Transformers

Shen et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2003.07845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07845)

## Key takeaways

- Diagnoses *why* batch normalization underperforms layer normalization in
  NLP, by instrumenting training: the per-batch mean and variance fluctuate
  far more than in vision, so the running estimates used at inference do not
  match what training saw
- **Centring is the fragile half.** The variation is in the batch *mean*, and
  the gradient contribution routed through `σ_B` carries many more outliers
  than the one through `ψ_B`
- Power Normalization drops the zero-mean step entirely and normalizes by a
  **running quadratic mean `ψ = √(E[x²])`** rather than a per-batch variance
- **+0.4/+0.6 BLEU** on IWSLT14/WMT14 and **−5.6/−3.0 PPL** on
  PTB/WikiText-103 against LN, **with no change of hyperparameters** — and
  +1.5/+2.0 BLEU, −7.7/−3.4 PPL against BN
- Reframes the normalization question from *which axis* to **which statistic
  is stable enough to normalize by**

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-056](../notes.d/NOTE-056.md). PowerNorm did not displace LayerNorm or RMSNorm, and a
0.4-BLEU margin at 2020 scale is not a reason to revisit that.

The value is the connection the reading surfaced. **This is the measurement
behind a claim `LIT-023` states as a hypothesis**: RMSNorm also drops centring
and normalizes by a root-mean-square, but along the feature axis and without
measuring why re-centring is dispensable. PowerNorm measures it on the batch
axis, forward and backward. `LIT-025` then acts on the same intuition from a
third direction by removing LayerNorm's learned gain and bias. Three papers
converging on *LayerNorm does more than it needs to*, and the record did not
previously connect them.
