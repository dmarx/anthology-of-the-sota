[#18](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/18) asks for three things: deepen the notes written from abstracts, verify
the claims marked as reported, and re-examine the `Proposed` practices whose
promotion condition was "the larger-scale evidence lives in the preprint".
The third turned out to be the interesting one, and not for the reason the
issue anticipated.

## Two conditions met, one applied

[SOTA-133](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-133.md) (Attention Residuals) and [SOTA-136](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-136.md) (mHC) were filed a day apart
with nearly identical conditions — one group, one paper, one production
model; *promote on an independent result*. The Qwen3.8-Next design study
([LIT-152](../../record/literature.d/LIT-152.md)) is an independent result for **both**: a different laboratory
ran both designs in its own harness against its own competing one, and found
all three level.

By the letter, both promote. Only one did.

**[SOTA-133](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-133.md) is now Active.** An outside group with a rival design implemented
AttnRes, measured it, and it worked — 1.762 against 1.789 for the pre-norm
baseline. That is the strongest form the condition could have asked for, and
there is nothing pulling the other way.

**[SOTA-136](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-136.md) stays Proposed**, and the practice now says why in those terms
rather than leaving the status as a silent non-decision. Two *other*
independent results arrived in the same window and both attack the specific
thing it recommends — the doubly-stochastic constraint. [LIT-151](../../record/literature.d/LIT-151.md) proves the
Birkhoff polytope is bounded above but not below; [LIT-181](../../record/literature.d/LIT-181.md) names identity
degeneration, an expressivity bottleneck from non-negativity, and unstable
Sinkhorn projection. Opposite remedies, same verdict on the constraint.

So the practice's **claim** has independent support and its **mechanism**
has independent opposition, and "an independent result" cannot tell those
apart. The condition was the wrong condition — it counted papers when it
should have asked what they were papers about.

That is the transferable lesson: a promotion condition phrased as a
*quantity of evidence* will eventually be satisfied by evidence pointing the
wrong way. The conditions in this record should say what a result has to
**show**, not how many there have to be.

## The same failure, caught earlier, in [SOTA-122](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-122.md)

[SOTA-122](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-122.md) asked for "an independent reproduction **or** a result above 1B".
[LIT-153](../../record/literature.d/LIT-153.md) is an independent group at 1.2B working on precisely the
phenomenon the practice rests on — the weight-decay equilibrium that fixes a
matrix's norm by hyperparameters rather than data — who confirm the
diagnosis and adopt the **opposite** remedy: pin the norms to constants
rather than give the scale a learned parameter.

Confirming the mechanism while declining the fix satisfies the `or` and
means nothing. The condition now asks for gains from learned per-row and
per-column multipliers specifically. [SOTA-144](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-144.md) gets the same treatment for
the same paper's depth-transfer result: two routes to one property is
evidence the property is reachable, not that this route works.

## The reported claims

Both outstanding items from [#18](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/18)'s checklist resolve against the primary
sources, and both were slightly wrong in ways worth having caught:

- DeepSeek-V4's FP4 quantization-aware training does cover "MoE expert
  weights and the indexer QK path" — but the report places it in
  **post-training**. [LIT-139](../../record/literature.d/LIT-139.md) had it in a pretraining-infrastructure bullet.
  The distinction matters: [LIT-186](../../record/literature.d/LIT-186.md) shows quantization damage grows with
  how much data a model was trained on, so *where in the pipeline* the low
  precision sits is the decision, not an implementation detail.
- Kimi K3's gated MLA is **not** quite [SOTA-134](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-134.md)'s gate. The report says "an
  input-dependent, channel-wise full-rank output gate"; the practice's source
  ablates a *head-specific* sigmoid. Same position in the block, same
  justification, finer granularity — so the practice covers it, and the
  granularity is a free parameter nobody has swept. [LIT-138](../../record/literature.d/LIT-138.md)'s "appears to be
  the same idea" is replaced with what the report actually says.

## What reading the report rather than the abstract bought

[LIT-131](../../record/literature.d/LIT-131.md) was the last note still declaring itself filed at abstract depth.
Reading it turned up something no abstract would carry and that bears on a
practice: **Kimi K3 applies no positional encoding at all to its MLA
layers**, letting the interleaved KDA layers supply position sensitivity and
recency instead.

The consequence the report names is the useful part — extending the context
no longer means retuning a RoPE frequency base or applying YaRN, because
there is no positional parameter in the global layers to retune. That is a
*second* argument for the hybrid layout, independent of cost per token, and
[SOTA-132](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-132.md) was filed without it. It also makes [SOTA-139](../../record/practices.d/SOTA-139.md)'s staged context
extension a smaller operation for anyone using the layout.

Which is the case for [#18](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/18) in one paragraph: the abstract tells you what a
paper claims, and the report tells you which of the record's other claims it
touches.
