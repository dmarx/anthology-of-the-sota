`#289` asked for a sweep of seven repositories where presence means people
use the thing. Two findings changed what that sweep is.

## Only three of the seven can be enumerated at all

`vllm` publishes a feature compatibility matrix. `diffusers` exports every
scheduler it ships. `transformers` tabulates its quantization backends. Those
three are machine-readable.

`sglang`, `Megatron-LM` and `pytorch-lightning` are not, and it is not a
parsing problem. Their READMEs' headings are *News*, *About*, *Getting
Started*, *Contributing* — navigation. What those projects implement is in
prose, and reading it is reading, not extraction. `ComfyUI` has a `Features`
section and is the one that could be added cheaply.

That is worth saying plainly because the issue assumed seven comparable
sweeps. There are three, plus four projects that want a person.

## The adoption/catalogue split is per *index*, not per repository

`#183` divided the repositories in two: adoption repositories, where presence
means people use the thing, and catalogues, where it means somebody found it
interesting. `transformers` is obviously an adoption repository — and its
quantization table lists **22 backends** of which the record holds **zero**:
`AQLM`, `EETQ`, `FBGEMM_FP8`, `HIGGS`, `HQQ`, `Quark`, `SINQ`, `SpQR`,
`VPTQ`, `AutoRound`, `FP-Quant`, `GPT-QModel`, `optimum-quanto`, `torchao`…

Twenty-two absent techniques is not a twenty-two item backlog. It is a list
of **integrations somebody contributed**, which is the same thing
x-transformers' README is and exactly what `#290` was told to be careful
about. Same repository, different index, different kind — so the split lives
on the file being read, not on the project reading it.

## What survives: the cross-repository intersection

The signal that does mean something is an item **two independent indexes both
carry**. Of 91 distinct items across the three, that is three:

| item | in | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **AWQ** | `transformers`, `vllm` | a technique, and absent |
| **bitsandbytes** | `transformers`, `vllm` | a library, not a technique |
| **GGUF** | `transformers`, `vllm` | a container format, not a technique |

Three of ninety-one. That ratio is the finding: presence in one index is
nearly worthless, and agreement between two is rare enough to be worth
reading.

## The two real gaps, and the one that was a naming artifact

**`AWQ` is a genuine hole.** GPTQ is in the record three times; its direct
competitor, published a year later and served at least as widely, is in it
zero times. Nothing weighed them — `SOTA-318` mentions "activation-aware SVD"
and is a low-rank compression practice, which is a different technique that
happens to share a word.

**`bitsandbytes` is a library name covering two techniques, and the record
holds one of them.** NF4 is `SOTA-230`, sourced to `LIT-378` and `LIT-055`.
`LLM.int8()` — the outlier-decomposition path, which is what
`load_in_8bit=True` actually does — is absent. So the row was simultaneously
a false positive and a true one, and only reading it apart showed which.

**`GGUF` is a container format.** Practitioner-adopted, no technique to
recommend, and the k-quant schemes inside it have almost no published
evidence. Declined, and the reason is worth writing down because it will come
back: this record files claims about what to do, and "use this file format"
is not one until somebody publishes a comparison.

## Not filed here

`AWQ` and `LLM.int8()` both want a note and a practice, and the network cannot
reach arXiv from this session — `luria lint` reports 140 identifiers nothing
has verified. Filing two papers whose metadata cannot be checked is how a
wrong identifier enters the record, and `ADR-009` exists because two papers
once reached the old corpus with no identifier at all. The worklist is on
`#289`; the filing waits for a session that can verify it.
