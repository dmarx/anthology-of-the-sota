Third unit down the reading feed. The gap this one filled was not one the
record had complained about — it was one the record had *named* and not
noticed naming.

## How it surfaced

[SOTA-188](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-188.md) lists `Stable Diffusion 3` under `implementations:`. The record
did not hold the paper. Nor did it hold anything on rectified flow, flow
matching, or continuous-time diffusion — it has DDPM, DDIM, latent diffusion,
EDM and the distillation line, and stops.

An `implementations:` entry naming a system whose paper is absent is a
detectable shape, and not one any query in [#86](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/86)'s trunk detector looks for.
Worth adding: the detector reads prose complaints, and this is a structured
field quietly asserting that the record knows about something it does not
hold.

## The half a summary drops

The headline of [LIT-tmps0tea](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmps0tea.md) is *rectified flow wins*. The finding is
narrower and much more useful: rectified flow **with a logit-normal timestep
distribution** wins, and rectified flow with **uniform** timesteps — the way
the prior literature wrote it — does not beat well-tuned
epsilon-prediction.

That explains two years of equivocal empirics for a formulation with better
theory. The path and the distribution over where you train along it are
separate decisions, and conflating them made a real advantage look absent.

I wrote the practice title with both halves in it for that reason. A reader
who takes the straight line and keeps uniform sampling has adopted the
version that does not win, and a title saying only "use rectified flow"
invites exactly that.

## The theory was the best thing in the batch

[LIT-tmp4xjcz](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmp4xjcz.md) is from 2021 and its central result is a change of
variables. Integrate the diffusion bound over signal-to-noise ratio rather
than over time — legitimate because the schedule is monotone and therefore
invertible — and the schedule vanishes from the integrand, surviving only in
the limits.

So in continuous time the bound depends on the noise schedule through two
numbers, and variance-preserving and variance-exploding specifications, which
the literature treated as different model classes, are the same model up to a
rescaling.

**This answers an objection [SOTA-188](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-188.md) has carried unanswered for a month.**
That practice says to concentrate training noise in the middle of the range,
and the obvious question is whether changing where you sample changes the
model. The answer is no — with a qualification I had to be careful about: the
invariance is stated for the *unweighted* bound, and [SOTA-188](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-188.md)'s loss
weighting is precisely what puts it outside that statement. What the theory
establishes is that the schedule is the kind of thing one is free to choose,
not that this particular choice is neutral.

`explains:` on [THEORY-tmp962qd](../../../../theory.d/THEORY-tmp962qd.md) names [SOTA-188](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-188.md) and [SOTA-tmpc0pb9](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-tmpc0pb9.md). Yesterday's
theory shipped with that field empty because the bridging experiment had not
been run. This one earns it.

## Filing a paper unread, on purpose

[LIT-tmpfnkux](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmpfnkux.md) is DiT, and there is no `NOTE` for it — which under
[ADR-025](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-025.md) is a statement, not an omission.

It is here because [LIT-tmpdkrfi](../../../../literature.d/LIT-tmpdkrfi.md)'s entire method is "DiT's architecture,
parameter count and GFLOPs held exactly fixed, transport changed", and
without the note that comparison names something the record does not have.
What the neighbouring documents need from it is the scaling claim and the
identity of the backbone. Claiming a reading I had not done would have been
worse than saying I had not done one, and the note says which.

## An independent arrival, and why the record could see it

Esser et al. adopt QK-normalization after mixed-precision training diverged
at high resolution, diagnosing it through the discriminative ViT literature's
attention-entropy result. The record already holds QK-norm — [SOTA-131](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-131.md),
[SOTA-192](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-192.md), [SOTA-050](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-050.md) — entirely from the language-model side. Neither line
cites the other.

[DP-007](../../../../principles.d/DP-007.md) says agreement has no author and therefore no arrival event. This
is the variant worth having: **two** arrival events, in two literatures, from
two different failures. The record can see both at once only because
[ADR-026](../../../../decisions.d/ADR-026.md) files by the kind of claim rather than by the domain it was found
in. That decision was argued on scope; this is the first time it has paid off
as a detection mechanism.

## Where I stopped short

Two statuses I could have inflated and did not.

[SOTA-tmp4jpf3](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-tmp4jpf3.md), the resolution-dependent timestep shift, is `Proposed`. The
argument is a derivation and it is obviously right, and *obviously right* is
not *measured*: the paper demonstrates it qualitatively inside a pipeline
where several things changed together, and there is no ablation saying what
the shift alone is worth. A correction that is obvious once stated is a
reason to suspect it is true, not evidence that anyone checked.

[SOTA-tmp28zyj](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-tmp28zyj.md), MMDiT, is `Proposed` because its two ingredients arrive
together — separate per-modality weights and joint attention — and nothing
separates them. A reader could implement one and credit it with the other's
benefit.

By contrast [SOTA-tmpf60k7](../../../../practices.d/SOTA-tmpf60k7.md) is `Active` on one group's evidence, because the
load-bearing part is an identity rather than a sweep: the diffusion
coefficient does not enter the velocity or the score, so it cannot be
downstream of training. The measurement only confirms that the freedom is
worth using.
