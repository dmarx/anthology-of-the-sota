`#180`'s reading feed produced a trunk, and the way it produced one is
different from every other trunk this session found.

## What was missing

Ten practices in this record mention Muon — `SOTA-121`, `SOTA-131`,
`SOTA-120`, `SOTA-122`, `SOTA-165`, `SOTA-168`, `SOTA-192`, `SOTA-221`,
`SOTA-001`, `SOTA-003`. The chain is filed end to end: `LIT-159` → `LIT-122`
→ `LIT-132` → `LIT-131`.

**`LIT-159` is a blog post.** Filed with a `url:`, and its own standing says
so plainly — "the clearest case yet for `ADR-009` — three laboratories
pretrain frontier models with this and there is no paper to cite."

There was a paper. Three of them. The record held none, and held no `THEORY`
saying why orthogonalising an update is the right move.

## The account

Gradients are dual vectors; weights are primal. `weight -= LR * grad`
subtracts one from the other, which is only harmless because the Euclidean
norm identifies the two spaces. Under a different norm the identification
fails and the update needs a **duality map**.

`LIT-438` builds that map for whole architectures: assign each layer an
operator norm *from its input-output semantics*, derive the layer's map,
recurse. For `Linear` and `Conv2D` the map is a rectangular Newton-Schulz
iteration — **which is the operation Muon performs.**

So the order inverts. Muon is not a trick that worked and later acquired a
rationalisation; it is the duality map for a particular norm, and the blog
post came out of the same group's work on the theory.

**The unification is the load-bearing part.** §4.1 shows muP and Shampoo both
emerge as *partial approximations to a single duality map*, the one induced by
the RMS–RMS operator norm. The record recommends both — `SOTA-143` and
`SOTA-168` — filed from different literatures at different times with nothing
connecting them. That they are one object seen from two sides changes neither
recommendation and changes what a reader understands on meeting the second,
which is exactly what `THEORY` is for.

## Why this find is methodologically new

Every other trunk this session came from the record complaining:

| trunk | the complaint that found it |
|---|---|
| linear attention (`LIT-428`) | twelve documents argued about a construction they could not cite |
| the RLHF antecedents | `LIT-377` wrote "leaves three others open" into its own standing |
| NeRF | `SOTA-205` recommended replacing "a large coordinate network" and could not point at one |

**This one had no complaint to find.** The record does not mention modular
duality, the modular norm, or the spectral condition — zero occurrences of the
first two, one of the third about something else. The trunk detector would
never have surfaced it, because the detector reads the record's own prose and
the record's prose was silent.

The signal came from **outside**: three papers at 8 distinct days and 14
reading sessions each, near the top of 4,076 in a feed that has nothing to do
with this corpus. `DP-007` says agreement has no author and therefore no
arrival event. That is true of the literature; it is not true of a reader.
**Somebody kept going back**, and that is an arrival event the record cannot
generate about itself.

The generalizable claim, and it is a claim about instruments rather than about
optimizers: **a record's internal signals find gaps the record has already
half-noticed. Finding the gaps it has not noticed at all requires a signal
from somewhere else.** The reading feed is such a signal, which is the answer
to the question `#180` was actually asking.

## What the theory does not do

It explains the *shape* of the update, not the constants. `SOTA-121`'s
decoupled weight decay and AdamW-matched update RMS, and `SOTA-131`'s QK-Clip
at a trillion parameters, are engineering this does not reach.

It also does not settle the norm assignment, which is the substantive
modelling move — argued from semantics, not derived. A different assignment
gives a different optimizer.

And all three papers share authors. `Active` because the argument is checkable
and the practices it explains are independently evidenced — not because
anybody outside that group has confirmed the frame. `ADR-010`'s neighbouring
caution holds: a line agreeing with itself is not replication.
