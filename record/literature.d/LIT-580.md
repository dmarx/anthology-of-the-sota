# LIT-580: How Powerful are Graph Neural Networks?

Xu, Hu, Leskovec, Jegelka, MIT and Stanford (ICLR 2019) — [ARXIV-1810.00826](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826)

## Key takeaways

- **Upper bound** (Lemma 2): any aggregate-and-combine GNN is at most as
  powerful as the 1-WL test at distinguishing graphs
- **Achieving it** (Theorem 3): it is achieved when the neighbor aggregation,
  the combine step and the graph readout are injective. Sums of an MLP over
  a multiset are injective (Lemma 5), and this gives
  `h_v = MLP((1 + ε)·h_v + Σ_u h_u)`
- **Mean and max are weaker** (§5): mean captures the distribution of
  neighbor features, not their counts. Max captures only the set. Figure 3
  gives simple structures both fail to distinguish
- **Training fit** (Figure 4): sum–MLP fits every training set almost
  perfectly, and mean, max and 1-layer variants underfit many of them, in
  the predicted order
- **Accuracy** (Table 1, 10-fold CV). §6 says these are "validation
  accuracies across the 10 folds", and the epoch count was selected on the
  same folds' average, so they are not held-out test scores. GIN-0 is best or statistically
  tied on all 9 datasets. The large gaps are on REDDIT-BINARY and MULTI-5K,
  which have no node features: sum 92.4 and 57.5, mean exactly at chance.
  Even with degree features, mean scores 71.2 and 41.3. Elsewhere,
  differences are mostly within one standard deviation. On PTC, mean–MLP
  (66.6) beats GIN-0 (64.6)

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from `#163`** ("graph representation / gnn"). It is
`compared_against` GCN ([LIT-582](LIT-582.md)), whose mean aggregator it analyzes. It
sources [THEORY-083](../theory.d/THEORY-083.md) and [SOTA-351](../practices.d/SOTA-351.md).

**What the table shows and what the abstract says.** "GIN achieves
state-of-the-art" rests on ties within noise on most datasets. The decisive
evidence is the featureless Reddit graphs and the training-fit curves,
which is where the theory predicts a difference.

**Retuned against a graph transformer.** Tönshoff et al. ([LIT-579](LIT-579.md)) use
GINE, this architecture's edge-feature variant, as one of three
message-passing baselines on the Long-Range Graph Benchmark, at the same
500k-parameter budget as the GPS graph transformer. After a hyperparameter
sweep, GINE goes from AP 0.550 to 0.662 on Peptides-func and from MAE 0.355
to 0.247 on Peptides-struct, ahead of GPS's 0.653 and 0.251, with most of
the gain from a multi-layer prediction head. On the PascalVOC-SP and COCO-SP
superpixel tasks GPS stays ahead. What is compared is tuned performance on
long-range tasks, not the expressiveness result above.

**As a baseline for a multi-dataset model.** One for All ([LIT-tmptwetd](LIT-tmptwetd.md))
reruns GIN on WN18RR and FB15K237, where it scores 57.30 and 70.70 on
relation classification, and quotes it on PCBA at 22.66 AP against OFA's
24.83. GIN takes each dataset's native features and OFA takes text
embeddings, so the gap mixes feature quality with architecture.

**Retrained as a graph transformer's baseline.** Graphormer ([LIT-tmpy7li2](LIT-tmpy7li2.md))
reruns GIN with a virtual node on PCQM4M-LSC with OGB's official
hyperparameters, reproducing the leaderboard's 0.1395 validation MAE.
Graphormer reaches 0.1234 with 47.1M parameters to GIN-VN's 6.7M, so the
comparison is not size-matched.

**Corrected on what Table 1 reports.** Errica et al. ([LIT-tmprm7bo](LIT-tmprm7bo.md))
point out, and §6 of this paper states, that the table reports validation
accuracy across the 10 folds with the epoch chosen on them, not accuracy on
held-out data. Rerun with hyperparameters selected inside each training
fold and scored on the test fold, GIN reaches 87.0 ± 4.4 on featureless
REDDIT-BINARY and 53.8 ± 5.9 on REDDIT-5K, against the 92.4 and 57.5 here.
That is still well above a structure-agnostic baseline (72.1 and 35.1).
The mean variants' collapse to chance is not rerun there, and as a failure
to separate graphs it would not be rescued by the protocol.
