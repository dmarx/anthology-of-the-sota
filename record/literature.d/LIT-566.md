# LIT-566: SDXL: Improving Latent Diffusion Models for High-Resolution Image Synthesis

Podell, English, Lacey, Blattmann, Dockhorn, Müller, Penna, Rombach,
Stability AI (2023) — [ARXIV-2307.01952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952)

## Key takeaways

- **Scale and text encoders:** the UNet moves most transformer compute to
  lower resolutions, and the cross-attention context doubles with two
  concatenated text encoders. 2.6B parameters in the UNet
- **Size conditioning:** instead of discarding small images (39% of the
  pretraining data below 256² would have been dropped) or upsampling them
  (which teaches blur), embed `(h_original, w_original)` with Fourier
  features and add it to the timestep embedding. At inference, the size
  condition sets the apparent resolution
- **Table 2 (class-conditional ImageNet 512², FID-5k / IS-5k):** discard
  below 512 (70k images) 43.84 / 110.64. All data, no conditioning 39.76 /
  211.50. All data, size-conditioned 36.53 / 215.34
- **Crop conditioning:** feed the random-crop offsets `(c_top, c_left)`, and
  set them to zero at inference. This removes SD 1.x/2.x's cut-off objects.
  Shown qualitatively
- **Multi-aspect fine-tuning** on buckets of aspect ratios, and a refiner
  that re-noises and denoises the base latents with a model specialized to
  high resolution
- **Evaluation:** user-preference studies against SD 1.5 and 2.1 (Figure
  1). The authors note FID does not track the improvement

## Standing in the anthology

**Filed from `#163`** (the stable diffusion lineage, "sdxl"). It `extends`
LDM ([LIT-062](LIT-062.md)) and sources [SOTA-341](../practices.d/SOTA-341.md). The other SDXL mentions in the
record are as a quantization target ([LIT-512](LIT-512.md)).

**As a teacher and as a baseline.** DMD2 ([LIT-646](LIT-646.md)) distils SDXL into a
4-step student and compares the two on 10K COCO prompts (its Table 2): FID
19.32 for the student against 19.36 for SDXL at guidance 6 and 20.39 at
guidance 8, with the student slightly less diverse (pairwise LPIPS 0.61
against 0.64). DMAD ([LIT-770](LIT-770.md)) distils SDXL into a four-step student on the
same protocol and reports FID 14.47 against SDXL's 19.36, with CLIP 0.328
against the teacher's 0.332; its SDXL and DMD2 rows are DMD2's published
numbers, not reruns. The DALL-E 3 captioning paper ([LIT-648](LIT-648.md)) puts SDXL last of
its three systems on 4,096 MSCOCO captions, CLIP score 30.5 against 31.4
for DALL-E 2 and 32.0 for DALL-E 3, and at −189.5 in a human
prompt-following Elo over 170 prompts, where DALL-E 3 scores 153.3. That
paper says its DALL-E 3 comparisons do not isolate recaptioning, so they
measure systems, not training captions.

Two later papers list SDXL's published GenEval of 0.55 as a reference row.
Flow-GRPO ([LIT-tmpdktqx](LIT-tmpdktqx.md)) sets it beside SD3.5-M at 0.63 before and 0.95
after RL on GenEval's own prompt templates. STARFlow ([LIT-tmpnm3dm](LIT-tmpnm3dm.md)) edges it
at 0.56, the one text-to-image row that latent normalizing flow beats.

Diffusion-DPO ([LIT-tmp4m2nj](LIT-tmp4m2nj.md)) fine-tunes SDXL base on Pick-a-Pic preference
pairs and is compared against it by human vote: the tuned model is preferred
70.0% of the time on PartiPrompts, and 69% against base plus refiner, which
has about 1.9 times its parameters (its §5.2, Figs. 3–4). Fine-tuning SDXL on
the preferred images alone makes it worse, because SDXL is already better
than the models that generated the data.
