Unit 3 of `#326`. The paper is a position-encoding proposal, which is why
`#290` filed it as a variant. What it also contains is a **measure**, and the
measure is what the record needed.

## The cluster had four practices and no instrument

`SOTA-063` recommends rotary position encoding. `SOTA-151` extends context by
rescaling it. `SOTA-179` truncates its low frequencies instead. `SOTA-153`
drops it from a hybrid's global-attention layers. All four are about the same
component, and `SOTA-151` is explicitly motivated by RoPE not extrapolating.

So the record knew the phenomenon and had no way to measure it. That is a
recognisable shape by now — it is the metric sweep's shape, arriving in a
cluster the sweep did not look at, because the sweep's third pass read
documents tagged `analysis-and-evaluation` and this cluster is tagged
`representation-and-encoding`.

**The sweep's own recorded limit was that it could not find a measure the
record uses without naming.** This is the adjacent miss: a measure the record
*needs* and never had a name for, in a cluster filed elsewhere. Worth adding
to that entry's list of what the query could not return.

## The finding that generalises

Every piece of evidence behind `SOTA-151` is perplexity at the extended
length. Sun et al. draw the distinction that evidence cannot:

> the perplexity does not explode but does not decrease at the same time. The
> ideal situation is to use the long context in the right way, in that case,
> the model should perform better instead of saturation.

**Not degrading and working are different claims.** A metric that can only go
badly wrong is a *safety* check, and passing it means the failure did not
happen — not that the capability arrived. Perplexity at 2× the training length
is that kind of metric: it detects catastrophe and is nearly silent about
competence.

This is `THEORY-039`'s neighbourhood — a measured capability is the capability
minus what the evaluation does to it — from the other side. There the
evaluation subtracts; here the evaluation cannot add, because it has no upper
region that means anything.

## The amendment, and why it did not change a status

`SOTA-151` stays `Active` and `converged`. The production adoption in its
consensus note is real and a serving configuration extending 262144 to 1M is
a strong usability signal.

What changed is that the practice now states what its evidence does and does
not establish. The new measurement **strengthens its motivation** — RoPE's
resolution collapsing 0.91 → 0.08 across the boundary is a bigger defect than
"does not extrapolate" conveys — and **narrows its claim**, because nobody has
measured position-recognisability in a rescaled model.

That is the second time in two units that the right operation was to edit the
evidence and leave the status alone. Unit 2 did it to `SOTA-358`. It is worth
noticing as a pattern rather than a coincidence: **a practice's status is a
claim about whether to do the thing, and most new evidence bears on what you
know rather than on what to do.** Reaching for `contested` whenever a
measurement lands would make the status track the literature's activity
instead of its conclusions.

## One more thing the paper does that most do not

It reports that **half its own headline result is not its contribution.** xPos
alone scores 0.54 resolution at 2048; with blockwise causal attention, 1.08.
BCA is an inference-time masking change applied to a normally-trained model.

The paper could have reported 1.08 against the baselines' 0.08 and 0.88 and
left the attribution unexamined. It split it. **An ablation that removes the
author's own contribution and finds the remainder still helps is the form that
is hardest to fake and least often reported** — and it is the reason the
resolution numbers here are worth carrying into the record at all.
