**`LIT-502` filed from [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) in rank order, for its section 3.**

MemGraphRAG is a multi-agent GraphRAG system with a three-tier memory, three
agents and Personalized PageRank, and it reports beating everything. That is
not what the record took.

What it took is the pilot study, where the authors put three published
GraphRAG systems next to vanilla RAG and measured both halves of retrieval
quality:

| | evidence recall | context relevance |
|---|---|---|
| vanilla RAG | 71.8% | **62.9%** |
| GFM-RAG | **84.3%** | 38.5% |

Twelve and a half points of coverage bought at twenty-four points of
precision, and end-task accuracy falls with it. Graph expansion finds more of
the relevant material and very much more of the irrelevant, and the generator
then has to locate the answer inside the pile.

**The reason this is `Active` on one benchmark family is whose interest it
runs against.** These authors are proposing a GraphRAG system. Their section 3
establishes that GraphRAG as a family trades relevance for recall at a net
loss on the task everyone cares about. Evidence that embarrasses the
presenter's own category is worth more than evidence that flatters it, and
they cite two independent benchmarks that got there first.

**One number I restated more strongly than the paper does.** They filter the
lowest-frequency 40% of extracted triples and accuracy moves 64.85% → 65.28%.
Their reading: evidence of thematically irrelevant noise. But 0.43 points is
inside anyone's noise, so as an accuracy claim it is nearly empty. As a *cost*
claim it is substantial — **you can throw away 40% of the constructed graph
and lose nothing**. That is a statement about what the construction step is
producing, and it is better supported by their own number than the inference
they drew from it.

The reading also names the control they did not run: deleting 40% *at random*.
The result says a large part of the graph is disposable; it does not show the
frequency ordering picks out the disposable part.

**Where I declined.** Section 5.4 swaps their graph into four other systems and
reports HippoRAG 51.07 → 51.78 and MS-GraphRAG 43.75 → 44.21 — under a point,
one run each — as "consistent improvement" that "substantially strengthens the
effectiveness of existing retrievers".

An hour ago I filed a paper that spent several hundred training runs
establishing what sub-point deltas are worth. Having done that, taking this
framing would be incoherent. So nothing in the record rests on MemGraphRAG
itself: single runs, no seeds, the authors' own system against baselines they
ran. `DP-006` — filing is not endorsement, and the converse holds too, so the
note says plainly that the underlying claim may well be true and is not
established here.

**A shape I am counting rather than concluding from.** `SOTA-210` already says
to report pass@k alongside pass@1, because reinforcement learning raises one
and lowers the other and the literature reports the favourable one. This is
the same failure with a different pair of metrics in a different field.

Two instances, two fields, no shared mechanism demonstrated. `DP-009` says two
is not several, so it goes in both documents as an observation and is promoted
to nothing. If a third turns up I will want to write it down properly, and I
would rather have the count than the theory.

**And the record now has a GraphRAG document.** `SOTA-269` has said to keep a
knowledge base outside the weights for a while; nothing said how to tell
whether the one you built is any good. The first thing the corpus has to say
on that is a caution, which is an odd way to open a topic and is what the
evidence supported.
