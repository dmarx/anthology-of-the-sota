`#290`'s triage put ViT and CLIP at the top of the queue as **defects rather
than candidates**: 47 documents in this record say "ViT" and 45 say "CLIP",
and the record held no note for either paper. This entry is the filing, and
the two things reading them turned up that the triage could not have.

## The first: the practice is never the architecture

Both papers gave a practice whose citable form is wrong, and in both cases
the correct form was in the paper and got dropped on the way out.

**ViT** is cited as "transformers beat CNNs". The paper does not say that. It
says large-scale training trumps inductive bias, and then measures where
"large" starts: with hyperparameters and regularisation held fixed on random
JFT subsets, ViT-B/32 is *much worse* than a comparable ResNet-50 at 9M
images and better at 90M+. Pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, ViT-Large is worse
than ViT-Base. A domain prior is a **substitute for data**, and below the
crossover it is the better bet. `SOTA-tmp9k3cq` therefore states the
threshold, not the architecture — because "use a ViT" is a fact about
2020–2026 defaults and the trade survives the next backbone.

**CLIP** is cited for the contrastive objective. The paper's justification
for it is an efficiency ladder nobody restates: predicting the caption's
words learns 3× slower than predicting a bag-of-words encoding of the same
text, and swapping predictive for contrastive gives a further 4×. That is a
claim about the cost of a supervision target, not about representation
quality, and `SOTA-tmph2zob` files it that way.

This is the same shape twice. **A paper's most transferable claim is usually
the conditional one, and conditionals do not survive citation.** Worth
watching for on the rest of `#290`'s queue, which is full of papers the
record will meet through what other documents say about them.

## The second: a consensus note that counted the record

Reading CLIP turned up a live error in `SOTA-196`, which says to report
zero-shot and in-distribution performance separately because a hyperparameter
can move them in opposite directions. Its `consensus_note` read:

> One clean measurement of the conflict, in a setting where both metrics
> mattered.

That was true of what the record had read and false about the literature.
CLIP measured the same conflict a year earlier and larger: fitting a
supervised linear classifier on ImageNet features raises ImageNet accuracy by
**9.2%** — "roughly 3 years of improvement in SOTA", the authors say — and
produces **no average improvement across seven natural distribution shifts**.
Not noise, either: the gain concentrates on ImageNetV2, the one shift dataset
built to follow ImageNet's own construction, while accuracy *falls* 4.7% on
ImageNet-R, 3.8% on ObjectNet, 2.8% on Sketch and 1.9% on ImageNet-A. CLIP
also sweeps 0- through 128-shot and fully supervised on the same features and
finds effective robustness decaying monotonically, so the trade is not a
property of one hyperparameter's setting but of how much
distribution-specific supervision the model has seen at all.

**The generalisable failure: a consensus reading is a judgement about a
community, and it is easy to write one that is really a judgement about the
corpus.** `luria.yaml` already warns that such a reading "needs grounds", and
this one had grounds — they were just grounds about the wrong population.
Every unfiled substrate paper is a chance for this to happen again, which is
an argument for `#290`'s tier A beyond tidiness.

## Two smaller things worth keeping

**Both papers' limitation sections are better than their abstracts, and CLIP's
is a `DP-010` specimen.** The sentence everybody cites — zero-shot matching a
supervised ResNet-50 on ImageNet — is in the abstract. §6 says that baseline
is "well below the overall state of the art" and estimates **~1000× more
compute** would be needed for zero-shot CLIP to reach it; that CLIP gets
**88% on MNIST, beaten by logistic regression on raw pixels**, because
nothing resembling MNIST is in the pre-training data; that performance
*drops* from zero-shot to one- and two-shot; and that the 27-dataset suite is
"somewhat haphazardly assembled" and "undeniably co-adapted" with CLIP, with
full validation sets repeatedly queried during development. ViT's equivalent
is quieter: its own self-supervised pre-training landed **4 points behind**
supervised, and contrastive pre-training was left as future work.

**A consensus reading verified in code beats one inferred from prominence.**
For `SOTA-tmp4xixm` (prompt templates and embedding-space ensembling) the
grounds are a file: `open_clip`'s `zero_shot_metadata.py` at `2d53460` holds
`OPENAI_IMAGENET_TEMPLATES` with exactly **80** entries — the count the paper
reports ensembling — plus a `SIMPLE_IMAGENET_TEMPLATES` of 7. A five-year-old
prompt list surviving verbatim as the default evaluation protocol is a fact
about adoption that took one `curl` to check, and is worth more than any
amount of "widely used".

## The network, third time

arXiv threw HTTP 406 again on the bulk metadata call and went through on the
same URL via `curl` with backoff. That is the throttling the previous entry
reached on the second attempt, and luria's own `throttled — HTTP 406` said so
before either. Full texts came from `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/<id>`, which
returned 200 for both papers and is the cheaper route when the reading
matters more than the metadata.
