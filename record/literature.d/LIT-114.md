# LIT-114: On Layer Normalization in the Transformer Architecture

Xiong et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2002.04745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745)

## Key takeaways

- Warmup had been a required, carefully hand-tuned stage with no account of
  *why*: it slows optimization down and adds hyperparameters, and everyone
  used it anyway. The paper's contribution is to explain it and then show
  when it can be dropped.
- The mean-field result: in the original Post-LN Transformer, which puts
  layer normalization between the residual blocks, the expected gradients of
  parameters near the output layer are large at initialization. A large
  learning rate applied to those gradients is what makes early training
  unstable, and warmup is the workaround.
- Move the normalization inside the residual block — Pre-LN — and the
  gradients are well-behaved at initialization, so the reason for warmup goes
  away. Pre-LN Transformers trained with no warmup stage reach comparable
  results in significantly less time and with less hyperparameter tuning.
- The general lesson the record cares about: a schedule that everyone treats
  as universal can turn out to be compensating for one architectural choice.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of [SOTA-100](../practices.d/SOTA-100.md), warmup proportional to model size. Read the practice
against the paper: this work says the *need* for warmup is a property of
where the normalization sits, and that under Pre-LN — which every model in
the record's modern half uses — it can be removed entirely. [LIT-152](LIT-152.md) reaches
a compatible conclusion for a different schedule parameter, finding that
Muon plus the architecture make batch-size warmup unnecessary. [SOTA-100](../practices.d/SOTA-100.md)
deserves a re-reading in that light; this note files the evidence rather
than making the call.
