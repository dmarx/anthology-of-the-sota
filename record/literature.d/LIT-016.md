# LIT-016: GPipe: Efficient Training of Giant Neural Networks using Pipeline Parallelism

Huang et al. (2018) — [ARXIV-1811.06965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06965)

## Key takeaways

- **Micro-batch splitting is the mechanism.** Split the mini-batch into `M`
  micro-batches and pipeline them across `K` partitions, then accumulate and
  take one synchronous step — so the optimization is unchanged and only the
  schedule differs
- **Bubble overhead** — idle time from partitioning — is
  `O((K−1)/(M + K−1))`, amortized over the micro-steps
- **Negligible once `M ≥ 4×K`**, the paper's empirical threshold. Helped by
  backward re-computation being schedulable early, without waiting on
  gradients from earlier layers
- **Peak activation memory** with re-materialization and partitioning is
  `O(N + (L/K) × (N/M))` — `N/M` the micro-batch size, `L/K` the layers per
  partition — against `O(N × L)` with neither
- **Only activation tensors cross partition boundaries**, so efficient
  scaling holds even without high-speed interconnects
- Scope: **any model expressible as a sequence of layers**. Demonstrated on a
  557M AmoebaNet (84.4% top-1, ImageNet-2012) and a 6B multilingual NMT model
- The bubble analysis **assumes evenly balanced partitions**, stated
  explicitly
