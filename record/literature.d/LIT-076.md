# LIT-076: DPM-Solver: A Fast ODE Solver for Diffusion Probabilistic Model Sampling in Around 10 Steps

Lu et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2206.00927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00927)

## Key takeaways

- Notices the diffusion ODE is **semi-linear** — a closed-form linear part plus
  the neural network — and solves the linear part **analytically**, leaving a
  change of variables to log-SNR that reduces the rest to an exponentially
  weighted integral of the noise-prediction model. Prior fast samplers handed
  the whole ODE to a black-box solver and paid for approximating the analytic
  half
- **4.70 FID at 10 NFE, 2.87 at 20** on CIFAR-10; **4–16× over previous
  training-free samplers**. First-, second- and third-order versions with
  convergence-order guarantees, plus uniform-in-λ and adaptive step schedules
- **DDIM is exactly DPM-Solver-1** (§4.1) — the first-order case. That
  retroactively explains DDIM and bounds it as the least accurate member of a
  family
- **Proposition 3.1: the exact solution is invariant to the noise schedule
  between endpoints.** The schedule does not change the solution being
  approximated, only where the steps land
- Works on pretrained discrete-time models by feeding non-integer times, which
  the authors report works and **hypothesise** is due to smooth positional time
  embeddings — a hypothesis holding up half the paper

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-043](../notes.d/NOTE-043.md).

The primary source of [SOTA-203](../practices.d/SOTA-203.md) — sample with a higher-order ODE
solver on weights you already trained. Filed by the [#121](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/121) audit; [NOTE-043](../notes.d/NOTE-043.md)
recorded that nothing was sourced here, which was that pass's policy rather
than a finding.

The reading sharpens how `SOTA-188`'s source should be read. `LIT-075`'s
reading records EDM's sampler as Heun's second-order method on a `ρ = 7` grid
between `σ_min = 0.002` and `σ_max = 80`, with those grid parameters tuned.
Proposition 3.1 says the exact solution does not depend on the schedule between
endpoints — so **EDM's `ρ` chooses where approximation error lands, not what is
being approximated.** That is a sharper reading than EDM offers of itself, and
the record now has both papers in view.
