**Filed `LIT-tmpzynnf` from the [#180](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/180) worklist for one sentence's worth of
mental model, and `THEORY-tmp38xax` is that sentence.**

Take two different state machines over the same states, start both in the same
place, feed both the same random string. Early on they mostly agree. Late on
they agree one time in `N`, which is chance. The theorem is about how fast the
middle ends: reading a random symbol applies a random permutation, so reading
a word is a random walk on `S_N × S_N`, the spectral gap is `1/(2N)`, and the
excess agreement above chance is bounded by `(1 − 1/2N)^T`.

So the quantity that tells you *which machine you are looking at* decays
exponentially in how much you have read. A learner that sees only statistics
is reading precisely that decaying quantity. Past the mixing time every
machine in the family looks the same — and the information wasn't lost by the
model or the optimizer. It was destroyed by the data.

That runs against the reflex that a longer context is more evidence. For a
structure that mixes, more of the same random input is strictly worse, and the
crossover point is computable from the gap.

**The theorem is clean and the regime is the part that will get dropped.** The
construction needs an alphabet of `Ω(N³ ln N)` — about 4.6 million symbols for
a hundred-state machine — and word lengths of `Ω(N² ln N)`. Nothing anyone
trains on has that shape. The result is an existence claim: *there is* a hard
family. The authors present it that way and do not overreach; the sentence
that will travel, though, is from related work — "suggesting our hardness
result has direct implications for a wide range of practical algorithms" —
and it rests entirely on Abbe et al.'s SQ–SGD equivalence, which holds with
large mini-batches or low-precision gradients and is quoted once and revisited
never. `DP-010`, in the position that principle predicts: the most citable
sentence in the paper is the only one with nothing under it.

**What made this worth a document rather than a note is that it gives the
limits cluster a third axis.** `SOTA-178` says to give a recurrence
vector-valued gating so it can track state "a softmax layer provably cannot".
`THEORY-038` says a decoder cannot compose over a long context in few layers.
Both are about **expressivity** — what the architecture can represent.
`SOTA-278` says to rule out the evaluation before reporting a model can't do
something — that's **measurement**. This is neither: it is **learnability**,
and it can disagree with expressivity in the direction that stings. A model
that provably represents a machine can still be provably unable to learn it by
any method that reads statistics.

I don't think the record had said that. It does now, and the theory's "what
this does not say" spends most of its length on keeping the three apart,
because reading a learnability result as an architecture verdict is the
obvious way to misuse it.

**No practice, and the thing I wanted to file is one experiment away.** The
implication that itches is: don't train on uniformly random sequences when
what you want learned is a hidden transition structure, and expect longer
random contexts to hurt. The argument for it is right there. The evidence
isn't — there are no experiments in the paper at all, and the mixing rate at
an alphabet of 2 or 256 rather than millions is simply not addressed.

Both measurements that would close it are cheap. Simulating walks to get
`P_agree` against sequence length at realistic `N` and `k` needs no training
whatsoever. Training a small model on random words at two context lengths and
showing the longer one does worse is an afternoon. They're in the note's open
questions, stated as what would move the theory rather than as things I
believe.

**And one pass-over that needed a different reason than the last.**
`2204.00300`, RNA-FM, ranked next by dwell. `2504.06209` was passed over
because none of the eighteen topics can express its claim. This one is the
opposite: the topics express it fine — pretrain self-supervised on the
unlabelled pool when labels are scarce — and that is the problem. It is the
founding claim of the entire self-supervised literature, the record carries it
implicitly across eighty practices that touch pretraining, and RNA-FM is a
domain instance rather than evidence that would change anyone's mind
(`DP-005`).

Also: the corpus holds no protein or biological sequence models at all — no
ESM, no AlphaFold. So filing the RNA descendant would land it on an empty
trunk, which is the mistake [#243](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/243) was opened about two hours ago.
Doing it again knowingly is worse than doing it once by accident. Whether this
anthology wants a biological-sequence trunk at all is a scope question for its
owner; I've left it as a question rather than answering it with a filing.

**A small thing worth noticing.** This paper's AI-use statement says the

on. The Duede paper filed earlier today carries a similarly detailed statement
— who used what, for which part, and who used nothing. Two papers in one day's
reading, from unrelated fields, both volunteering it at that granularity.
Recorded as an observation. Two is not several (`DP-009`).
