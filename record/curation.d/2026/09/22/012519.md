**Second and final unit of the re-read.** Nine papers had been triaged out
against one question; re-read on their merits, **seven merit filing and two do
not**. The first unit took the three that formed a cluster. This takes the
four independents and records the two declines, so the reasoning sits in one
place rather than in a commit message nobody greps.

**The strongest habit in this unit was checking which rows change one thing.**
*Leaner Transformers* reports twelve redesigned architectures and a 30–50%
parameter saving. Six of the twelve also halve the MLP width, so their savings
are not evidence for the heads-for-depth trade the title names. The other six
hold the MLP fixed, and five of those — XCiT-M, TNT-B, DaViT-B, XCiT-L,
DaViT-L — hold or improve accuracy at 29–53% fewer parameters. The practice is
filed on those five and says so. This is the same check that decided the
RNA-FM unit, where it went the other way and killed the practice.

**A tension filed as a tension.** `SOTA-190` says increase depth before any
other dimension. This says remove depth and buy it back with head count. The
axes are not identical and neither paper knows the other exists, so no
relation is declared and both documents name the other. That is the handling
the owner asked for on [#234](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/234) and it keeps being the right one: asserting the
connection invents a claim, filing nothing hides the question.

**One paper supplied something the record had been circling without a name.**
`THEORY-041` says unconstrained transformer matrices drift into a badly
conditioned shape and is `Proposed` because nothing intervenes on
conditioning alone. *Leaner Transformers* proves conditioning improves with
head count and then intervenes on it. Not the experiment `THEORY-041` asks
for — that wants conditioning separated from norm constraint — and the nearest
thing the record holds.

**The provenance gap was the surprise.** Three hundred practices about
building, training, quantizing, serving and evaluating models, and not one
about establishing where a model came from. No watermarking, no fingerprinting,
no provenance. `DP-004` again: an absence produces no unresolved code and no
unbound relation, so the machinery would never have pointed at it, and the
subject has already had public instances.

**And its method is built on the matrix this whole week has been about.**
GhostSpec fingerprints a model by the singular spectra of `W_q W_k^T` and
`W_v W_o`, because those products absorb permutation and rescaling. That
same product is the one whose spectral energy concentration predicts a
training crash and whose spectral norm bounds attention entropy. Two
literatures, one matrix, no citation in either direction. Recorded as an
observation, with the open question — does spectral-controlled training blur
the fingerprint? — in the note.

**The provenance practice is `Proposed` on a missing measurement, not on
thin results.** F1 0.9867 over 55 pairs is a good number. Every one of those
pairs is a case where derivation is real, and the case that decides whether
anyone can say anything publicly is two models that look alike because they
were trained alike. That is not in the paper, so the practice's conditions say
the method is evidence and not proof, and say it loudly, because a provenance
claim is the kind that ends up in an argument.

**One paper is filed partly for how it reports itself.** *Concepts Whisper*
grades its own six claims robust, partial or suggestive; reports a robust null
against the hypothesis it set out to confirm; and marks as partial the two
results a less careful paper would have put in the abstract. After a week of
finding abstracts that outran their appendices, the other case is worth
filing.

**Third instance of one shape, and the count stops.** Small singular
directions of a weight matrix carry disproportionate information; the residual
a low-rank branch hands its quantizer is not the negligible part; concept
directions live in the quiet tail of the unembedding covariance. Three
matrices, three unrelated literatures. I could not write down a mechanism
connecting them that I would defend, so it is counted in three documents and
promoted to nothing. `DP-009`.

**The two declines, and why they are not `Rejected`.** Neither is filed, so
neither has a status — declining to file is not the same as retiring
something. *The emergence of clusters in self-attention dynamics* is careful
mathematics about frozen weights as `t → ∞`; the gap between that and a
trained model is too wide for it to be evidence about one, and it underwrites
no practice the record holds. It is a candidate trunk if rank collapse is ever
filed, and that is recorded rather than lost. *Fine-tuning CLIP in spectral
space* perturbs principal singular subspaces instead of adding a low-rank
matrix — a real variant — evaluated on three multimodal-sentiment benchmarks
against `SOTA-184`, which the record already holds. The evidence is narrower
than the recommendation would have to be. `DP-005`.

**What the whole re-read cost and bought.** Cost: a second pass over fourteen
PDFs. Bought: two practices and two theories in the first unit, two practices
and two theories in this one, a subject the corpus was missing entirely, and
the correction of a `DP-004` failure I committed myself. The lesson is not
that the first triage was careless — it was answering the question it was
given — but that a triage is only as wide as its query, and a batch of papers
deserves one pass per question worth asking.

**A trap worth writing down, because it cost three dangling references.** The
previous unit cited documents filed in the unit before it, by their temp
codes, and lint was clean — because the branch was cut before CI concretized
those codes, so the temp files still existed locally. CI then concretized them
on `main` and the citations died. This run's lint caught three of them —
citations to temporary codes that had by then been concretized into
`LIT-520`, `LIT-517` and `NOTE-263`, leaving the citations resolving to
nothing — and they are fixed here along with a stale `inactive-ok` directive that
shipped in the same PR because I read only the head of the lint output and the
directive line sits at the bottom.

The rule that follows: **when a contribution cites documents merged earlier in
the same session, rebase onto `main` and look up the concrete codes before
citing them.** A temp code is only valid inside the contribution that mints
it. And read the whole of `luria lint`, not the first screen — `EXIT=0` covers
warnings, unresolved codes and stale directives alike, and only the first of
those is at the top.
