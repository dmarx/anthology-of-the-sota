`SOTA-279` was filed naming three things it did not cover. One of them —
retrieval — turned out not to exist as a subject. The other two are filed
here, and both land in `in-context-learning`, which did not exist when they
were named.

Worth noting that the vocabulary work paid immediately rather than
eventually: these are the first documents filed *into* the new topic rather
than retagged into it, and neither needed a judgement call about where it
went.

## The number everyone quotes, and the one that matters

Zero-shot chain of thought is remembered for MultiArith 17.7% → 78.7%. That
is a real number and it is against *zero-shot prompting*, which is the right
baseline for the paper's claim and the wrong one for a practitioner deciding
what to do.

The two sentences that matter are both in the paper and neither is in the
abstract's headline:

> While Zero-shot-CoT naturally underperforms Few-shot-CoT, it substantially
> outperforms standard Few-shot prompting with even 8 examples per task.

So the ordering is: hand-written chains beat the trigger sentence, the
trigger sentence beats eight-shot standard prompting, and everything beats
bare zero-shot. That ordering is the practice. It makes this a *first move*
rather than a better method, and it means `SOTA-279` is sharpened rather than
contradicted — write the exemplars, but measure the free version first and
find out what they buy on your task.

A practice sourced to the 17.7→78.7 number alone would have been true and
misleading, which is the shape `DP-005` and `SOTA-278` are both about from
different directions.

## Self-consistency counts answers, not reasoning

The method is a decoding change, not a prompt change — which is why it earns
`inference-optimization` alongside the new topic, and why it is the first
document to use the new topic *beside* an old one rather than in place of it.

The thing I wanted to get right is what its by-products are. The paper
reports better calibration and an uncertainty estimate, and it is tempting to
read those as the traces being more trustworthy. They are not: the statistic
comes from the distribution over *answers*. The authors say models sometimes
generate nonsensical reasoning paths, and `LIT-467`'s own error analysis found
two of fifty correct answers reached through incorrect chains.

So an evaluation that reads a chain as an explanation of the answer is not
rescued by sampling more chains. That connects straight to `SOTA-278`, filed
yesterday, and the note says so rather than leaving a reader to notice.

## What the practices carry that the papers bury

Both cost statements went into conditions rather than prose:

- Zero-shot CoT is **two generation calls** per question — reasoning
  extraction then answer extraction. The instruction is free; the inference
  is not.
- Self-consistency is **linear in paths**, and "five or ten" is the authors'
  own rule of thumb with no scaling story. Whether the saturation point moves
  with task difficulty is not measured, and that is exactly the thing a
  practitioner needs.

Also recorded: the "single task-agnostic prompt" claim is slightly stronger
than the method, because the answer-extraction stage differs between multiple
choice and free numeric. Small, and the kind of small thing that makes a
reproduction fail.

## Composition, noted and not claimed

Nothing prevents self-consistency from sampling chains elicited by a trigger
sentence instead of exemplars. It is the obvious combination and neither
paper measured it, so both practices say so and neither implies otherwise.
