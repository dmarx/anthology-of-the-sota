# LIT-055: P-Tuning v2: Prompt Tuning Can Be Comparable to Fine-tuning Universally Across Scales and Tasks

Liu et al. (2021) — [ARXIV-2110.07602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07602)

## Key takeaways

- The paper is a correction to a prior claim rather than a new family. Prompt
  tuning was known to match fine-tuning at very large scale and to fall short
  below it, and to fail on hard sequence-labeling tasks. The diagnosis here is
  that tuning only the input embeddings gives the optimizer too little to work
  with and too indirect a route to the layers that matter.
- The fix is depth: continuous prompts as prefixes at *every* layer, not just
  the first. That raises the tuned-parameter count from ~0.01% to 0.1–3% of
  the model, which is still small, and closes the gap.
- With that change, the result is comparable to full fine-tuning across model
  sizes from 330M to 10B and across task types including sequence labeling —
  the two axes the earlier claim did not hold on.

## Standing in the anthology

**Carries no practice, deliberately.** The record recommends low-rank
adaptation for the job this method also does — the practice sourced to
[LIT-046](LIT-046.md) — and recommending both would be recommending neither.

Kept `Active` because the comparison is the content. This is the strongest
version of the branch of parameter-efficient fine-tuning that did *not*
become the default, and it is worth knowing why it lost rather than assuming
it was worse: it is competitive on quality, and it costs at inference. A
prefix occupies context at every layer and every forward pass, where a
low-rank update folds into the weight and disappears. The deciding property
was deployment shape, not accuracy — which is a thing worth being able to
say, and which a corpus holding only the winner cannot say.

Read — [NOTE-tmp2t6lw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp2t6lw.md). The reading confirms "competitive on quality" as a
measured claim: fine-tuning parity from 330M to 10B and on hard sequence
labelling, at 0.1-3% of parameters. It also surfaces something neither this
paper nor [LIT-378](LIT-378.md) could have said, because they are four years and one
mechanism apart — **both conclude that coverage decides and the knob people
tune does not.** Here it is prompt depth that closes the gap while
reparameterization is inconsistent across datasets; in QLoRA it is the number
of adapted matrices while the rank `r` has no effect across the sweep. That
is a claim about parameter-efficient adaptation rather than about either
method, and `SOTA-231` is where it currently lives, held by one paper.

Not treated as replication of `SOTA-231`, and the distinction matters:
different mechanism, different setting, four years apart. `ADR-010` — contrast
is not support — has a cousin here, which is that structural agreement is not
measurement either.
