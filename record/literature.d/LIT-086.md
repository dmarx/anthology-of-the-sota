# LIT-086: K-Planes: Explicit Radiance Fields in Space, Time, and Appearance

Fridovich-Keil et al. (2023) — [ARXIV-2301.10241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10241)

## Key takeaways

- Represents a `d`-dimensional scene with **`C(d,2)` planes** — one per pair of
  axes. Static is `d = 3`, dynamic is `d = 4`, and moving between them changes a
  number rather than a design. **1000× compression** over a full 4D grid, in
  **pure PyTorch** with no custom kernels
- **The factorisation is chosen for what it makes expressible.** Because each
  plane spans a named pair of axes, **dimension-specific priors have somewhere to
  attach** — temporal smoothness on the planes involving time, multi-resolution
  structure on the spatial ones — and **static/dynamic decomposition falls out**,
  since the planes not involving time describe what does not move
- **A linear feature decoder with a learned colour basis matches a nonlinear
  black-box MLP decoder.** Once the representation carries the structure, the
  decoder need not
- Competitive and often state-of-the-art fidelity across synthetic and real,
  static and dynamic, fixed and varying-appearance scenes

## Standing in the anthology

Read — [NOTE-037](../notes.d/NOTE-037.md). Nothing is sourced here.

What leaves the domain is that a factorisation indexed by the axes you have
assumptions about **gives each assumption somewhere to live.** The record's
architecture practices reason about capacity and connectivity; a representation
that makes a prior *expressible* is doing something a more general one cannot, at
equal capacity.
