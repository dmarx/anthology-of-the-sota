Twenty-one relation blurbs, one relation rewired, and a request from two
previous passes withdrawn.

## Two passes asked for the wrong thing, and I was the second

`ADR-036` looked at `SOTA-083` (custom kernels for critical ops) joined to
`SOTA-085` (flash attention) by `compared_against`, saw that it read as a
comparison nobody had run, and concluded the record needed a `specializes:`
relation. `ADR-tmpv09o2` — mine, hours ago — repeated that and called it the
highest-value scheme change on the table.

Both of us reasoned from the edge to a missing word. Neither checked whether
the words already there had definitions. They did not: `references:` entries
carried `scheme`, `required`, `many` and `converse`, and nothing that said
what the relation *asserts*. The generated contract page makes the asymmetry
plain — `status`, `consensus` and `tags` each render with an italic gloss,
and every relation renders as `optional, one or more SOTA codes when
present`.

So the question "is this a comparison or a specialization?" had no place to
be answered, and two passes read that absence as a gap in the vocabulary
rather than a gap in its documentation. **A vocabulary nobody wrote down is
indistinguishable from a vocabulary that is missing a word.**

## The knowledge existed in a form nothing could use

`luria.yaml` already carried paragraphs about these fields — what `source:`
holds, why `introduced_by:` is separate, how a practice line differs from a
paper line. All of it in YAML comments, which is precisely the case luria's
own `Reference` docstring makes for the `label:`/`blurb:` keys it has
supported since `LU-#254`:

> What a relation IS, as data rather than as a TOML comment nothing could
> render, quote or scaffold from.

No record had filled them in, luria's own included. So this was not writing
new knowledge, it was moving existing knowledge from a place that renders
nowhere to a place that renders on the page a filer reads while filing.

## Writing the definition dissolved the question

Drafting `extends`'s blurb was the whole decision:

> the earlier practice this one builds on and could not stand without — a
> later version of the same recommendation, a narrower case of it, or a rule
> that only exists because the earlier one is followed

Three senses, which from a distance look like three relations and are one
assertion: *B carries A's claim further and does not stand without A*. The
line reads oldest-first in all three, and the flash-attention line proves it
— `SOTA-106` is a later version, `SOTA-085` is a narrower case, `SOTA-161` is
a rule that only exists because flash attention is used, and all three sit
correctly under one arrow:

    custom kernels for critical ops
      flash attention
        flash-attention-2
        FP32 attention output

That line did not exist before this pass. It was three practices and a
comparison nobody ran.

## What did not change, and that is the point

The edge is still unbound. `SOTA-083` is `systems-optimization` and
`SOTA-085` is `attention-techniques`, so the report still shows the crossing
— now as part of a line instead of as a comparison. `SOTA-085`'s frontmatter
asked that this edge *"keep showing up until someone answers it"*; what got
answered is what the relation is, not what the tags are, and the second
question is still open and still correctly reported.

Worth keeping separate, because it would have been easy to claim this pass
closed both.
