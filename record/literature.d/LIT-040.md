# LIT-040: Scaling Laws for Autoregressive Generative Modeling

Henighan et al. (2020) — [ARXIV-2010.14701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14701)

## Key takeaways

- Extends the Kaplan-era scaling laws to **four more domains** — generative
  image, video, multimodal image↔text, and mathematical problem solving — and
  finds the same **power-law-plus-constant** form, the constant being the
  data's irreducible entropy
- **`N_opt(C) ∝ C^β` with β ≈ 0.7, nearly the same in every domain.** The
  cross-domain universality of the *exponent* is the headline
- **That exponent implies `D ∝ N^0.4` — sub-linear data scaling**, and the
  paper says so: "strongly suggests sub-linear scaling of dataset size with
  model size", conditioned on one epoch. **Chinchilla later measured `D ∝ N`.
  Do not take this number from here**
- **Section 6 names the inconsistency that undid it:** the `L(C)` and `L(D)`
  trends, extrapolated, cross — and cannot, since every learning curve is
  bounded below by `L(D)`. The authors also warn their projections are
  extremely sensitive, moving visibly under a ±5% change in the exponent
- **Model size buys no strong generalisation.** On mathematical extrapolation
  and on off-distribution image loss, performance depends predominantly on
  in-distribution loss and is otherwise independent of model size

sits in the anthology as `Active` while containing a prominently derived
`D ∝ N^0.4` that the record elsewhere says is wrong. The takeaways it carried —
"universal scaling behaviours", "compute-optimal model sizing", "resource
allocation guidance" — advertised exactly that conclusion without stating it,
so a reader could neither use the paper nor know to distrust it. They now name
the number *and* say it was overturned. Not retiring a superseded paper, but
stopping it being quietly wrong.

(sub-linear, 2020) → Chinchilla (1:1, 2022) → `LIT-099` (1:1 confirmed
