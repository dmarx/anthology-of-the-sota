The consensus re-derivation was supposed to be a small tidy-up after
[#58](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/58): seventeen practices had their support lists changed without
their consensus readings being re-read. Two of those readings needed work.
The pass found something else on the way, and the something else is the more
useful half.

## What re-reading the notes found

`SOTA-141` was `emerging` while its own note ended "the field has not moved".
`emerging` means "several independent groups, moving toward default"; the
note says the opposite in plain words, three lines below the value. It is now
`unreplicated`, with the note saying precisely what [LIT-145](../../record/literature.d/LIT-145.md) corroborates (the
endpoint) and what it does not (the claim).

`SOTA-125` said "at one scale" while its sources span 90M and 1.5B. The value
survives — two scales from one group is still one group — and the note did
not.

That is two out of twenty-two, which is roughly what a tidy-up should look
like.

## What the method could not see

[#58](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/58)'s candidate list was *codes cited in the body that `source:` does not
name*. Every batch could therefore **add** a source and none could **remove**
one, because a code wrongly present in the field produces no body-only
citation to flag it. `SOTA-132` was caught in the last batch only because it
happened to be missing a source as well.

Asking the opposite question — which `source:` entries are model reports? —
returns nineteen practices, and most are fine: Falcon-H1-Tiny is a blogpost
full of its own ablations, and a report that ran the experiment is a source
whatever else it is. Three were not fine, and one of them says so itself:

> Then four generations of adoption rather than one result:
> — `SOTA-147`, *The evidence*

All four were in its source list. The practice wrote down the distinction
[ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md) would later make, and then filed against it.

## The lesson, which is about passes and not about sources

**A pass finds the defect its query is shaped like, and is silent on the
inverse.** Nothing about [#58](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/58) was wrong; it was a search for absence, so it
could not see presence. The check [ADR-017](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-017.md) shipped has the same shape —
`source:` ⊆ the body — and the same blind spot, which is worth knowing before
someone treats a clean run as a clean record.

The general form: after a pass over a relation, run it once in the other
direction. It is cheap, it takes minutes, and here it found three practices
whose consensus readings could not be checked against their own evidence
because the evidence and the adoption were in the same list.
