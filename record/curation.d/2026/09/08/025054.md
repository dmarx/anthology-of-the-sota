**The sweep is done.** Every post-migration note carrying a practice-eligible
topic now either sources a practice or says why it does not, in its own
Standing section, in terms a reader can disagree with.

## The count

Twenty-four practices added across six clusters, 157 → 181. But the
distribution is the part worth keeping:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `Proposed` | 14 | 28 |
| carrying a `consensus:` value | 28 | 50 |

**The provisional half of the status vocabulary doubled.** That is the direct
consequence of the entry rule this all started from — file the claim, let
`status:` carry the belief — and it is the measurable version of what
[DP-006](../../docs/design-principles.md) argues. A vocabulary whose provisional values were used fourteen
times in a corpus of 157 was barely a vocabulary; at 28 of 181 it is doing
work.

## What the sweep actually found, sorted by kind

Not one kind of defect. Six, and only the first was the one it went looking
for:

1. **Refused for adoption** — the `Active` bar applied to whether a document
   should exist. Three cases, all refused in writing, one of them in an ADR.
2. **Never noticed at all** — deduplication and multi-token prediction, in
   every pipeline the record describes, argued about by nobody, filed by
   nobody.
3. **A trunk with its branches filed** — three times, in three unrelated
   subjects, found by three different methods.
4. **A refinement filed before the thing it refines** — the loss-masking
   footnote four months ahead of the practice of constructing a fill-in-the-
   middle sample at all.
5. **A line entered halfway** — the interleave practice beginning two steps
   after the interleave does, losing the fork that explains its own shape.
6. **Exegesis filed as a note** — four papers read closely, in detail,
   because a frontier report cited them, and filed as explanations of
   somebody else's decision rather than as advice.

The through-line is in [DP-007](../../docs/design-principles.md): the filing habit runs on arrivals, and
none of these six had one.

## What it cost, honestly

Six pull requests of content, plus three of infrastructure that the content
work forced: views moved off branches, runs serialized per ref, and a
skip-marker caution corrected. Two of those three were caused by my own
mistakes — regenerating shared files on branches, and merging four pull
requests in forty seconds — and both were the kind that only appear at a
tempo a person would not have reached.

That is worth naming as a cost of working this way rather than as bad luck.
**Working faster than a human would find the same bugs faster, and it finds
bugs a human would not have hit at all.** Both halves are real, and the
second one is not free: three of the nine contributions here were repairs to
damage the pace caused.

## The one number that says it worked

Not the practice count. **Zero.** That is how many post-migration notes with
a practice-eligible topic now lack either a practice or a written reason —
and it is checkable in about fifteen lines of Python, which is the property
that makes it worth having. The count of practices will drift. The invariant
either holds or it does not, and the next person can find out in a minute.

The invariant is also, per [DP-004](../../docs/design-principles.md), evidence about exactly one query. It says
nothing about the inherited 119 notes, which were never audited under these
rules, and nothing about whether the practices filed are *good*. Those are
different passes and they are filed as such.
