Twelve papers. This one was named in the previous pass's uncovered list, read
for that reason, and the first useful thing it produced was not in its own
abstract — it was in its bibliography.

**The record spent four passes calling a paper independent without opening its
author list.** `SOTA-154` was promoted on `LIT-233`, and the promotion turned
entirely on that paper being an outside check: "an independent group", "a
group with no stake in the result", "nobody here wanted ES to win". The
practice's own condition had excluded "further results from the same group",
so the classification was load-bearing.

**Yulu Gan, `LIT-233`'s first author, is the second author of `LIT-211`.**
It is in `LIT-211`'s author list, in `LIT-233`'s bibliography, and in the
related-work section where that paper cites its own prior work. Nothing was
hidden. Four passes read around it.

The reason is worth naming because it is general. **Independence was inferred
from affiliation and framing** — a different institution, a different senior
author, a paper about something else, the ES comparison arriving as a baseline
rather than as a claim. Every one of those is a proxy, and all of them pointed
the same way, which is exactly when a proxy stops being questioned. The actual
test is one line: *is any author of the new paper an author of the old one?*
It costs a glance at two author lists and it was never run.

**The correction that matters is not to the status.** `SOTA-154` stays
`Active`, because Hoy et al. at 4B with both arms swept and ES highest on all
four tasks is a clean independent positive, and Ba et al., EGGROLL and ESSAM
share no author with `LIT-211` either. The practice would have reached the
same place. **But it would not have got there when it did, on what it did**,
and a record whose promotions rest on misread provenance is failing at the one
thing it exists to do. The consensus note is now tallied by authorship rather
than by institution, and doing that moved four of eight positive results
inside the line they had been counted as independent of.

The corollary, and it belongs beside `DP-005`: **counting is only as good as
the equivalence class you count over.** That principle separates adoption from
evidence; this is the same failure a level down, where several "independent"
results turn out to be one group's line with different mastheads.

**A second thing this paper corrected, in the opposite direction.**
`SOTA-213` has now been rewritten twice in this branch. It began as a
stopping rule, was rewritten when the prior-task dip turned out to recover,
and is rewritten again here — because rise-then-decay means the *target*
reward really does peak and then degrade, so early stopping is right after
all, for a different curve. Prior-task accuracy dips and recovers; target
reward rises and falls. Two curves, two answers, and the first two drafts each
had one of them.

That is three versions of one practice in one afternoon, and it is not a sign
the practice was badly conceived. It is a sign of what filing a line **in
citation order rather than publication order** does: each paper arrived
already answered by one the record had not read yet. Liang et al. is January,
Hoy is April, Schweighofer is May; the record met them backwards.

**On `THEORY-006` and `THEORY-007`, which are not rivals.** They look
like competing explanations of the same phenomenon and they are not, because
`LIT-233` says so itself: the thicket is a broad pretrained basin intersected
with a low-dimensional, degenerate set of task directions, and it cites Liang
for the second half. **The record filed the later paper first and would have
filed them as rivals had the citation not been checked** — the same failure as
the authorship one, caught this time because reading the related-work section
is part of a full reading.

Their one real disagreement is about scale, and it is methodological rather
than substantive: `LIT-233` fixes `σ = 1e-3` at every model size and finds
density rising 0% to 64% across 0.5B–32B; `LIT-236` picks a viable `σ`
per model and finds accessibility flat across 0.5B–7B. A perturbation scale
that suits a 32B model need not suit a 0.5B one, which would produce exactly
this pattern with no disagreement about the geometry. **That also weakens the
scale-boundary table this record built two passes ago**, whose low end may be
a tuning artifact rather than a threshold. The table stays, with the competing
reading beside it.

**What this pass did not cover.** No new names came out of this paper's
references that the record lacks — the first time that has been true in this
line. What remains outstanding is the same two items as last time, both now
overdue: the bibliography sweep prescribed two entries ago and still not run
on any of the twelve papers, and `LIT-235`'s linear-mode-connectivity
result, which if it generalizes is a claim about post-training reaching one
basin regardless of optimizer and still has no document of its own.

And one new one, cheap and specific: **check authorship overlap when filing a
paper into an existing line**, and record the answer rather than the
impression. It would have caught this in the pass that introduced it.
