The retag sweep, aimed at the invariant report. Twenty-two unbound relations
to sixteen, six documents changed, `ADR-036` superseded in one of its four
categories.

## The report had already been read

Worth saying first, because it changes what "sweep the invariant failures"
means. `ADR-036` classified twenty-four unbound relations, retagged fifteen
documents and removed two spurious links. The twenty-two standing when this
pass started were **exactly** the ones it left, deliberately, with reasons
written down.

So there was no backlog of missed work here. The only question worth asking
was narrower: does `ADR-046`'s liberal tagging change any of `ADR-036`'s four
verdicts? One, and only one. Its fourth category ends with *trunk papers whose
contribution spans two topics*, left alone because "splitting a trunk paper's
topic would be filing it under half its contribution". That is an argument
against **splitting**, written when one tag was the norm and splitting was the
only move. Carrying both was not on the table. Now it is.

## The discipline was the work

The test from `ADR-046` — *would this tag go on if the relation did not
exist?* — sounds like a formality and is not. Eleven candidates looked
plausible when I listed them from titles. Three failed on inspection, and
they are the more instructive half.

**`SOTA-085` and `SOTA-106` (flash attention) do not gain
`systems-optimization`**, though the tag is defensible and the document's own
body is about kernels, on-chip memory per SM and tile sizes. What stopped it
was reading the frontmatter: a `flash-attention` tag had been **removed** from
this very document, by an earlier pass, precisely because it had been added to
bind this edge. The comment left behind says the edge *"is real, it crosses a
fault line in the vocabulary, and it should keep showing up until someone
answers it."* Binding it now with a different word would be the same act with
better camouflage. The answer it is waiting for is a `specializes:`
relation — `SOTA-085` *is an instance of* `SOTA-083` — and that is a scheme
change, not a tag.

**`SOTA-031` does not gain `training-optimization`.** Its claim is about
memory and throughput per device; the practices it crosses, `SOTA-092` and
`SOTA-093`, are about sample efficiency. The tag would have bound the edge and
been false.

**`SOTA-161` does not gain `attention-techniques`**, though it is literally
about the attention output. `SOTA-085`'s body argues its filing directly — the
rounding bias compounds over a long run, so *"it is filed under stability
rather than attention for exactly that reason"*. When the record has already
reasoned about a document's filing in prose, overriding it silently from
outside is not a retag, it is a disagreement pretending to be maintenance.

That is the pattern worth keeping: **where the record argues its own filing,
the retag pass defers.** It held three times here, twice in frontmatter
comments and once in a decision.

## Why supersede rather than amend

`ADR-036`'s verdict was *right when it was made*. Under minimal tagging,
splitting GShard between conditional computation and automatic sharding would
genuinely have filed it under half its contribution, and leaving it alone was
the better of the two available moves. Editing that reasoning out of the
record would lose the fact that it was correct — `DP-003`, keep what you
stopped believing. So the old decision keeps its body, gains a
`status_note:` saying which category moved, and points at the successor.

## What is left, and what it is asking for

Sixteen relations, and all of them wanted. Seven are `analysis-and-evaluation`
crossings, where the invariant is over-strong because that topic names a *kind
of work* rather than a subject — every line that grows an analysis paper will
unbind. `ADR-036` said the fix belongs to the chain declaration and this pass
agrees; retagging exactly those seven would be indistinguishable from its
legitimate half.

The other nine are the general-and-instance and rival-fixes-at-different-
layers shapes. Both are true things about the work. The first of them is a
standing request for a `specializes:` relation, made twice now by two
different passes, and it is the single highest-value scheme change this report
is asking for.
