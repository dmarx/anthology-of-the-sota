`#161` said "We have RWKV7, but none of the work that led up to it." That is
true and it undersells the gap: the missing trunk is not RWKV's, it is linear
attention's, and the record had been arguing about it for months.

## The shape of the absence

Grepping for `linear attention` returns fourteen mentions across twelve
documents. Every one of them is a claim about the same construction:

| document | says |
|---|---|
| `LIT-194` | "linear attention has no decay term, so it cannot forget" |
| `LIT-176` | "efficient and **compromises recall over long** contexts" |
| `LIT-195` | "linear attention **lacks precise** associative recall" |
| `SOTA-167`, `LIT-165` | "'linear attention won' is not the right summary" |

Those are not four topics. They are four statements about what
`φ(Q)(φ(K)ᵀV)` gives up relative to a softmax — the trade Katharopoulos et
al. made in 2020 — and the record could not point at the paper that made it.

`DP-007` predicts this precisely: what everyone agrees on has no author, so
it has no arrival event, so nothing prompts anyone to file it. The delta-rule
and SSM lines were filed because they are *arguments*; the thing they argue
with went unwritten.

## Two false leads worth recording

**"RWKV lineage" is the wrong search.** Looking for RWKV-4 and Eagle/Finch
finds two papers and stops. The question that finds four is *what does the
record assume when it says linear attention*, and the answer reaches back
past RWKV entirely.

**AFT looks skippable and is not.** An Attention Free Transformer is not a
live recommendation, nothing here is measured on it, and by citation count it
would not clear the bar `#137` set. It earns its place structurally: the
`W` in RWKV is AFT's learned position bias, so without it the line has a
step where three of four letters appear unexplained. A bridge document is
worth filing when its absence makes two documents that *are* load-bearing
unreadable as a sequence.

## What it changes for the practice that needed it

`SOTA-178` recommends vector-valued gating and in-context learning rates,
and its conditions section says: *"RWKV-7 'Goose', 0.19B to 2.9B. Nothing
else in the record."* That sentence reads one way if RWKV-7 is a one-off and
another if it is the fifth step of a line that has been scaled to 14B and
re-derived four times. It is the second, and the reader can now see that
without leaving the record.

Two further claims were being made against the same absence: `SOTA-135` says
RWKV reached a correction-style update "independently and a year earlier"
than the line it sits beside, and `LIT-194` makes the same timing
observation. Both are claims about *when this lineage did something*, argued
while the record held only its latest member.

## The chain binds, and that was not automatic

All five documents share `attention-techniques`, so the new line adds
nothing to the unbound-lineage report, which stayed at 0 relations and 0
lines. Worth checking rather than assuming: the report was emptied two
contributions ago, and the first new chain filed after that is exactly where
a regression would show up.
