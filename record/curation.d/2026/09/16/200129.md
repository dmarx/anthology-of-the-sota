[#139](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/139) closes with two loose ends, and the second one — *"no reading in the port
was re-verified against its paper"* — turned out to be the one that mattered.

I did not set out to audit anything. I set out to file five practices, and a
practice has to quote numbers, so I went to five papers to get them right.

**Two of the five readings were wrong, and both in the direction that hurts.**

`NOTE-100` says to prefer decentralized SGD "when inter-node bandwidth is
below ~1 Gbps or latency exceeds ~5ms". The 5 ms is the paper's — it labels a
figure panel with it. The 1 Gbps is not in `LIT-302` anywhere. The paper's
low-bandwidth configuration is **10 Mbps**, and its bandwidth figure sweeps
`1/bandwidth` out to 1 Mbps. So the reading turned a tested configuration into
a recommended threshold a hundred times higher, which would tell a reader to
abandon their parameter server on a network where the paper's own sweeps put
both methods in the flat region.

`NOTE-153` places `LIT-323`'s experiments on "CIFAR-10 and ImageNet". They are
CIFAR-10. It also files the anchor model's `alpha` and `beta` as a variant for
non-IID data; the anchor model is the method, and those are its standard
settings.

The other three held. `LIT-056`'s warmup ramp is 75%, 93.75%, 98.4375%, 99.6%,
99.9% exactly as the note says. `LIT-254`'s exact consensus in `log2(n)` rounds
is in the appendix, verbatim. `LIT-315`'s `O(n^3/s^2)` and its 1.7× are in the
abstract.

Two of five. Small sample, one direction of query, and a lower bound — I only
checked the sentences I needed. `DP-004` says what a clean run on the other
three is evidence about.

## The first loose end cannot be closed and should be struck

`LIT-017` and `LIT-028` were supposed to have two readings each, with only one
filed, and [#139](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/139) asks for them to be compared. **The second readings are not in
this repository.** They were in the import and were dropped at port time,
because notes already existed; `data/` is the *old anthology*, not the incoming
batch. There is nothing to compare.

What was possible is the more useful half, so I did that instead: I re-read
`NOTE-062`'s paper. Everything `SOTA-198` rests on checks out — the eight
domains, the noise scale rising as the loss falls, the independence from
training set size. And it turned up the thing this whole session kept turning
up.

## The contradictions were not contradictions, three times running

[#139](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/139) named four, called them "the valuable ones", and every one of them
dissolved on contact with the papers. Not because the issue was careless —
because the readings were made one paper at a time, and a contradiction
between two papers is visible only from a third place.

- **LAMB against Shallue** (closed in [#146](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/146)): LAMB cites Shallue approvingly,
  as its motivation.
- **DiLoCo's `H = 500` against post-local SGD's 16 to 32**: two numbers chosen
  against different objectives, on schemes that do different things at the
  synchronisation.
- **SlowMo against `SOTA-155`**: `LIT-212`'s related work names `LIT-373` as
  where its outer optimizer came from. The record had them filed as rivals;
  one is the other's parent.
- **The "duplicated" noise scale**: not duplicated. Two different quantities
  with the same name — and `LIT-017` cites `LIT-305` for predicting a
  dataset-size dependence, adding *"which we do not observe"*.

Four for four. The pattern is worth naming because it will recur: **a reading
records what a paper says; it does not record what the paper says about the
other papers in your record.** Related-work sections are where the field does
its own reconciling, and they are the part a summariser skips. Three of these
four were settled by a related-work paragraph.

## What I did to my own drafts, which is the same mistake one level up

I wrote all five practices from the notes first, then went to the papers. Two
of the five had to be rewritten — including one whose *title* carried the 1
Gbps number. Had I filed from the notes and stopped, this record would now
carry a threshold that no paper supports, in a title, with a citation that
resolves.

The lint cannot catch that, and neither can review. The only thing that
catches it is opening the paper, which costs about ten minutes per practice
and is the difference between a citation and a claim.
