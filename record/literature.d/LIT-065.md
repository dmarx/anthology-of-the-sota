# LIT-065: Using DeepSpeed and Megatron to Train Megatron-Turing NLG 530B, A Large-Scale Generative Language Model

Smith et al. (2022) — [ARXIV-2201.11990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11990)

## Key takeaways

- **3D parallelism**: 8-way tensor × 35-way pipeline, data-parallel over the
  rest, on 560 DGX A100 nodes. 530B parameters — 105 layers, hidden 20480, 128
  heads, sequence 2048
- **Why all three are needed:** the batch size a model can train with has an
  upper bound, so at ~4000 GPUs pure data parallelism "would only allow for a
  batch size of 1 per GPU". That ceiling is `LIT-017`'s critical batch size —
  the reason model parallelism exists is statistical, not architectural
- **A complete published recipe**, at a detail level frontier reports abandoned
  soon after: LR 5.0e-5, 1B tokens linear warmup, cosine to 10% over 340B;
  **batch ramped 32 → 1920 in steps of 32 over the first 12B tokens**; Adam
  β₁ = 0.9, **β₂ = 0.95**, ε = 1e-8; clip 1.0; weight decay 0.1; init
  `N(0, σ²)` with **σ ≈ √(1/(3H))**
- **Three stability findings:** β₂ was lowered from the standard 0.99
  **specifically "to reduce spikes in the training loss"**; higher-variance
  initialisation fails to converge; and the learning rate was *projected* from
  a model-size plot rather than swept, because higher rates destabilise
- **Per-GPU throughput falls as the cluster grows** — 126 / 121 / 113
  teraFLOP/s at 280 / 350 / 420 nodes against a 312 peak. And **270B tokens for
  530B parameters**, about 0.5 tokens per parameter

Two things are worth carrying. Lowering **β₂ to suppress loss spikes** is a
stability lever the record names nowhere, reported here as an operational
finding at 530B; unablated, which is why no practice is filed, but the
stability neighbourhood (`SOTA-035`, `SOTA-052`) has a gap where it belongs.

And the **`LIT-017` connection** is the more valuable one: the record carries
tensor and pipeline parallelism as systems techniques with systems
justifications, when the cause of the whole arrangement is a statistical
property of the gradient. The 32→1920 batch ramp is likewise exactly what
`LIT-017` predicts from a noise scale that grows as the loss falls. Neither
paper cites the other.

At **0.5 tokens per parameter** against Chinchilla's ~20, this is also the
corpus's clearest artefact of the era `LIT-040` derived — a 530B model trained
on fewer tokens than a 7B model gets today. The recipe outlived the model.
