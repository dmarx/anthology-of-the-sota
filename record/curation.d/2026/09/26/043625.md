Top of the reversal list from the `#290` re-read, taken as a pair because
guided backpropagation is defined as a variant of the deconvnet and neither
reads alone. `SOTA-430` has had a headline failure case since it was filed —
Guided Backprop's maps staying "visually and quantitatively similar" after the
weights above the lowest layers are destroyed — and until now the record could
state that verdict without being able to say what the method was.

## The finding

Guided backprop's masking rule, `LIT-tmpw88iy` §4.2, is one sentence. At each
ReLU, zero the gradient wherever **either** the top gradient or the bottom
activation is negative — both masks at once, where plain backprop applies the
bottom one and the deconvnet applies the top one.

The interesting part is **why the method exists**. Springenberg et al. were not
building an attribution method; they were building an all-convolutional net,
and their architecture has no max-pooling. The deconvnet needs max-pooling
"switches" recorded in a forward pass, so they needed a version that works
without them. They found one, and reported it as the win:

> unlike the 'deconvnet', guided backpropagation works remarkably well without
> switches

> the 'deconvnet' approach fails completely in the absence of switches

> In a sense, the bottom-up signal in form of the pattern of bottom ReLU
> activations substitutes the switches.

The deconvnet's dependence on a forward pass through the trained model is what
made it awkward. Guided backprop removes that dependence and the maps get
*better*. Four years later, `LIT-713` measures that those maps survive
destroying the weights.

**Generalising: a method's selling point and its later defect can be the same
property, described from two directions.** "Works without needing the trained
model's forward pass" in 2014 and "insensitive to the trained model's weights"
in 2018 are not obviously the same sentence, and the record now holds both ends
of it.

## The over-reach I did not take, and why it was tempting

That last quote makes the mechanism look settled: if the bottom ReLU pattern
substitutes for the switches, then the map's structure comes from the input, and
of course it survives randomizing the weights.

**It does not follow.** Bottom ReLU activations are computed by the network —
randomize the weights and they change. Springenberg's sentence says the bottom
pattern can do the *job* the switches did, which is conditioning the
reconstruction on the image; it does not say the map is independent of the
weights. The actual argument in the literature is about the sign masking
composing to a non-negative operator, and it is not in this paper.

So the record now holds three pieces and not the fourth:

| piece | where |
| --- | --- |
| the masking rule | `LIT-tmpw88iy` §4.2 |
| that the maps survive randomization | `LIT-713` |
| that this happens "for different reasons" than the multiplier methods | `LIT-725`, one sentence |
| **why** | **nowhere** |

`THEORY-113` covers the multiplier side only, and says so. No theory is filed
here, and the paper that argues the missing one is named with a **verified**
identifier rather than gestured at: Sixt, Granz and Landgraf (2020), *When
Explanations Lie: Why Many Modified BP Attributions Fail*, `1912.09818`. I
resolved that id against arXiv rather than recalling it, which is the habit this
session has needed twice.

**That is the improvement over "a later literature the record does not hold",**
which is the phrase `SOTA-430` carried for a day before this run and which
turned out to mean one specific unread paper. A gap sentence that names its
paper is a task; one that does not is a mood.

## What the second paper turned out to be for

Zeiler and Fergus went in as "the deconvnet, the thing guided backprop is a
variant of". It is that, and it is also something the dependence count could
not see.

**Occlusion sensitivity is the origin of the perturbation-based family, and
`LIT-725` says so in its own words:** "perturbation-based attributions measure
the prediction difference after perturbing a set of features. Zeiler & Fergus
2014 use such perturbations with grey patch occlusions on CNNs." `LIT-725` is
yesterday's filing; its infidelity measure is a perturbation-based fidelity
criterion, and its "Square" perturbation — uniform over square patches — is a
grey square with a distribution over it.

So the record acquired a perturbation-based metric on 2026-09-25 and did not
hold the perturbation-based method it descends from, which is the same shape as
every unit this week and was invisible to the re-read's grep, because the word
`occlusion` in this record mostly means a robot arm or a viewpoint.

**And the 2013 paper makes a move `SOTA-430` structurally cannot.** `SOTA-430`
is "a rejection rule, not a certificate" by its own words. Zeiler and Fergus
validate their visualizations *positively*: occlude what the map highlights,
and the feature map's activity drops, "hence validating the other
visualizations". Three examples and a figure, so it is not evidence of much —
but it is the direction the record's attribution cluster has none of except
inside `LIT-725`'s infidelity.

No practice filed for it. The quantitative form is already held under
`SOTA-435`, and filing the qualitative antecedent separately would be filing on
antecedence rather than on a defect.

## Churn worth admitting

`SOTA-430` is at v4 and all four versions are dated today. v2 added the
critique's step and moved consensus to `contested`; v3 added a third source for
the metric split and sharpened the `promote_when`; v4 names the two referents.

Each was driven by a source arriving rather than by a rereading, which is the
distinction that makes it acceptable — the practice was not unstable, the
cluster around it went from two documents to seven in one day. The history entry
says that, because a reader seeing four versions in one date stamp should be
able to tell which kind of churn it was.

## Residue

Five reversals left from the `#290` re-read: Grad-CAM, DeepLIFT,
InputXGradient, plain-gradient saliency, SmoothGrad. All referent gaps —
`SOTA-430` states each verdict correctly — so they rank below `1912.09818`,
which is now the cluster's highest-value unread paper and the only one that
would let the record say *why* half its verdict table fails.
