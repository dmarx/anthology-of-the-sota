# LIT-793: Flow map matching with stochastic interpolants: A mathematical framework for consistency models

Boffi, Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden, Courant Institute (2024) —
[ARXIV-2406.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07507). Read at v2 (3 Jun 2025), main text and Appendices A–D.
v1 (11 Jun 2024) was titled "Flow Map Matching"; v2 added the subtitle.

## Key takeaways

- **The object is the two-time map** (§3.2, Def. 3.4, Props. 3.5–3.6).
  X_{s,t} carries any ODE solution from time s to time t. It satisfies a
  Lagrangian equation, ∂_t X_{s,t}(x) = b_t(X_{s,t}(x)), and an Eulerian
  one, ∂_s X_{s,t} + b_s·∇X_{s,t} = 0. Its composition rule
  X_{t,τ}∘X_{s,t} = X_{s,τ} is the consistency property, stated over two
  times. A consistency model is the one-time special case that always maps
  to the data end (App. C.2, Eq. C.12).
- **Two distillation losses, one minimizer, different behaviour** (§3.3,
  Cors. 3.7–3.8). Lagrangian map distillation (LMD) penalizes
  ∂_t X − b(X). Eulerian map distillation (EMD) penalizes the Eulerian
  residual, which needs the map's spatial Jacobian. EMD is the
  continuous-time limit of consistency distillation (App. C.2, Eq. C.18).
  Both control the squared 2-Wasserstein distance to the teacher (Props.
  3.9–3.10). The LMD bound carries a Lipschitz-constant factor; the EMD
  bound does not, but involves ∇X.
- **The Lagrangian loss wins by a wide margin** (Table 1, Fig. 4B). On
  CIFAR-10 with a 5.53-FID teacher, LMD gets 7.13 FID at two steps and 6.04
  at four, against EMD's 48.32 and 44.35. Teacher-FID is 1.27 and 1.05
  against 34.19 and 30.74. On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32, LMD's loss falls an
  order of magnitude faster. The authors attribute EMD's failure to the
  spatial gradient of a nearly singular map (§3.4).
- **Direct training, with no teacher** (§3.5, Prop. 3.11). The loss
  replaces the unknown velocity with the interpolant's time derivative,
  evaluated at X_{s,t}(X_{t,s}(I_t)), plus an invertibility term. Learning
  the full one-step map directly was "challenging". Training on a strip
  |t − s| ≤ 1/K and then distilling the K-step map into one step (§3.7,
  Lemma 3.13) is what worked.
- **Few-step numbers without a teacher** (Table 2, ImageNet-32, strip
  0.25). FID 16.90, 14.48, 12.61 and 9.68 at 4, 6, 8 and 20 steps, against
  minibatch-OT flow matching's 38.86, 22.08, 15.64 and 7.71.
- **Consistency training is not gradient descent on a loss** (§3.6, Prop.
  3.12). Replacing the teacher velocity by the interpolant's derivative does
  not give an equivalent loss, because the squared term is quadratic in it.
  Consistency models put a stop-gradient there. The flow map is then a
  critical point of the update, with no guarantee it is stable or
  attractive.

## Where the hedges are

Per [DP-010](../../docs/design-principles.md#dp-10):

- **"Sample quality comparable to flow matching while reducing generation
  time by a factor of 10–20"** (abstract). The supporting number is
  CIFAR-10 LMD: 6.04 at four steps against a 5.53 teacher run with an
  adaptive fifth-order solver. The paper does not count the teacher's
  evaluations. One-step image quality appears only as training curves
  (Fig. 4B), not in a table.
- **"Consistency models do not benefit from multistep sampling"** (§2).
  Consistency Models ([LIT-093](LIT-093.md)) and iCT ([LIT-786](LIT-786.md)) both report two-step
  sampling improving on one-step on ImageNet-64: 6.20 to 4.70 for
  consistency distillation, 3.25 to 2.77 for iCT-deep. What a one-time map
  cannot do is stop at an intermediate time; it can still re-noise and
  denoise again.
- **The 2D figure and its text disagree.** §4.1 says the one-step map learned
  over all of [0, 1]² "performs worse" than the four-step strip map. Fig. 3's
  caption says it "performs as well as SI", with KL 0.017 against SI's 0.016
  and the four-step map's 0.008.
- **The teachers were "trained with limited compute"** (§4.2). The
  experiments are 32 × 32, one U-Net, four GPUs, and 1–2.5 × 10⁵ steps
  (Table 4). No consistency, trajectory or progressive-distillation model
  is run on the same setup.
- **The ImageNet-32 baselines are quoted**, not re-run (Table 2 caption).
  FMM edges ahead only below about 8 steps. At 20 steps minibatch OT is
  better.
- **LMD is better in distribution and worse at keeping the teacher's
  coupling** (App. D, Table 3, Fig. 6). On the checkerboard its KL is lower
  than EMD's, but it sends more points to a different checker than the
  teacher does.

## Standing in the anthology

It extends the stochastic-interpolant framework ([LIT-645](LIT-645.md)), from the same
group. The velocity it distils or learns is that framework's
b_t(x) = E[İ_t | I_t = x] (Prop. 3.2), and every loss is an expectation over
the interpolant I_t. The paper adds a second time to the learned object.

On ImageNet-32 it quotes DDPM ([LIT-036](LIT-036.md)) and minibatch-OT flow matching
([LIT-789](LIT-789.md)) from the latter's tables. At 20 steps DDPM scores 63.08 FID
and BatchOT 7.71; at 4 steps, 362.37 and 38.86. Its own direct
training lands between them, 9.68 and 16.90.

It is the theory the other two-time papers in this batch cite. MeanFlow
([LIT-784](LIT-784.md)) notes that the flow map is its average velocity times the
interval. Shortcut Models ([LIT-787](LIT-787.md)) is the discrete, binary-step
version of the same composition rule. Appendix C.4 writes progressive
distillation ([LIT-067](LIT-067.md)) as matching one map step to two steps of the
previous map.

**On [SOTA-206](../practices.d/SOTA-206.md) it is a theoretical argument, with thin numbers behind it.**
A two-time map can be used at any step count, and §1 calls post-training
step choice a central advantage. The image results run two to twenty steps.
