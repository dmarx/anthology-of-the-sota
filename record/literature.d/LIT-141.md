# LIT-141: Hyper-Connections

Zhu et al., ByteDance (2024; ICLR 2025) — [ARXIV-2409.19606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606)

## Key takeaways

- The residual connection's variants trade one failure for another: too
  little skip and gradients vanish, too much and representations collapse
  into sameness across depth — the paper's *seesaw*. Hyper-connections
  widen the residual into n parallel streams (n = 2 in most experiments)
  with learnable, possibly input-dependent, mixing between them.
- The learned mixing lets the network adjust connection strength between
  features at different depths and effectively rearrange layers; streams
  exchange information laterally as well as vertically.
- Pretraining experiments on dense and sparse (MoE) language models show
  consistent improvements over standard residual connections.

## Standing in the anthology

Superseded by [LIT-140](LIT-140.md). The successor's premise is that the free mixing here
discards the identity mapping a residual guarantees, which is what made
HC unstable and hard to scale; constraining the mixing keeps the width and
restores the property. Kept because what it fixed is the content: a reader who meets
mHC needs to know what free mixing cost. The
