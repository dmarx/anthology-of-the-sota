C4 and The Pile, closing the pretraining-corpus half of the same gap the
vision datasets opened. Three things worth keeping.

## The filing threshold is the wrong instrument

C4 has forty-six documents behind it and The Pile has five. By the count this
record has been using — BBH at seven, OlympiadBench at six — The Pile is
under the line, and the tail entries this journal declined last pass
(RedPajama 2, Dolma 1) are under it by the same margin.

The Pile is worth more than its count and they are not, and the difference is
not volume. **It is whether the instrument appears in the denominator.**
`SOTA-238`'s headline is +6.5 points *over The Pile's default weights*;
`LIT-391` and `LIT-117` are the same shape. Those weights are a judgement its
authors made, stated as one, and never claimed to be optimal — so the size of
every gain in the data-mixing line is partly a property of a baseline nobody
optimised. A corpus shipped with better defaults would make the same method
look worse without the method changing.

That is a bound no citing document states and none of them could, because
there was nowhere to write it. RedPajama and Dolma, by contrast, are mentioned
in passing as corpora a model was trained on; nothing is measured against
them. **A benchmark cited twice as a yardstick earns a note; a benchmark cited
twenty times as a setting may not.** Count is a proxy for that and a poor one.

## The Pile is the substrate *because* it ships domains

Worth stating plainly because it is easy to read the data-mixing line as
being about mixing and incidentally about The Pile. It is the other way
round. A corpus that is one undifferentiated mass of web text cannot host a
question about domain proportions at all — 22 named sub-corpora with declared
weights is the precondition, not the setting. Whoever next reads a data-mixing
result should know that the instrument selected the research question.

## C4 is two instruments wearing one name, again

Not two *datasets* this time — the ImageNet/ILSVRC problem — but two roles,
and they need opposite caveats.

As **a corpus with a known defect** C4 is irreplaceable in `SOTA-164`, whose
entire case is that `LIT-202` found a 61-word English sentence repeated
thousands of times in it. Chasing that down produced the sharper version now
in both documents: **C4 shipped already deduplicated by its own authors.**
T5's cleaning pipeline includes a deduplication pass, and the repeats
survived it. That is what makes the two-granularity recommendation a
requirement rather than a refinement — one pass is demonstrably not enough,
and C4 is the demonstration.

As **a standard workload** for `SOTA-155`, `LIT-212`, `LIT-252`, `LIT-276`
and `THEORY-014`, nothing depends on the corpus being C4. It is there because
everyone else used it, which is `DP-007` exactly. The two roles sit in one
note with a paragraph each, and a reader who takes the second for the first
will over-read every distributed-training result in the record.

## An aside on where the paper was

T5 is named in passing in several documents and the paper was not in the
record at all. It is one of the most-cited architectures of its period, and it
entered here as a data-pipeline note, filed for its corpus rather than its
framework — tagged `data-pipeline` alone, with no second tag, because `ADR-035`
means an incidental `model-architecture` tag would bind chain edges this note
has no business binding. The document says what the record wants it for, not
what the paper contains.
