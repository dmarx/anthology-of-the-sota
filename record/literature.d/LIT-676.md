# LIT-676: DPM-Solver++: Fast Solver for Guided Sampling of Diffusion Probabilistic Models

<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-104 — Proposed, and this note is its only source. Open is exactly what is asserted about it: the FID
     inversion is measured and the convergence-radius reason is argued, which is why it is not Active. -->

Lu, Zhou, Bao, Chen, Li and Zhu (2022) — [ARXIV-2211.01095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01095)

## Key takeaways

**The gap it opens with.** Guided sampling with a large guidance scale is how
text-to-image models are actually run, and the paper's charge is that nobody had
checked the fast samplers there:

> Although recent works propose dedicated high-order solvers and achieve a
> further speedup for sampling without guidance, **their effectiveness for guided
> sampling has not been well-tested before.** In this work, we demonstrate that
> previous high-order fast samplers suffer from instability issues, and they even
> become slower than DDIM when the guidance scale grows large.

**The table that settles it.** ImageNet 256×256, discrete-time model of Dhariwal
& Nichol, classifier guidance scale **8.0**, no thresholding, FID (lower better):

| sampler | 10 NFE | 15 | 20 | 25 | 250 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DDIM — order 1 | **13.04** | 11.27 | 10.21 | 9.87 | 9.37 |
| PNDM — order 2 | 99.80 | 37.59 | 15.50 | 11.54 | — |
| DPM-Solver-2 | **114.62** | 44.05 | 20.33 | 9.84 | — |
| DPM-Solver-3 | **164.74** | 91.59 | 64.11 | 29.40 | — |
| DEIS-3 | 66.86 | 24.48 | 12.98 | 10.87 | — |
| DPM-Solver++(2S) | 12.20 | 9.85 | 9.19 | 9.32 | — |
| DPM-Solver++(2M) | 14.44 | **9.46** | **9.10** | **9.11** | — |

At 10 NFE the ordering is **monotone in the wrong direction** — 13.04, 114.62,
164.74 for orders 1, 2, 3. DPM-Solver-2 needs 25 evaluations to match what DDIM
does in 25, and DPM-Solver-3 is still four times worse there. Meanwhile
DPM-Solver++(2M) reaches **9.10 at 20 NFE**, better than DDIM manages at **250**.

**Two causes, and only one of them is new to this record.**

*Convergence radius.* A large guidance scale amplifies both the model's output
and its derivatives. High-order solvers are built out of high-order derivatives,
which are "generally more sensitive to the amplifications", so the convergence
radius narrows and the solver needs a much smaller step size to converge at all.
At a fixed evaluation budget the higher-order method is therefore worse than the
lower-order one. Filed as [THEORY-104](../theory.d/THEORY-104.md).

*Train–test mismatch.* Large guidance pushes the conditional noise prediction
away from the true noise, so the converged `x₀` "fall[s] out of the bound" of the
data range. **This record already holds that mechanism**, as [SOTA-202](../practices.d/SOTA-202.md), from
Imagen — where high guidance weights were destroying images and the cause was
arithmetic. The contribution here is what it implies for solver design, below.

**The fix, in three parts.**

1. **Solve the ODE for the data prediction `x_θ` rather than the noise
   prediction `ε_θ`.** The paper's reason is that this is what makes bounding
   *available*: "thresholding methods are further available to keep the samples
   bounded". You cannot clamp `x̂₀` at every step if your solver's state is not
   `x̂₀`. That is the connection [SOTA-202](../practices.d/SOTA-202.md) could not make on its own.
2. **Multistep rather than singlestep**, to reduce the effective step size and
   so work inside the narrowed convergence radius — DPM-Solver++(2M) against
   (2S).
3. **Stop at order 2.** Stated flatly: "For `k ≥ 3`, as discussed in Sec. 3,
   high-order solvers may be unsuitable for large guidance scales, thus we mainly
   consider `k = 2` in this work". The ladder does not continue.

**Where the headline lands.** 15 to 20 steps for high-quality guided samples, in
pixel space and latent space. On COCO with Stable Diffusion the comparison is run
at guidance 7.5, "which is the recommended setting for stable-diffusion" — so the
evaluation regime is the shipped one rather than a favourable one.

## Standing in the anthology

Re-measured into first place among the remaining
[#342](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/issues/342) Tier B items,
from last by mention count. The reason is [SOTA-203](../practices.d/SOTA-203.md), which is `Active` with
`consensus: universal`, recommends sampling "with a higher-order ODE solver on
the weights you already trained", and mentions guidance **nowhere** — in a
document whose own implementations line reads "default or selectable sampler in
essentially every diffusion serving stack", which is exactly where guidance is on
by default.

The practice is not wrong; its scope was never written down. What this note
supplies:

- the bound, carried onto [SOTA-203](../practices.d/SOTA-203.md), including the inversion of its own
  rhetorical point about DDIM being the family's weakest member;
- [SOTA-410](../practices.d/SOTA-410.md), the recommendation for the guided regime;
- [THEORY-104](../theory.d/THEORY-104.md), the convergence-radius account;
- and the parameterization consequence for [SOTA-202](../practices.d/SOTA-202.md), which had the mechanism and
  not its implication for solvers.

**What it does not cover.** Orders above 2 under guidance are left open by the
authors rather than ruled out — "leave the solvers for higher orders for future
study". Everything is image diffusion, pixel- and latent-space.

**The gap this note named is closed.** It was filed saying the record did not
hold classifier-free guidance (`2207.12598`) and that this made it the
most-referenced unheld paper in the cluster. It is now [LIT-tmpy02tv](LIT-tmpy02tv.md), filed
the same day and partly because this note said so. What is still unheld is the
antecedent on the other side — Dhariwal & Nichol (`2105.05233`), whose model
this paper's entire ImageNet table is run on.
