Unit 5 of `#342`, on the record's largest cluster — 127 documents naming
quantization, 18 practices. The finding is a defect of a kind this record has
not filed before: not a missing document, not a wrong edge, but **a correct
forecast made about the wrong variable.**

## The bet

`LIT-586` is LLM.int8(). Its Standing section ends:

> "Without any performance degradation" is doing a lot of work… The
> **emergent-outlier characterization is the part that has been independently
> built on**; the no-degradation headline is the part a later, harder
> evaluation could move.

That is a good sentence. It identifies that the paper makes two separable
claims, flags the one that is an absence-of-evidence result, and predicts
where the paper will erode. It is exactly the kind of judgement the record
exists to hold.

It is also backwards. **The no-degradation headline has held.** What moved is
*emergent* — and it moved because somebody ran the controlled version of the
question that the original could not, holding architecture fixed and varying
weight decay, dropout, gradient clipping and training dtype from 410M to 52B.

## Why the bet was wrong, which is the useful part

The sentence reasoned about **the form of the claim**: a no-degradation result
is an absence-of-evidence claim on a fixed evaluation suite, and those are
fragile. True, and it is `DP-010`'s shape.

What it did not reason about is **what kind of evidence each claim rested on**.

- *No degradation up to 175B* — a measurement. Fragile to a harder
  evaluation, robust to everything else.
- *Outliers are emergent at scale* — an **inference from a correlation across
  models trained by different groups with different recipes.** Fragile to
  anyone who trains the models themselves.

The second is much weaker and the section read it as the strong one, because
it had been **cited more**. The give-away is in the sentence itself:
*"independently built on."* That measures adoption. `DP-005` exists to keep
adoption out of the evidence column, and this is that principle applied one
level up — not to a recommendation, but to **which of a paper's claims the
record expects to survive.**

**A characterization that many papers build on is well adopted. It is not
well replicated, and those come apart exactly as they do for practices.**

## What replication found

Worth recording in full, because it is the only independent attempt at this
detection the record holds:

> we find that a threshold of 6.0 is **too high** to classify a feature
> dimension as an outlier for all the variants we consider. After
> correspondence with the authors, we also explored various adaptations of
> this outlier detection recipe… However, **we did not observe a clear
> correlation between these measures and sensitivity to quantization.**

`SOTA-355`'s step 2 is *detect the feature dimensions whose magnitudes are far
outside the rest*. One group, in contact with the original authors, could not
do it in a way that predicted what it is supposed to predict.

The practice keeps its status, and the reason is worth being precise about:
**the shipped implementations work.** `load_in_8bit=True` has a detector that
holds up in production, and the decomposition is sound on models that have
outliers. What failed is the published *criterion*, reproduced from the paper
by someone else. Those are different objects and the practice now says so.

## A detail that explains more than it looks like

OPT-6B's LayerNorm gain parameters are **all hardcoded to 1.0**. Ahmadian et
al. flag it, say they could not find the decision documented in the OPT paper
or repository, and note it makes `STD(g)` exactly zero for that model.

OPT is the family the emergent-outlier story was largely measured on. An
undocumented architectural decision in the reference model for a phenomenon is
the kind of thing that turns a property of scale into a property of one lab's
configuration, and nobody would find it without training their own models and
going looking.

## What this unit files, and one thing it does not

Three documents plus two corrections. The one it does **not** file is a
retraction: outliers are real, `SOTA-355` handles them correctly, and the new
practice reduces the outliers you get rather than showing that everyone else's
do not exist. The claim that moved is **inevitability**, which is weaker than
existence and much more useful — it is what turns a repair into a choice.

The broader question the source raises and the record should hold onto: a
discontinuity measured across models trained by *different groups with
different recipes* is not the same object as one measured across models
trained identically at increasing size. The emergent-capabilities literature
mostly has the first kind.
