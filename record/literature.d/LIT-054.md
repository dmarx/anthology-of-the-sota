# LIT-054: GLM-130B: An Open Bilingual Pre-trained Model

Zeng et al., Tsinghua and Zhipu.AI (2022) — [ARXIV-2210.02414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02414)

## Key takeaways

- A 130B-parameter bilingual model, open-sourced together with its training
  logs and its "lessons learned" — which is the reason it is in the record.
  The paper describes itself as unveiling *how* a model of this scale can be
  successfully pre-trained, and names loss spikes and divergence as the
  numerous unexpected challenges it faced.
- Stability is treated as a design problem with training strategies attached,
  not as bad luck: the choices that make the run survive are reported next to
  the choices that make it efficient.
- On benchmarks it outperforms GPT-3 175B (davinci) across a range of English
  tasks — an advantage the authors note is *not* observed for OPT-175B or
  BLOOM-176B, which is a claim about the training recipe rather than the
  scale.
- A scaling property of the model permits INT4 quantization with no
  post-training and almost no loss, making it the first 100B-scale model
  runnable on 4×RTX 3090 or 8×RTX 2080 Ti.

## Standing in the anthology

The source of the record's instability practices: [SOTA-069](../practices.d/SOTA-069.md) (monitor the
loss on a scale where a spike is visible early), [SOTA-070](../practices.d/SOTA-070.md) (track gradient
norm ratios between layers), [SOTA-071](../practices.d/SOTA-071.md) (clip with a threshold that adapts,
extending [LIT-037](LIT-037.md)) and [SOTA-072](../practices.d/SOTA-072.md) (catch NaNs before they propagate). It also
carries [SOTA-099](../practices.d/SOTA-099.md), gradient-norm statistics as an instability detector.

These practices reached the record with a fabricated citation, which is a
particular shame for this subject: instability handling is folklore
precisely because most runs do not publish their failures. GLM-130B and PaLM
([LIT-069](LIT-069.md)) are in the record because they did.
